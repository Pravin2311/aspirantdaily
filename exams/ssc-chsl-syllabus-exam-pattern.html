SSC CHSL 2026 Syllabus and Exam Pattern: Tier-I, Tier-II and Skill Test from the Official Notice

Posts and pay levels • Age and qualification • Tier-I pattern • Tier-II sections • Skill and typing test • Indicative syllabus • How the merit list is made

SSC 📅 Last updated: 2026-11-01

The Staff Selection Commission published the notice for the Combined Higher Secondary (10+2) Level Examination, 2026 on 7 September 2026. Online applications ran from 7 September to 7 October 2026. The notice does not fix the exam dates; it says the Tier-I and Tier-II schedules will be notified later. This page sets out the pattern and syllabus exactly as the notice gives them, with page references so you can check each point in the original.

The age limit is 18 to 27 years, counted on 1 August 2026. In terms of dates of birth, that means candidates born not before 2 August 1999 and not later than 1 August 2008. Reserved categories get the usual relaxations in the upper age limit; the notice lists them category by category.

For LDC/JSA and most DEO posts, a pass in Class 12 or an equivalent examination from a recognised board is enough. DEO and DEO Grade 'A' posts in the Ministry of Consumer Affairs, Food and Public Distribution, the Staff Selection Commission itself and the Ministry of Culture need Class 12 in the Science stream with Mathematics. The qualification had to be held by the cut-off date of 7 October 2026.

Tier-I is an objective, multiple-choice test of four parts. The parts are not set in a fixed order.

PartSubjectQuestionsMarks
IEnglish Language (Basic Knowledge)2550
IIGeneral Intelligence2550
IIIQuantitative Aptitude (Basic Arithmetic Skill)2550

That makes 100 questions in total.

The maximum is 200 marks, and the whole test lasts 60 minutes with a sectional timer of 15 minutes for each subject. Candidates eligible for a scribe get 80 minutes, with 20 minutes per subject.

Each wrong answer costs 0.50 marks. So 20 correct and 5 wrong answers in one part give 40 − 2.5 = 37.5 marks.

Tier-II is held on a single day in two sessions. Session-I runs for 2 hours and 15 minutes and covers Sections I, II and III, each of which closes automatically when its time ends. Session-II is the skill or typing test.

Each question in Sections I to III carries 3 marks, and each wrong answer in those sections costs 1 mark. Candidates must qualify every section of Tier-II.

For example, 50 correct and 10 wrong answers in Section-I score 150 − 10 = 140 out of 180.

The Computer Knowledge Test is compulsory but only qualifying, and so is the skill or typing test.

Skill and typing test

  • DEO in the three offices named above: 15,000 key depressions per hour, judged on a 15-minute passage.
  • Other DEO posts: 8,000 key depressions per hour, also in 15 minutes.
  • LDC/JSA: typing at 35 words per minute in English or 30 in Hindi, on a passage typed in 10 minutes. The medium chosen in the application is final, and Hindi must be typed in Mangal font.

The notice calls its syllabus 'indicative'. Tier-I and Tier-II share most topics; Tier-II goes deeper and adds a few.

English

Spot the error, fill in the blanks, synonyms and homonyms, antonyms, spellings, idioms and phrases, one-word substitution, sentence improvement, active and passive voice, direct and indirect speech, shuffling of sentence parts and of sentences, cloze passage and comprehension. In Tier-II, comprehension uses two or more paragraphs, one based on a book or story and one on a current-affairs editorial or report.

General Intelligence and Reasoning

Verbal and non-verbal questions: semantic, symbolic, number and figural analogy and classification, series, space orientation, Venn diagrams, drawing inferences, punched-hole and pattern folding, embedded figures, critical thinking, problem solving, emotional and social intelligence, word building, coding-decoding and numerical operations.

Quantitative Aptitude and Mathematical Abilities

  • Number systems and fundamental arithmetic: percentages, ratio and proportion, square roots, averages, simple and compound interest, profit and loss, discount, partnership, mixture and alligation, time and distance, time and work.
  • Algebra: basic identities, elementary surds and graphs of linear equations.
  • Geometry: triangles and their centres, congruence and similarity, circles, chords and tangents.
  • Mensuration: plane figures and solids from prisms and cylinders to spheres and pyramids.
  • Trigonometry: ratios, complementary angles, heights and distances, standard identities.
  • Data: histograms, frequency polygons, bar diagrams and pie charts. Tier-II adds mean, median, mode, standard deviation and simple probability.

General Awareness

Current events, everyday science, and India and its neighbours, especially history, culture, geography, the economic scene, general policy and scientific research.

Computer Knowledge (Tier-II only)

Organisation of a computer, CPU, input and output devices, memory, ports and keyboard shortcuts; Windows and MS Office basics; web browsing, e-mail and e-banking; and networking devices, protocols and security threats such as viruses, worms and Trojans.

Minimum qualifying marks apply to Tier-I and to Sections I, II and III of Tier-II: 30% for unreserved candidates, 25% for OBC and EWS, and 20% for all other categories. Tier-I performance decides who is called to Tier-II.

The final merit list is drawn from the aggregate of Sections I and II of Tier-II only, a maximum of 360 marks, provided the candidate has qualified Section III and the skill or typing test. Posts are then allotted by merit and the preferences the candidate submits. If two candidates tie, the notice breaks the tie by Section-I marks of Tier-II, then Tier-I marks, and finally date of birth, with the older candidate placed higher.

Because Tier-II carries the merit, the quantitative and English modules decide most outcomes. Topic notes that match the syllabus include the trigonometry table, standard deviation and variance and squares and cubes. For the graduate-level exam, see the SSC CGL syllabus.

Do Tier-I marks count in the final merit?

No. The notice uses Tier-I only to shortlist candidates for Tier-II. The merit list is made from the aggregate of Sections I and II of Tier-II, provided the candidate also qualifies Section III and the skill or typing test. Tier-I marks are used only as the second tie-breaker.

When is the CHSL 2026 Tier-I exam?

The notice says the Tier-I and Tier-II schedules are 'to be notified later'. Watch the SSC notice board at ssc.gov.in for the exam schedule and city intimation; this page will be updated once the Commission announces it.

Is English compulsory in Tier-II?

Yes. Section II includes English Language and Comprehension, and the notice says that module is not offered in other languages. Every Tier-II section must be qualified.
