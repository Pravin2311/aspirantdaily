SSC Stenographer Grade C and D Syllabus and Exam Pattern: CBE, Skill Test Speeds and Transcription Time from the 2026 Notice

Candidates eligible for a scribe get 2 hours 40 minutes (40, 40 and 80 minutes). The paper is objective and set in English and Hindi, and each wrong answer costs 0.25 marks. English is half the paper, and it is also the first tie-breaker, so it matters more than any other section.

General Intelligence and Reasoning: verbal and non-verbal questions, including analogies, similarities and differences, space visualisation, problem solving, analysis, judgement, decision making, visual memory, observation, relationship concepts, arithmetical reasoning, verbal and figure classification, and number and non-verbal series, along with questions on abstract ideas and symbols.

General Awareness: the environment and its application to society, current events, and everyday science, plus India and its neighbouring countries: sports, history, culture, geography, the economic scene, general polity including the Indian Constitution, and scientific research. The notice says these questions should not need special study of any discipline.

English Language and Comprehension: vocabulary, grammar, sentence structure, synonyms, antonyms and correct usage. The notice adds that writing ability will also be tested.

Practice: reasoning topics and current affairs. The SSC CHSL and SSC MTS pages cover similar sections.

Candidates shortlisted from the CBE take a skill test: one dictation lasting 10 minutes, in English or Hindi as chosen in the application, which must then be transcribed on a computer.

The word counts are speed multiplied by 10 minutes. To transcribe 1,000 words in 40 minutes, a Grade C candidate must type about 25 words a minute from their shorthand notes; Grade D needs about 16 words a minute over 50 minutes. Grade C demands both faster shorthand and faster typing.

If you take the test in Hindi, you must learn English stenography after appointment, and the reverse for English. Your probation may not be cleared otherwise, and you work in whichever language the office needs.

CBE marks decide who is shortlisted, by category, for the skill test of each post applied for. The skill test is mandatory but only qualifying; SSC fixes a category-wise standard for each post. Those who pass are then allocated posts on CBE merit and their post and department preferences.

If CBE totals tie, the tie goes to the candidate with more English marks, then more General Awareness marks, then the older candidate, then alphabetical order.

Do skill test marks count in the SSC Stenographer merit list?

No. The skill test is qualifying only. Once you clear it, your rank and post allocation depend on your CBE marks and your post preferences.

What dictation speed is needed for SSC Stenographer?

100 words per minute for Grade C and 80 words per minute for Grade D, in a single 10-minute dictation in English or Hindi.

Can I apply for both Grade C and Grade D?

Yes. The application form asks you to choose Grade C, Grade D or both, and you are shortlisted for the skill test of each post you applied for.
