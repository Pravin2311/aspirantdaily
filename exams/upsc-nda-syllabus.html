All papers are objective. The Mathematics paper and Part B of the GAT are set in both Hindi and English. Calculators and mathematical or logarithmic tables are not allowed, and no scribe is permitted. UPSC may fix qualifying marks in any or all subjects.

Each wrong answer costs one third (0.33) of the marks assigned to that question. With four options per question, a blind guess loses marks on average, so mark only answers you can narrow down.

  1. Algebra: sets, Venn diagrams, De Morgan's laws, relations; complex numbers and cube roots of unity; binary numbers; AP, GP and HP; quadratic equations; linear inequations; permutations and combinations; binomial theorem; logarithms.
  2. Matrices and determinants: types and operations, properties of determinants, adjoint and inverse, Cramer's rule and the matrix method.
  3. Trigonometry: degrees and radians, ratios, identities, sum and difference formulae, multiple and sub-multiple angles, inverse functions, heights and distances, properties of triangles.
  4. Analytical geometry (2D and 3D): lines, circles, parabola, ellipse and hyperbola, eccentricity; direction cosines, planes, lines and spheres in space.
  5. Differential calculus: functions, limits, continuity, derivatives and their applications, second-order derivatives, maxima and minima.
  6. Integral calculus and differential equations: substitution and parts, definite integrals and areas; order and degree, first-order equations, growth and decay.
  7. Vector algebra: dot and cross products, work done and moment of a force.
  8. Statistics and probability: frequency distributions and graphs; mean, median, mode, variance and standard deviation; correlation and regression; classical and statistical probability, conditional probability, Bayes' theorem, binomial distribution.

For quick revision of two of these units, see the trigonometric ratios table and standard deviation and variance.

The GAT has two parts: English for 200 marks and General Knowledge for 400.

Part A, English, tests understanding of English and 'workmanlike use of words': grammar and usage, vocabulary, comprehension and cohesion in extended text.

Part B, General Knowledge, is divided into six sections, with approximate weightages given in the notice:

  • Physics: properties of matter, motion and Newton's laws, heat, sound, light, magnetism, electricity, and how everyday devices from pendulums to periscopes work.
  • Chemistry: physical and chemical changes, elements and compounds, air and water, common gases, acids, bases and salts, forms of carbon, fertilisers, atomic structure and valency.
  • General Science: cells and tissues, the human body, epidemics, food and balanced diet, the solar system, and eminent scientists.
  • History: Indian history and culture, the freedom movement, the Constitution and administration, Panchayati Raj, Gandhian ideas, and world events from the Renaissance to the United Nations.
  • Geography: the earth and its movements, rocks, weathering, earthquakes and volcanoes, ocean currents and tides, the atmosphere, winds and climate, and India's resources, industries, ports and trade.
  • Current Events: important recent events in India and the world, and prominent personalities.

The atmosphere topics are covered in detail on layers of the atmosphere.

Candidates who clear the written exam appear before a Services Selection Board for an intelligence and personality test. Stage I has the Officer Intelligence Rating tests and the Picture Perception and Description Test; only those who clear it go on to Stage II. Stage II runs over four days: an interview, Group Testing Officer tasks, psychology tests and a conference. Candidates choosing the Air Force who clear the SSB also take the Computerised Pilot Selection System.

Candidates must reach the qualifying marks in the written exam and the SSB separately. They are then ranked on a single combined list using the written total and the SSB marks, a maximum of 1,800. Ties go first to the higher written total, then to more marks in the GAT, then to the older candidate. Allocation to the Army, Navy, Air Force or Naval Academy follows merit, eligibility, medical fitness and the order of preferences given in the application.

How much negative marking is there in NDA?

One third (0.33) of the marks assigned to a question is deducted for each wrong answer. Unanswered questions are not penalised, so guessing among four options without eliminating any is a losing bet on average.

Is the syllabus the same for the NDA (I) and (II) exams?

This page follows the NDA and NA (II), 2026 notice. Each sitting has its own notice with its own scheme and syllabus, so check the next notice on upsc.gov.in when it is published; this page will be updated against it.

Which part of the GAT carries the most marks?

Part B, General Knowledge, carries 400 of the 600 marks. Within it, Physics carries about 25%, and History and Geography about 20% each.
