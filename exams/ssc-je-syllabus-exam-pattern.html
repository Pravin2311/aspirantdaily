SSC JE Syllabus and Exam Pattern: Paper I, Paper II and Branch Topics from the Official 2026 Notice

1,748 vacancies • Age 30 or 32 • Paper I: 200 marks, qualifying • Paper II: 300 marks, decides merit • Civil, Electrical, Mechanical and new parts • Qualifying percentages

The Staff Selection Commission's Junior Engineer Examination recruits civil, electrical and mechanical engineers for central government organisations, and in 2026 it also covers Scientific Assistants for the India Meteorological Department and Junior Engineers (Telecom). This page follows the notice uploaded on 2 September 2026. Applications ran until 22 September 2026, and the tentative schedule placed Paper I in October/November 2026 and Paper II in December 2026.

Candidates eligible for a scribe get 2 hours 40 minutes for each paper. Both papers are computer-based and objective, in Hindi and English, and a scientific calculator is available on screen throughout.

The negative marking differs between papers. In Paper I each question carries 1 mark and a wrong answer costs 0.25. In Paper II each question carries 3 marks and a wrong answer costs 1 mark, a third of the question's value (about 0.33). Guessing is riskier in Paper II, so attempt only questions you can narrow down.

You attempt the same part in Paper I and Paper II, and it must match the stream you chose in the application; it cannot be changed later. Parts A to F are held in one batch and Part G in a separate batch. A candidate who qualifies for more than one post, for example Telecom and Electrical, sits the paper for each post applied for.

General Intelligence and Reasoning includes verbal and non-verbal questions: analogies, similarities and differences, space visualisation, problem solving, analysis, judgement, decision making, visual memory, observation, relationship concepts, arithmetical reasoning, verbal and figure classification and number series, along with questions on abstract ideas and symbols.

General Awareness tests awareness of the environment and its application to society, current events, and everyday science, plus India and its neighbouring countries: history, culture, geography, the economic scene, general polity and scientific research. The notice says these questions should not need special study of any discipline.

Practice material: reasoning topics and current affairs. The reasoning and GA sections of the SSC CHSL exam follow a similar style.

Part A, Civil and Structural: engineering mechanics, solid mechanics, structural analysis, construction materials and management, estimating, costing and valuation, concrete structures, steel structures, surveying, soil mechanics, fluid mechanics and hydraulics, hydrology and irrigation, environmental engineering, and transportation engineering.

Part B, Electrical: fundamentals of electrical engineering, magnetic circuits, AC fundamentals, measurement and measuring instruments, electrical machines, special machines and synchronous generators, generation, transmission and distribution, estimation and costing, utilisation of electrical energy, basic electronics and power electronics.

Part C, Mechanical: engineering mechanics, strength of materials, theory of machines, machine design, materials science, thermal engineering, fluid mechanics and hydraulic machinery, industrial engineering, manufacturing engineering, metrology and measurement, and advanced manufacturing processes.

Part D (Computer Science and IT) covers engineering mathematics, digital logic, computer organisation, programming and data structures, algorithms, theory of computation, compilers, operating systems, databases and networks. The notice prints full subtopic lists for every part; use it as your checklist.

Paper I is only a screening step. Candidates who reach the minimum are shortlisted for Paper II by category on their normalised Paper I marks, with separate lists for Civil, Electrical and Mechanical together, IMD and Telecom. Final selection and allocation to a post and organisation depend on normalised Paper II marks and the preferences you give. Electrical and Mechanical scores are normalised against each other because they compete for one list.

If Paper II totals tie, the notice breaks the tie using Paper I reasoning marks, then Paper I general awareness marks, then date of birth (older first), then alphabetical order of names.

Do Paper I marks count in the SSC JE final merit?

No. Paper I is qualifying and is used to shortlist candidates for Paper II. Final selection is based on Paper II and your post preferences; Paper I marks are used only to break ties.

How much negative marking is there in SSC JE Paper II?

One mark for each wrong answer. Each Paper II question carries 3 marks, so a wrong answer costs a third of a question's value. In Paper I the deduction is 0.25 on a 1-mark question.

Is a calculator allowed in SSC JE?

A scientific calculator is shown on the computer screen for the whole exam. You cannot bring your own.
