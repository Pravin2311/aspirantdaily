SSC CPO Sub-Inspector Syllabus and Exam Pattern: Paper I, PET/PST, Paper II and Vacancies from the Official 2026 Notice

SI in Delhi Police, CAPFs and CISF Fire • Age 20-25 (18-30 for SI Fire) • Paper I: 100 marks • Paper II: 320 marks • PET races and jumps • NCC bonus marks • Tie rules

SSC 📅 Last updated: 2026-11-01

The Staff Selection Commission's Sub-Inspector exam, usually called SSC CPO, recruits Sub-Inspectors for Delhi Police and the Central Armed Police Forces (CAPFs), and SI (Fire) for the CISF. This page follows the notice uploaded on 10 September 2026. Applications closed on 30 September 2026, and the notice said the computer-based exam schedule would be announced later.

All posts are in pay Level 6 (Rs 35,400 to 1,12,400). Vacancies are tentative and may change; the notice prints the category-wise split for each post.

  • Age on 1 August 2026: 20 to 25 for all posts except SI (Fire), which is 18 to 30. Category relaxations apply as per the notice.
  • Education: a bachelor's degree for SI in Delhi Police and SI (GD) in CAPFs. Final-year students may apply if they hold the degree by the cut-off date. SI (Fire) needs a B.Sc. with Physics, Chemistry and Mathematics, or Class 10 plus a three-year diploma in a listed engineering branch.
  • Driving licence: men applying for SI in Delhi Police need a valid LMV licence for motorcycle and car on the PET date. Without it, they are eligible for CAPF posts only.

All four stages are mandatory: Paper I, the Physical Standard and Endurance Tests (PST/PET), Paper II, and a Detailed Medical Examination.

Paper I totals 100 marks and Paper II totals 320, with English alone worth 200 of them. Questions are in Hindi and English for every part except English Comprehension. Each wrong answer costs 0.25 in Paper I and 0.50 in Paper II, which is a quarter of the question's value in both.

NCC certificate holders get bonus marks added to their normalised score in each paper: 5% of the maximum marks for a C certificate, 3% for B and 2% for A. On Paper II, a C certificate is worth 16 marks.

Both papers share one indicative syllabus; Paper II is set at a higher difficulty.

  • General Intelligence and Reasoning: verbal and non-verbal questions, including analogies, classification, series, coding-decoding, space visualisation and orientation, Venn diagrams, statement-conclusion and syllogistic reasoning.
  • General Knowledge and General Awareness: current events, everyday science, and India and its neighbours: history, culture, geography, the economic scene, general polity, the Indian Constitution and scientific research.
  • Quantitative Aptitude: number work, percentage, ratio and proportion, square roots, averages, interest, profit and loss, discount, partnership, mixture and alligation, time and distance, time and work, school algebra and surds, graphs of linear equations, triangles and their centres, congruence and similarity, circles and tangents, mensuration of prisms, cones, cylinders, spheres and pyramids, trigonometric ratios, identities and heights and distances, and histograms, frequency polygons, bar diagrams and pie charts.
  • English Comprehension: error recognition, fill in the blanks (verbs, prepositions, articles), vocabulary, spellings, grammar, sentence structure, synonyms, antonyms, sentence completion, phrases and idioms, and comprehension.

Revision pages: successive discount, weighted average and trigonometric ratios. The SSC CHSL syllabus covers similar reasoning and English topics.

The PST checks height, chest (men only; there is no chest requirement for women) and weight corresponding to height. Height and chest norms differ between Delhi Police, the CAPFs and SI (Fire), with relaxations for some groups, so check the tables in the notice. PST/PET carry no marks and are pass-or-fail. Ex-servicemen skip the PET but must meet the physical standards.

These minimums are counted without NCC bonus marks. Paper I results decide who is called to PST/PET; only those who pass the physical tests sit Paper II; and Paper II results decide who goes to the medical exam. Final selection and allocation to Delhi Police or a particular force are based on Paper II (with NCC bonus) and your preferences.

When the Paper I plus Paper II aggregate is tied, the tie goes to the higher Paper II total, then to more English marks in Paper II, then to the older candidate, then alphabetically.

Does SSC CPO Paper I count in the final merit?

Final selection is based on Paper II (plus any NCC bonus). Paper I shortlists candidates for the physical tests, and Paper I plus Paper II aggregate is the starting point for breaking ties.

What is the negative marking in SSC CPO?

0.25 marks per wrong answer in Paper I, where each question is worth 1 mark, and 0.50 per wrong answer in Paper II, where each question is worth 2 marks. In both papers a wrong answer costs a quarter of the question's value.

Is a driving licence needed for SSC CPO?

Only for male candidates who want SI in Delhi Police: a valid LMV licence for motorcycle and car on the PET date. Without it, men can still be considered for SI in the CAPFs.
