SSC MTS and Havaldar Syllabus and Exam Pattern: Two Sessions, Marks, PET and Cut-offs from the Official Notice

Class 10 eligibility • Age 18-25 or 18-27 • Session I: maths and reasoning, no negative marking • Session II: GA and English • Havaldar walk test and physical standards • Qualifying marks

SSC 📅 Last updated: 2026-11-01

The Staff Selection Commission's Multi-Tasking (Non-Technical) Staff exam fills Group C, Pay Level 1 posts across central ministries, departments and bodies, and the same exam recruits Havaldars for the Central Board of Indirect Taxes and Customs (CBIC) and the Central Bureau of Narcotics (CBN). This page follows the MTS and Havaldar Examination, 2025 notice, the latest MTS notice on ssc.gov.in when checked. When SSC publishes the next notice, compare its scheme with the one below.

Both sessions are compulsory and run back to back on the same day: Session I closes automatically after 45 minutes and Session II starts immediately. Candidates eligible for a scribe get 60 minutes per session. Session I totals 120 marks and Session II 150.

Every question carries 3 marks. Session I has no negative marking, so leave nothing blank there. In Session II a wrong answer costs one mark, a third of the question's value. Questions are available in Hindi, English and 13 regional languages for Session I and for General Awareness; the English section is in English.

Numerical and Mathematical Ability: integers and whole numbers, LCM and HCF, decimals and fractions, relationships between numbers, BODMAS, percentage, ratio and proportion, work and time, direct and inverse proportion, averages, simple interest, profit and loss, discount, area and perimeter of basic figures, distance and time, lines and angles, simple graphs and data, and squares and square roots.

Reasoning Ability and Problem Solving: alpha-numeric series, coding and decoding, analogy, following directions, similarities and differences, jumbling, problem solving and analysis, non-verbal reasoning with diagrams, age calculations, and calendar and clock.

General Awareness: social studies (history, geography, art and culture, civics, economics), general science and environmental studies, all up to Class 10 level.

English Language and Comprehension: vocabulary, grammar, sentence structure, synonyms, antonyms and correct usage, plus questions on a short passage.

For practice, see successive discount, weighted average and the reasoning topics. For the next exam up the SSC ladder, see the SSC CHSL pattern.

Height is relaxed by 5 cm for men and 2.5 cm for women, and women's weight by 2 kg, for Garhwalis, Assamese, Gorkhas and members of Scheduled Tribes. A candidate who fails PET/PST is dropped for Havaldar but still considered for MTS on CBE merit.

Session I is marked first, and Session II is evaluated only for those who qualify in Session I. For MTS, cut-offs are then set by category and by State/UT on normalised Session II marks, and final allocation follows Session II merit and your post-cum-State/UT preferences. For Havaldar, candidates are called to PET/PST at a ratio of 1 vacancy to 7 candidates, again on Session II.

If Session II totals tie, the tie goes to the candidate with more General Awareness marks, then more Session I marks, then the older candidate.

Is there negative marking in the SSC MTS exam?

Only in Session II (General Awareness and English), where each wrong answer costs one mark. Session I (maths and reasoning) has no negative marking, so attempt every question there.

Do Session I marks count for the MTS merit list?

Session I must be cleared, and it is used to break ties, but shortlisting and final merit are based on Session II. Session II is evaluated only if you qualify in Session I.

Is there a physical test for MTS?

No. Only Havaldar candidates take the PET (a timed walk) and PST (height, chest or weight). A candidate who fails PET/PST stays in the running for MTS on CBE merit.
