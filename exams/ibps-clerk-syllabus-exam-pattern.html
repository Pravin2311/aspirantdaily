IBPS Clerk Exam Pattern 2026 (CRP CSA-XVI): Prelims, Mains, Language Test and Eligibility

Post and pay • Age, graduation and computer literacy • Prelims pattern • Mains pattern • Negative marking • Local language test • State-wise vacancies

Banking 📅 Last updated: 2026-11-01

IBPS recruits Customer Service Associates, the clerical cadre of public sector banks, through its Common Recruitment Process. The sixteenth round, CRP CSA-XVI, covers vacancies of 2027-28. Registration ran from 1 to 21 August 2026. The tentative schedule placed the prelims in October 2026, the mains in December 2026 and provisional allotment in March 2027.

Candidates must be between 20 and 28 years old on 1 August 2026, that is, born between 2 August 1998 and 1 August 2006. The upper limit is relaxed for reserved categories as listed in the notification.

You need a degree in any discipline, held by 21 August 2026. Two further conditions are specific to the clerical post:

  • Local language. You must be able to read, write, speak and understand the specified local language of the State/UT you apply for, chosen from the list in the notification's Annexure III.
  • Computer literacy. A certificate, diploma or degree in computer operations or a language, or computers or IT studied as a subject at school or college, is mandatory.

Because recruitment is State/UT-wise, you may apply for the vacancies of one State/UT only, and you must sit the exam at a centre in that State/UT.

Each test is separately timed. To reach the mains you must clear the cut-off fixed by IBPS in all three tests, not only in the total.

The main examination is objective only, with four tests that are not held in a fixed sequence. Unlike the PO exam, there is no descriptive paper.

Every test has its own minimum score, and there is also a minimum total. Cut-offs depend on the number of vacancies in each State/UT. Only mains marks count for the final merit list; prelims marks are for screening.

In both the prelims and the mains, each wrong answer costs one fourth (0.25) of the marks assigned to that question. Questions left blank carry no penalty. Since the mains tests give different marks per question, the cost of a wrong answer varies by test.

Worked example: mains Reasoning Ability

60 marks over 40 questions means 1.5 marks per question and a penalty of 0.375 for a wrong answer.

With 28 correct and 8 wrong, the score is 28 × 1.5 − 8 × 0.375 = 42 − 3 = 39 out of 60.

Scores from different sessions are normalised using the equi-percentile method.

After the mains or provisional allotment, but before joining a bank, candidates take a test in the local language they opted for. It is marks-based and qualifying, and candidates who fail it are not offered appointment. Candidates who can show a Class 10 or higher marksheet or certificate proving they studied that language are exempt.

Is there an interview for IBPS Clerk?

No. The CSA process has an online preliminary and an online main examination, followed by a local language proficiency test before joining. Final merit is based only on mains marks.

Can I apply for more than one state?

No. Clerical recruitment in public sector banks is done state- or UT-wise, so you can apply for the vacancies of one State/UT only and must take the exam at a centre there. You should also be proficient in that State/UT's specified local language.

What is the total number of IBPS Clerk vacancies?

The updated annexure of 29 September 2026 gives vacancies state by state rather than a single all-India figure. Adding up the state and UT tables gives 11,663, with Union Bank of India yet to report. The figures are indicative and allotment follows the banks' actual vacancies.
