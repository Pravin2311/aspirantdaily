TNPSC Group IV (CCSE-IV) 2026: Syllabus, Exam Pattern, Vacancies and Key Dates from Notification 11/2026

Notification 11/2026 • Apply by 05.11.2026 • Exam 10.01.2027 • Single paper: 200 questions, 300 marks, 3 hours • Tamil test qualifier: 60 of 150 • SSLC standard

TNPSC 📅 Last updated: 2026-11-01

The Tamil Nadu Public Service Commission (TNPSC) fills Group IV posts such as Village Administrative Officer (VAO), Junior Assistant, Bill Collector, Typist, Steno Typist, Forest Guard and Forest Watcher through the Combined Civil Services Examination - IV (CCSE-IV). This page follows Notification No. 11/2026, dated 06.10.2026, and the Group IV syllabus file on TNPSC's website.

The vacancy count is tentative and can change before counselling, and the department-wise split is announced later. In the application you also choose whether to be considered for the Forest Guard with Driving Licence and Forest Watcher posts, because those posts add physical tests.

Group IV is a single written exam with no interview. The rank list comes straight from your total marks in this one paper, which is set at SSLC standard and answered on an OMR sheet.

Each question is worth 1.5 marks. Two thresholds apply:

  • Tamil test gate: Parts B and C are evaluated only if you score at least 40% in Part A, which is 60 of 150 marks. Below that, nothing else on your sheet counts.
  • Overall minimum: 90 marks out of 300 for all communities, which is 30% of the paper.

The Tamil test is not just a qualifier. Its marks are added to Parts B and C for ranking, so it is half of your total score.

Differently abled candidates who studied only English as a language at board or university level may take General English (SSLC standard) in place of the Tamil test, with the same 60-mark minimum. The notification says this paper has no translation part. TNPSC's syllabus file still lists a 5-question translation unit for it, so follow the notification.

Where the marks are: Units IV to VI carry 55 of the 75 questions. Science and geography together carry only 10, so a long science revision gives a poor return here.

  • Indian Polity: the Preamble and salient features, citizenship, Fundamental Rights, Fundamental Duties and DPSP, the Union and State executive and legislature, local government and Panchayat Raj, centre-state relations, elections and the judiciary, Lokpal and Lokayukta, the Right to Information, women's empowerment, consumer protection forums and the human rights charter. Start with Fundamental Rights, DPSP and Duties and Panchayati Raj.
  • Economy and development administration: the nature of the Indian economy, five-year plans, the Planning Commission and NITI Aayog, sources of revenue, the RBI, the Finance Commission, GST, employment, land reforms, industrial growth, rural welfare programmes, social justice, welfare schemes, and Tamil Nadu's education and health systems.
  • Tamil Nadu history and heritage: Tamil society and archaeological discoveries, Tamil literature from the Sangam age to the present, the Thirukkural and its values, and Tamil Nadu's role in the freedom struggle and its socio-political movements.
  • History of India: the Indus Valley, Guptas, Delhi Sultans, Mughals and Marathas, South Indian history, the early uprisings, the Indian National Congress, and leaders including B.R. Ambedkar, Bharathiar, V.O. Chidambaranar, Periyar, Kamarajar and Gandhi.
  • Science and geography: everyday physics, chemistry and life science, plus India's and Tamil Nadu's physical features, monsoon, soils, resources, population and disaster management.

These are SSLC-level sums, so speed matters more than difficulty. Practise with simplification MCQs and the quantitative aptitude section.

You may not leave any bubble blank. If you do not know an answer, you must shade option E. Each question left blank costs 0.5 mark, so ten blanks cost 5 marks. The notification's deduction rules do not include a penalty for a wrong answer.

The paper has 200 questions in 180 minutes, which is 0.9 minute per question on average. A workable split is about 70 minutes for the Tamil test, 75 for General Studies and 35 for Aptitude. Do the Tamil test with care, because it decides whether the rest is marked at all.

  1. Rank list: made from total marks in the written exam, with Parts A, B and C added together.
  2. Onscreen certificate verification: candidates are called in a 1:3 ratio for the general category and 1:2 for reserved categories for VAO, Junior Assistant, Typist and Steno Typist, and in a 1:6 ratio for the forest posts.
  3. Forest posts: eligible candidates who opted for Forest Guard or Forest Watcher take a Physical Standards Test and Endurance Test in a 1:3 ratio.
  4. Counselling: posts are allotted by rank.

If marks are equal, the candidate with the higher qualification is placed first. For Typist and Steno Typist, the higher typewriting or shorthand grade counts first. If qualification also ties, the older candidate goes first, and after that the candidate who applied earlier. For other state exam patterns, see the state PSC syllabus guide.

Is there negative marking in TNPSC Group IV?

The deduction rules in the notification do not include a penalty for a wrong answer. They do deduct 0.5 mark for every question left blank, so mark option E when you do not know an answer.

What happens if I score less than 60 in the Tamil test?

Your General Studies and Aptitude parts are not evaluated at all. Part A needs at least 60 of its 150 marks before the rest of the answer sheet is checked.

Is there an interview for Group IV posts?

No. The notification describes a single written exam, followed by onscreen certificate verification and counselling. Forest Guard and Forest Watcher candidates also take physical and endurance tests.

Are the unit-wise question numbers fixed?

No. The notification calls them indicative, and the Commission may raise or lower the count in any unit slightly. Use them as a guide to where the marks usually lie.
