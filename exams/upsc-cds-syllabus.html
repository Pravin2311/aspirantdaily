UPSC CDS Syllabus and Exam Pattern: English, GK, Elementary Maths and SSB from the Official Notice

Defence 📅 Last updated: 2026-11-01

The Combined Defence Services Examination is UPSC's route for graduates into officer training at the Indian Military Academy (IMA), Indian Naval Academy (INA), Air Force Academy (AFA) and Officers' Training Academy (OTA). This page follows the notice for the CDS (II) Examination, 2026, held on 13 September 2026, with courses starting in July and October 2027. IMA, INA and AFA candidates therefore write 300 marks; OTA candidates write 200. The SSB interview carries the same maximum as the written part, so the overall total is 600 for IMA, INA and AFA and 400 for OTA. If you apply for both, the maths paper is what separates the two lists.

All papers are objective. The GK and maths papers are set in both Hindi and English; the English paper is in English only. Calculators are not allowed and no scribe is permitted. Each wrong answer costs one third (0.33) of the marks for that question.

The notice keeps these two syllabi short. The English paper is designed to test the candidates' understanding of English and 'workmanlike use of words', so expect comprehension, vocabulary, grammar and sentence-ordering questions rather than literature.

General Knowledge covers current events and matters of everyday observation and experience in their scientific aspects, at the level expected of an educated person who has not specially studied science. It also includes Indian history and geography that a candidate should be able to answer without special study. Both papers are pitched at about graduate level.

Useful background: modern India and the freedom struggle and physiography of India.

The maths paper is set at Matriculation (Class 10) level. The notice lists these units:

  • Arithmetic: natural numbers to real numbers, square roots, decimal fractions, unitary method, time and distance, time and work, percentages, simple and compound interest, profit and loss, ratio and proportion, variation.
  • Elementary number theory: the division algorithm, primes and composites, divisibility tests for 2, 3, 4, 5, 9 and 11, HCF and LCM, the Euclidean algorithm, and logarithms to base 10.
  • Algebra: remainder theorem, polynomials, quadratic equations and the relation between roots and coefficients (real roots only), simultaneous linear equations and inequations in two variables, sets, laws of indices.
  • Trigonometry: sine, cosine and tangent for angles between 0° and 90°, values at 0°, 30°, 45°, 60° and 90°, simple identities, and heights and distances.
  • Geometry: lines and angles, parallel lines, triangles, congruence and similarity, medians and altitudes, quadrilaterals, circles with tangents and normals, and loci.
  • Mensuration: areas of plane figures, and surface area and volume of cuboids, cones, cylinders and spheres.
  • Statistics: collecting and tabulating data, frequency polygons, histograms, bar and pie charts, and measures of central tendency.

Revision pages for some of these: trigonometric ratios table, square roots and cube roots and mean, variance and standard deviation.

Candidates who clear the written exam are called to a Services Selection Board for an intelligence and personality test. Stage I consists of the Officer Intelligence Rating tests and the Picture Perception and Description Test; only those who clear it continue. Stage II runs over four days: an interview, Group Testing Officer tasks, psychology tests and a conference.

You must reach the qualifying marks in the written exam and in the SSB separately. Merit is then the total of the two. If totals tie, the candidate with more written marks ranks higher, and if those also tie, the one with more marks in General Knowledge. For the Class 12 entry to the same services, see the UPSC NDA syllabus.

Is there a maths paper for OTA in CDS?

No. OTA candidates sit only the English and General Knowledge papers, 100 marks each. Elementary Mathematics is needed only for IMA, INA and AFA.

How much negative marking is there in CDS?

One third (0.33) of the marks for a question is deducted for each wrong answer. Unanswered questions lose nothing.

Can final-year students apply for CDS?

Yes. Students in the final year or semester of their degree may apply, but must show proof of passing by the date the notice sets, before the course begins.
