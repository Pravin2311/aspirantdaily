SSC CGL vs CHSL: Which One Should You Prepare For?

Published on December 23, 2025 | Updated October 24, 2026 | For SSC Aspirants

If you’re preparing for SSC exams, you’ve likely asked: “Should I go for CGL or CHSL?”

Both are conducted by the Staff Selection Commission (SSC), but they differ in eligibility, posts, pay and difficulty. This guide breaks down the key differences to help you choose.

📌 Quick Overview

Feature SSC CGL SSC CHSL
Full Form Combined Graduate Level Combined Higher Secondary Level
Educational Qualification Graduate (some posts need specific subjects) 12th Pass
Age Limit Varies by post, from 18–27 up to 18–32 years 18–27 years
Exam Stages Tier 1 → Tier 2 → document verification Tier 1 → Tier 2 (including a skill/typing test) → document verification
Pay Level (7th CPC) Level 4 to Level 8 (basic pay ₹25,500 to ₹47,600 at entry) Level 2 to Level 5 (basic pay ₹19,900 to ₹29,200 at entry)

🎯 Key Differences Explained

1. Posts & Job Profile

SSC CGL fills Group B (gazetted and non-gazetted) and Group C posts, for example:

  • Assistant Audit Officer / Assistant Accounts Officer (Group B gazetted)
  • Assistant Section Officer in ministries
  • Inspector (CBIC, Central Excise, Examiner, Preventive Officer), Inspector (CBN), Sub-Inspector (CBI, NIA)
  • Assistant Enforcement Officer (ED), Junior Statistical Officer
  • Auditor, Accountant, Tax Assistant, Upper Division Clerk

→ A mix of office, audit, tax and field roles; some posts have physical standards.

SSC CHSL fills Group C posts:

  • Lower Division Clerk (LDC) / Junior Secretariat Assistant (JSA)
  • Data Entry Operator (DEO) and DEO Grade 'A'

→ Mainly clerical and data entry work in central ministries and offices.

2. Pay

Both use 7th Pay Commission pay levels. The take-home salary adds DA, HRA (depending on the city) and other allowances to the basic pay, so it differs by posting.

  • CGL: Level 4 (₹25,500, e.g. some Tax Assistant and clerical posts) to Level 7 (₹44,900, e.g. Assistant Section Officer, Inspector) and Level 8 (₹47,600, Assistant Audit/Accounts Officer).
  • CHSL: LDC/JSA at Level 2 (₹19,900–63,200); DEO at Level 4 (₹25,500–81,100) or Level 5 (₹29,200–92,300); DEO Grade 'A' at Level 4.

3. Exam Difficulty

Tier 1 of both exams has the same four sections: General Intelligence & Reasoning, General Awareness, Quantitative Aptitude and English. CGL Tier 1 has 100 questions for 200 marks in one hour, with −0.50 for each wrong answer (see our SSC CGL syllabus for the full 2026 pattern).

Section SSC CGL SSC CHSL
Quantitative Aptitude Wider: algebra, geometry, trigonometry, mensuration, DI Arithmetic-heavy, with basic algebra and geometry
English Larger weight in Tier 2 Grammar, vocabulary and comprehension
Reasoning Verbal and non-verbal, at a slightly harder level Verbal and non-verbal
Tier 2 Longer paper with maths, reasoning, English, GA, computer and data-entry tests; extra papers for JSO and statistics posts Objective paper plus a skill test (typing or data entry)

→ CGL is the harder exam, mainly because of the wider maths syllabus and the longer Tier 2.

4. Career Growth

  • CGL: posts start higher, and several (such as Assistant Section Officer, Inspector and Assistant Audit Officer) lead by promotion to Group B gazetted and later Group A posts in their cadre.
  • CHSL: LDC/JSA officers can rise to UDC and beyond through seniority promotions and departmental exams, but the path starts lower.

Promotion speed depends on the department and cadre, so look up the cadre of the post you want.

✅ Which One Is Right for You?

Choose SSC CGL if you:

  • Are a graduate (or will graduate by the notice's cut-off date)
  • Want a higher starting pay level and post
  • Can prepare for the wider maths syllabus and the longer Tier 2
  • Are interested in audit, tax, investigation or ministry posts

Choose SSC CHSL if you:

  • Are 12th pass and not yet a graduate
  • Are comfortable with a typing or data-entry test
  • Prefer clerical work in a central office
  • Plan to attempt CGL later while working

💡 Pro Tip: You Can Prepare for Both!

Because Tier 1 of CGL and CHSL has the same four sections, preparation for one helps with the other. Graduates who are eligible for both often attempt both, using CHSL as a backup while aiming for CGL.
