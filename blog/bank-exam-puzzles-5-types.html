Bank Exam Puzzles: 5 Types That Appear Every Year (With Shortcuts)

Published on December 23, 2025 | For IBPS PO, SBI PO, Clerk & RBI Exams

In bank exams like IBPS PO, SBI PO, and Clerk, puzzles and seating arrangements often make up around half of the Reasoning section. Mastering 5 common puzzle types covers a large share of that section.

This guide reveals the 5 most frequent puzzle types from 2020–2025 exams, along with time-saving shortcuts used by toppers.

5 Puzzle Types That Appear Every Year

1 Linear & Circular Seating Arrangement

Frequency: Very high — often 1–2 sets per paper

Pattern: 8–10 people facing North/South (linear) or inside/outside a circle.

Shortcut: Start with definite clues (e.g., “A sits 3rd to the left of B”) before handling conditional ones (“If C is not at end…”).

Practice Tip: Draw two parallel lines for North/South arrangements to avoid confusion.

2 Box-Based Puzzles

Frequency: High, especially in IBPS papers

Pattern: Items (books, boxes, people) stacked in 7–10 positions with color/number attributes.

Shortcut: Use a vertical stack diagram. Mark “top” and “bottom” clearly. Handle “not adjacent” clues last.

Example: “Box A is not at top. Box B is 2 places below C.” → Fix C first.

3 Month/Date-Based Puzzles

Frequency: High

Pattern: People with birthdays in different months/dates (e.g., Jan, Mar, May, Jul).

Shortcut: Create a 2-row table (Month | Person). Use “before/after” clues to eliminate options fast.

Pro Tip: Note if months have 30/31 days — sometimes used in “date” variants.

4 Floor-Based Puzzles

Frequency: High

Pattern: People living on floors 1–8 with cars/colors/professions.

Shortcut: Start from top or bottom floor if clue mentions “ground floor” or “topmost”. Use “not adjacent” to split possibilities.

Common Trap: “G lives immediately above H” ≠ “G is on floor 5 if H is on 4” — could be 3&4, 6&7, etc.

5 Comparison-Based Puzzles (Height/Weight/Rank)

Frequency: Medium — often mixed with other types

Pattern: “A is taller than B but shorter than C”, “D ranks 3rd from top”.

Shortcut: Draw a vertical ladder (↑ = tallest). Place known extremes first, then fill middle.

Time-Saver: These take <2 minutes if you avoid overcomplicating.

🎯 General Puzzle-Solving Strategy

  1. Read all clues twice before drawing anything.
  2. Spend max 5 minutes per puzzle — if stuck, move on.
  3. Practice 2 puzzles daily — consistency builds speed.
  4. Never skip “easy” puzzles — they’re free marks!

🧩 Worked Example: A Floor Puzzle With Case Splitting

Five people — P, Q, R, S and T — live on floors 1 to 5 of a building (floor 1 is the bottom). One person lives on each floor.

  1. Only one person lives between R and T.
  2. Q lives immediately below T.
  3. S lives on a floor below Q.
  4. P lives on an even-numbered floor.
  5. R lives above Q.

Step 1 — combine the fixed links. Clues 2 and 3 give S < Q < T with Q directly below T. So T can only be on floor 3, 4 or 5.

Answer: floor 1 S, floor 2 Q, floor 3 T, floor 4 P, floor 5 R. Notice how clue 5 was needed only to break the tie between cases — this is why you should draw all cases on paper before dropping any.

⏱️ Which Puzzle to Attempt First

  • Read the first two clues of every puzzle set. If one fixes a position directly ("A is on the top floor"), start with that set.
  • Prefer sets with 5–6 variables in Prelims; sets with 8+ people and two attributes take longer and can wait.
  • If a set gives you three or more cases after the first four clues, move on and return later.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

How many puzzle questions come in bank Prelims?

It varies by exam and year. Puzzles and seating arrangements often make up around half of the Reasoning section, usually as sets of 3–5 questions each.

What is the best way to solve puzzles faster?

Start from the clues that fix a position directly, combine linked clues into one block, and draw every possible case in a table before eliminating. Practising 2–3 sets daily under a timer is the most reliable way to build speed.

Should I skip puzzles in the exam?

Do not skip all of them, as they carry many marks. Pick the sets with the most definite clues first and leave the ones with many cases for the end.
