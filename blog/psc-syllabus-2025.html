State PSC Syllabus 2026 (MPPSC, BPSC, TNPSC Group 1)

Last updated: October 28, 2026 | From the commissions' own notices

State Public Service Commission (PSC) exams recruit for state civil services, and each commission sets its own pattern. This guide compares three of them from their current official documents: the MPPSC State Service Exam 2026 plan (revised 21 January 2026), the BPSC Integrated 72nd CCE advertisement (May 2026) and TNPSC's scheme and Group I Prelims syllabus.

UPPSC is not covered yet: its PCS 2026 notice (Advt. A-1/E-1/2026) is no longer linked on uppsc.up.nic.in, and we only publish patterns we have read in the official notice.

📌 PSC Exam Pattern (Common Structure)

All three commissions use a three-stage process:

  1. Preliminary Exam – Objective, screening only
  2. Mains Exam – Written, merit-based
  3. Interview – Personality test

Prelims: How MPPSC, BPSC and TNPSC Compare

CommissionPrelims papersMarks and timeWrong or blank answers
MPPSC (Madhya Pradesh)Two: General Studies and General Aptitude Test, 100 questions each300 marks each (3 per question), 2 hours each1 mark deducted per wrong answer
BPSC (Bihar)One General Studies paper that includes general mental ability150 marks, 2 hoursOne-third mark deducted per wrong answer; options A–E, where E means "not attempting", and a question left fully blank also loses one-third
TNPSC Group I (Tamil Nadu)One paper: 175 General Studies (degree standard) + 25 Aptitude and Mental Ability (SSLC standard)200 questions, 300 marksMark option E if you don't know; 0.5 mark deducted for each question left blank

✅ In all three, Prelims is only a screening test: its marks do not count in the final merit list. MPPSC calls up to 20 times the vacancies to Mains and BPSC 10 times. MPPSC also needs at least 40% in each Prelims paper (30% for SC, ST, OBC, EWS and PwBD candidates); BPSC's Prelims minimum is 40% for general, 36.5% for BC, 34% for EBC and 32% for SC/ST, women and PwBD candidates.

📚 PSC Syllabus 2026 – What the Notices List

1. BPSC Prelims (General Studies)

  • General science and current events of national and international importance
  • History of India and the main features of Bihar's history
  • General geography, Bihar's main geographical divisions and its important rivers
  • India's polity and economy, and the major changes in Bihar's economy since independence
  • The Indian national movement and Bihar's contribution to it
  • General mental ability

2. TNPSC Group I Prelims (questions per unit)

  • General Science – 10; Geography of India – 10; History, Culture of India and Indian National Movement – 25
  • Indian Polity – 40
  • Indian Economy and Development Administration in Tamil Nadu – 50
  • History, Culture, Heritage and Socio-Political Movements in Tamil Nadu – 40 (including Thirukkural)
  • Aptitude – 15 and Reasoning – 10 (SSLC standard)

3. MPPSC General Aptitude Test

  • A separate, qualifying paper of 100 questions in 2 hours
  • Both papers are set in Hindi and English, except the Hindi comprehension questions, which are in Hindi only

🎯 Preparation Tips for PSC Exams 2026

  • ✅ Master your state’s GK — in TNPSC Group I, the two Tamil Nadu units carry 90 of the 175 General Studies questions
  • ✅ Read state govt. portal — latest schemes, budgets, awards
  • ✅ Practice answer writing for Mains (start early!)
  • ✅ Don’t ignore the aptitude paper — MPPSC needs 40% in it (30% for reserved categories) before your GS score counts
  • ✅ Learn your commission's marking rule — MPPSC and BPSC penalise wrong answers, TNPSC penalises blanks

🗺️ How State-Specific Questions Are Actually Asked

State GK is rarely a list of facts to recite. Commissions link it to the national syllabus; BPSC, for instance, lists "the national movement and Bihar's contribution to it" as one topic:

  • History: a national event with a local hero — e.g., Kunwar Singh in 1857 (Bihar), Begum Hazrat Mahal at Lucknow (Uttar Pradesh), Tantia Tope's last stand in central India (Madhya Pradesh).
  • Geography: rivers, dams and protected areas within the state — e.g., which river the Indira Sagar dam is on (Narmada, Madhya Pradesh).
  • Economy: the state budget, its flagship schemes and its rank in national reports.
  • Culture: folk dances, fairs, crafts with a GI tag, and regional literature.

📝 What Mains Looks Like

  • BPSC (72nd CCE): General Hindi, 100 marks, which you must pass with 30% but which does not count in merit; General Studies I and II, 300 marks each; Essay, 300 marks; an optional subject of 100 objective questions in 2 hours that has to be passed but does not count in merit; interview, 120 marks. Final merit is out of 1,020.
  • TNPSC Group I: a qualifying Tamil Eligibility Test of 100 marks (SSLC standard), then General Studies I, II and III of 250 marks each and an interview of 100 marks — 850 in all.
  • MPPSC: the Mains papers are set out in the same 2026 exam-plan document as Prelims.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

Are State PSC Prelims marks counted in the final merit?

No. MPPSC, BPSC and TNPSC all use Prelims only to shortlist candidates for Mains. Final merit comes from Mains and the interview.

Is there negative marking in State PSC Prelims?

In MPPSC and BPSC, yes. MPPSC gives 3 marks per right answer and takes away 1 per wrong answer. BPSC deducts one-third of a mark per wrong answer. TNPSC asks you to mark option E when you do not know an answer and deducts 0.5 mark for every question left blank.

Is the aptitude paper qualifying?

MPPSC has a separate General Aptitude Test that only has to be passed: 40% for unreserved candidates and 30% for SC, ST, OBC, EWS and PwBD candidates, and its marks do not count in the Prelims merit. BPSC and TNPSC have no separate aptitude paper; mental ability questions sit inside their single Prelims paper.

How should I prepare state-specific GK?

Study it alongside the national syllabus: for every history, geography or economy topic, note the state's people, places and schemes. Then practise your own commission's previous papers.
