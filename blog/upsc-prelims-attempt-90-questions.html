UPSC Prelims: How to Attempt 90+ Questions Without Negative Marking

Published on December 22, 2025 | For UPSC Civil Services Prelims 2025

In UPSC Prelims, attempting 90+ questions can be the difference between clearing the cut-off and missing it by a few marks. But fear of negative marking (⅓ per wrong answer) stops many aspirants. The good news? With the right strategy, you can safely attempt 90–95 questions while keeping negative marks below 10.

This guide reveals the 5-step framework used by toppers to maximize attempts without reckless guessing.

5-Step Framework to Attempt 90+ Questions

1 Classify Questions in Real-Time

As you scan the paper, mentally tag each question into one of three buckets:

  • ✅ Known: You know the answer → Attempt immediately
  • 🤔 Eliminable: You can eliminate 1–2 options → Attempt with caution
  • ❌ Unknown: No clue → Skip (do not guess randomly)

Target: 60 Known + 30 Eliminable = 90 safe attempts.

2 Master the Elimination Technique

UPSC loves “close-call” options. Use these filters:

  • Extreme words: “Always”, “Never”, “Only” → Usually wrong
  • Chronology mismatch: e.g., “Ashoka founded Mauryan Empire” → False (it was Chandragupta)
  • Geographical errors: e.g., “Kaveri flows through Rajasthan” → False
  • Constitutional inaccuracies: e.g., “DPSP is justiciable” → False

If you can eliminate 2 options, your odds jump from 25% → 50%.

3 Use the “Confidence Meter” Rule

Only attempt a question if your confidence is ≥60%. Ask yourself:

“Would I bet ₹100 on this answer?”

If yes → attempt. If no → skip. This prevents emotional guessing.

4 Prioritize High-Yield Subjects

Focus your “eliminable” attempts on subjects where you can make educated guesses:

  • ✅ Polity: Eliminate based on constitutional logic
  • ✅ Environment: Eliminate based on geography/biology basics
  • ✅ Current Affairs: Use recent memory (last 12 months)
  • ⚠️ History/Science: Avoid guessing if unsure

5 Practice with a “Negative Marking Tracker”

In your daily mocks:

  1. Mark guessed questions with a “?”
  2. After checking answers, calculate: Negative Marks = (Wrong Guesses) × 0.66
  3. Aim to keep negative marks < 8 per mock

This builds instinctive risk awareness.

🎯 Realistic Attempt Breakdown (Target: 90–95 Qs)

  • Known (Safe): 60 questions at 90% accuracy → 54 right, 6 wrong → 108 − 4 = ~104 marks
  • Eliminable (Smart Guess): 30 questions at 50% accuracy → 15 right, 15 wrong → 30 − 10 = ~20 marks
  • Total Expected Score: ~124 — comfortably above the 2024 general-category cut-off of 87.98 (it was 75.41 in 2023)

🧮 The Maths of Guessing

Each GS Paper I question carries 2 marks and a wrong answer costs 0.66. The expected marks for a guess depend on how many options you can rule out:

Options eliminatedChance of being rightExpected marks per attempt
None (blind guess)1 in 40.25 × 2 − 0.75 × 0.66 = 0
One1 in 30.33 × 2 − 0.67 × 0.66 ≈ +0.22
Two1 in 20.5 × 2 − 0.5 × 0.66 ≈ +0.67

This is why the framework above skips blind guesses (no expected gain, only risk) and attempts every question where two options can be eliminated.

⏱️ Time Plan for 120 Minutes

  • Round 1 (0–60 min): go through all 100 questions; answer the ones you know and mark the eliminable ones.
  • Round 2 (60–100 min): return to the marked questions and apply elimination.
  • Round 3 (100–115 min): fill the OMR sheet carefully if you haven't already, and recheck bubbled answers.
  • Buffer (115–120 min): no new guesses — this is when careless errors happen.
Don't forget CSAT: Paper II is qualifying, but you must score at least 33% (66 of 200), or your GS score is not evaluated. Take a few timed CSAT tests even if you are strong in maths.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

What was the UPSC Prelims cut-off in 2024?

The general-category cut-off for UPSC Prelims 2024 was 87.98 out of 200. In 2023 it was 75.41.

Is it safe to guess in UPSC Prelims?

A blind guess among four options has an expected value of zero, so it adds risk without gain. If you can eliminate even one option, guessing has a positive expected value, and with two options eliminated it is clearly worth it.

How many questions should I attempt in UPSC Prelims?

It depends on your accuracy. Most successful candidates attempt about 80–95 questions, choosing them by confidence rather than a fixed number.
