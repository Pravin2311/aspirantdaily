SSC CGL Tier 1: How to Score 180+ (2026 Pattern)

Published on December 22, 2025 | Updated October 20, 2026 for the CGL 2026 pattern

A 180+ score in SSC CGL Tier 1 needs about 90 correct answers out of 100, with very few mistakes. This guide shares 7 actionable tips based on the exam pattern in SSC's CGL 2026 notice. The tips are our advice, not SSC guidance.

7 Tips to Score 180+ in SSC CGL Tier 1

1 Master the Sectional Breakdown

SSC CGL Tier 1 has 4 sections (25 Qs each): Reasoning, GK, Quant, English. To score 180+, aim for:

  • Reasoning: 45–50/50
  • Quant: 40–45/50
  • English: 40–45/50
  • GK: 35–40/50

Key: Don’t ignore GK — it’s the biggest differentiator!

2 Prioritize High-Weightage Quant Topics

These areas come up repeatedly in Quant. The mix changes from shift to shift, so do not rely on fixed question counts:

  1. Data Interpretation (bar, pie and table charts)
  2. Algebra
  3. Geometry & Mensuration
  4. Percentage, Profit & Loss
  5. Time & Work, Speed

→ Practice 10 DI sets daily. They’re fast and scoring.

3 Build a Current Affairs Bank

General Awareness mixes static GK with current affairs. Focus on:

  • Latest Union Budget & Economic Survey highlights
  • New government schemes (PM Vishwakarma, etc.)
  • Science & Tech (ISRO missions, DPDPA, ARKA supercomputer)
  • Important appointments (CJI, CEC, Finance Secretary)

→ Revise weekly. Use our December 2025 CA summary.

4 Pace Each Section Inside Its 15 Minutes

From CGL 2026, each Tier-I section has its own 15-minute timer (20 minutes for scribe-eligible candidates). Time left in one section cannot be used in another. That is about 36 seconds per question. Within each section:

  1. First pass (about 10 minutes): answer every question you can solve quickly; skip the rest.
  2. Second pass (about 5 minutes): return to skipped questions, in order of how close you were.

Practise mocks with the same sectional timers so the pacing becomes automatic.

5 Master the Short Reasoning Types

CGL reasoning is mostly short, one-step questions rather than long bank-style puzzles. Master these types:

  • Analogy, classification and series (letter, number and figure)
  • Coding-decoding and mathematical operations
  • Mirror and water images, paper folding, embedded figures and Venn diagrams

→ Solve 25 mixed reasoning questions daily in under 20 minutes. Speed comes with repetition.

6 Improve English with “Error Spotting”

English is the easiest to score in. Focus on:

  • Error Detection
  • Sentence Improvement
  • Cloze Test and Reading Comprehension

→ Read 1 editorial daily (The Hindu, Indian Express).

7 Take Full-Length Mocks Weekly

Mocks build speed, accuracy, and confidence. Follow this cycle:

  1. Take a mock under exam conditions
  2. Analyze mistakes (why did you get it wrong?)
  3. Revise weak topics
  4. Repeat

Aim for 15+ mocks before exam day.

🎯 Final Tip: Practice Daily — For Free!

Consistency beats intensity. Spend just 20 minutes daily on focused MCQ practice.

🧮 What 180 Actually Requires

Each question carries 2 marks and each wrong answer costs 0.5. Here is how attempts and accuracy combine:

AttemptedCorrectWrongScore
100928184 − 4 = 180
95914182 − 2 = 180
90855170 − 2.5 = 167.5
85805160 − 2.5 = 157.5

The lesson: 180 needs 90+ correct answers. You cannot reach it by skipping a section, and every 4 wrong answers cost you a full question's marks.

🕐 Budgeting Time Under Sectional Timers

Under the 2026 pattern you cannot give Quant extra time by rushing General Awareness, because each section has its own 15 minutes. Plan each section separately:

  • General Awareness: you either know the answer or you don't. Answer, mark and move on; use the spare minutes to recheck marked questions in the same section.
  • English: do vocabulary and grammar questions first and leave long comprehension passages to the end of the section.
  • Reasoning: skip any question that needs more than about 45 seconds on the first pass.
  • Quant: the tightest section for most candidates. Solve the quick arithmetic first and come back to long geometry or DI questions.

Check the order the sections appear in on exam day in SSC's instructions; the point of practice is to finish each section's easy questions inside its own timer.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

Are SSC CGL Tier 1 marks added to the final result?

No. Under the CGL 2026 notice, Tier 1 marks are used only to shortlist candidates for Tier 2; the final merit list is based on Tier 2 only.

What is the negative marking in SSC CGL Tier 1?

Each question carries 2 marks and 0.50 marks are deducted for every wrong answer. There is no penalty for unanswered questions.

How many correct answers do I need for 180 in Tier 1?

About 92 correct with 8 wrong out of 100, or 91 correct with 4 wrong if you attempt 95.
