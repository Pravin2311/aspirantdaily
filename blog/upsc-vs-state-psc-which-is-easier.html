UPSC vs State PSC: Which Exam is Easier to Crack?

Published on December 23, 2025 | Updated October 24, 2026 | For Civil Services Aspirants

“Should I prepare for UPSC or State PSC?” This is one of the most common dilemmas among civil service aspirants.

Neither exam is easy, and the answer depends on what you want from the job as much as on difficulty. This guide compares the two on the exam pattern, syllabus, optional subjects, posting and promotion, so you can decide which suits you.

📌 Quick Overview

Parameter UPSC Civil Services State PSC (e.g., UPPSC, MPPSC)
Conducting Body Union Public Service Commission The state's Public Service Commission
Posts Offered All India Services (IAS, IPS, IFS) and Central Services (IRS and others) State civil and police services (e.g., Deputy Collector/SDM, DSP)
Stages Prelims → Mains → Interview Prelims → Mains → Interview (in most states)
Syllabus GS, Essay and an optional subject Largely the same core GS, plus state-specific GK
Posting Anywhere in India (allotted a state cadre or a central service) Within the state

🎯 Detailed Comparison

1. Exam Pattern

UPSC:

  • Prelims: GS Paper I (100 questions, 200 marks) decides who qualifies; CSAT (Paper II) is qualifying at 33%.
  • Mains: two qualifying language papers, then seven papers counted for merit — Essay, GS I–IV and two optional papers, 250 marks each (1,750 marks).
  • Interview: 275 marks, for a total of 2,025.

State PSC: the pattern differs by state, so read your commission's latest notification. Two examples:

  • UPPSC has dropped the optional subject from PCS Mains. It now has six GS papers (two of them on Uttar Pradesh), plus General Hindi and Essay.
  • MPPSC State Services Mains also has no optional subject; its GS papers include Madhya Pradesh topics.

2. Syllabus & Depth of Study

UPSC:

  • Covers national and international topics in depth
  • The optional subject (500 marks over two papers) needs its own preparation
  • Mains and Essay demand analytical answer writing

State PSC:

  • The core — polity, history, geography, economy, science, current affairs — overlaps heavily with UPSC
  • State-specific focus: the state's history, geography, economy, schemes and culture
  • Current affairs include state news and state government schemes

✅ If your state has no optional paper, the State PSC syllabus is narrower, but the state-specific portion is new material that UPSC preparation does not cover.

3. Competition & Cut-Offs

Both exams draw lakhs of applicants for a limited number of posts, and the numbers change every year. For reference, in UPSC CSE 2024 the general-category Prelims cut-off was 87.98 out of 200, and the final result (April 2025) recommended 1,009 candidates.

State PSC vacancies and cut-offs vary widely between states and years. Check your commission's notification for the number of posts, and its published cut-offs for past years, rather than relying on averages.

4. Preparation & Strategy

  • UPSC: start with NCERTs and standard books, choose the optional early, and practise Mains answer writing regularly.
  • State PSC: the same base, plus dedicated notes on the state and practice with that commission's past papers, since question styles differ.

5. Career & Posting

  • UPSC: All India Service officers can be posted anywhere in their cadre and on deputation to the Centre, up to the top posts such as Secretary or DGP.
  • State PSC: officers serve in their home state, closer to family and the local language.

State civil service officers can later be promoted into the IAS: up to one-third of a state's IAS cadre posts are filled by promoting state service officers. This is a promotion based on seniority and record, not a way of clearing the UPSC exam.

✅ Which One Is Right for YOU?

Choose UPSC if you:

  • Want an All India or Central Service career (IAS/IPS/IFS)
  • Can commit to a long preparation with uncertain results
  • Are ready for Mains-style answer writing and an optional subject
  • Are okay with being posted anywhere in India

Choose State PSC if you:

  • Prefer working in your home state
  • Know (or are willing to learn) the state's history, geography and schemes well
  • Want an exam without an optional subject, if your state has dropped it
  • Want to keep a state service career open alongside UPSC attempts

💡 Pro Strategy: Prepare for Both!

Because the core GS syllabus overlaps, many aspirants prepare for UPSC and their State PSC together. Build the common base first, then add state-specific notes and practise the state commission's past papers before its exam.
