Environment Cheat Code: 15 Must-Know Facts (COP30, Species & Laws)

Published on December 24, 2025 | For UPSC & State PSC Exams

Environment & Ecology is a regular part of UPSC Prelims, and many questions return to the same conventions, protected areas and concepts. Master these 15 Environment facts to cover those recurring themes.

1 India’s target for Net Zero emissions is 2070 (announced at COP26, reaffirmed at COP28/COP29).

2 COP30 (2025) was held in Belém, Brazil (Amazon region), held November 10–21, 2025 — Brazil’s hosting was confirmed at COP28 in December 2023.

3 India has 4 Biodiversity Hotspots: Himalayas, Western Ghats, Indo-Burma (north-east and the Andamans) and Sundaland (Nicobar Islands).

4 Project Tiger was launched in 1973; India now has 58 tiger reserves — Madhav (Madhya Pradesh) became the 58th in March 2025.

5 Wildlife (Protection) Act was enacted in 1972 — provides legal framework for species protection.

6 Environment (Protection) Act was enacted in 1986 — umbrella legislation for pollution control.

7 National Green Tribunal (NGT) was established in 2010 under the NGT Act, 2010.

8 Ramsar Convention is about wetland conservation; India has 90+ Ramsar Sites (as of 2025), the most in Asia.

9 Bonn Convention is related to conservation of migratory species.

10 Montreux Record is a register of Ramsar sites under threat; India has 2 sites in it (Loktak Lake, Keoladeo NP).

11 IPCC (Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change) releases assessment reports every 5–7 years; AR7 is upcoming.

12 Green Climate Fund (GCF) was established under UNFCCC to assist developing countries in climate action.

13 National Clean Air Programme (NCAP) originally targeted a 20–30% cut in PM2.5 and PM10 by 2024 (base year 2017); the target was revised to up to 40% reduction in PM10 by 2025–26.

14 Great Indian Bustard is critically endangered; found in Rajasthan & Gujarat; protected under Project Great Indian Bustard.

15 One-Horned Rhinoceros is found mainly in Kaziranga (Assam), which counted 2,613 rhinos in its 2022 census — about two-thirds of the world’s population.

🐅 IUCN Status of Indian Species

  • Critically Endangered: Great Indian Bustard, Gharial.
  • Endangered: Bengal tiger, Red panda, Ganges river dolphin.
  • Vulnerable: Snow leopard, Indian one-horned rhinoceros.

The Ganges river dolphin is India's National Aquatic Animal (2009). The Wild Life (Protection) Amendment Act, 2022 cut the number of schedules from six to four.

⚠️ Common traps: Chilika Lake was removed from the Montreux Record in 2002 — only Keoladeo and Loktak remain. There are 36 biodiversity hotspots in the world, of which India has parts of four. CITES regulates trade; it does not create protected areas.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between the Ramsar Convention and the Montreux Record?

The Ramsar Convention (1971) lists wetlands of international importance. The Montreux Record is a sub-list of Ramsar sites whose ecology is threatened; in India, Keoladeo National Park and Loktak Lake are on it.

How many tiger reserves are there in India?

India has 58 tiger reserves. Madhav Tiger Reserve in Madhya Pradesh was notified as the 58th in March 2025.

Which convention deals with ozone depletion?

The Montreal Protocol (1987). Its Kigali Amendment of 2016 added a phase-down of hydrofluorocarbons (HFCs), which are strong greenhouse gases.
