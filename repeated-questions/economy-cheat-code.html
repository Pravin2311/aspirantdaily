Economy Cheat Code: 15 Must-Know Terms (Budget 2025 & Beyond)

Published on December 24, 2025 | For UPSC, SSC & Banking Exams

Economy is a high-scoring, static + current hybrid subject in UPSC Prelims, SSC, and Bank exams. These 15 Economy terms and figures cover themes that come up often. The figures date from the period noted against each one.

1 India’s GDP growth in Q2 FY26 (July–Sept 2025) was 8.2% (NSO, released November 28, 2025).

2 After the October 5–7, 2026 policy, RBI’s Repo Rate is 5.50% (raised from 5.25%) — the rate at which RBI lends to banks.

3 CRR (Cash Reserve Ratio) = 3% (cut from 4% in four tranches, September–November 2025) — banks must keep this % of deposits as cash with RBI.

4 SLR (Statutory Liquidity Ratio) = 18% — banks must maintain this in govt. securities, gold, or cash.

5 NITI Aayog’s India Innovation Index (latest edition: 2021, released 2022) was topped by Karnataka in the Major States category.

6 PLI Scheme (Production Linked Incentive) covers 14 sectors including electronics, pharma, and textiles.

7 VB-G RAM G Act, 2025 replaced MGNREGA from July 1, 2026, raising guaranteed wage employment from 100 to 125 days per rural household per year.

8 PM-KISAN provides ₹6,000/year in 3 equal installments to eligible farmer families.

9 India’s rank in Global Innovation Index 2025 is 38th of 139 (up from 39th in 2024 and 40th in 2023).

10 Fiscal Deficit target for 2026–27 (Union Budget, February 1, 2026) is 4.3% of GDP; the revised estimate for 2025–26 is 4.4%.

11 Primary Deficit = Fiscal Deficit minus interest payments.

12 Revenue Deficit occurs when revenue expenditure > revenue receipts.

13 Monetary Policy is controlled by RBI; Fiscal Policy by Ministry of Finance.

14 Inflation in India is measured by CPI (Consumer Price Index) for monetary policy.

15 India Semiconductor Mission offers 50% fiscal support for semiconductor fab units (e.g., Micron in Gujarat).

🧮 Deficit Maths: One Worked Example

Exams often give a few budget figures and ask for a deficit. Suppose the government has (₹ lakh crore):

ItemAmount
Revenue receipts (taxes + non-tax revenue)30
Non-debt capital receipts (loan recoveries, disinvestment)1
Revenue expenditure37
— of which, interest payments11
Capital expenditure10
  • Revenue deficit = revenue expenditure − revenue receipts = 37 − 30 = 7.
  • Fiscal deficit = total expenditure − (revenue receipts + non-debt capital receipts) = 47 − 31 = 16. This is the amount the government must borrow.
  • Primary deficit = fiscal deficit − interest payments = 16 − 11 = 5. It shows borrowing for current needs, excluding past debt.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between fiscal deficit and primary deficit?

Fiscal deficit is the government's total borrowing requirement in a year. Primary deficit is the fiscal deficit minus interest payments on past loans, so it shows how much is borrowed for current spending.

Who decides the repo rate in India?

The six-member Monetary Policy Committee of the RBI. Three members are from the RBI, including the Governor, and three are appointed by the Central Government.

What is the fiscal deficit target for 2026–27?

The Union Budget presented on February 1, 2026 set the fiscal deficit target for 2026–27 at 4.3% of GDP, with the 2025–26 revised estimate at 4.4%.
