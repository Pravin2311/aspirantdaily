English Cheat Code: 15 Must-Know Grammar & Vocabulary Tips

Published on December 25, 2025 | For SSC, Bank & Railway Exams

English in SSC CGL, IBPS and RRB exams tests the same grammar and vocabulary rules again and again. These 15 cheat codes cover the rules asked most often.

1 Subject-Verb Agreement:
Singular subject → singular verb
❌ “The list of items are on the table.”
✅ “The list of items is on the table.”

2 Tense Consistency:
Don’t mix past and present in the same context.
❌ “She goes to school and played cricket.”
✅ “She went to school and played cricket.”

3 Preposition after “Different”:
Always use “different from” (not “different than”).
✅ “This book is different from that one.”

4 “Less” vs “Fewer”:
Less = uncountable (less water, less time)
Fewer = countable (fewer books, fewer cars)

5 “Each” and “Every” are singular:
✅ “Each of the students has a book.”

6 Vocabulary Root: “Bene” = good:
Benediction (blessing), Benefactor (one who does good)

7 Vocabulary Root: “Mal” = bad:
Malignant (harmful), Malpractice (wrong doing)

8 Vocabulary Root: “Omni” = all:
Omnipotent (all-powerful), Omniscient (all-knowing)

9 Idiom: “Beat around the bush” = avoid saying directly

10 Idiom: “Hit the nail on the head” = exactly right

11 Idiom: “Bite the bullet” = endure pain bravely

12 One-word Substitute: “One who eats human flesh” = Cannibal

13 One-word Substitute: “One who knows everything” = Omniscient

14 Spelling Rule:
“i before e except after c”
✅ believe, receive, ceiling

15 Active vs Passive:
Active: “She wrote a letter.”
Passive: “A letter was written by her.”

⚡ 10 More Grammar Rules With Exam Examples

RuleCorrect sentence
"One of the" + plural noun + singular verbOne of the boys is absent.
Neither…nor / either…or: the verb agrees with the nearer subjectNeither the teacher nor the students were present.
Hardly / scarcely … when; no sooner … thanNo sooner had he arrived than it began to rain.
Lest is followed by should, never "not"Work hard lest you should fail.
Since + point of time; for + period of timeI have lived here since 2020 / for six years.
Senior, junior, superior, inferior, prefer → to, not "than"He is senior to me. I prefer tea to coffee.
Unless already means "if not"Unless you hurry, you will miss the bus. (not "unless you don't")
Pronoun after a preposition takes the object formThis is between you and me.
Collective noun acting as one unit → singular verbThe committee has approved the plan.
"The number of" → singular; "a number of" → pluralThe number of applicants is rising. A number of students were late.

Confusing Word Pairs

  • Affect (verb, to influence) vs effect (noun, result).
  • Principal (head; main) vs principle (rule).
  • Stationary (not moving) vs stationery (paper, pens).
  • Complement (completes) vs compliment (praise).
  • Eminent (famous) vs imminent (about to happen).
  • Council (a body of people) vs counsel (advice; a lawyer).

Six More Idioms and Eight One-Word Substitutes

Idioms: a blessing in disguise (a hidden benefit) • once in a blue moon (very rarely) • spill the beans (reveal a secret) • burn the midnight oil (work late) • turn a blind eye (ignore deliberately) • cry over spilt milk (regret what cannot be undone).

One-word substitutes: philanthropist (one who loves mankind) • bibliophile (lover of books) • misogynist (one who hates women) • insolvent (unable to pay debts) • panacea (cure for all ills) • posthumous (after death) • ambidextrous (able to use both hands equally) • amateur (one who does something for pleasure, not pay).

⚠️ Common trap: "i before e except after c" has well-known exceptions that examiners use in spelling questions — weird, seize, neither, their, science, foreign.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

How can I improve at error spotting in SSC and bank exams?

Check every sentence in a fixed order: subject–verb agreement, tense, pronoun case, prepositions, articles and then comparatives. Most errors in SSC and bank papers come from these six areas.

Is it "neither of them is" or "neither of them are"?

"Neither of them is" is correct in exam English. Neither, either, each and every take a singular verb.

Which is correct: "different from" or "different than"?

For competitive exams, use "different from". "Different than" is common in American speech but is marked wrong in most answer keys.
