🌪️ Disaster Management in India

Vulnerability • DM Act 2005 • NDMA & NDRF • Sendai Framework • Early Warning • Heat Waves • CDRI

High Yield Environment 📅 Last updated: Oct 2026
🔍 Quick Search: NDMA chaired by, NEC chaired by Home Secretary, NDRF raised year, Sendai framework 2015-2030 priorities, Hyogo framework, nodal ministry for drought, tsunami warning centre India, IMD heat wave definition, CDRI headquarters
  • India is one of the world's most disaster-prone countries. A large share of its land is vulnerable to earthquakes, much of it to floods and droughts, and most of its coastline to cyclones and tsunamis.
  • After the 2004 Indian Ocean tsunami, India moved from a relief-centred approach to one focused on prevention, preparedness and mitigation, through the Disaster Management Act, 2005.
  • Disaster management is an explicit part of the UPSC GS Paper 3 syllabus, and questions on institutions and frameworks also appear in Prelims.

A hazard becomes a disaster only when it overwhelms a community's ability to cope. Risk is often expressed as: Risk = Hazard × Vulnerability ÷ Capacity.

The Disaster Management Cycle

Before: prevention, mitigation and preparedness. During: response. After: relief, recovery, rehabilitation and reconstruction — ideally "building back better".

Policies and Plans

  • National Policy on Disaster Management (2009).
  • National Disaster Management Plan (2016, revised 2019) — the first national plan, aligned with the Sendai Framework.
  • Community programmes such as Aapda Mitra train local volunteers as first responders.
  • The Disaster Management (Amendment) Act, 2025 updated the 2005 Act, for example by providing for disaster databases and urban disaster management authorities for large cities.

Sendai Framework for Disaster Risk Reduction (2015–2030)

Adopted at the UN conference in Sendai, Japan, it replaced the Hyogo Framework for Action (2005–2015). It has 7 global targets — reducing deaths, affected people, economic losses and damage to infrastructure, and increasing early-warning systems, national strategies and international cooperation — and 4 priorities:

  1. Understanding disaster risk.
  2. Strengthening disaster risk governance.
  3. Investing in disaster risk reduction for resilience.
  4. Enhancing preparedness for effective response and "building back better".

India also launched the Coalition for Disaster Resilient Infrastructure (CDRI) at the UN Climate Action Summit in September 2019; its secretariat is in New Delhi.

Heat Waves (IMD criteria)

  • Considered only when the maximum temperature reaches at least 40°C in the plains, 37°C on the coast or 30°C in hilly areas.
  • Heat wave: 4.5°C–6.4°C above normal; severe heat wave: more than 6.4°C above normal.
  • Or by actual temperature: 45°C or more is a heat wave, and 47°C or more a severe heat wave.
  • Many states now prepare Heat Action Plans; Ahmedabad's (2013) was the first in South Asia.

Q1. The National Executive Committee under the Disaster Management Act, 2005 is chaired by the:

✅ Answer: (c) Union Home Secretary

💡 Explanation: The NDMA is chaired by the PM; the NEC by the Union Home Secretary.

Q2. The Sendai Framework for Disaster Risk Reduction covers the period:

✅ Answer: (a) 2015–2030

💡 Explanation: It replaced the Hyogo Framework (2005–2015).

Q3. Tsunami early warnings in India are issued by:

✅ Answer: (d) INCOIS, Hyderabad

💡 Explanation: The Indian Tsunami Early Warning Centre at INCOIS has been operational since 2007.

Q4. Which ministry is the nodal ministry for drought management?

✅ Answer: (b) Ministry of Agriculture

💡 Explanation: Drought is handled by the Ministry of Agriculture; most other disasters by Home Affairs.

Q5. For a heat wave to be declared in the plains, the maximum temperature must be at least:

✅ Answer: (d) 40°C

💡 Explanation: The threshold is 40°C in the plains, 37°C on the coast and 30°C in hilly regions.

Who heads disaster management bodies in India?

The National Disaster Management Authority is chaired by the Prime Minister, the National Executive Committee by the Union Home Secretary, State Disaster Management Authorities by Chief Ministers, and District Disaster Management Authorities by District Collectors or Magistrates.

What is the Sendai Framework?

The Sendai Framework for Disaster Risk Reduction 2015–2030, adopted in Sendai, Japan, sets 7 global targets and 4 priorities — understanding risk, strengthening risk governance, investing in resilience, and improving preparedness and "building back better". It replaced the Hyogo Framework.
