National Action Plan on Climate Change (NAPCC): The Eight National Missions, Their Targets and Exam Facts

What NAPCC is • The eight missions at a glance • Solar and energy efficiency • Habitat and water • Himalaya and Green India • Agriculture and knowledge • Exam traps

UPSC SSC State PSC 📅 Last updated: 2026-11-10

The National Action Plan on Climate Change (NAPCC) was released by the Prime Minister on 30 June 2008. It sets out a national strategy to help India adapt to climate change and make its development path more ecologically sustainable, while stressing that a high growth rate is still essential to lift living standards.

Eight National Missions form the core of the plan. Each is institutionalised by the ministry concerned and run through inter-sectoral groups. Together they cover understanding climate change, adaptation and mitigation, energy efficiency and conservation of natural resources.

The plan's principles include protecting the poor through an inclusive, climate-sensitive development strategy, meeting growth and poverty goals while keeping ecological sustainability, and wide use of technologies for adaptation and mitigation.

Memory aid: two energy missions (solar, efficiency), two resource missions (water, agriculture), two ecosystem missions (Himalaya, Green India), and two cross-cutting missions (habitat, knowledge).

National Solar Mission: launched in January 2010, it aims to make India a global leader in solar energy by creating policy conditions for the technology to spread quickly. It was planned in three phases: up to 2012-13, 2013-17 and 2017-22.

Its original target was 20 GW of solar power by 2022, raised to 100 GW in early 2015, five times the first goal.

NMEEE: implemented since 2011, it builds a market for energy efficiency through regulation, policy and new business models. It has four initiatives for energy-intensive industries:

  • PAT: Perform, Achieve and Trade
  • MTEE: Market Transformation for Energy Efficiency
  • EEFP: Energy Efficiency Financing Platform
  • FEEED: Framework for Energy Efficient Economic Development

National Mission on Sustainable Habitat: approved by the Prime Minister's Council on Climate Change in June 2010. Its deliverables include sustainable habitat standards, city development plans that address adaptation and mitigation, comprehensive mobility plans for energy-efficient transport, and capacity building.

National Water Mission: it promotes integrated water resource management to conserve water, cut waste and share it more fairly across and within States. Its five goals are:

  1. a comprehensive public water database and assessment of climate impacts on water;
  2. citizen and State action for conservation, augmentation and preservation;
  3. focused attention on vulnerable and over-exploited areas;
  4. raising water use efficiency by 20 per cent;
  5. basin-level integrated water resources management.

It also looks to recycled waste water for urban needs and to low-temperature desalination for coastal cities short of water.

NMSHE: its goal is to prevent melting of Himalayan glaciers and protect biodiversity in the region. The Union Cabinet approved its mission document on 28 February 2014, with an outlay of ₹550 crore for the Twelfth Five Year Plan. It covers all 12 Himalayan States, and the Department of Science and Technology coordinates it.

Green India Mission: approved by the Cabinet Committee on Economic Affairs as a Centrally Sponsored Scheme, on a proposal of the Ministry of Environment and Forests. It treats greening as a tool for both adaptation and mitigation. Its objectives:

  • forest or tree cover on 5 million ha and better forest quality on another 5 million ha (10 million ha in all);
  • better ecosystem services on that 10 million ha;
  • higher forest-based income for about 3 million households;
  • annual CO₂ sequestration raised by 50-60 million tonnes by 2020.

NMSA: operational from 2014-15, it aims to make farming more productive, sustainable, remunerative and climate resilient. It does this through integrated farming systems, soil and moisture conservation, soil health management and efficient water use. Its Farm Water Management component was later merged into Per Drop More Crop under PMKSY in 2015-16. The Soil Health Card scheme has run since February 2015.

NMSKCC: builds a knowledge system to support national climate action. Deliverables include at least 10 thematic knowledge networks, regional climate models, 50 chair professorships, about 200 trained climate researchers and technology watch groups.

Q1. When was India's National Action Plan on Climate Change released? easy

✅ Answer: (a) 30 June 2008

💡 Explanation: The Prime Minister released the NAPCC on 30 June 2008; its eight National Missions form the core of the plan.

Q2. Which of these is NOT one of the eight NAPCC missions? medium

✅ Answer: (b) National Wind Energy Mission

💡 Explanation: The eight missions are Solar, Enhanced Energy Efficiency, Sustainable Habitat, Water, Himalayan Eco-system, Green India, Sustainable Agriculture and Strategic Knowledge. There is no wind mission.

Q3. Perform, Achieve and Trade (PAT) is an initiative under which mission? medium

✅ Answer: (c) National Mission for Enhanced Energy Efficiency

💡 Explanation: NMEEE has four initiatives: PAT, MTEE, EEFP and FEEED, aimed at energy-intensive industries.

Q4. The National Water Mission aims to raise water use efficiency by: medium

✅ Answer: (d) 20 per cent

💡 Explanation: The NWM seeks to optimise water use by increasing water use efficiency by 20 per cent.

Q5. Which body coordinates the National Mission for Sustaining the Himalayan Ecosystem? medium

✅ Answer: (a) Department of Science and Technology

💡 Explanation: The Cabinet approved the NMSHE mission document on 28 February 2014 and gave DST the responsibility to coordinate it.

Q6. The Green India Mission aims to increase forest or tree cover on how much land, and improve forest quality on how much more? hard

✅ Answer: (b) 5 million ha each (10 million ha in all)

💡 Explanation: GIM targets forest/tree cover on 5 m ha and improved quality on another 5 m ha, a total of 10 m ha.

Q7. The initial National Solar Mission target of 20 GW by 2022 was raised in 2015 to: medium

✅ Answer: (c) 100 GW

💡 Explanation: The PIB FAQ says the 20 GW target was upscaled to 100 GW in early 2015.

What are the 8 missions of NAPCC?

National Solar Mission, Enhanced Energy Efficiency, Sustainable Habitat, National Water Mission, Sustaining the Himalayan Eco-system, Green India, Sustainable Agriculture, and Strategic Knowledge for Climate Change.

Who released the NAPCC and when?

The Prime Minister released the National Action Plan on Climate Change on 30 June 2008. Its eight National Missions are run by the respective ministries through inter-sectoral groups.

Which NAPCC mission covers glaciers?

The National Mission for Sustaining the Himalayan Eco-system. Its goal is to prevent melting of Himalayan glaciers and protect biodiversity, and it covers all 12 Himalayan States.
