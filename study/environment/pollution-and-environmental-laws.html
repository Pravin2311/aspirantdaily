  • Pollution is any undesirable change in the physical, chemical or biological character of air, water or land that harms living organisms.
  • India's environmental law framework grew after the Stockholm Conference (1972) and the Bhopal gas tragedy (1984).
  • The Constitution adds two duties: Article 48A (a Directive Principle to protect the environment) and Article 51A(g) (a Fundamental Duty), both inserted by the 42nd Amendment (1976).

The National Air Quality Index (2014)

India's AQI, launched in 2014, is based on eight pollutants: PM10, PM2.5, NO₂, SO₂, CO, O₃, NH₃ and lead (Pb). The worst-performing pollutant sets the day's index.

Major Air Pollutants

  • PM2.5 — particles under 2.5 micrometres; reach deep into the lungs and bloodstream.
  • CO — binds to haemoglobin about 200 times more strongly than oxygen, forming carboxyhaemoglobin.
  • SO₂ and NOₓ — cause acid rain (pH below 5.6); acid rain has damaged the marble of the Taj Mahal ("marble cancer").
  • Ground-level ozone — a secondary pollutant formed from NOₓ and hydrocarbons in sunlight; a component of photochemical smog.

Water Pollution

  • Biological Oxygen Demand (BOD) is the oxygen microbes need to decompose organic matter in water. High BOD = more pollution. The CPCB criterion for outdoor bathing water is a BOD of 3 mg/L or less.
  • Eutrophication: runoff rich in nitrates and phosphates (fertilisers, sewage) causes algal blooms, which use up dissolved oxygen when they decay and kill fish.
  • Biomagnification: non-biodegradable toxins such as DDT and mercury become more concentrated at each higher trophic level — top predators like fish-eating birds are hit hardest (DDT thinned their eggshells).

Q1. A high Biological Oxygen Demand (BOD) in a river indicates:

✅ Answer: (c) High organic pollution

💡 Explanation: High BOD means microbes need a lot of oxygen to break down organic waste — a sign of pollution.

Q2. Minamata disease is caused by:

✅ Answer: (a) Mercury

💡 Explanation: It was first identified in Minamata, Japan, from mercury-contaminated fish.

Q3. The Central Pollution Control Board was constituted under the:

✅ Answer: (b) Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1974

💡 Explanation: The CPCB was set up in 1974 under the Water Act and later given powers under the Air Act.

Q4. Which of these is NOT one of the eight pollutants in India's National AQI?

✅ Answer: (d) Methane

💡 Explanation: The eight are PM10, PM2.5, NO₂, SO₂, CO, O₃, NH₃ and Pb; methane is not included.

Q5. Articles 48A and 51A(g) on environmental protection were added by the:

✅ Answer: (b) 42nd Amendment

💡 Explanation: The 42nd Amendment of 1976 inserted both provisions.

🔁 Revise in 60 Seconds

  • AQI 2014: 8 pollutants, 6 categories (Good to Severe, 0–500).
  • High BOD = polluted; eutrophication = nutrient overload → algal bloom → oxygen loss.
  • Mercury → Minamata; cadmium → itai-itai; nitrate → blue baby; fluoride → fluorosis.
  • Acts: Wildlife 1972, Water 1974, Forest 1980, Air 1981, EPA 1986, BD 2002, NGT 2010.

📌 Prelims Traps to Avoid

  • ✘ Ozone is harmful at ground level but protective in the stratosphere.
  • ✘ The EPA (1986) came after Bhopal (1984), not before.
  • ✘ Acid rain has a pH below 5.6, not below 7 — normal rain is already slightly acidic.

What are the AQI categories in India?

Six: Good (0–50), Satisfactory (51–100), Moderately polluted (101–200), Poor (201–300), Very poor (301–400) and Severe (401–500). The index uses eight pollutants, and the worst one sets the value.

What is biomagnification?

The increase in concentration of a non-biodegradable toxin, such as DDT or mercury, at each successive level of a food chain. Top predators therefore carry the highest levels.
