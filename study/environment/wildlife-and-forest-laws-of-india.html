🐅 Wildlife & Forest Laws of India

Article 48A & 51A(g) • Wildlife Protection Act • Protected Areas • Forest Conservation Act • FRA 2006 • Biodiversity Act

High Yield Environment 📅 Last updated: Oct 2026
🔍 Quick Search: Article 48A 51A(g) 42nd amendment, Wildlife Protection Act 2022 amendment schedules, difference national park and sanctuary, conservation reserve community reserve, NTCA created, Van Sanrakshan Evam Samvardhan Adhiniyam, FRA gram sabha, NBA Chennai
  • India's environmental law grew in three waves: colonial forest laws aimed at timber, post-1970s protection laws such as the Wildlife (Protection) Act and the Forest (Conservation) Act, and rights-based laws such as the Forest Rights Act, 2006.
  • UPSC asks about the schedules, protected-area categories, the bodies each Act creates and recent amendments.
  • Pollution laws (Water Act, Air Act, NGT) are covered in our Pollution & Environmental Laws notes.

Constitutional Provisions

  • Article 48A (Directive Principle) — the State shall protect and improve the environment and safeguard forests and wildlife.
  • Article 51A(g) (Fundamental Duty) — every citizen shall protect the natural environment and have compassion for living creatures.
  • Both were added by the 42nd Amendment (1976), which also moved forests and the protection of wild animals and birds to the Concurrent List.
  • Courts read the right to a clean environment into Article 21 (right to life).

Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972

  • Provides for protected areas, bans hunting of listed species, and regulates trade in wildlife.
  • The 2002 amendment added conservation reserves and community reserves. The 2006 amendment created the National Tiger Conservation Authority (NTCA) and the Wildlife Crime Control Bureau.
  • The 2022 amendment cut the schedules from six to four: Schedule I (highest protection), Schedule II (lesser protection), Schedule III (protected plants) and Schedule IV (specimens listed under CITES). The separate vermin schedule was removed, and the Act now gives effect to CITES.
  • The National Board for Wildlife is chaired by the Prime Minister.

Forest Laws

LawKey points
Indian Forest Act, 1927Classifies reserved, protected and village forests. A 2017 amendment removed bamboo grown outside forests from the definition of a tree, so it can be cut and moved freely
Forest (Conservation) Act, 1980Requires the Centre's prior approval before forest land is used for non-forest purposes
Forest (Conservation) Amendment Act, 2023Renamed the law the Van (Sanrakshan Evam Samvardhan) Adhiniyam. Exempts some land, such as strategic linear projects within 100 km of international borders, and allows activities such as zoos and eco-tourism in forests. Critics argue it narrows the meaning of "forest"
Compensatory Afforestation Fund Act, 2016 (CAMPA)Money paid by projects that use forest land goes into funds for afforestation — 90% to states, 10% to the Centre

In the T.N. Godavarman case (1996), the Supreme Court held that "forest" should be understood by its dictionary meaning, covering all forests whatever their ownership or legal status.

Forest Rights Act, 2006

  • Full name: the Scheduled Tribes and Other Traditional Forest Dwellers (Recognition of Forest Rights) Act. The nodal ministry is the Ministry of Tribal Affairs.
  • Recognises individual rights (land under occupation, up to 4 hectares) and community rights (grazing, fishing, minor forest produce, managing community forest resources).
  • The Gram Sabha starts the process of recognising rights.
  • "Other traditional forest dwellers" must show they have lived in forests for three generations (75 years) before December 13, 2005.

Biological Diversity Act, 2002

Passed to implement the CBD. It has a three-tier structure: the National Biodiversity Authority (Chennai, 2003), State Biodiversity Boards, and Biodiversity Management Committees at the local level, which prepare People's Biodiversity Registers. A 2023 amendment decriminalised many offences and eased rules for AYUSH practitioners and research.

Environment (Protection) Act, 1986

An umbrella law passed after the Bhopal gas tragedy (1984). It lets the Centre set standards and issue notifications — such as the Environmental Impact Assessment notification (2006), the Coastal Regulation Zone notifications (2011, 2019) and eco-sensitive zones around protected areas.

  1. Amendment ↔ change: 2002 (conservation and community reserves), 2006 (NTCA, WCCB), 2022 (four schedules, CITES).
  2. National park vs sanctuary statement questions — grazing and private rights.
  3. Who does what: NBWL (PM), FRA (Gram Sabha, Tribal Affairs), NBA (Chennai), GEAC (MoEFCC).

Q1. Article 48A and Article 51A(g) were added to the Constitution by the:

✅ Answer: (b) 42nd Amendment

💡 Explanation: The 42nd Amendment (1976) added both and moved forests and wildlife to the Concurrent List.

Q2. After the 2022 amendment, how many schedules does the Wildlife (Protection) Act have?

✅ Answer: (a) Four

💡 Explanation: The schedules were cut from six to four, including a new schedule for CITES specimens.

Q3. The National Board for Wildlife is chaired by the:

✅ Answer: (d) Prime Minister

💡 Explanation: The PM chairs the NBWL; its Standing Committee is chaired by the Environment Minister.

Q4. Under the Forest Rights Act, 2006, the process of recognising rights is started by the:

✅ Answer: (c) Gram Sabha

💡 Explanation: The Gram Sabha receives and verifies claims before they go to higher committees.

Q5. Which of these was created by the 2006 amendment to the Wildlife (Protection) Act?

✅ Answer: (d) National Tiger Conservation Authority

💡 Explanation: The 2006 amendment created the NTCA and the Wildlife Crime Control Bureau.

📌 Prelims Traps to Avoid

  • ✘ Grazing is not allowed in a national park, but may be allowed in a sanctuary.
  • ✘ The nodal ministry for the Forest Rights Act is Tribal Affairs, not Environment.
  • ✘ The Environment Protection Act was passed after Bhopal (1984), not Stockholm (1972).

What is the difference between a national park and a wildlife sanctuary?

A national park has stricter protection: no grazing or private rights are allowed, and its boundaries can be changed only on a resolution of the state legislature. A wildlife sanctuary may permit some regulated activities, such as grazing, and has comparatively flexible rules.

What changed in the Wildlife (Protection) Amendment Act, 2022?

It reduced the schedules from six to four, added a schedule for specimens listed under CITES so the Act gives effect to the convention, removed the separate vermin schedule, and raised penalties for violations.
