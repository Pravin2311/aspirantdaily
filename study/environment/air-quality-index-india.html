National Air Quality Index (AQI) of India: Six Categories, Eight Pollutants, Breakpoints and How AQI Is Calculated

Six categories and ranges • Health impacts • Eight pollutants • Breakpoint table • Sub-index and the worst-pollutant rule • Worked example • Exam traps

The National Air Quality Index (AQI) is the Central Pollution Control Board's tool for telling people how clean or polluted the air is. In CPCB's words, it turns complex data on many pollutants into "a single number (index value), nomenclature and colour".

The index was developed by an Expert Group of medical professionals, air quality experts, academics, NGOs and State Pollution Control Boards, with technical information from IIT Kanpur. A draft was released for public comment in October 2014 and then finalised.

The index serves several users at once. Ordinary citizens, especially people with asthma or heart disease, can plan their day around it, while regulators use it to compare cities, track trends over time and decide where pollution control is needed most.

CPCB documents name the third band both "Moderate" and "Moderately polluted". Either answer refers to 101-200.

The AQI uses the eight pollutants that have short-term (up to 24-hour) national ambient air quality standards:

  • Particulate matter: PM10 and PM2.5
  • Gases: nitrogen dioxide (NO₂), sulphur dioxide (SO₂), carbon monoxide (CO), ozone (O₃) and ammonia (NH₃)
  • Metal: lead (Pb)

CO and O₃ are measured as 8-hour averages; the other six use 24-hour averages. CO is in mg/m³ and the rest in µg/m³.

The national ambient air quality standards of 2009 cover 12 parameters. The other four (nickel, arsenic, benzo(a)pyrene and benzene) have only annual standards, so they are left out of a daily index.

All values are in µg/m³. Notice the pattern CPCB used: a concentration up to half the national standard is "Good", and up to the standard itself is "Satisfactory". So the 24-hour PM2.5 standard sits at the top of the Satisfactory band, 60 µg/m³.

  1. Sub-index for each pollutant. The measured concentration is converted to a sub-index by straight-line interpolation within its band: I = [(IHI − ILO) / (BHI − BLO)] × (C − BLO) + ILO. Here BLO and BHI are the band's concentration limits, ILO and IHI its AQI limits, and C the measured concentration.
  2. Worst sub-index wins. The overall AQI is the highest sub-index: AQI = max(I₁, I₂, … Iₙ). The pollutant behind it is the one to act on.
  3. Minimum data. AQI is calculated only when data are available for at least three pollutants, one of which must be PM2.5 or PM10.

CPCB's own example: PM2.5 has a sub-index of 51 at 31 µg/m³ and 100 at 60 µg/m³, so 45 µg/m³ gives 51 + (45 − 31) × 49 / 29 ≈ 75.

Worked example: find the AQI

A station reports 24-hour averages of PM10 = 150, PM2.5 = 75 and NO₂ = 50 µg/m³.

  • PM10 150 is in the 101-250 band (AQI 101-200): 101 + (150 − 101) × 99 / 149 ≈ 134.
  • PM2.5 75 is in the 61-90 band (AQI 101-200): 101 + (75 − 61) × 99 / 29 ≈ 149.
  • NO₂ 50 is in the 41-80 band (AQI 51-100): 51 + (50 − 41) × 49 / 39 ≈ 62.

AQI = max(134, 149, 62) = 149, which is Moderately polluted, with PM2.5 as the main pollutant.

  • ✘ Saying the AQI is an average of pollutants. It is the maximum sub-index.
  • ✘ Listing benzene or arsenic among the AQI pollutants. They have only annual standards; the eight are PM10, PM2.5, NO₂, SO₂, CO, O₃, NH₃ and Pb.
  • ✘ Giving five categories. India uses six; the top two are Very Poor (301-400) and Severe (401-500).
  • ✘ Mixing up averaging periods. CO and O₃ use 8-hour values; the others use 24-hour values.
  • ✘ Crediting IMD or IITM. The National AQI is CPCB's, developed with technical input from IIT Kanpur.

Q1. How many categories does India's National Air Quality Index have? easy

✅ Answer: (a) Six

💡 Explanation: CPCB's AQI has six categories: Good, Satisfactory, Moderately polluted, Poor, Very Poor and Severe.

Q2. An AQI value of 350 falls in which category? easy

✅ Answer: (b) Very Poor

💡 Explanation: Very Poor covers 301-400; Poor is 201-300 and Severe 401-500.

Q3. Which of these is NOT one of the eight pollutants in India's AQI? medium

✅ Answer: (c) Benzene

💡 Explanation: The eight are PM10, PM2.5, NO2, SO2, CO, O3, NH3 and Pb. Benzene has only an annual standard, so it is not in the AQI.

Q4. How is the overall AQI of a location decided? medium

✅ Answer: (d) By the worst (highest) sub-index

💡 Explanation: CPCB states that the worst sub-index determines the overall AQI: AQI = max(I1, I2, ..., In).

Q5. AQI can be calculated only if data are available for at least how many pollutants? medium

✅ Answer: (a) Three, including PM2.5 or PM10

💡 Explanation: CPCB requires at least three pollutants, one of which must be PM2.5 or PM10.

Q6. A 24-hour PM2.5 level of 100 µg/m³ puts the air in which category? hard

✅ Answer: (b) Poor

💡 Explanation: In CPCB's breakpoint table, a 24-hour PM2.5 level of 91-120 µg/m³ maps to the Poor category (AQI 201-300).

Q7. Which institution provided technical support for developing India's National AQI? medium

✅ Answer: (c) IIT Kanpur

💡 Explanation: CPCB's report says IIT Kanpur provided the technical information; an Expert Group developed the index.

Q8. For which pollutants does India's AQI use 8-hour average values? hard

What are the AQI categories in India?

Good (0-50), Satisfactory (51-100), Moderately polluted (101-200), Poor (201-300), Very Poor (301-400) and Severe (401-500), as set by the Central Pollution Control Board.

Which pollutants are used in India's AQI?

Eight: PM10, PM2.5, nitrogen dioxide, sulphur dioxide, carbon monoxide, ozone, ammonia and lead. These are the pollutants with short-term national ambient air quality standards.

Who publishes the AQI in India?

The Central Pollution Control Board, under the Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change. It developed the National AQI with an Expert Group and technical input from IIT Kanpur.

Is AQI the average of all pollutants?

No. A sub-index is worked out for each pollutant, and the highest sub-index becomes the AQI. The pollutant behind it is the one driving poor air on that day.
