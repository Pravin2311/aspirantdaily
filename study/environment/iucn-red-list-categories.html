IUCN Red List Categories and Criteria: The 9 Categories and the A to E Thresholds for CR, EN and VU

EX • EW • CR • EN • VU • NT • LC • DD • NE • What 'threatened' means • Data Deficient vs Not Evaluated • Criteria A to E with thresholds

UPSC SSC State PSC 📅 Last updated: 2026-11-04

The IUCN Red List places every species (or other taxon) assessed into one of nine categories. The rules are set out in IUCN's Categories and Criteria, Version 3.1, the document assessors use. Exam questions usually test the order of the categories, which ones count as "threatened", and the difference between Data Deficient and Not Evaluated. Here they are from highest risk to lowest, followed by the two categories for species that cannot be placed on the scale.

Threatened means only three categories: Critically Endangered, Endangered and Vulnerable. The document notes that every taxon listed as CR also qualifies for EN and VU, and every EN taxon qualifies for VU. Near Threatened is not a threatened category, and nor is Data Deficient, which the document says "is therefore not a category of threat".

These two are the most confused pair. A Not Evaluated species has never been assessed. A Data Deficient species has been looked at, but the data on its numbers or range are too thin to place it. The IUCN text adds that a DD species may be well studied and its biology well known. It is the abundance or distribution data that is missing. Listing a species as DD signals that more research is needed, and it allows that future work may show the species to be threatened.

A species is listed as CR, EN or VU if it meets any one of five criteria for that category. Each criterion looks at a different kind of evidence. The thresholds tighten from Vulnerable to Critically Endangered.

Where a period is given as "years or generations", the longer of the two applies, up to a maximum of 100 years. Vulnerable D2 covers a species with a very restricted area of occupancy, typically under 20 km², or typically five or fewer locations. Such a species is prone to human activity or chance events and could become Critically Endangered or extinct in a very short time.

  • Any one criterion is enough. A species does not need to fail all five. A population with fewer than 50 mature individuals is Critically Endangered under D even if its range is large.
  • Mature individuals, not all individuals. Criteria C and D count only individuals capable of reproduction.
  • Extent of occurrence vs area of occupancy. Extent of occurrence is the overall area within which the species is found. Area of occupancy is the smaller area it actually occupies within that. That is why the B2 thresholds are much smaller than B1.
  • Criterion E is the only purely statistical one. It needs a quantitative model of the probability of extinction, not just counts or areas.
  • Conservation action does not change the listing. The criteria are applied whatever conservation action is affecting the species.

Q1. Which of these is NOT one of the IUCN 'threatened' categories? easy

✅ Answer: (c) Near Threatened

💡 Explanation: Only CR, EN and VU are described as threatened. Near Threatened is for taxa close to qualifying but not qualifying now.

Q2. A species known to survive only in zoos is classed as: easy

✅ Answer: (a) Extinct in the Wild

💡 Explanation: Extinct in the Wild covers taxa known only to survive in cultivation, in captivity or as a naturalised population well outside the past range.

Q3. A population of 40 mature individuals with a wide range qualifies as Critically Endangered under criterion: medium

✅ Answer: (d) D

💡 Explanation: CR criterion D is a population of fewer than 50 mature individuals. Meeting any one criterion is enough, whatever the range.

Q4. Under criterion E, the extinction-risk threshold for Vulnerable is: medium

✅ Answer: (b) At least 10% within 100 years

💡 Explanation: VU E is a probability of extinction in the wild of at least 10% within 100 years. 50% in 10 years (or 3 generations) is CR, and 20% in 20 years (or 5 generations) is EN.

Q5. Which statement about Data Deficient (DD) is correct? medium

✅ Answer: (d) DD means data on abundance or distribution are inadequate

💡 Explanation: DD species have been looked at but the data are inadequate to assess risk. Never-assessed species are Not Evaluated, and DD is not a category of threat.

How many Red List categories are there?

Nine: Extinct, Extinct in the Wild, Critically Endangered, Endangered, Vulnerable, Near Threatened, Least Concern, Data Deficient and Not Evaluated.

What is the difference between extent of occurrence and area of occupancy?

Extent of occurrence is the area inside the shortest boundary that encloses all known sites of a species. Area of occupancy is the smaller area within that which the species actually occupies, since it rarely lives everywhere inside the boundary.
