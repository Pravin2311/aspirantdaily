Kyoto Protocol vs Paris Agreement: Targets, Binding Nature, Mechanisms, Dates and India's Position Compared

Side-by-side table • Kyoto: Annex I targets and three mechanisms • Paris: temperature goal, NDCs and stocktake • Entry into force • India's dates • US withdrawal • Exam traps

UPSC SSC State PSC 📅 Last updated: 2026-11-10

The Kyoto Protocol and the Paris Agreement are the two treaties under the UN Framework Convention on Climate Change that set out how countries will cut greenhouse gas emissions. They take opposite approaches. Kyoto handed binding targets to a list of mainly developed countries; Paris lets every country set its own contribution but binds all of them to a common goal and a regular review cycle.

Under Article 3, the Parties listed in Annex I must keep their emissions within assigned amounts, with a view to cutting their overall emissions by at least 5 per cent below 1990 levels. The first commitment period ran from 2008 to 2012. Parties outside Annex I had no quantified reduction targets.

Kyoto covers six gases, listed in Annex A:

  • carbon dioxide (CO₂), methane (CH₄) and nitrous oxide (N₂O);
  • hydrofluorocarbons (HFCs), perfluorocarbons (PFCs) and sulphur hexafluoride (SF₆).

To enter into force, Kyoto needed at least 55 Parties, including Annex I Parties responsible for at least 55% of Annex I carbon dioxide emissions in 1990. That took until 16 February 2005.

  1. Joint implementation (Article 6): an Annex I Party can transfer or acquire emission reduction units from projects in another Annex I Party.
  2. Clean Development Mechanism (Article 12): projects in non-Annex I countries generate certified emission reductions. The host country gains sustainable development and the Annex I buyer uses the credits toward its target.
  3. Emissions trading (Article 17): Annex I Parties may trade to meet their commitments, but any trading must be supplemental to domestic action.

Article 2 sets the long-term goal: hold the global average temperature rise to well below 2°C above pre-industrial levels and pursue efforts to limit it to 1.5°C. The Agreement also aims to build adaptive capacity and to make finance flows consistent with low-emission, climate-resilient development.

It is implemented on the principle of common but differentiated responsibilities and respective capabilities, in the light of different national circumstances (often shortened to CBDR-RC).

The key features of Article 4:

  • every Party must prepare, communicate and maintain successive nationally determined contributions (NDCs);
  • each new NDC must be a progression beyond the last and reflect the Party's highest possible ambition;
  • NDCs are communicated every five years;
  • developed countries should keep taking the lead with economy-wide absolute cuts, while developing countries keep strengthening their efforts;
  • the aim is a balance between emissions and removals by sinks in the second half of this century.

Under Article 14, the Parties take a global stocktake of collective progress, the first in 2023 and then every five years, so the next falls in 2028.

Article 6 keeps a role for markets, on a voluntary basis. Parties may cooperate using internationally transferred mitigation outcomes (ITMOs) toward their NDCs, with robust accounting to avoid double counting, and the Article also establishes a new mechanism to support mitigation and sustainable development.

Paris entered into force on 4 November 2016, once at least 55 Parties covering at least an estimated 55% of global emissions had ratified it.

Under Article 28, a Party can withdraw, and the withdrawal takes effect one year after the UN Secretary-General receives the notice. The United States has used this twice. It left with effect from 4 November 2020, rejoined on 20 January 2021, and notified a second withdrawal on 27 January 2025, which took effect on 27 January 2026.

  • ✘ Saying Kyoto bound all countries. Only Annex I Parties had quantified targets.
  • ✘ Saying Paris sets a fixed cut for each country. Each country sets its own NDC; what is binding is the obligation to prepare, communicate and update it.
  • ✘ Mixing the dates. Kyoto was adopted in 1997 and came into force in 2005; Paris was adopted in 2015 and came into force in 2016.
  • ✘ Putting the CDM under Paris. The CDM, joint implementation and emissions trading are Kyoto mechanisms; Paris has its own Article 6 cooperation.
  • ✘ Listing ozone as a Kyoto gas. The six are CO₂, CH₄, N₂O, HFCs, PFCs and SF₆.

Q1. Under the Kyoto Protocol, which countries took on binding emission reduction commitments? easy

✅ Answer: (a) Annex I (developed and transition) countries

💡 Explanation: Article 3 binds the Parties in Annex I to keep within assigned amounts, aiming at an overall cut of at least 5% below 1990 levels.

Q2. Which Kyoto mechanism lets Annex I countries earn credits from projects in developing countries? medium

✅ Answer: (b) Clean Development Mechanism

💡 Explanation: Under the CDM (Article 12), non-Annex I countries host projects that generate certified emission reductions, which Annex I countries can use toward compliance.

Q3. The Paris Agreement aims to hold the global temperature rise to: easy

✅ Answer: (c) Well below 2°C, pursuing 1.5°C

💡 Explanation: Article 2 sets the goal of well below 2°C above pre-industrial levels while pursuing efforts to limit warming to 1.5°C.

Q4. How often must each party communicate a nationally determined contribution under the Paris Agreement? medium

✅ Answer: (d) Every five years

💡 Explanation: Article 4.9 requires each Party to communicate an NDC every five years, informed by the global stocktake.

Q5. When did the Paris Agreement enter into force? medium

✅ Answer: (a) 4 November 2016

💡 Explanation: Adopted on 12 December 2015, it entered into force on 4 November 2016 after the 55 Parties and 55% of emissions threshold was met.

Q6. When did India ratify the Paris Agreement? medium

✅ Answer: (b) 2 October 2016

💡 Explanation: India signed on 22 April 2016 and deposited its ratification on 2 October 2016, per the UN Treaty Collection.

Q7. Which gas is NOT among the six greenhouse gases in Annex A of the Kyoto Protocol? hard

✅ Answer: (c) Ozone

💡 Explanation: Annex A lists CO2, CH4, N2O, HFCs, PFCs and SF6. Ozone is not on the list.

Q8. The first global stocktake under the Paris Agreement was due in: medium

✅ Answer: (d) 2023

💡 Explanation: Article 14.2 sets the first global stocktake in 2023 and every five years after that.

What is the main difference between the Kyoto Protocol and the Paris Agreement?

Kyoto set binding, quantified emission targets only for Annex I (mainly developed) countries. Paris asks every Party to set its own nationally determined contribution, updated every five years, toward a shared temperature goal.

When did India join the Kyoto Protocol and the Paris Agreement?

India acceded to the Kyoto Protocol on 26 August 2002. It signed the Paris Agreement on 22 April 2016 and ratified it on 2 October 2016, per the UN Treaty Collection.

Is the United States still in the Paris Agreement?

No. The United States notified its withdrawal on 27 January 2025, and it took effect on 27 January 2026. It had earlier left in 2020 and rejoined in 2021.

What were the three Kyoto mechanisms?

Joint implementation between Annex I countries (Article 6), the Clean Development Mechanism with developing countries (Article 12) and emissions trading (Article 17), which must be supplemental to domestic action.
