Federal and Unitary Features of the Indian Constitution, With the Articles Behind Each

Union of States • Seventh Schedule lists • Article 131 • Governor appointed by President • Article 3 • Articles 248, 249, 254, 312 • Emergency

UPSC SSC State PSC 📅 Last updated: 2026-10-31

Exams ask whether India is federal or unitary because the Constitution is deliberately both. It sets up a Union and States with their own powers, which is federal. Then it gives the Union enough overriding powers to hold the country together in a crisis, which is unitary. The useful answer is not a one-word label but the list of features on each side, with the Article behind each.

The State List is meant to be the States' own territory, but the Constitution opens several doors through which Parliament can enter it. Knowing the conditions for each is what separates a strong answer from a list of features.

ArticleConditionWho triggers it
249National interestRajya Sabha resolution supported by two-thirds of members present and voting
250A Proclamation of Emergency is in operationAutomatic while the emergency lasts
252Two or more States want a common lawResolutions by the Legislatures of those States; other States may adopt the Act later

Two of these routes give the States a say. Under Article 249 the trigger is the Rajya Sabha, the House where the States are represented, and under Article 252 the States themselves ask for the law. Only Article 250 overrides the States without any vote of theirs, and only while an emergency is in force. That pattern supports the "federal in normal times, unitary in a crisis" reading.

Conflicts are settled the same way in every case. Under Article 254, if a State law on a Concurrent List matter is repugnant to a law made by Parliament, Parliament's law prevails to the extent of the repugnancy. Clause (2) allows an exception: a State law that has been reserved for and received the President's assent prevails in that State. Even then, Parliament can at any time pass a law on the same matter, including one that amends or repeals the State law.

Avoid two weak answers: calling India simply "federal" because it has States, or simply "unitary" because the Centre is strong. A balanced answer has three parts. First, India has the essential federal features. Second, the Constitution adds strong unitary features, so textbooks call it quasi-federal or "federal with a unitary bias". Third, the balance shifts with circumstances: normally federal, it can work almost as a unitary State in an emergency.

  • ✘ "The Constitution calls India a federation." Article 1 says "Union of States".
  • ✘ "Residuary powers lie with the States, as in the USA." In India they lie with Parliament (Article 248).
  • ✘ "A State must consent before its boundary is changed." Under Article 3 its views are sought but do not bind Parliament.

For how the two levels share legislative, administrative and financial powers in detail, see Federalism and Centre-State relations; for the three lists, see the Schedules of the Constitution.

Q1. Article 1 describes India as a: easy

✅ Answer: (b) Union of States

💡 Explanation: Article 1(1) reads 'India, that is Bharat, shall be a Union of States'. The word 'federation' is not used.

Q2. Residuary powers of legislation in India rest with: easy

✅ Answer: (b) Parliament

💡 Explanation: Article 248 gives Parliament exclusive power over matters not in the Concurrent or State List. This is a unitary feature; in the USA residuary powers lie with the states.

Q3. Which Article lets Parliament legislate on a State List subject after a Rajya Sabha resolution? medium

✅ Answer: (b) Article 249

💡 Explanation: Article 249 requires a Rajya Sabha resolution supported by two-thirds of members present and voting, in the national interest.

Q4. Which of these is a FEDERAL feature of the Constitution? medium

✅ Answer: (c) Supreme Court's original jurisdiction in Centre-State disputes

💡 Explanation: Article 131 makes the Supreme Court the arbiter between the Union and States, a federal feature. The others strengthen the Centre.

Q5. Under Article 3, when Parliament changes a State's boundaries, the State Legislature's views are: hard

✅ Answer: (c) sought but not binding

💡 Explanation: The President refers the Bill to the State Legislature 'for expressing its views', but Parliament is not bound by them. This is why India is called an 'indestructible Union of destructible States'.

Is India a federation?

The Constitution has the main federal features: a written Constitution, two levels of government, a division of powers and an independent judiciary to settle disputes. But it never uses the word 'federation' and it gives the Centre strong overriding powers. Textbooks therefore describe it as quasi-federal, or federal with a unitary bias.

Why is India called an 'indestructible Union of destructible States'?

Because the two halves of the phrase come from different provisions. The Union is indestructible: Article 1 calls India a Union of States, and the Constitution gives no State a right to leave it. The States are destructible: under Article 3 Parliament can, by ordinary law, form new States, merge or divide existing ones, and change their boundaries or names, with the affected State only asked for its views. Several States have been reorganised this way since 1950.

Does a Centre-State dispute go to the High Court?

No. Under Article 131 the Supreme Court has original jurisdiction 'to the exclusion of any other court' in disputes between the Government of India and States, or between States, where a legal right is involved.
