GST Council Under Article 279A: Composition, Weighted Voting with Worked Examples, and Functions

Article 279A • 101st Amendment • Centre one-third, States two-thirds • Three-fourths majority • Quorum one-half • Petroleum items • Article 366(26B)

UPSC SSC Banking State PSC 📅 Last updated: 2026-11-05

The GST Council is a constitutional body created by Article 279A. The 101st Amendment (2016) inserted the Article. Article 279A(1) required the President to constitute the Council by order within sixty days of that Amendment coming into force. It is the forum where the Union and the States jointly decide how the goods and services tax works. Article 246A gives both Parliament and the State Legislatures the power to make GST laws.

DecisionsThree-fourths of weighted votes of members present and voting (279A(9))

All States' weight togetherTwo-thirds of the total votes cast

For Article 279A, "State" includes a Union territory with a Legislature, by Article 366(26B). So a Union territory such as Delhi or Puducherry, which has its own Legislature, is represented on the Council in the same way as a State.

Article 279A(9) is the clause exams test with numbers. Every decision is taken at a meeting "by a majority of not less than three-fourths of the weighted votes of the members present and voting". Two weights apply:

  • the vote of the Central Government has a weightage of one-third of the total votes cast;
  • the votes of all the State Governments taken together have a weightage of two-thirds of the total votes cast.

The Article does not say how the two-thirds is split among the States. The worked examples below assume each State present and voting counts equally.

Step 1: Can the Centre pass a decision alone? No. Its maximum is one-third, about 33.3%, which is well below 75%.

Step 2: Can all the States pass a decision without the Centre? No. Together they hold two-thirds, about 66.7%, which is still below 75%. So the Centre has an effective veto.

Step 3: How many States must join the Centre? The Centre's 33.3% plus the States' share must reach 75%. The States must supply 75 − 33.3 = 41.7 percentage points out of their 66.7. That is 41.7 ÷ 66.7 = 0.625, so 62.5% of the States present and voting must vote with the Centre.

Step 4: How many States can block? If more than 37.5% of the States present and voting oppose, the Centre and the remaining States cannot reach 75%. A large enough group of States can therefore also block a decision.

The figure of 31 State members is only an example to show the arithmetic. The rule itself depends on who is present and voting at the meeting. With 31 voting, 0.625 × 31 = 19.4, so 20 States are needed alongside the Centre.

The Council makes recommendations to the Union and the States on:

  1. the taxes, cesses and surcharges of the Union, the States and local bodies that may be subsumed in GST;
  2. the goods and services that may be subjected to, or exempted from, GST;
  3. model GST laws, principles of levy, apportionment of inter-State GST under Article 269A, and the principles that govern the place of supply;
  4. the threshold limit of turnover below which goods and services may be exempted;
  5. the rates, including floor rates with bands;
  6. any special rate for a specified period to raise additional resources during a natural calamity or disaster;
  7. special provisions for Arunachal Pradesh, Assam, Jammu and Kashmir, Manipur, Meghalaya, Mizoram, Nagaland, Sikkim, Tripura, Himachal Pradesh and Uttarakhand;
  8. any other GST matter the Council decides.

Under Article 279A(5), the Council also recommends the date from which GST will be levied on five petroleum items: petroleum crude, high speed diesel, motor spirit (petrol), natural gas and aviation turbine fuel. Until then these items stay outside GST. The current rate structure is covered on taxation and GST.

  • Harmonisation (279A(6)): the Council is to be guided by the need for a harmonised structure of GST and a harmonised national market for goods and services.
  • Own procedure (279A(8)): the Council determines its own procedure.
  • Defects do not invalidate (279A(10)): no act of the Council is invalid merely because of a vacancy, a defect in its constitution or in a member's appointment, or a procedural irregularity not affecting the merits.
  • Dispute mechanism (279A(11)): the Council must set up a mechanism to adjudicate disputes arising out of its recommendations or their implementation. These may be between the Government of India and one or more States, between the Centre with some States and other States, or between two or more States.

The word the Constitution uses is "recommendations". Article 279A does not itself say they bind Parliament or the State Legislatures. Exam options that call the Council a body whose decisions are automatically law misread the text.

The GST Council is one of several bodies through which the Union and the States share fiscal decisions. The Finance Commission (Article 280) recommends how taxes are divided. The GST Council decides the design of the shared GST itself. The wider picture is on federalism and Centre-State relations and constitutional and statutory bodies.

  • Article: 279A, inserted by the 101st Amendment (2016).
  • Chair: Union Finance Minister. The Vice-Chairperson is chosen by the State members.
  • Quorum: one-half of all members.
  • Voting: three-fourths of weighted votes present and voting. The Centre has 1/3 and the States together 2/3.
  • Effect: neither side can pass a decision alone. The Centre needs 62.5% of the States present and voting.
  • Petroleum: five items wait for a Council recommendation on the date GST applies.
  • "State": includes a Union territory with a Legislature (Article 366(26B)).

Q1. In the GST Council, the weightage of the Central Government's vote is: easy

✅ Answer: (b) One-third

💡 Explanation: Article 279A(9)(a): one-third of the total votes cast. All States together have two-thirds.

Q2. A GST Council decision requires: easy

✅ Answer: (c) Three-fourths of weighted votes of members present and voting

💡 Explanation: Article 279A(9) requires not less than three-fourths of the weighted votes of members present and voting.

Q3. The quorum for a GST Council meeting is: easy

✅ Answer: (b) One-half of members

💡 Explanation: Article 279A(7) fixes the quorum at one-half of the total number of members of the Council.

Q4. If every State present votes Yes and the Centre votes No, the weighted Yes vote is about: medium

✅ Answer: (b) 66.7%, so it fails

💡 Explanation: All States together carry two-thirds, about 66.7%, which is below the three-fourths needed.

Q5. The Vice-Chairperson of the GST Council is: medium

✅ Answer: (b) Chosen by the State members from among themselves

💡 Explanation: Under Article 279A(3) the State members choose one amongst themselves as Vice-Chairperson.

Can the Centre block a GST Council decision?

Yes, in effect. Without the Centre's one-third, all States together reach only two-thirds, which is below the three-fourths needed.

Which Amendment created the GST Council?

The 101st Amendment (2016) inserted Article 279A, which required the President to constitute the GST Council within sixty days.
