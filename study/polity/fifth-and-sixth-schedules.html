Fifth Schedule vs Sixth Schedule: Scheduled Areas, Tribal Areas and Autonomous District Councils

Article 244 • Fifth Schedule paras 3-7 • Sixth Schedule paras 1-3 and 20 • Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura, Mizoram

UPSC State PSC 📅 Last updated: 2026-11-01

Article 244 creates two separate systems for areas with large tribal populations:

  • Article 244(1): the Fifth Schedule governs "Scheduled Areas and Scheduled Tribes" in every State other than Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura and Mizoram.
  • Article 244(2): the Sixth Schedule governs the "tribal areas" in Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura and Mizoram.

The two systems differ in approach. The Fifth Schedule protects tribal interests through the Governor and the Union. The Sixth Schedule gives tribal areas their own elected councils with real law-making power.

Who decides which areas are covered? The President. Paragraph 6(1) says "Scheduled Areas" means "such areas as the President may by order declare". The President can later increase, reduce or redraw them under paragraph 6(2).

Union oversight. Under paragraph 3, the Governor of each State with Scheduled Areas reports to the President "annually, or whenever so required", and the executive power of the Union extends to giving directions to the State on administering these areas. This is an exception to the usual rule that State administration is a State matter.

Tribes Advisory Council (TAC). Paragraph 4 requires a TAC in each State with Scheduled Areas, and, if the President directs, in a State with Scheduled Tribes but no Scheduled Areas. It has not more than twenty members, about three-fourths of whom are Scheduled Tribe members of the Legislative Assembly. It advises on matters of ST welfare referred to it by the Governor.

Paragraph 5 gives the Governor two strong powers:

  • Disapplying laws. By public notification, the Governor may direct that any Act of Parliament or of the State Legislature shall not apply to a Scheduled Area, or shall apply with exceptions and modifications.
  • Making regulations. The Governor may make regulations for the "peace and good government" of a Scheduled Area. These may in particular prohibit or restrict the transfer of land by or among Scheduled Tribe members, regulate allotment of land to them, and regulate money-lending to them. Regulations may even repeal or amend an Act of Parliament as it applies to the area.

Two safeguards apply. The Governor must first consult the Tribes Advisory Council, and every regulation must go to the President and has "no effect" until he assents (paragraph 5(4)-(5)).

Under paragraph 7, Parliament can amend the Fifth Schedule by ordinary law, and that law is not treated as an amendment of the Constitution under Article 368.

Paragraph 1(1) says the tribal areas listed in the table to paragraph 20 shall each be an autonomous district. If a district has different Scheduled Tribes, the Governor may divide it into autonomous regions (paragraph 1(2)).

District Councils. Each autonomous district has a District Council of not more than thirty members: not more than four nominated by the Governor and the rest elected on adult suffrage (paragraph 2(1)). For Assam, a later amendment allows the Bodoland Territorial Council up to forty-six members, forty of them elected.

Law-making power. Under paragraph 3, District Councils can make laws on subjects including:

  • allotment, occupation or use of land (other than reserved forest);
  • management of forests that are not reserved forests;
  • regulation of jhum (shifting cultivation);
  • appointment or succession of chiefs or headmen;
  • inheritance of property, marriage and divorce, and social customs.

In both Schedules the Governor is central, but in different ways. Under the Fifth Schedule, the Governor acts for the State: he can switch off ordinary laws for a Scheduled Area, make regulations that override them, and must report to the President every year. The safeguard against misuse is the President, whose assent every regulation needs.

Under the Sixth Schedule, the Governor mainly shapes the system and the councils do the governing. He can create new autonomous districts, change their areas, unite them or rename them under paragraph 1(3), though only after considering the report of a Commission for most of these changes. He also nominates up to four members of each District Council. Day-to-day law-making on land, forests and customs belongs to the elected councils.

  • ✘ "Nagaland and Manipur are Sixth Schedule States." Article 244(2) names only Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura and Mizoram.
  • ✘ "The Tribes Advisory Council can make laws." It only advises; the Governor makes regulations.
  • ✘ "The Governor declares Scheduled Areas." The President does, by order.
  • ✘ "Amending the Fifth Schedule needs Article 368." Paragraph 7(2) says an ordinary law is enough.

Q1. The Sixth Schedule applies to tribal areas in: easy

✅ Answer: (a) Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura and Mizoram

💡 Explanation: Article 244(2) applies the Sixth Schedule only to the tribal areas in Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura and Mizoram.

Q2. Who declares an area to be a Scheduled Area under the Fifth Schedule? easy

✅ Answer: (c) The President by order

💡 Explanation: Paragraph 6(1) of the Fifth Schedule: Scheduled Areas are areas the President may by order declare.

Q3. The maximum strength of a Tribes Advisory Council is: medium

✅ Answer: (b) 20

💡 Explanation: Paragraph 4(1) of the Fifth Schedule: not more than twenty members, about three-fourths of them ST members of the Assembly.

Q4. A District Council under the Sixth Schedule (other than Bodoland) can have at most: medium

✅ Answer: (b) 30 members, 4 nominated

💡 Explanation: Paragraph 2(1): not more than thirty members, of whom not more than four are nominated by the Governor. Bodoland's council may have up to 46.

Q5. A law of Parliament amending the Fifth Schedule: hard

✅ Answer: (c) is not treated as a constitutional amendment under Article 368

💡 Explanation: Paragraph 7(2) of the Fifth Schedule says such a law is not deemed an amendment of the Constitution for Article 368.

Why do the North-Eastern States have a separate Schedule?

The Sixth Schedule gives tribal areas in four States their own elected District Councils with law-making powers over land, forests, customs and similar matters. The Fifth Schedule instead works through the State government and the Governor, advised by a Tribes Advisory Council.

Does the Panchayati Raj system apply in Scheduled Areas?

Not automatically. Article 243M(1) says Part IX does not apply to Fifth Schedule Scheduled Areas or Sixth Schedule tribal areas. Article 243M(4)(b) lets Parliament extend it by law with modifications.
