12 Schedules of the Indian Constitution: What Each One Contains

First to Twelfth Schedule • Linked Articles • 9th, 10th, 11th and 12th added later • 22 languages • Union, State and Concurrent Lists

UPSC SSC State PSC 📅 Last updated: 2026-10-31

The Schedules hold the detail that would clutter the Articles: lists of States, salaries, oath wordings, subject lists, languages. Exams test them in a narrow way. You need to know what each Schedule contains and which ones were added later by amendment. The table below covers both.

The original Constitution had eight Schedules. Four were added later, and their amendment numbers are a favourite question:

  • Ninth Schedule: First Amendment Act, 1951. It shielded land reform laws listed in it from challenge on the ground that they violated Fundamental Rights (Article 31B).
  • Tenth Schedule: 52nd Amendment Act, 1985. The anti-defection law for members of Parliament and State Legislatures.
  • Eleventh Schedule: 73rd Amendment Act, 1992. Subjects that may be devolved to Panchayats.
  • Twelfth Schedule: 74th Amendment Act, 1992. Subjects that may be devolved to Municipalities.

The 73rd and 74th Amendments are covered in the Panchayati Raj and Municipalities notes.

The Eighth Schedule lists 22 languages: Assamese, Bengali, Bodo, Dogri, Gujarati, Hindi, Kannada, Kashmiri, Konkani, Maithili, Malayalam, Manipuri, Marathi, Nepali, Odia, Punjabi, Sanskrit, Santhali, Sindhi, Tamil, Telugu and Urdu.

Fourteen were in the original list. The additions came in three steps:

AmendmentLanguages added
21st Amendment, 1967Sindhi
71st Amendment, 1992Konkani, Manipuri, Nepali
92nd Amendment, 2003Bodo, Dogri, Maithili, Santhali

The 96th Amendment Act, 2011 did not add a language. It changed the spelling "Oriya" to "Odia".

The Seventh Schedule divides law-making subjects between the Union and the States. Under Article 246, Parliament alone makes laws on the Union List, States alone on the State List, and both on the Concurrent List. Subjects in none of the lists belong to Parliament (Article 248). How this division shapes Centre-State relations is covered in federal and unitary features.

One way to hold the order: Territory, Emoluments, Oaths, Rajya Sabha seats, Scheduled Areas, Tribal areas (North-East), Lists, Languages, then the four later additions: Land laws, Defection, Panchayats, Municipalities. Make your own sentence from the initials if it helps; what matters is linking each number to its content.

  • ✘ "The Ninth Schedule is about languages." Languages are the Eighth.
  • ✘ "The Sixth Schedule covers all tribal areas." It covers only Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura and Mizoram; others are under the Fifth.

Q1. Which Schedule of the Constitution deals with anti-defection? easy

✅ Answer: (c) Tenth

💡 Explanation: The Tenth Schedule, added by the 52nd Amendment Act, 1985, sets out disqualification on ground of defection.

Q2. The allocation of seats in the Rajya Sabha is given in the: easy

✅ Answer: (c) Fourth Schedule

💡 Explanation: The Fourth Schedule allocates seats in the Council of States (Rajya Sabha) to the States and Union territories.

Q3. Which amendment added the Ninth Schedule? medium

✅ Answer: (a) First Amendment, 1951

💡 Explanation: The Ninth Schedule, linked to Article 31B, was added by the Constitution (First Amendment) Act, 1951.

Q4. The Sixth Schedule applies to tribal areas in which group of States? medium

✅ Answer: (a) Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura, Mizoram

💡 Explanation: The Sixth Schedule covers tribal areas in Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura and Mizoram. Scheduled Areas in other States fall under the Fifth Schedule.

Q5. Which pair of languages was added to the Eighth Schedule by the 92nd Amendment Act, 2003? hard

✅ Answer: (b) Bodo and Santhali

💡 Explanation: The 92nd Amendment added Bodo, Dogri, Maithili and Santhali. Konkani, Manipuri and Nepali came in 1992, and Sindhi in 1967.

How many Schedules did the original Constitution have?

Eight. The Ninth was added in 1951, the Tenth in 1985, and the Eleventh and Twelfth in 1992, bringing the total to twelve.

What is the difference between the Fifth and Sixth Schedules?

The Fifth Schedule covers the administration of Scheduled Areas and Scheduled Tribes in States generally. The Sixth Schedule gives tribal areas in Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura and Mizoram a separate system of autonomous district councils.
