Indian Judiciary MCQs: 25 Questions on the Supreme Court and High Courts With Article-Wise Answers

Articles 124 qualifications and removal • 129 to 131 • 136, 137, 139A • 141, 143, 145(3) • 215, 217, 220 • 226 to 229 • 231

UPSC SSC State PSC 📅 Last updated: 2026-11-06

These 25 original questions cover the Supreme Court (Articles 124 to 147) and the High Courts (Articles 214 to 231). Each answer cites the Article and clause, so you can check it against the text. Attempt all of them before opening the answers.

Appointment of judges is tested here only where the Constitution's text is settled. The printed text of Articles 124(2), 127 and 217(1) still refers to the National Judicial Appointments Commission, which the Supreme Court struck down in 2015 according to the edition's own footnote. Those clauses are therefore left out of this set.

Removal of a Supreme Court judge under Article 124(4) needs an address by each House, supported by a majority of its total membership and two-thirds of members present and voting, on the ground of proved misbehaviour or incapacity. Article 217(1)(b) applies the same procedure to High Court judges.

  • Original, appellate, advisory and writ jurisdiction: Supreme Court jurisdiction.
  • Articles 226, 227 and 228: High Court jurisdiction.
  • The five writs: writs under Articles 32 and 226.
  • Cases and appointments: judiciary and landmark cases.

Most mistakes in this topic come from mixing up the two courts' numbers: 65 and 62 for retirement, five and ten years for qualification, and the different rules on practising after retirement. Learn the table above first.

Q1. The retirement age of a Supreme Court Judge is: easy

✅ Answer: (a) 65

💡 Explanation: Article 124(2): a Supreme Court Judge holds office until the age of sixty-five years.

Q2. Which of these does NOT qualify a person to be a Supreme Court Judge? medium

✅ Answer: (b) Ten years as a district judge

💡 Explanation: Article 124(3) lists the first three. Ten years in judicial office is a High Court qualification under 217(2).

Q3. A Supreme Court Judge may be removed on the ground of: easy

✅ Answer: (c) Proved misbehaviour or incapacity

💡 Explanation: Article 124(4) allows removal only on the ground of proved misbehaviour or incapacity.

Q4. The address for removing a Supreme Court Judge must be supported in each House by: medium

✅ Answer: (d) A majority of total membership and two-thirds of those present and voting

💡 Explanation: Article 124(4) requires both conditions in each House in the same session.

Q5. After retirement, a Supreme Court Judge: medium

✅ Answer: (a) Shall not plead or act in any court or before any authority in India

💡 Explanation: Article 124(7) bars practice in any court or before any authority in India.

Q6. The Supreme Court is a court of record under: easy

✅ Answer: (b) Article 129

💡 Explanation: Article 129. Article 215 makes High Courts courts of record.

Q7. Where does the Supreme Court sit? medium

✅ Answer: (c) Delhi or other places appointed by the Chief Justice with the President's approval

💡 Explanation: Article 130 lets the Chief Justice, with the President's approval, appoint other places of sitting.

Q8. Disputes between the Government of India and a State fall under the Supreme Court's: easy

✅ Answer: (d) Exclusive original jurisdiction

💡 Explanation: Article 131 gives exclusive original jurisdiction to the Supreme Court.

Q9. Special leave to appeal from any court or tribunal is granted under: easy

✅ Answer: (a) Article 136

💡 Explanation: Article 136 lets the Supreme Court grant special leave to appeal from any judgment of any court or tribunal, except military tribunals.

Q10. The Supreme Court's power to review its own judgments is in: medium

✅ Answer: (b) Article 137

💡 Explanation: Article 137, subject to law and rules under Article 145.

Q11. The Supreme Court may withdraw cases to itself from High Courts on an application by: hard

✅ Answer: (c) The Attorney-General of India or a party

💡 Explanation: Article 139A(1): where the same substantial questions are pending in it and High Courts, on its own motion or on an application by the Attorney-General of India or a party.

Q12. Under Article 141, the law declared by the Supreme Court is binding on: easy

✅ Answer: (d) All courts within the territory of India

💡 Explanation: Article 141: binding on all courts within the territory of India.

Q13. Advisory jurisdiction of the Supreme Court is in: easy

✅ Answer: (a) Article 143

💡 Explanation: Article 143: the President may refer a question of law or fact of public importance for its opinion.

Q14. The minimum number of Judges to decide a substantial question of constitutional interpretation is: medium

✅ Answer: (b) Five

💡 Explanation: Article 145(3): five, also for hearing an Article 143 reference.

Q15. The retirement age of a High Court Judge is: easy

✅ Answer: (c) 62

💡 Explanation: Article 217(1): a High Court Judge holds office until the age of sixty-two years.

Q16. Which qualifies a citizen to be a High Court Judge? medium

✅ Answer: (d) Ten years in judicial office in India

💡 Explanation: Article 217(2)(a): ten years in judicial office, or (b) ten years as a High Court advocate. 'Distinguished jurist' applies only to the Supreme Court.

Q17. A retired permanent High Court Judge may plead in: medium

✅ Answer: (a) Only the Supreme Court and other High Courts

💡 Explanation: Article 220 bars the High Court where they served and lower courts.

Q18. High Courts are courts of record with power to punish for contempt of themselves under: easy

✅ Answer: (b) Article 215

💡 Explanation: Article 215 makes every High Court a court of record with contempt powers.

Q19. High Courts can issue writs 'for any other purpose' under: easy

✅ Answer: (c) Article 226

💡 Explanation: Article 226(1) covers Part III rights and any other purpose; Article 32 is limited to Part III.

Q20. An application to vacate an ex parte interim order under Article 226 must be decided within: hard

✅ Answer: (d) Two weeks

💡 Explanation: Article 226(3): two weeks, or the interim order stands vacated.

Q21. High Courts have NO superintendence over: medium

✅ Answer: (a) Armed Forces courts and tribunals

💡 Explanation: Article 227(4) excludes courts and tribunals under laws relating to the Armed Forces.

Q22. If a lower court case involves a substantial constitutional question, under Article 228 the High Court: medium

✅ Answer: (b) Shall withdraw the case

💡 Explanation: Article 228: it shall withdraw the case and either decide it or decide the question and return it.

Q23. Officers and servants of a High Court are appointed by: medium

✅ Answer: (c) The Chief Justice of the High Court or someone directed by them

💡 Explanation: Article 229(1). The Governor may require PSC consultation for certain posts.

Q24. A common High Court for two or more States is established by: easy

✅ Answer: (d) Parliament by law

💡 Explanation: Article 231(1) lets Parliament by law establish a common High Court for two or more States.

Q25. A High Court Judge is removed: medium

✅ Answer: (a) In the same manner as a Supreme Court Judge under Article 124(4)

💡 Explanation: Proviso (b) to Article 217(1) applies the Article 124(4) procedure.

What is the difference between the retirement ages of Supreme Court and High Court judges?

Supreme Court Judges retire at sixty-five under Article 124(2), while High Court Judges retire at sixty-two under Article 217(1).

Why are appointment questions left out?

The printed text of Articles 124(2), 127 and 217(1) still refers to the National Judicial Appointments Commission, which the edition's own footnote says was struck down in 2015.
