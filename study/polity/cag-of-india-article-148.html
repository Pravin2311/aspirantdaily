Comptroller and Auditor-General of India: Articles 148 to 151, Appointment, Term, Removal, Duties and Audit Reports

Article 148 clause by clause • 1971 Act: six years or 65 • Articles 149-151 • Article 279 certificate • PAC context • Safeguards compared • Exam traps

UPSC SSC State PSC Banking 📅 Last updated: 2026-11-07

The Comptroller and Auditor-General of India (CAG) is the constitutional auditor of public money. Chapter V of Part V of the Constitution (Articles 148 to 151) sets up the office, and the CAG's (Duties, Powers and Conditions of Service) Act, 1971 fills in the details.

The CAG audits the accounts of:

  • the Union;
  • the States;
  • other authorities and bodies that Parliament places within the CAG's audit by law.

The audit reports go to Parliament and the State Legislatures.

The CAG's own salary, allowances and pension are also listed in Article 112(3)(e) as expenditure charged on the Consolidated Fund. Charged expenditure is not voted on by Parliament, which shields the office from budget pressure.

The term and resignation rules come from section 4 of the 1971 Act. India Code was unavailable for a text check, so treat them as the standard exam-guide statement; the salary rule is recorded in the Constitution's own footnote.

  • Term: six years from the date of taking office. If the CAG turns sixty-five before then, the term ends on that birthday.
  • Resignation: the CAG may resign at any time by writing to the President.
  • Salary: a footnote to the Second Schedule records that section 3 of the 1971 Act fixes the salary equal to that of a Supreme Court Judge.

The original Second Schedule figure was four thousand rupees a month.

  • Article 149, duties and powers: the CAG audits the accounts of the Union, the States and any other authority or body, as Parliament provides by law. Until such a law was made, the CAG had the powers of the pre-1950 Auditor-General of India. That law is the 1971 Act.
  • Article 150, form of accounts: the President prescribes the form in which Union and State accounts are kept, on the advice of the CAG. The 44th Amendment changed the wording from "after consultation with" to "on the advice of".
  • Article 151, reports:
    • reports on Union accounts go to the President, who has them laid before each House of Parliament;
    • reports on a State's accounts go to the Governor, who has them laid before the State Legislature.

Each safeguard in Article 148 closes a different route of pressure on the auditor:

  1. Removal: because the CAG can be removed only like a Supreme Court Judge, the government of the day cannot dismiss an auditor whose reports embarrass it.
  2. Pay and conditions: they cannot be cut after appointment, so Parliament cannot punish the CAG through pay.
  3. No later posts: the bar on any further office under the Union or a State removes the temptation to please the government in the hope of a post-retirement job.
  4. Budget: the office's expenses are charged on the Consolidated Fund, so its budget does not depend on a vote.

The Constitution gives the CAG one more protection, shared with Supreme Court and High Court Judges, the Chief Election Commissioner and Public Service Commission members. Article 312A lets Parliament vary the service conditions of officers originally appointed by the Secretary of State. Its proviso says that where such an officer holds or has held one of these offices, including CAG, that power cannot be used to vary his conditions to his disadvantage after appointment.

Exam-guide context, not quoted from the sources cited here:

  • CAG reports on the Union are examined by Parliament's Public Accounts Committee (PAC). The PAC is traditionally chaired by a member of the Opposition, and the CAG is often described as its "friend, philosopher and guide".
  • The CAG conducts three broad kinds of audit: compliance (legality), financial (accounts) and performance (economy, efficiency and effectiveness).
  • The CAG does not audit the secret service expenditure of some agencies in detail, and has no role in releasing money from the Consolidated Fund.

The last point is why "Comptroller" is sometimes called a misnomer in India.

  • The CAG is appointed by the President but removed only like a Supreme Court Judge, never at the President's pleasure.
  • The term is six years or age 65, whichever is earlier. That comes from the 1971 Act, not the Constitution.
  • Union audit reports go to the President (Art. 151(1)), not directly to the Speaker.
  • Article 150 now reads "on the advice of" the CAG, a change made by the 44th Amendment.
  • The CAG certifies net proceeds of taxes under Article 279, and that certificate is final.

Q1. The CAG of India is removed from office: easy

✅ Answer: (a) In the same manner as a Supreme Court Judge

💡 Explanation: Article 148(1) says the CAG shall only be removed in like manner and on the like grounds as a Judge of the Supreme Court.

Q2. Reports of the CAG on Union accounts are submitted to: easy

✅ Answer: (b) The President

💡 Explanation: Article 151(1) says Union audit reports go to the President, who has them laid before each House of Parliament.

Q3. What is the CAG's term under the 1971 Act? easy

✅ Answer: (c) Six years or till age 65, whichever is earlier

💡 Explanation: Section 4 of the 1971 Act, as given in exam guides, fixes six years, ending earlier at age sixty-five; see the page note.

Q4. Under Article 279, whose certificate of the 'net proceeds' of a tax is final? medium

✅ Answer: (d) The CAG's

💡 Explanation: Article 279(1) says net proceeds are ascertained and certified by the CAG, whose certificate shall be final.

Q5. After leaving office, the CAG is: medium

✅ Answer: (a) Not eligible for any further office under the Union or a State

💡 Explanation: Article 148(4) bars the CAG from further office under the Government of India or any State after ceasing to hold office.

Q6. The form of Union and State accounts is prescribed by the President: medium

✅ Answer: (b) On the advice of the CAG

💡 Explanation: Article 150, as amended by the 44th Amendment, says the President prescribes the form of accounts on the CAG's advice.

Q7. The CAG's oath of office is set out in which Schedule? medium

✅ Answer: (c) Third Schedule

💡 Explanation: Article 148(2) refers to the Third Schedule, whose oath form is shared with Supreme Court Judges.

Q8. The administrative expenses of the CAG's office are: hard

✅ Answer: (d) Charged on the Consolidated Fund of India

💡 Explanation: Article 148(6) charges the office's administrative expenses, including salaries and pensions, on the Consolidated Fund of India.

Who appoints the CAG and for how long?

The President appoints the CAG by warrant. Under the 1971 Act the CAG serves six years, or until turning sixty-five if that comes earlier.

Where do CAG reports go?

Reports on Union accounts go to the President, who has them laid before Parliament; reports on a State's accounts go to the Governor, for the State Legislature.
