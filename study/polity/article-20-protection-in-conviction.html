Article 20: Protection Against Ex Post Facto Laws, Double Jeopardy and Self-Incrimination

Article 20(1) ex post facto • 20(2) double jeopardy • 20(3) self-incrimination • Article 359 and the 44th Amendment

UPSC State PSC 📅 Last updated: 2026-10-31

Article 20 is headed "Protection in respect of conviction for offences". It gives three separate protections to anyone accused or convicted of a crime:

ClauseProtectionCommon name
20(1)No conviction except under a law in force when the act was done; no penalty greater than the one that law allowedProtection against ex post facto laws
20(2)No one prosecuted and punished for the same offence more than onceProtection against double jeopardy
20(3)No accused compelled to be a witness against himselfProtection against self-incrimination

Every clause begins "No person". Article 20 is therefore available to citizens and non-citizens alike, unlike Article 19, which is for citizens only.

An ex post facto law makes something a crime after it was done, or increases the punishment after the act. Article 20(1) blocks both:

  • A person can be convicted only "for violation of a law in force at the time of the commission of the Act charged as an offence". If the act was legal when done, a later law cannot make it punishable.
  • The penalty cannot be "greater than that which might have been inflicted under the law in force at the time of the commission of the offence". A later, harsher sentence cannot be applied backwards.

Read the words closely. The clause speaks of being convicted and of a penalty for an offence. So it is understood to cover criminal law only. Parliament can still pass retrospective civil or tax laws; Article 20(1) does not deal with them. And the clause protects against conviction and a heavier penalty, not against a change in the procedure of trial.

Worked case. Riya publishes a post in January. In June, a new law makes such posts an offence. She cannot be prosecuted for the January post under the June law, because no law made it an offence when she published it.

Article 20(2) says "No person shall be prosecuted and punished for the same offence more than once." Three words carry the meaning:

  • Prosecuted and punished: both are needed. If the first trial ended in acquittal or was not completed, the clause as worded speaks only of a person who was both prosecuted and punished.
  • Same offence: the second case must be for the same offence, not merely arising from the same facts. One act can amount to two different offences under two different laws.
  • Prosecution: the earlier proceeding must be a prosecution before a court for an offence. A departmental inquiry against an employee is not a prosecution, so standard answers treat it as outside the clause.

Worked case. Arjun is convicted of theft and serves his sentence. The police cannot charge him again with the same theft. But if the same incident also involved assault, a separate offence, a separate prosecution for assault is not barred by Article 20(2).

Article 20(3) reads: "No person accused of any offence shall be compelled to be a witness against himself." Break it into its parts:

  • Accused of any offence: the protection belongs to a person accused, not to every witness in every proceeding.
  • Compelled: it bars compulsion. A statement given voluntarily is not protected by this clause.
  • Witness against himself: the protection is against being made to give evidence that incriminates oneself.

Mains answers often link clause (3) to fair-trial guarantees in Articles 21 and 22. Article 22(2), for example, requires an arrested person to be produced before the nearest magistrate within twenty-four hours, excluding journey time.

Article 20 is about the trial and punishment of offences, Article 21 is the general guarantee of life and liberty, and Article 22 is about arrest and detention. Questions that ask "which Article" usually turn on this division.

During an Emergency, Article 359(1) lets the President suspend the right to move any court for the enforcement of Part III rights named in the order. Since the Constitution (Forty-fourth Amendment) Act, 1978, with effect from 20 June 1979, the text reads "the rights conferred by Part III (except articles 20 and 21)".

So Article 20, together with Article 21, stays enforceable even during a National Emergency under Article 352. The protections against retrospective punishment, double punishment and forced self-incrimination cannot be switched off.

  • ✘ "Article 20 is only for citizens." Each clause says "No person".
  • ✘ "Article 20(1) bars all retrospective laws." It covers conviction and penalty for offences.
  • ✘ "Articles 19 and 21 cannot be suspended." The non-suspendable pair under Article 359 is 20 and 21.

For the remedies when a right is violated, see the five writs under Articles 32 and 226.

Q1. Article 20(2) of the Constitution of India deals with: easy

✅ Answer: (b) double jeopardy

💡 Explanation: Article 20(2): no person shall be prosecuted and punished for the same offence more than once. Clause (1) is ex post facto law and clause (3) self-incrimination.

Q2. Which Fundamental Rights cannot be suspended during a National Emergency? easy

✅ Answer: (c) Articles 20 and 21

💡 Explanation: Article 359(1), as amended by the 44th Amendment, excludes Articles 20 and 21 from any suspension order.

Q3. A law passed in 2026 raises the maximum sentence for an offence from 3 years to 7 years. A person who committed the offence in 2025 is convicted in 2027. The maximum sentence is: medium

✅ Answer: (b) 3 years

💡 Explanation: Article 20(1) forbids a penalty greater than the one that 'might have been inflicted under the law in force at the time of the commission of the offence', so 3 years.

Q4. Article 20(3) protects: medium

✅ Answer: (b) a person accused of an offence from being compelled to be a witness against himself

💡 Explanation: The text is limited: 'No person accused of any offence shall be compelled to be a witness against himself.'

Q5. Article 20 is available to: easy

✅ Answer: (b) any person

💡 Explanation: Each clause begins 'No person', so foreigners and companies charged with offences are also protected.

Does Article 20(1) stop all retrospective laws?

No. Its words cover only conviction for an offence and the penalty for it. Retrospective tax or civil laws are outside Article 20(1); they are tested against other provisions.

Is a departmental inquiry after a criminal trial double jeopardy?

Article 20(2) speaks of being 'prosecuted and punished for the same offence'. A departmental inquiry is not a prosecution for an offence, so the clause is generally not attracted; exams treat this as the standard answer.
