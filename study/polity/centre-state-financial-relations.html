Centre-State Financial Relations: Who Levies, Collects and Keeps Each Tax, Grants and Borrowing (Articles 264 to 293)

Article 265 • 266 and 267 Funds • 268, 269, 269A, 270, 271 distribution • 275 vs 282 grants • 276 profession tax • 285 and 289 immunity • 292 and 293 borrowing

UPSC SSC Banking State PSC 📅 Last updated: 2026-11-06

Part XII, Chapter I ("Finance") sets out how money moves between the Union and the States. The Seventh Schedule decides who may levy which tax. Articles 264 to 293 then decide who collects it, who keeps it, how it is shared, which grants flow from the Centre and who may borrow. This page follows that order.

  • No tax without law (265). "No tax shall be levied or collected except by authority of law." The executive cannot impose a tax by order.
  • Consolidated Funds and public accounts (266). All revenues of the Government of India, its loans raised by treasury bills, loans or ways and means advances, and loan repayments form the Consolidated Fund of India. Each State has its own Consolidated Fund. All other public money goes to the public account. Under 266(3), nothing can be appropriated from a Consolidated Fund except in accordance with law.
  • Contingency Funds (267). Parliament may by law set up a Contingency Fund of India "in the nature of an imprest", at the disposal of the President, for unforeseen expenditure pending Parliament's authorisation. Each State Legislature may set up a Contingency Fund of the State at the Governor's disposal.

Articles 268 to 271 sort Union-levied taxes into groups according to who collects them and who keeps the proceeds. This is the most tested part of the topic.

How the 270 share is fixed. The percentage of net proceeds that goes to the States, and its distribution among them, is "prescribed" by the President by order after considering the recommendations of the Finance Commission (270(3)). The Commission itself is covered on Finance Commission (Article 280).

Surcharges and cesses. Article 270(1) leaves out surcharges under Article 271 and cesses levied for specific purposes. Their proceeds are not part of the pool shared with the States. Under Article 271, surcharge proceeds "form part of the Consolidated Fund of India" in full.

Net proceeds (279). "Net proceeds" means proceeds reduced by the cost of collection. They are certified by the Comptroller and Auditor-General, "whose certificate shall be final".

Article 268A, on service tax, was inserted by the 88th Amendment but never notified, and was omitted by the 101st Amendment (2016). GST itself is covered on GST Council (Article 279A).

  • Statutory grants (275(1)). Parliament may by law charge sums on the Consolidated Fund of India each year as grants-in-aid to States it decides need assistance, and different sums may be fixed for different States. The first proviso requires grants for schemes, approved by the Government of India, to promote the welfare of Scheduled Tribes or raise the administration of Scheduled Areas.
  • Discretionary grants (282). The Union or a State may make grants "for any public purpose", even if the purpose is outside its legislative competence.
  • Jute grants (273). Grants to Assam, Bihar, Odisha and West Bengal in lieu of a share of jute export duty. They ran only while the duty continued, or for ten years from the commencement of the Constitution, whichever was earlier.

The distinction exam questions test: Article 275 grants are made by law and charged on the Consolidated Fund; Article 282 grants are open-ended and need no link to legislative competence.

  • President's recommendation (274). No Bill or amendment that imposes or varies a tax in which States are interested, or affects the principles of distribution, or imposes a Union surcharge, can be introduced in Parliament without the President's recommendation.
  • Profession tax (276). A State or local body may tax professions, trades, callings and employments even though it relates to income. The total per person cannot exceed two thousand five hundred rupees a year. This does not limit Parliament's income-tax power.
  • Old taxes saved (277). Taxes lawfully levied by a State or local body before the Constitution may continue, even if they are now in the Union List, until Parliament provides otherwise.

Under 293(2), the Government of India may lend to States or guarantee their loans, and the sums are charged on the Consolidated Fund of India. Consent under 293(3) may be given with conditions (293(4)). The consent rule is one of the ways the Union can influence a State's borrowing in normal times.

The emergency override is Article 360, under which the Union can direct States on financial matters; see Financial Emergency. The wider picture is on federalism and Centre-State relations and the Seventh Schedule.

  • 265: no tax without law. 266: Consolidated Funds and public accounts. 267: Contingency Funds (imprest).
  • 268: Union levies, States collect and keep (stamp duties).
  • 269: Union levies and collects, assigned to States (inter-State sale and consignment).
  • 269A: inter-State GST, apportioned on GST Council recommendation.
  • 270: shared pool; States' share prescribed on Finance Commission advice.
  • 271: surcharges wholly for the Union, not on GST.
  • 275 vs 282: statutory grants vs grants for any public purpose.
  • 276: profession tax cap of Rs 2,500 a year.
  • 293(3): States need Union consent to borrow while Union loans are outstanding.

Q1. Stamp duties mentioned in the Union List are levied by the Union but collected and kept by the States under: medium

✅ Answer: (a) Article 268

💡 Explanation: Article 268: Union levies; States collect, and proceeds do not enter the Consolidated Fund of India.

Q2. The whole proceeds of which levy go only to the Union? easy

✅ Answer: (b) Surcharge under Article 271

💡 Explanation: Article 271 surcharge proceeds form part of the Consolidated Fund of India in full. It cannot be levied on GST.

Q3. The maximum profession tax payable by one person per year is: easy

✅ Answer: (c) Rs 2,500

💡 Explanation: Article 276(2) caps it at two thousand five hundred rupees per annum.

Q4. Who certifies the 'net proceeds' of a tax for distribution? medium

✅ Answer: (b) Comptroller and Auditor-General

💡 Explanation: Article 279(1): the CAG certifies net proceeds and the certificate is final.

Q5. A State needs the Union's consent to borrow when: medium

✅ Answer: (b) Any Union loan or Union-guaranteed loan to it is outstanding

💡 Explanation: Article 293(3). States may borrow only within India under 293(1).

What is the difference between Article 275 and Article 282 grants?

Article 275 grants are fixed by Parliament by law for States in need and charged on the Consolidated Fund; Article 282 lets the Union or a State make grants for any public purpose.

Are cesses shared with the States?

Article 270(1) excludes cesses levied for specific purposes and Article 271 surcharges from the pool of taxes distributed between the Union and the States.
