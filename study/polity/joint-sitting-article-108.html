Joint Sitting of Parliament Under Article 108: When, Who Presides and What It Cannot Do

Article 108 • Article 118(3)-(4) • Article 107 lapse rules • Money Bills, Article 109 • Constitutional amendments, Article 368(2)

UPSC SSC State PSC 📅 Last updated: 2026-10-31

An ordinary Bill must pass both Houses. Article 107(2) says a Bill is not passed "unless it has been agreed to by both Houses", in the same form. If the Lok Sabha and the Rajya Sabha cannot agree, the Bill would be stuck for ever. Article 108 provides the way out: the President can summon both Houses to a joint sitting, where members of both vote together.

The idea is simple; the conditions are precise, and exams test the precise words.

After a Bill has been passed by one House and sent to the other, a joint sitting can be called if:

  • the other House rejects the Bill;
  • the Houses have finally disagreed on the amendments to be made in the Bill; or
  • more than six months pass from the date the other House received the Bill without it being passed.

Two refinements apply. Under Article 108(2), periods when the House is prorogued or adjourned for more than four consecutive days do not count towards the six months. And the President cannot call a joint sitting if the Bill has already lapsed because the Lok Sabha was dissolved.

StepWhat happensProvision
1. DeadlockOne of the three situations arises108(1)(a)-(c)
2. NotificationThe President notifies the intention to summon a joint sitting, by message if the Houses are sitting or by public notification if not108(1)
3. FreezeNeither House may proceed further with the Bill108(3)
4. SummonsThe President summons the joint sitting at any time after the notification108(3)
5. PresidingThe Speaker of the Lok Sabha presides, or in his absence the person fixed by the rules118(4)
6. VotePassed by a majority of the total number of members of both Houses present and voting108(4)

The rules of procedure for joint sittings are made by the President after consulting the Chairman of the Rajya Sabha and the Speaker, under Article 118(3).

A joint sitting is not a fresh debate on the whole Bill. The proviso to Article 108(4) limits what can be proposed:

  • If the other House simply did not pass the Bill (rejected it or let six months pass), only amendments made necessary by the delay can be proposed, such as changing a date.
  • If the other House passed the Bill with amendments and returned it, the sitting can also take up amendments relevant to the matters on which the Houses disagreed.

The presiding person decides which amendments are admissible, and that decision is final.

BillWhy no joint sitting
Money BillThe proviso to Article 108(1) excludes it. It cannot be introduced in the Rajya Sabha, which must return it within 14 days with recommendations the Lok Sabha may accept or reject (Article 109), so no deadlock can arise.
Constitutional amendment BillArticle 368(2) requires passage "in each House" by a special majority, so the two Houses cannot be combined.

The Constitution resolves disagreement between the Houses differently for different kinds of Bill. Placing joint sittings in this table answers many comparison questions.

Kind of BillIf the Houses disagreeWho has the last word

The Lok Sabha passes an ordinary Bill on 1 February and sends it to the Rajya Sabha, which receives it the same day. The Rajya Sabha neither passes nor rejects it. Between March and April it is adjourned for a ten-day recess.

  • Six months would ordinarily end on 1 August.
  • The ten-day adjournment is more than four consecutive days, so under Article 108(2) those ten days are not counted. The six months end ten days later, around 11 August.
  • After that, the President may notify the intention to hold a joint sitting. From that moment neither House can proceed with the Bill.
  • If the Lok Sabha is dissolved after the notification, the joint sitting can still be held under Article 108(5).

Article 107 decides what happens to Bills when the Lok Sabha is dissolved. A Bill pending in the Lok Sabha, or passed by it and pending in the Rajya Sabha, lapses, but this is "subject to the provisions of article 108". A Bill pending in the Rajya Sabha that the Lok Sabha has not passed does not lapse.

Article 108(5) gives the joint sitting a protection of its own: once the President has notified the intention to summon one, the joint sitting can go ahead and pass the Bill even if the Lok Sabha is dissolved in the meantime.

  • ✘ "The Vice-President presides over a joint sitting." The Speaker does, under Article 118(4).
  • ✘ "A joint sitting needs a special majority." It needs a majority of members of both Houses present and voting; see types of majority.
  • ✘ "Money Bills can be referred to a joint sitting." The proviso to Article 108(1) rules this out.

Q1. Who presides over a joint sitting of the two Houses of Parliament? easy

✅ Answer: (c) The Speaker of the Lok Sabha

💡 Explanation: Article 118(4) says the Speaker of the House of the People presides, or in his absence the person fixed by the joint-sitting rules.

Q2. A joint sitting under Article 108 is NOT possible for: easy

✅ Answer: (b) a Money Bill

💡 Explanation: The proviso to Article 108(1) says the clause does not apply to a Money Bill. The other three are exactly the three deadlock situations.

Q3. In counting the six months under Article 108(1)(c), which period is left out? medium

✅ Answer: (b) Periods when the House is prorogued or adjourned for more than four consecutive days

💡 Explanation: Article 108(2) excludes periods when the House is prorogued or adjourned for more than four consecutive days.

Q4. A Bill passed at a joint sitting needs: medium

✅ Answer: (c) a majority of the total number of members of both Houses present and voting

💡 Explanation: Article 108(4): a majority of the total number of members of both Houses present and voting. Because the Lok Sabha is larger, it usually carries the vote.

Q5. After the President notifies a joint sitting, the Lok Sabha is dissolved. What happens? hard

✅ Answer: (b) The joint sitting can still be held

💡 Explanation: Article 108(5) allows the joint sitting and passage of the Bill even if the Lok Sabha is dissolved after the notification.

Can a constitutional amendment be passed at a joint sitting?

No. Article 368(2) requires the amendment Bill to be passed in each House by a special majority, so there is no provision for resolving a deadlock by a joint sitting.

Does the President have to call a joint sitting?

No. Article 108(1) says the President 'may' notify the intention to summon one, so the power is discretionary in form; in practice the President acts on the advice of the Council of Ministers.
