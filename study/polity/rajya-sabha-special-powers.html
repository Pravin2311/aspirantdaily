What Only the Council of States Can Do: Articles 249, 312, 67(b) and Its Role in Emergencies

Article 249 • Article 312 • Article 67(b) • Emergency approvals when the Lok Sabha is dissolved • Article 109 Money Bills • Article 75(3)

UPSC SSC State PSC 📅 Last updated: 2026-11-05

The Constitution calls the Rajya Sabha the "Council of States". It represents the States in the Union, and it is a continuing House. Article 83(1) says it "shall not be subject to dissolution". Instead, about one-third of its members retire every second year. Because the Council speaks for the States and is never dissolved, the Constitution gives it a few powers that the Lok Sabha does not have, and it also takes away some powers on money matters.

Exams ask about both sides. This page covers the exclusive powers (Articles 249, 312 and 67(b)), the role the Council plays when the Lok Sabha is dissolved during an emergency, and the limits under Articles 109 and 75(3).

Normally Parliament cannot make laws on matters in the State List of the Seventh Schedule. Article 249 opens one door. If the Council of States "has declared by resolution supported by not less than two-thirds of the members present and voting" that it is necessary or expedient in the national interest, Parliament may make laws on the State List matter named in the resolution. The law may apply to the whole or any part of India.

The rules on time limits are tested often:

  • One year at a time. Under Article 249(2), the resolution stays in force for the period it specifies, up to one year.
  • Renewable without limit. It can be continued for a further year each time a fresh resolution is passed in the same way. The Constitution sets no cap on the number of renewals.
  • Six months' grace. Under Article 249(3), a law that Parliament could make only because of the resolution ceases to have effect, to the extent of that incompetency, six months after the resolution lapses. Things done before then are protected.

Since the 101st Amendment (2016), Article 249(1) also covers the goods and services tax under Article 246A. Only the Rajya Sabha passes this resolution. The Lok Sabha has no equivalent role, which is why Article 249 is called a special power of the Council of States. The Union, State and Concurrent Lists are explained on the Seventh Schedule.

Article 312(1) works the same way. If the Council of States declares by resolution, supported by not less than two-thirds of the members present and voting, that it is necessary or expedient in the national interest, Parliament may by law create one or more all-India services common to the Union and the States, including an all-India judicial service, and regulate recruitment and conditions of service.

  • IAS and IPS: Article 312(2) says the Indian Administrative Service and the Indian Police Service, which existed when the Constitution began, "shall be deemed to be services created by Parliament under this article". So they needed no Rajya Sabha resolution.
  • All-India judicial service: the words were inserted by the 42nd Amendment (1976). Under Article 312(3), such a service cannot include any post below that of a district judge.

Any all-India service beyond these two must therefore begin with a Rajya Sabha resolution of this kind before Parliament can legislate.

The Vice-President is the ex officio Chairman of the Council of States under Article 89(1), and the removal procedure reflects that link. Under Article 67(b), the Vice-President may be removed "by a resolution of the Council of States passed by a majority of all the then members of the Council and agreed to by the House of the People". At least fourteen days' notice must be given before the resolution is moved.

So the removal can only start in the Rajya Sabha. The Lok Sabha only agrees to it, and Article 67(b) sets no special majority for that agreement. The full procedure is on the Vice-President, and the meaning of "majority of all the then members" is explained on types of majority in Parliament.

Every kind of emergency proclamation must be approved by both Houses of Parliament within a time limit. The Lok Sabha may not exist at that moment, because it has been dissolved or is dissolved during the approval period. In that case the Rajya Sabha's approval keeps the proclamation alive. Articles 352(4), 356(3) and 360(2) each say the same thing. If the Council of States has passed a resolution approving the proclamation, it continues until thirty days after the reconstituted Lok Sabha first sits. Unless the new Lok Sabha also approves it within those thirty days, it then ceases to operate.

This is not a power to approve an emergency alone indefinitely. It is a bridge that relies on the Council being a continuing House that is never dissolved. The three emergencies are covered on National Emergency, President's Rule and Financial Emergency.

The special powers are balanced by clear limits.

  1. Money Bills (Article 109). "A Money Bill shall not be introduced in the Council of States." Once the Lok Sabha passes one, the Rajya Sabha must return it with recommendations within fourteen days. The Lok Sabha may accept or reject any of them. If the Bill is not returned in fourteen days, it is deemed passed by both Houses in the form the Lok Sabha passed it.
  2. Council of Ministers (Article 75(3)). The Council of Ministers "shall be collectively responsible to the House of the People". The Rajya Sabha can question and debate, but it cannot pass a no-confidence motion that removes the government.
  3. Joint sittings. Under Article 108, a deadlock on an ordinary Bill is settled at a joint sitting. There the Lok Sabha's larger numbers usually decide the outcome. Money Bills never go to a joint sitting. See joint sitting and Money Bill vs Financial Bill.

In other matters the two Houses stand equal. These include ordinary Bills, constitutional amendments under Article 368, the election and impeachment of the President, and the removal of judges.

Article 80(1) fixes the Council of States at twelve members nominated by the President and not more than two hundred and thirty-eight representatives of the States and Union territories. The nominated members are people with special knowledge or practical experience in literature, science, art and social service. Under Article 80(4), State representatives are elected by the elected members of the State Legislative Assembly, using proportional representation by means of the single transferable vote. Seat allocation is in the Fourth Schedule. More on both Houses is on Parliament of India.

  • Article 249: a Rajya Sabha resolution backed by two-thirds of members present and voting lets Parliament legislate on a State List matter. The resolution lasts up to one year, is renewable, and laws made under it lapse six months after it ends.
  • Article 312: the same majority is needed to create a new all-India service. The IAS and IPS are deemed to have been created under it.
  • Article 67(b): Vice-President removal starts only in the Rajya Sabha, by a majority of all the then members.
  • Emergencies: if the Lok Sabha is dissolved, Rajya Sabha approval keeps a proclamation alive until 30 days after the new Lok Sabha first sits.
  • Limits: no Money Bill can start in the Rajya Sabha, it has 14 days to return one, and the Council of Ministers answers to the Lok Sabha.

Q1. Under Article 249, the Rajya Sabha resolution must be supported by: easy

✅ Answer: (b) Two-thirds of members present and voting

💡 Explanation: Article 249(1) requires not less than two-thirds of the members present and voting. Article 312 uses the same majority.

Q2. A resolution under Article 249 remains in force for a maximum of: easy

✅ Answer: (b) One year, renewable

💡 Explanation: Article 249(2): up to one year, renewable for a further year each time a fresh resolution is passed.

Q3. A resolution to remove the Vice-President can be introduced: medium

✅ Answer: (c) Only in the Rajya Sabha

💡 Explanation: Article 67(b): a Council of States resolution passed by a majority of all the then members, agreed to by the Lok Sabha.

Q4. Which service is deemed to have been created by Parliament under Article 312? medium

✅ Answer: (c) Indian Police Service

💡 Explanation: Article 312(2) deems the IAS and IPS, which existed at the commencement of the Constitution, to be created under the Article.

Q5. Within how many days must the Rajya Sabha return a Money Bill? easy

✅ Answer: (b) 14

💡 Explanation: Article 109(2). If not returned in 14 days it is deemed passed by both Houses in the form the Lok Sabha passed it.

Can the Rajya Sabha be dissolved?

No. Article 83(1) says the Council of States is not subject to dissolution; about one-third of its members retire every second year.

Can the Rajya Sabha remove the government?

No. Under Article 75(3) the Council of Ministers is collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha only.
