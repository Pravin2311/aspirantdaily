How a Bill Becomes Law in India: Types of Bills, the Stages in Parliament, the Other House, Joint Sitting and the President's Assent

Government and private member's bills • Cabinet approval and drafting • Introduction, committee, vote • Other House • Joint sitting, Article 108 • Lapse rules, Article 107 • Money bills, Article 109 • Assent or return, Article 111

UPSC SSC State PSC 📅 Last updated: 2026-11-04

A bill is a draft of a proposed law. NCERT's Class XI Political Science sorts bills in two ways:

BasisTypesMeaning
Who moves itGovernment bill; private member's billA bill proposed by a minister is a government bill; one proposed by a non-minister is a private member's bill
What it deals withOrdinary (non-money) bill; money bill; constitution amendment billEach follows a different procedure. This page traces the ordinary bill

The Constitution sets the framework in Articles 107 to 111. Some steps are written there; NCERT notes that others have grown out of conventions. Following a bill shows that law-making is technical and even tedious.

Debate on a bill often starts long before it reaches Parliament. A political party may press the government to bring a law to keep an election promise, and interest groups, the media and citizens' forums may also lobby for it. NCERT stresses that law-making is a political process as well as a legal one.

The government weighs the resources needed, the support or opposition the bill may draw, and its electoral effect. In a coalition, the bill must also suit the partners. The Cabinet considers all of this. Once it approves the policy, the concerned ministry drafts the bill. NCERT's example: a bill to raise the marriageable age of girls from 18 to 21 would be prepared by the law ministry, possibly with the ministry of women and child welfare.

The ordinary bill procedure in parliament, as NCERT lays it out, has three stages in each House.

  1. Introduction. Under Article 107(1), an ordinary bill may start in either House. Any member may introduce it, though usually the minister in charge of the subject does. Money Bills and certain financial Bills are the exception: a money bill can be introduced only in the Lok Sabha.
  2. Committee stage. The bill may be sent to a committee or discussed in the House itself. A large part of the discussion on bills takes place in committees, whose recommendations go back to the House; that is why NCERT calls them miniature legislatures. The House may or may not accept a committee's report.
  3. Voting. After detailed discussion, the bill is voted on and accepted or rejected.

A non-money bill passed by one House goes to the other, where it goes through exactly the same procedure. Under Article 107(2), a bill is passed only when both Houses agree to it, with no amendments or only amendments both accept.

If the Houses cannot agree, Article 108 allows the President to call a joint sitting when, after one House has passed the bill and sent it on:

  • the other House rejects it;
  • the Houses finally disagree on amendments; or
  • more than six months pass without the other House passing it.

At the joint sitting the bill passes by a majority of the total members of both Houses present and voting. This does not apply to Money Bills. NCERT notes that in the few joint sessions held so far, the decision has always gone in favour of the Lok Sabha, the larger House. For details see joint sitting under Article 108.

Article 107 also settles what happens to pending bills when a session ends or the Lok Sabha is dissolved.

SituationResultClause
The Houses are proroguedNo bill lapsesArt. 107(3)
Bill pending in the Rajya Sabha, not yet passed by the Lok Sabha; Lok Sabha dissolvedDoes not lapseArt. 107(4)
Bill pending in the Lok Sabha; Lok Sabha dissolvedLapsesArt. 107(5)
Bill passed by the Lok Sabha and pending in the Rajya Sabha; Lok Sabha dissolvedLapsesArt. 107(5)

Both lapse rules in Article 107(5) are expressly subject to Article 108: Article 108(1) does not let the President call a joint sitting on a bill that has already lapsed through dissolution.

When both Houses have passed a bill it goes to the President. Under Article 111, the President either assents or withholds assent. For a bill that is not a Money Bill, the President may instead return it with a message asking the Houses to reconsider it or particular provisions. If the Houses pass it again, with or without changes, the President cannot withhold assent. On assent, the bill becomes an Act.

In one line, the full route is: Cabinet approval and drafting by the ministry, introduction in either House, committee scrutiny, a vote, the same three stages in the other House, a joint sitting only if the Houses deadlock, and finally the President's assent, which NCERT says turns the draft into law.

Money bills follow a shorter route: the Rajya Sabha can approve or suggest changes but cannot reject them, and if it does nothing for 14 days the bill is deemed passed. See money bill vs financial bill, the President's veto powers and Parliament of India.

Q1. A bill introduced by a member of Parliament who is not a minister is called: easy

✅ Answer: (b) A private member's bill

💡 Explanation: NCERT: when a non-minister proposes a bill it is a private member's bill; a bill proposed by a minister is a government bill.

Q2. Under Article 108, which of these is NOT a ground for calling a joint sitting? medium

✅ Answer: (d) The bill is a Money Bill rejected by the Rajya Sabha

💡 Explanation: Article 108's proviso excludes Money Bills, and in any case the Rajya Sabha cannot reject a money bill; it has only 14 days to return it.

Q3. A bill passed by the Lok Sabha is pending in the Rajya Sabha when the Lok Sabha is dissolved. Subject to Article 108, the bill: hard

✅ Answer: (a) Lapses

💡 Explanation: Article 107(5): a bill pending in the Lok Sabha, or passed by it and pending in the Rajya Sabha, lapses on dissolution of the Lok Sabha.

Q4. What can the President do when an ordinary bill is first presented for assent? medium

✅ Answer: (c) Assent, withhold assent, or return it once for reconsideration

💡 Explanation: Article 111 lets the President assent or withhold assent, or return a non-money bill for reconsideration; if passed again, assent cannot be withheld.

Q5. Why does NCERT call parliamentary committees 'miniature legislatures'? easy

✅ Answer: (c) A large part of the discussion on bills takes place in them

💡 Explanation: Much of the detailed discussion on bills happens in committees, whose recommendations then go back to the House.

Can an ordinary bill start in the Rajya Sabha?

Yes. Article 107(1) lets a bill originate in either House, except Money Bills and certain financial Bills, which must start in the Lok Sabha.

What happens if the two Houses disagree on an ordinary bill?

The President may call a joint sitting under Article 108. The bill passes if a majority of the members of both Houses present and voting support it. NCERT notes that in the few joint sessions held, the Lok Sabha's view has always prevailed.

Does a bill lapse when Parliament is prorogued?

No. Article 107(3) says prorogation does not make a bill lapse. Dissolution of the Lok Sabha does make bills pending in the Lok Sabha, or passed by it and pending in the Rajya Sabha, lapse, subject to Article 108.
