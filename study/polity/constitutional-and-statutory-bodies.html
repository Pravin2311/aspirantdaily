🏢 Constitutional, Statutory & Other Bodies

Election Commission • UPSC • CAG • Finance Commission • NHRC • CVC • Lokpal • NITI Aayog

High Yield Polity 📅 Last updated: Oct 2026
  • A constitutional body is created by the Constitution itself; a statutory body by an Act of Parliament; a non-statutory (executive) body by a government resolution.
  • The difference matters: constitutional bodies can only be changed or abolished by a constitutional amendment, while statutory bodies can be changed by ordinary law.
  • The most-asked detail for each body is its article or Act, followed by who appoints and who removes its head.

Non-Statutory Bodies

  • NITI Aayog — set up by a Cabinet resolution on January 1, 2015, replacing the Planning Commission (1950). It is neither constitutional nor statutory. The PM is its chairperson; its Governing Council includes all Chief Ministers and Lieutenant Governors.
  • National Development Council (1952) — also set up by executive resolution; it has been largely inactive since NITI Aayog was formed.

  2. Article ↔ body: 148 (CAG), 280 (Finance Commission), 324 (EC), 338/338A/338B (commissions).
  3. Amendment ↔ body: 89th (separate NCST), 101st (GST Council), 102nd (NCBC).

Q1. Which of the following is a constitutional body?

✅ Answer: (c) Finance Commission

💡 Explanation: The Finance Commission is created under Article 280; the others are statutory or executive bodies.

Q2. The Comptroller and Auditor General holds office for:

✅ Answer: (b) 6 years or until 65

💡 Explanation: The CAG serves six years or until age 65, whichever is earlier.

Q3. The National Commission for Backward Classes got constitutional status through the:

✅ Answer: (a) 102nd Amendment

💡 Explanation: The 102nd Amendment (2018) inserted Article 338B.

Q4. Who was the first Lokpal of India?

✅ Answer: (d) Justice Pinaki Chandra Ghose

💡 Explanation: Justice P.C. Ghose was appointed in March 2019.

Q5. Under the 2023 Act, the selection committee for Election Commissioners includes the PM, the Leader of the Opposition in the Lok Sabha and:

✅ Answer: (c) A Union Cabinet Minister nominated by the PM

💡 Explanation: The Chief Election Commissioner and Other Election Commissioners Act, 2023 replaced the CJI with a Union Cabinet Minister.

🔁 Revise in 60 Seconds

  • Constitutional: EC (324), UPSC (315), FC (280), CAG (148), AG (76), NCSC (338), NCST (338A), NCBC (338B), GST Council (279A).
  • Statutory: NHRC 1993, CVC 2003 (origin 1964), CIC 2005, Lokpal 2013, NCW 1990, NGT 2010.
  • Non-statutory: NITI Aayog (Jan 1, 2015), NDC (1952); CBI from a 1963 resolution.
  • CAG and CEC removed like SC judges.

📌 Prelims Traps to Avoid

  • ✘ NITI Aayog is neither constitutional nor statutory.
  • ✘ The CBI is not a statutory body, though it draws powers from the DSPE Act, 1946.
  • ✘ The CVC was set up in 1964 but became statutory only in 2003.

What is the difference between a constitutional and a statutory body?

A constitutional body, such as the Election Commission or CAG, is created directly by the Constitution and can be changed only by a constitutional amendment. A statutory body, such as the NHRC or Lokpal, is created by an Act of Parliament and can be changed by ordinary legislation.

Is NITI Aayog a constitutional body?

No. NITI Aayog was set up by a resolution of the Union Cabinet on January 1, 2015, to replace the Planning Commission. It is neither a constitutional nor a statutory body.
