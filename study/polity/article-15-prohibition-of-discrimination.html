Article 15 Explained: The Five Grounds, Access to Public Places and Special Provisions in Clauses 3 to 6

15(1) five grounds • 15(2) public places • 15(3) women and children • 15(4) 1st Amendment • 15(5) 93rd Amendment • 15(6) EWS 103rd Amendment • vs 16(2)

UPSC SSC State PSC 📅 Last updated: 2026-11-06

Article 15 sits in the Right to Equality group of Part III. Article 14 sets the general rule of equality before law. Article 15 turns that rule against particular grounds of discrimination. It has two halves:

  • Clauses (1) and (2) forbid discrimination against citizens on five named grounds.
  • Clauses (3) to (6) allow the State to make special provisions for groups that need them, without breaching clauses (1) and (2).

Four of the six clauses were added later. Exam questions test which amendment added each clause, so the timeline below is worth memorising.

Article 15(1): "The State shall not discriminate against any citizen on grounds only of religion, race, caste, sex, place of birth or any of them."

Three words decide most exam questions:

  • "citizen": Article 15 protects citizens only. A foreigner cannot claim it, unlike Article 14, which protects "any person".
  • "only": the bar is on discrimination based only on a listed ground. A classification that rests on some other basis, even if it partly overlaps a listed ground, is not automatically barred by this clause.
  • The five grounds: religion, race, caste, sex and place of birth.

A common trap compares Article 15 with Article 16(2), which deals with public employment. Article 16(2) lists seven grounds: religion, race, caste, sex, descent, place of birth and residence. Descent and residence appear in Article 16(2) but not in Article 15(1).

Clause (1) binds "the State". Clause (2) is wider in one respect: it is written from the citizen's side and covers access to places that private persons may run. No citizen, on grounds only of the five listed grounds, shall be subject to any disability, liability, restriction or condition with regard to:

  1. (a) access to shops, public restaurants, hotels and places of public entertainment; or
  2. (b) the use of wells, tanks, bathing ghats, roads and places of public resort maintained wholly or partly out of State funds or dedicated to the use of the general public.

Shops, restaurants and hotels in (a) are often privately owned. That is why Article 15(2) is often described as a Fundamental Right that can be enforced against private persons, alongside Article 17 (untouchability) and Article 23 (forced labour). The full list of rights is on Fundamental Rights, DPSP and Duties.

Clause (3) is original: "Nothing in this article shall prevent the State from making any special provision for women and children." It lets the State treat women and children more favourably without that being discrimination on the ground of sex under clause (1). Separate, later Articles deal with seats for women in legislatures; see women's reservation.

Clause (4) was added by the 1st Amendment (1951). It lets the State make any special provision "for the advancement of any socially and educationally backward classes of citizens or for the Scheduled Castes and the Scheduled Tribes". It overrides both Article 15 and Article 29(2), the right of citizens not to be denied admission to State-maintained or State-aided institutions on grounds only of religion, race, caste or language.

The expression "socially and educationally backward classes" is used here, not just "backward classes". Article 16(4), on public employment, uses "backward class of citizens" instead.

The 93rd Amendment (2005) inserted clause (5), in force from 20 January 2006. It lets the State, by law, make special provision for socially and educationally backward classes, SCs or STs in admissions to educational institutions, "including private educational institutions, whether aided or unaided by the State".

Two limits are written into the clause:

  • It must be made by law, unlike clause (4).
  • It does not apply to minority educational institutions under Article 30(1).

Clause (5) also overrides Article 19(1)(g), the freedom to carry on an occupation, which private institutions could otherwise rely on.

The 103rd Amendment (2019) added clause (6), in force from 14 January 2019, and the Explanation that follows it. It allows special provision for "economically weaker sections of citizens other than the classes mentioned in clauses (4) and (5)". In admissions, including private institutions other than minority institutions, any reservation is:

  • in addition to the existing reservations; and
  • subject to a maximum of ten per cent of the total seats in each category.

The Explanation says economically weaker sections are those notified by the State "on the basis of family income and other indicators of economic disadvantage". The same Explanation applies to Article 16. Groups already covered by clauses (4) and (5), meaning backward classes, SCs and STs, are excluded from the EWS category by the words "other than".

  • 15(1): no discrimination by the State against citizens on grounds only of religion, race, caste, sex or place of birth.
  • 15(2): equal access to shops, restaurants, hotels, entertainment, wells, tanks, ghats, roads and public resorts.
  • 15(3): special provision for women and children (original).
  • 15(4): backward classes, SCs and STs (1st Amendment, 1951).
  • 15(5): admissions, including private unaided institutions but not minority ones (93rd Amendment).
  • 15(6): EWS, up to 10% in admissions, in addition to existing reservations (103rd Amendment).
  • Article 16(2) adds descent and residence to the grounds.

Q1. Which ground is listed in Article 16(2) but NOT in Article 15(1)? medium

✅ Answer: (c) Residence

💡 Explanation: Article 16(2) adds descent and residence. Article 15(1) lists religion, race, caste, sex and place of birth only.

Q2. Clause (4) of Article 15 was added by: easy

✅ Answer: (a) The 1st Amendment

💡 Explanation: The 1st Amendment (1951) added 15(4) for socially and educationally backward classes, SCs and STs.

Q3. Article 15(5) allows reservation in admissions to private unaided institutions, except: medium

✅ Answer: (b) Minority educational institutions under Article 30(1)

💡 Explanation: Clause (5) expressly excludes the minority educational institutions referred to in Article 30(1).

Q4. Under Article 15(6), EWS reservation in admissions is limited to: easy

✅ Answer: (b) 10% of seats in each category

💡 Explanation: Clause (6)(b) caps it at ten per cent of the total seats in each category, in addition to existing reservations.

Q5. Article 15 protects: easy

✅ Answer: (b) Citizens only

💡 Explanation: Article 15(1) and (2) speak of 'any citizen'. Article 14, by contrast, applies to any person.

Can a foreigner claim protection under Article 15?

No. Clauses (1) and (2) protect citizens only. A foreign national can rely on Article 14, which applies to any person within India.

Is Article 15(2) enforceable against private shops and hotels?

Clause (2) is framed as a right of citizens to access shops, restaurants, hotels and entertainment places, many of which are privately run, so it reaches beyond the State.
