Speaker of the Lok Sabha: Election, Powers, Casting Vote and Removal Under Articles 93 to 96

Articles 93-96, 100, 110, 112 and 118 • Tenth Schedule, paragraph 6 • Deputy Speaker

UPSC SSC State PSC 📅 Last updated: 2026-11-01

The Speaker presides over the Lok Sabha, keeps order, and makes several decisions that the Constitution declares final. Article 93 provides for the office; Articles 94 to 96 cover vacancy, resignation and removal; and several other provisions hand the Speaker specific powers. This page takes each one from the text.

Article 93 says the House of the People shall, "as soon as may be", choose two of its members to be Speaker and Deputy Speaker. Whenever either office falls vacant, the House chooses another member.

Three points follow from the text:

  • The Speaker must be a member of the Lok Sabha. There are no other qualifications in the Constitution.
  • The Speaker is chosen by the House, not appointed by the President.
  • The Constitution sets no fixed date, only "as soon as may be".
How the office endsRuleClause
Ceasing to be a memberVacates office automatically94(a)
ResignationIn writing, addressed to the Deputy Speaker94(b)
RemovalBy resolution passed by a majority of all the then members of the House94(c)
NoticeAt least 14 days' notice of the intention to move the resolution94, first proviso

The majority for removal, "a majority of all the then members of the House", is what textbooks call an effective majority: a majority of the House's actual strength, leaving out vacant seats. See types of majority.

Dissolution does not remove the Speaker. Under the second proviso to Article 94, when the Lok Sabha is dissolved, the Speaker "shall not vacate his office until immediately before the first meeting" of the new House. This ensures the office is never empty between two Lok Sabhas.

While a resolution to remove the Speaker is under consideration, Article 96(1) says the Speaker "shall not, though he is present, preside". Someone else presides under the rules in Article 95(2).

The Speaker is not silenced, though. Article 96(2) lets the Speaker speak and take part in the proceedings, and vote in the first instance on the resolution, like any member. What the Speaker loses during these proceedings is the casting vote: he cannot vote "in the case of an equality of votes".

The Money Bill power is significant. Under Article 110(3), "the decision of the Speaker of the House of the People thereon shall be final". Because the Rajya Sabha can only make recommendations on a Money Bill, the Speaker's certificate effectively decides how much say the upper House gets. See Money Bill vs Financial Bill and joint sittings.

Paragraph 6(1) of the Tenth Schedule says a question whether a member has become disqualified for defection "shall be referred for the decision of ... the Speaker" and "his decision shall be final". If the question concerns the Speaker himself, it goes to a member the House elects for the purpose.

This makes the Speaker the first judge in every anti-defection case in the Lok Sabha. How far courts can review those decisions has been settled by Supreme Court rulings; see the anti-defection law page.

  • The Speaker's salary and allowances are charged on the Consolidated Fund of India under Article 112(3)(b), so they are not voted on each year.
  • Removal needs an effective majority and 14 days' notice, not a simple majority.
  • The Speaker does not vote in ordinary divisions, staying out of the contest except to break a tie.

The Deputy Speaker is chosen the same way under Article 93. Under Article 95(1), while the Speaker's office is vacant, the Deputy Speaker performs its duties; if both offices are vacant, a member appointed by the President does. When the Speaker is merely absent from a sitting, the Deputy Speaker acts as Speaker under Article 95(2). The Deputy Speaker resigns to the Speaker, and is removed in the same way as the Speaker.

  • A vote on an ordinary motion ends 250-250. The Speaker, who did not vote in the first instance, now uses the casting vote under Article 100(1) to decide it.
  • The Lok Sabha is dissolved in March and the new House first meets in June. The old Speaker continues in office until immediately before that June meeting, then the new House chooses a Speaker under Article 93.
  • A notice to remove the Speaker is given on 1 August. The resolution cannot be moved before 15 August, because at least fourteen days' notice is needed. While it is debated, the Speaker does not preside but may speak and vote once.
  • The Rajya Sabha says a Bill is not really a Money Bill. Under Article 110(3), the Speaker's decision on that question is final.
  • ✘ "The Speaker resigns to the President." He resigns to the Deputy Speaker.
  • ✘ "The Speaker vacates office when the Lok Sabha is dissolved." He continues until just before the new House first meets.
  • ✘ "The Speaker votes on every division." Only a casting vote in a tie.
  • ✘ "The Speaker is removed by a two-thirds majority." By a majority of all the then members.

Q1. The Speaker of the Lok Sabha addresses his resignation to: easy

✅ Answer: (c) the Deputy Speaker

💡 Explanation: Article 94(b): the Speaker resigns by writing addressed to the Deputy Speaker, and the Deputy Speaker to the Speaker.

Q2. A resolution to remove the Speaker needs: medium

✅ Answer: (b) a majority of all the then members of the House

💡 Explanation: Article 94(c): a majority of all the then members of the House, often called an effective majority, after 14 days' notice.

Q3. The Speaker's casting vote is used: easy

✅ Answer: (c) only in case of an equality of votes

💡 Explanation: Article 100(1): the Speaker does not vote in the first instance but has a casting vote in case of a tie.

Q4. When a resolution to remove the Speaker is under consideration, the Speaker: hard

✅ Answer: (c) cannot preside but may speak and vote in the first instance

💡 Explanation: Article 96: he shall not preside, but may speak and vote in the first instance, not in case of a tie.

Q5. When the Lok Sabha is dissolved, the Speaker: medium

✅ Answer: (b) continues until just before the first meeting of the new House

💡 Explanation: The second proviso to Article 94 says the Speaker shall not vacate office until immediately before the first meeting of the new House.

Can a court review the Speaker's decision on defection?

Paragraph 6 of the Tenth Schedule says the Speaker's decision 'shall be final'. The extent of judicial review over it has been settled by Supreme Court rulings, which this page does not cover; see the anti-defection page.

Who decides if a Bill is a Money Bill?

The Speaker. Article 110(3) makes the Speaker's decision final, and Article 110(4) requires the Speaker's certificate on every Money Bill sent to the Rajya Sabha and to the President.
