Reservation of Seats in the Lok Sabha and State Assemblies: Articles 330 to 334, the 104th Amendment and Anglo-Indian Nomination

Article 330 Lok Sabha • 332 Assemblies • 331 and 333 Anglo-Indians • 334 time limit • 104th Amendment • 2001 census • 330A, 332A, 334A women

UPSC SSC State PSC 📅 Last updated: 2026-11-06

Part XVI provides for reserved seats in the Lok Sabha and the State Legislative Assemblies, and for nominated Anglo-Indian members. Article 334 then puts a time limit on both. The time limit has been extended many times, and the 104th Amendment (2019) treated the two differently. That difference is a frequent exam question.

Article 330(1) reserves seats in the House of the People for:

  1. (a) the Scheduled Castes;
  2. (b) the Scheduled Tribes, except those in the autonomous districts of Assam; and
  3. (c) the Scheduled Tribes in the autonomous districts of Assam.

How many seats. Under 330(2), the number of seats reserved in a State or Union territory bears, as nearly as may be, the same proportion to the State's total Lok Sabha seats as the SC or ST population bears to the State's total population. Reservation is therefore population-based and State-wise, not a single national percentage. Clause (3) gives the autonomous districts of Assam a share not less than their ST population share.

Which census. The Explanation says "population" means the last census whose figures are published, but until figures for the first census after 2026 are published, it means the 2001 census. The year 2026 came from the 84th Amendment and the 2001 reference from the 87th Amendment.

Article 332(1) reserves seats for SCs and STs, except STs in Assam's autonomous districts, in the Legislative Assembly of every State. Clause (3) uses the same population-proportion rule as Article 330.

Special rules exist for particular States:

  • Assam (332(2), (4) to (6)). Seats are reserved for the autonomous districts. Only a member of a Scheduled Tribe of that district may be elected from those constituencies. A proviso added by the 90th Amendment (2003) preserves existing ST and non-ST representation in constituencies in the Bodoland Territorial Areas District.
  • Arunachal Pradesh, Meghalaya, Mizoram and Nagaland (332(3A)). Added by the 57th Amendment (1987). Until seats are readjusted after the first census after 2026, ST seats follow a formula based on the ST membership of the Assembly when the amendment came into force. Where every seat was held by STs, all seats except one are reserved.
  • Tripura (332(3B)). Added by the 72nd Amendment (1992), with a similar formula based on ST membership on that date.

The text of Articles 331 and 333 is still in the Constitution, but Article 334(b) ends the effect of these provisions after seventy years. See the next section.

Article 333 originally let the Governor nominate "such number of members ... as he considers appropriate". The 23rd Amendment (1969) changed this to one member.

Article 334 says the provisions on (a) SC and ST reserved seats and (b) Anglo-Indian nomination in the Lok Sabha and State Assemblies "shall cease to have effect on the expiration of a period of eighty years in respect of clause (a) and seventy years in respect of clause (b) from the commencement of this Constitution."

The footnotes show how the period grew:

The Constitution's main provisions came into force on 26 January 1950. So the 104th Amendment extended SC and ST reservation to eighty years from then, but kept Anglo-Indian nomination at seventy years, which means it was not continued beyond January 2020. The proviso protects existing members until the House or Assembly then in existence is dissolved.

Earlier extensions (thirty to forty years) are recorded in the same footnote.

The 106th Amendment (2023) added Articles 330A and 332A. As nearly as may be, one-third of all directly elected seats in the Lok Sabha and in every State Assembly are reserved for women, including one-third of the SC and ST seats.

Article 334A delays the effect. These seats come into effect only after a delimitation based on the first census taken after the 106th Amendment commences, and they cease after fifteen years from that commencement, subject to Parliament extending them by law. Rotation follows each later delimitation. Full details are on women's reservation (106th Amendment).

  • 330 and 332: SC and ST seats in proportion to population, State by State, on the 2001 census until post-2026 figures are published.
  • 331 and 333: President up to two, Governor one Anglo-Indian nominee; ended after seventy years under 334(b).
  • 334: eighty years for SC/ST seats (104th Amendment, in force 25-1-2020).
  • 332(3A), (3B): special ST formulas for four north-eastern States and Tripura.
  • 330A, 332A, 334A: one-third for women after the post-amendment census and delimitation, for fifteen years.

Q1. After the 104th Amendment, Article 334 provides SC and ST reservation in legislatures for: easy

✅ Answer: (c) Eighty years

💡 Explanation: Eighty years for clause (a), SC and ST seats, from the commencement of the Constitution.

Q2. Under the 104th Amendment, Anglo-Indian nomination to legislatures: medium

✅ Answer: (b) Was kept at seventy years and so not continued after January 2020

💡 Explanation: Clause (b) stayed at seventy years from commencement, so the nominations were not continued beyond January 2020.

Q3. Seats for SCs and STs in the Lok Sabha are allotted: medium

✅ Answer: (b) State by State in proportion to their population

💡 Explanation: Article 330(2) ties each State's reserved seats to its SC or ST population share.

Q4. Until figures for the first census after 2026 are published, reservation is based on the: hard

✅ Answer: (c) 2001 census

💡 Explanation: The Explanation to Article 330, as amended by the 87th Amendment, refers to the 2001 census.

Q5. How many Anglo-Indian members could a Governor nominate to a State Assembly under Article 333? medium

✅ Answer: (a) One

💡 Explanation: One member, since the 23rd Amendment (1969) replaced the earlier open-ended wording.

Until when are SC and ST seats reserved in the Lok Sabha?

Article 334 sets eighty years from the Constitution's commencement on 26 January 1950, after the 104th Amendment, subject to any further amendment.

Is there reservation for OBCs in the Lok Sabha?

Articles 330 and 332 reserve seats only for Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes; they make no provision for other backward classes in the Lok Sabha or Assemblies.
