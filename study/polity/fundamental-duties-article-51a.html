Fundamental Duties (Article 51A): All 11 Duties, the Amendments and Exam Traps

Part IVA • Article 51A (a) to (k) • 42nd Amendment, 1976 • 86th Amendment, 2002 • Duty of every citizen

UPSC SSC Railway State PSC 📅 Last updated: 2026-10-31

Part IVA of the Constitution has a single Article, Article 51A. It begins: "It shall be the duty of every citizen of India" and then lists the duties as clauses (a) to (k). Two facts carry most exam questions:

  • 42nd Amendment, 1976: inserted Part IVA with ten duties, clauses (a) to (j), with effect from 3 January 1977.
  • 86th Amendment, 2002: added the eleventh duty, clause (k), with effect from 1 April 2010.

The duties bind citizens, not every person and not the State. That is the reverse of Part IV, where the Directive Principles are addressed to the State.

ClauseDuty
(a)Abide by the Constitution and respect its ideals and institutions, the National Flag and the National Anthem
(b)Cherish and follow the noble ideals of the national struggle for freedom
(c)Uphold and protect the sovereignty, unity and integrity of India
(d)Defend the country and render national service when called upon
(e)Promote harmony and the spirit of common brotherhood beyond religious, linguistic, regional or sectional differences; renounce practices derogatory to the dignity of women
(f)Value and preserve the rich heritage of our composite culture
(g)Protect and improve the natural environment, including forests, lakes, rivers and wild life, and have compassion for living creatures
(h)Develop the scientific temper, humanism and the spirit of inquiry and reform
(i)Safeguard public property and abjure violence
(j)Strive towards excellence in all spheres of individual and collective activity
(k)As a parent or guardian, provide opportunities for education to a child or ward between six and fourteen years (added in 2002)

Group the eleven into five themes. This grouping is a memory aid, not something the Constitution does.

  • The nation and its symbols: (a) Constitution, Flag, Anthem; (b) freedom-struggle ideals; (c) sovereignty, unity, integrity; (d) defence and national service.
  • Society: (e) harmony and the dignity of women; (f) composite culture.
  • Nature: (g) environment and living creatures.
  • The mind: (h) scientific temper; (j) excellence.
  • Property and children: (i) public property and non-violence; (k) education of children.

The 86th Amendment works from both sides. Article 21A, a Fundamental Right, says the State "shall provide free and compulsory education to all children of the age of six to fourteen years". Clause (k) puts the matching duty on the parent or guardian to provide opportunities for that education. Exams like to pair the two: same amendment, same age band, one a right and one a duty. For the rest of Part III, see the Fundamental Rights, DPSP and Duties notes.

  • ✘ "There are 10 Fundamental Duties." That was true only until the 86th Amendment took effect; the count is now 11.
  • ✘ "Fundamental Duties apply to all persons." Article 51A says "every citizen of India".
  • ✘ "Voting is a Fundamental Duty." It is not in Article 51A.
  • ✘ "Duties were in the original Constitution." Part IVA dates from 1976.

For how the Directive Principles that sit just before Part IVA are grouped, see the DPSP classification.

Q1. How many Fundamental Duties does Article 51A list today? easy

✅ Answer: (b) 11

💡 Explanation: Clauses (a) to (j) came with the 42nd Amendment, 1976, and clause (k) was added by the 86th Amendment, 2002, making 11.

Q2. Which amendment added the duty of parents to provide opportunities for education to children aged 6 to 14? easy

✅ Answer: (c) 86th Amendment

💡 Explanation: Clause (k) was inserted by the Constitution (Eighty-sixth Amendment) Act, 2002, the same amendment that brought in Article 21A.

Q3. Fundamental Duties apply to: easy

✅ Answer: (b) citizens of India only

💡 Explanation: Article 51A begins 'It shall be the duty of every citizen of India'. Foreigners are not addressed by it.

Q4. 'To renounce practices derogatory to the dignity of women' is part of which clause of Article 51A? medium

✅ Answer: (c) (e)

💡 Explanation: Clause (e) joins two duties: promoting harmony and common brotherhood, and renouncing practices derogatory to the dignity of women.

Q5. Which of these is NOT a Fundamental Duty in Article 51A? medium

✅ Answer: (c) To vote in every election

💡 Explanation: Voting is not listed in Article 51A. Public property is clause (i), scientific temper clause (h) and the environment clause (g).

Can a court punish someone for breaking a Fundamental Duty?

Article 51A itself sets no penalty and no court remedy. A duty is enforced only when Parliament or a State turns it into an ordinary law, for example a law on protecting the National Flag or the environment.

Are Fundamental Duties part of the original Constitution?

No. The original Constitution of 1950 had no Part IVA. It was added by the 42nd Amendment and came into force on 3 January 1977.
