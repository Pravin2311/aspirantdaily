Financial Emergency Under Article 360: Grounds, Parliamentary Approval and Powers Over the States

Clause by clause • Two-month approval • No time limit once approved • Canons of financial propriety • State Money Bills reserved • Judges' salaries • 352 vs 356 vs 360

UPSC SSC State PSC 📅 Last updated: 2026-11-04

Article 360 is the third and least-discussed of the Constitution's emergency provisions. It lets the President proclaim a financial emergency if satisfied that "the financial stability or credit of India or of any part of the territory thereof is threatened". Article 352 covers war, external aggression and armed rebellion, and Article 356 covers the failure of constitutional machinery in a State. Article 360 is about money. Its text is short, in four clauses, and exam questions test its clauses almost word for word.

ClauseWhat it says
360(1)The President may, by Proclamation, declare that the financial stability or credit of India, or of any part of it, is threatened
360(2)The Proclamation may be revoked or varied, must be laid before both Houses, and lapses after two months unless both Houses approve it
360(3)Union executive power extends to directing any State to observe canons of financial propriety, and any other directions the President thinks necessary
360(4)Directions may require salary cuts for State employees and the reservation of State Money Bills for the President; the President may cut Union salaries, including those of Supreme Court and High Court Judges

Under clause (2), the Proclamation must be laid before each House of Parliament. It "shall cease to operate at the expiration of two months" unless both Houses pass resolutions approving it within that time.

A proviso covers a dissolved Lok Sabha. If the Proclamation is issued while the House of the People is dissolved, or the House is dissolved during the two months, the Rajya Sabha may approve it. The Proclamation then continues until thirty days after the reconstituted Lok Sabha first sits. It lapses unless the Lok Sabha also approves it within those thirty days.

Three points are often tested:

  • No special majority. Article 360 asks only for "resolutions of both Houses". Article 352(6), by contrast, requires a majority of the total membership of each House and two-thirds of those present and voting.
  • No time limit once approved. Article 360 sets no maximum duration and no periodic renewal. Once approved, the Proclamation continues until it is revoked by a later Proclamation.
  • Current wording since 1979. Clause (2) in its present form was substituted by the 44th Amendment. That Amendment also omitted clause (5), which the 38th Amendment had inserted in 1975.

While the Proclamation is in force, the Union's executive power extends to giving any State directions to observe "such canons of financial propriety as may be specified", and any other directions the President considers necessary. Clause (4) spells out what those directions may include:

  1. Salary cuts in the States: reducing the salaries and allowances of all or any class of persons serving the State.
  2. Control over State Bills: requiring all Money Bills, and other Bills covered by Article 207, to be reserved for the President after the State Legislature passes them.
  3. Salary cuts at the Union level: the President may direct reductions in the salaries and allowances of persons serving the Union, "including the Judges of the Supreme Court and the High Courts".

The third power stands out. Judges' salaries are otherwise protected: Article 125(2) and Article 221(2) bar changing a judge's allowances to their disadvantage after appointment. Clause (4)(b) of Article 360 is the exception.

The full rules for the other two are on Article 352 and Article 356. How the Union and the States share financial powers in normal times is covered in federalism and Centre-State relations and the Finance Commission.

  • Article 360 can cover part of India's territory, not only the whole country.
  • It is the President who proclaims it, on being satisfied of the threat. In practice the President acts on ministerial advice under Article 74.
  • The two-month approval period is the same as for Article 356, not the one month that applies to Article 352.
  • Financial emergency does not suspend any fundamental right. That is done only under Articles 358 and 359, which are tied to Article 352.

Clause (4)(a)(ii) refers to "Money Bills or other Bills to which the provisions of article 207 apply". Article 207 covers State financial Bills: Bills dealing with the matters listed in Article 199(1)(a) to (f), such as imposing taxes or borrowing. Normally such Bills can be introduced only on the Governor's recommendation and become law once the Governor assents. During a financial emergency, the Union can require them to be sent to the President after the State Legislature passes them. In effect, this gives the Centre a check on State taxation and spending for as long as the emergency lasts.

Q1. Within what period must a financial emergency be approved by both Houses? easy

✅ Answer: (b) Two months

💡 Explanation: Article 360(2)(c): the Proclamation ceases to operate after two months unless both Houses approve it. One month applies to Article 352.

Q2. Once approved, a financial emergency remains in force: medium

✅ Answer: (c) Until it is revoked

💡 Explanation: Article 360 sets no maximum period and no renewal requirement. It continues until revoked or varied by a later Proclamation.

Q3. During a financial emergency, whose salaries can the President order to be reduced? medium

✅ Answer: (d) Union employees including Supreme Court and High Court Judges

💡 Explanation: Article 360(4)(b) allows directions to reduce salaries of persons serving the Union, including the Judges of the Supreme Court and the High Courts.

Q4. Which statement about Article 360 is correct? medium

✅ Answer: (a) It can apply to a part of India's territory

💡 Explanation: Article 360(1) covers the financial stability or credit of India 'or of any part of the territory thereof'. It suspends no right, needs only simple resolutions, and is proclaimed by the President.

Q5. Under Article 360, State Money Bills may be required to be: medium

✅ Answer: (b) Reserved for the President's consideration

💡 Explanation: Article 360(4)(a)(ii) allows a direction requiring Money Bills and other Bills covered by Article 207 to be reserved for the President after the State Legislature passes them.

What majority is needed to approve a financial emergency?

A simple majority. Article 360 needs only 'resolutions of both Houses of Parliament'. The special majority (total membership plus two-thirds present and voting) applies to a national emergency under Article 352(6).

Does a financial emergency suspend fundamental rights?

No. Suspension of Article 19 and of the enforcement of other rights is tied to a national emergency under Articles 358 and 359. Article 360 deals only with financial matters.
