Impeachment of the President Under Article 61: Ground, Notice, Two-Thirds Majorities and Who Takes Part

Violation of the Constitution • Notice by one-fourth of members • Two-thirds of total membership • Investigation by the other House • Nominated MPs vs MLAs • Vacancy within six months

UPSC SSC State PSC Railway 📅 Last updated: 2026-11-04

The President of India holds office for five years under Article 56. That term can end early in only a few ways: death, resignation by a letter addressed to the Vice-President, or removal by impeachment. Article 56(1)(b) says the President may be removed "for violation of the Constitution" by impeachment "in the manner provided in article 61". There is no other ground. Misbehaviour, incapacity or loss of confidence are not grounds for removing a President, unlike the grounds for removing a judge.

Article 61 sets out the procedure in four clauses. It is a quasi-judicial process run entirely by Parliament: one House brings the charge, and the other investigates it and decides.

  1. Charge preferred by either House (61(1)). The impeachment can start in the Lok Sabha or the Rajya Sabha.
  2. Notice of fourteen days (61(2)(a)). The resolution can be moved only after at least fourteen days' written notice, signed by not less than one-fourth of the total number of members of that House.
  3. Two-thirds of total membership (61(2)(b)). The House bringing the charge must pass the resolution by a majority of not less than two-thirds of its total membership.
  4. Investigation by the other House (61(3)). The other House investigates the charge itself or has it investigated. The President has the right to appear and to be represented at the investigation.
  5. Two-thirds again (61(4)). If the investigating House, by a majority of not less than two-thirds of its total membership, passes a resolution declaring the charge sustained, the President is removed from office from the date the resolution is passed.

Note the majority. It is two-thirds of the total membership of the House, not of members present and voting. This is stricter than the special majority for a constitutional amendment under Article 368, which needs a majority of total membership and two-thirds of those present and voting. The different kinds are explained on types of majority in Parliament.

The impeachment is a matter for Parliament alone. All members of both Houses take part, including nominated members, because the majorities are counted on total membership. The President's election is different. Under Article 54 the electoral college has only the elected members of both Houses of Parliament and of the State Legislative Assemblies, along with the Assemblies of Delhi and Puducherry.

  • Nominated MPs do not vote in the President's election but do take part in impeachment.
  • Elected MLAs vote in the election but have no role in impeachment.

This contrast is one of the most frequently asked points. The election itself is covered on election of the President.

If a President is removed, or the office falls vacant through death or resignation, Article 62(2) requires an election "as soon as possible after, and in no case later than six months from" the date of the vacancy. The person elected gets a full five-year term from the date of taking office, not just the remainder of the predecessor's term. In the meantime, Article 65(1) makes the Vice-President act as President until the new President takes office.

A resignation is simpler. Under Article 56(1)(a) the President writes to the Vice-President, who must communicate it at once to the Speaker of the Lok Sabha (Article 56(2)).

Only the President's removal is formally called "impeachment" in the Constitution. Vice-President removal is covered in detail on the Vice-President.

Article 361 explains why impeachment is the only way to hold a sitting President to account. The President is "not answerable to any court" for the exercise of the powers and duties of the office. During the term of office, no criminal proceedings can be started or continued against the President in any court, and no court can issue process for the President's arrest or imprisonment. Civil proceedings for acts done in a personal capacity need two months' written notice first.

The first proviso to Article 361(1) links this immunity directly to impeachment. The President's conduct "may be brought under review by any court, tribunal or body appointed or designated by either House of Parliament for the investigation of a charge under article 61". So the investigating House under Article 61(3) may set up such a body, and the President's immunity does not shield the President from it.

A second proviso keeps open the right to bring proceedings against the Government of India. A citizen who is wronged by an official act sues the government, not the President personally.

  • Ground: violation of the Constitution, and nothing else (Article 56(1)(b)).
  • Starts in: either House (Article 61(1)).
  • Notice: 14 days, signed by at least one-fourth of the total members.
  • Majority: two-thirds of total membership, first in the charging House and then in the investigating House.
  • Right of the President: to appear and be represented at the investigation.
  • Effect: removal from the date the second resolution is passed.
  • Vacancy: filled within six months. The Vice-President acts as President in the meantime.

Q1. The President of India can be impeached on the ground of: easy

✅ Answer: (c) Violation of the Constitution

💡 Explanation: Article 56(1)(b) and Article 61(1) name only 'violation of the Constitution'. Proved misbehaviour or incapacity is the ground for removing judges.

Q2. The notice for an impeachment resolution must be signed by at least: medium

✅ Answer: (a) One-fourth of the total members of the House

💡 Explanation: Article 61(2)(a): at least fourteen days' written notice signed by not less than one-fourth of the total number of members of the House.

Q3. Who takes part in the impeachment of the President but NOT in the President's election? medium

✅ Answer: (d) Nominated members of Parliament

💡 Explanation: Impeachment majorities are counted on total membership, which includes nominated MPs. Article 54 limits the electoral college to elected members.

Q4. The majority needed in each House for impeachment is: easy

✅ Answer: (b) Two-thirds of the total membership

💡 Explanation: Both the charging resolution (61(2)(b)) and the resolution sustaining the charge (61(4)) need not less than two-thirds of the total membership of the House.

Q5. An election to fill a vacancy caused by the President's removal must be held within: easy

✅ Answer: (a) Six months

💡 Explanation: Article 62(2): as soon as possible and in no case later than six months from the date of the vacancy. The new President gets a full five-year term.

Can impeachment start in the Rajya Sabha?

Yes. Article 61(1) says the charge may be preferred by either House. The other House then investigates and votes on whether the charge is sustained.

Does the Supreme Court have any role in impeachment?

No. Article 61 leaves the whole process to the two Houses of Parliament. The Supreme Court's role under Article 71 is limited to disputes about the President's election.
