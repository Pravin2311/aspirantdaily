All India Services Under Article 312: The Rajya Sabha Resolution, IAS and IPS, and the All-India Judicial Service

312(1) two-thirds Rajya Sabha resolution • 312(2) IAS and IPS deemed created • 312(3)-(4) all-India judicial service • 312A (28th Amendment) • 313 • 314 omitted

UPSC SSC State PSC 📅 Last updated: 2026-11-06

An all-India service is a service common to the Union and the States. Under Article 312, Parliament may create a new one by law only after the Rajya Sabha passes a resolution, supported by at least two-thirds of the members present and voting, declaring it necessary or expedient in the national interest. The Indian Administrative Service and the Indian Police Service existed when the Constitution began. Article 312(2) treats them as if Parliament had created them under this Article.

This page reads Articles 312, 312A, 313 and 314 from the official text and shows how they connect to the rest of Part XIV. It covers the constitutional framework, not the rules made under the All India Services Act.

Creating an all-India service takes two steps:

  1. Rajya Sabha resolution. The Council of States declares, by a resolution supported by not less than two-thirds of the members present and voting, that creating the service is necessary or expedient in the national interest.
  2. Parliamentary law. Parliament then provides by law for the creation of one or more all-India services common to the Union and the States. The same law may regulate recruitment and conditions of service.

Clause (1) opens "Notwithstanding anything in Chapter VI of Part VI or Part XI". Chapter VI of Part VI deals with subordinate courts, and Part XI with relations between the Union and the States. Without this override, a service that works for the States could clash with the States' control over their own services and courts. The 42nd Amendment (1976) substituted these words and added the all-India judicial service to clause (1).

The Rajya Sabha is the Council of States. It represents the States. An all-India service puts officers chosen and controlled by the Union into posts that run State administration, so the Constitution asks the House of the States to agree first.

Article 249 follows the same pattern: a Rajya Sabha resolution with two-thirds present and voting lets Parliament make laws on a State List matter in the national interest. Both are listed among the special powers of the Rajya Sabha. The majority needed is two-thirds of members present and voting, a special majority explained in types of majority in Parliament.

Clause (2) says that the services "known at the commencement of this Constitution as the Indian Administrative Service and the Indian Police Service shall be deemed to be services created by Parliament under this article". No Rajya Sabha resolution was needed for these two. They carried over from before 1950 and the Constitution gave them the same footing as a service created under Article 312.

Any further all-India service needs the full procedure in clause (1).

The 42nd Amendment inserted a power to create an all-India judicial service. Two clauses limit it:

  • Clause (3) says the service shall not include any post inferior to that of a district judge, as defined in Article 236. "District judge" there includes judges of a city civil court, additional and assistant district judges, a chief judge of a small cause court and certain other judicial posts.
  • Clause (4) lets the law creating the service amend Chapter VI of Part VI, on subordinate courts, as far as needed. Such a law is not treated as an amendment of the Constitution for the purposes of Article 368.

So Parliament can adjust the subordinate courts chapter by ordinary law with a Rajya Sabha resolution, without the Article 368 procedure. The power exists in the text; this page does not say whether or when it will be used.

Article 312A was inserted by the Twenty-eighth Amendment (1972), in force from 29 August 1972. It concerns persons appointed by the Secretary of State, or the Secretary of State in Council, to a civil service of the Crown in India before the Constitution began. It lets Parliament by law:

  • vary or revoke their conditions of service as to remuneration, leave, pension and disciplinary rights, if they continued in service after the amendment; and
  • vary or revoke the pension conditions of those who had already retired or left service.

A proviso protects those who held office as a Supreme Court or High Court Judge, the CAG, a member of a Public Service Commission or the Chief Election Commissioner. Their conditions cannot be varied to their disadvantage after appointment, except so far as they apply to them as former Secretary of State appointees. Clause (3) bars courts from hearing certain disputes about their pre-1950 covenants.

Article 313 is a transitional provision. Laws in force before 1950 that applied to a public service continuing after 1950, including an all-India service, stay in force as far as consistent with the Constitution, until other provision is made.

Article 314 once protected existing officers of certain services. The Twenty-eighth Amendment omitted it, with effect from 29 August 1972, at the same time as it inserted Article 312A.

Members of an all-India service serve under both the Union and the States, yet Article 310(1) places them in the President's pleasure group, not the Governor's. Read this with the doctrine of pleasure under Articles 310 and 311.

  • The resolution comes from the Rajya Sabha alone, with two-thirds of members present and voting, not of total membership.
  • The resolution is followed by a parliamentary law; the resolution alone creates nothing.
  • The IAS and IPS are the two services named in the Constitution. Article 312(2) deems them created by Parliament.
  • The all-India judicial service was added by the 42nd Amendment and cannot include posts below district judge.
  • Article 312A came from the 28th Amendment (1972), which also omitted Article 314.

Why does only the Rajya Sabha pass the resolution under Article 312?

The Rajya Sabha is the Council of States. A service common to the Union and the States affects State administration, so the Constitution asks the House representing the States to agree first.

Does the Constitution name the Indian Forest Service?

No. Article 312(2) names only the IAS and IPS. Any other all-India service rests on a law made by Parliament under Article 312(1).
