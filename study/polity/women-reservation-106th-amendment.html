Women's Reservation in the Lok Sabha and Assemblies: The 106th Amendment Explained

Constitution (One Hundred and Sixth Amendment) Act, 2023 • Article 330A • Article 332A • Article 239AA(2)(ba)-(bc) • Article 334A

UPSC SSC State PSC 📅 Last updated: 2026-10-31

The Constitution (One Hundred and Sixth Amendment) Act, 2023 reserves one-third of the directly elected seats for women in three legislatures: the Lok Sabha, every State Legislative Assembly and the Legislative Assembly of the National Capital Territory of Delhi. It does this through four provisions:

ProvisionCovers
Article 330AHouse of the People (Lok Sabha)
Article 332ALegislative Assembly of every State
Article 239AA(2)(ba) to (bc)Legislative Assembly of Delhi
Article 334AWhen the reservation starts, how long it lasts and rotation

Article 330A has three clauses, and Article 332A copies the same structure for the States.

  • Clause (1): seats "shall be reserved for women in the House of the People".
  • Clause (2): as nearly as may be, one-third of the seats reserved for Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes under Article 330(2) are reserved for women of those groups.
  • Clause (3): as nearly as may be, one-third of all directly elected seats are reserved for women, including the SC and ST women's seats from clause (2).

The word "including" matters. The SC and ST women's seats are not added on top of the one-third; they are part of it. The phrase "as nearly as may be" allows for rounding, because one-third of a State's seat total is rarely a whole number.

Worked case. Take an imaginary State Assembly with 120 directly elected seats, of which 18 are reserved for Scheduled Castes and 12 for Scheduled Tribes. Women get as nearly as may be one-third of the SC seats (6) and of the ST seats (4). The overall women's share is one-third of 120, which is 40. Those 40 include the 6 SC and 4 ST women's seats, so 30 of the women's seats come from the general seats.

This is the most tested part. Article 334A(1) says the reservation "shall come into effect after an exercise of delimitation is undertaken for this purpose after the relevant figures for the first census taken after commencement" of the Amendment have been published. So two things must happen first, in order:

  • a census taken after the Amendment's commencement, with its relevant figures published; and
  • a delimitation exercise based on those figures.

Article 334A(4) adds that the reservation does not affect any sitting House or Assembly until it is dissolved. So the reserved seats first apply at a general election held after both steps are complete. For how delimitation works, see the notes on Parliament of India.

Article 334A(1) says the reservation ceases fifteen years from the commencement. Article 334A(2) lets the seats "continue till such date as the Parliament may by law determine", so Parliament can extend the arrangement by ordinary law rather than another constitutional amendment.

Article 334A(3) provides for rotation: the seats reserved for women change "after each subsequent exercise of delimitation as the Parliament may by law determine". A constituency reserved for women in one delimitation need not stay reserved in the next.

Compare the much older reservation for Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes. Under Article 334, as amended by the 104th Amendment, 2019, that reservation lasts eighty years from the commencement of the Constitution. The women's reservation has its own separate clock under Article 334A.

  • Rajya Sabha and State Legislative Councils: none of the new provisions mentions the Council of States or a Legislative Council. They cover only directly elected Houses.
  • OBC women: there is a share inside SC and ST seats, but no separate share for Other Backward Classes.
  • Panchayats and municipalities: these already have women's reservation under Articles 243D and 243T, which the 106th Amendment did not change. See Panchayati Raj and municipalities.

The 73rd and 74th Amendments gave women reserved seats in local bodies long before 2023. Comparing the wording is a good way to fix both in memory.

The difference between "as nearly as may be" and "not less than" matters. The local-body rule is a floor, so a State can reserve more than one-third. The parliamentary rule aims at one-third, adjusted only for rounding.

A balanced answer usually covers four points: what the Articles provide (one-third, including SC and ST women); when it takes effect (census, then delimitation, under Article 334A); how long it lasts (fifteen years, extendable by law) and how seats rotate; and what is left out (Rajya Sabha, Legislative Councils, a separate OBC share). Close with the link to local bodies, where reservation under Articles 243D and 243T has already been in force for decades.

  • ✘ "The reservation applied from the 2024 general election." It takes effect only after the census-based delimitation in Article 334A(1).
  • ✘ "SC and ST women's seats are extra to the one-third." Clause (3) says the one-third includes them.
  • ✘ "It covers the Rajya Sabha." It covers only the Lok Sabha, State Assemblies and the Delhi Assembly.
  • ✘ "It lasts as long as SC and ST reservation." Its own limit is fifteen years from commencement, extendable by law.

Q1. Which Article reserves seats for women in the Lok Sabha? easy

✅ Answer: (b) Article 330A

💡 Explanation: Article 330A, inserted by the 106th Amendment, covers the House of the People. Article 332A covers State Assemblies and Article 334A says when the reservation takes effect.

Q2. The reservation for women under the 106th Amendment takes effect: medium

✅ Answer: (c) after delimitation based on the first census taken after the Act's commencement

💡 Explanation: Article 334A(1) links it to a delimitation exercise undertaken after the figures of the first census taken after the Amendment's commencement are published.

Q3. For how long does the reservation last under Article 334A(1)? easy

✅ Answer: (b) 15 years

💡 Explanation: It ceases fifteen years from commencement, though Article 334A(2) lets Parliament continue the seats by law.

Q4. Which of these is NOT covered by the 106th Amendment's reservation for women? medium

✅ Answer: (d) Rajya Sabha

💡 Explanation: Articles 330A, 332A and 239AA(2)(ba) cover the House of the People, State Assemblies and the Delhi Assembly. None of them deals with the Council of States or State Legislative Councils.

Q5. Of the Lok Sabha seats reserved for Scheduled Castes, what share is reserved for SC women? medium

✅ Answer: (b) One-third

💡 Explanation: Article 330A(2) reserves, as nearly as may be, one-third of the SC and ST seats for women of those groups. These count within the overall one-third.

Is there a separate quota for OBC women?

No. Articles 330A and 332A provide a women's share inside the SC and ST reserved seats, but they make no separate provision for Other Backward Classes.

Why is it called Nari Shakti Vandan?

That was the name used for the Bill when it was introduced in 2023. The constitutional text itself refers to it only as the Constitution (One Hundred and Sixth Amendment) Act, 2023.
