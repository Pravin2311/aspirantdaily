Types of Majority in the Indian Constitution: Simple, Absolute, Effective and Special

Article 100 • Articles 67, 90, 94 • Article 368(2) • Articles 61, 124(4), 249, 312, 352(6) • Joint sitting, Article 108(4)

UPSC SSC State PSC 📅 Last updated: 2026-10-31

The Constitution does not use one voting rule for everything. A routine Bill needs far fewer votes than a constitutional amendment, and removing a Speaker is counted differently from removing a Judge. Textbooks sort these rules into four labels: simple, absolute, effective and special majority. The labels are not in the Constitution itself, but the formulas behind them are, and exam questions turn on reading each formula exactly.

Every formula answers one question: what is the denominator? There are three choices.

  • Members present and voting: only those who actually vote. Absentees and abstainers drop out.
  • All the then members: the sitting strength of the House, leaving out vacant seats.
  • Total membership: the full strength of the House, counting vacant seats too.

Article 100(1) is the default rule. All questions at a sitting of either House, or a joint sitting, are decided "by a majority of votes of the members present and voting", leaving out the presiding officer. The Speaker or Chairman does not vote in the first instance but has a casting vote in a tie.

Ordinary Bills, Money Bills, motions of thanks, adjournment motions and the confidence motion are all decided this way. A meeting can only take place with a quorum of one-tenth of the total number of members of the House, under Article 100(3).

An absolute majority is more than half of the total membership of the House, whether or not seats are vacant and whoever turns up. It rarely stands alone in the Constitution. It appears as one half of the special majority described below, and political commentary uses it for a party that holds more than half the seats.

An effective majority is more than half of the House's sitting strength: total membership minus vacancies. The Constitution's phrase is "a majority of all the then members". It is used for removing presiding officers:

Removal ofArticleRule
Speaker or Deputy Speaker, Lok Sabha94(c)Resolution of the House passed by a majority of all the then members; at least 14 days' notice
Deputy Chairman, Rajya Sabha90(c)Resolution of the Council passed by a majority of all the then members; at least 14 days' notice
Vice-President (Chairman, Rajya Sabha)67(b)Resolution of the Rajya Sabha passed by a majority of all the then members, agreed to by the Lok Sabha

Note the Vice-President case: only the Rajya Sabha needs the effective majority. The Lok Sabha merely has to agree, which means a simple majority there.

"Special majority" covers every rule stricter than the ones above. There are three versions, and confusing them is the commonest mistake.

TypeFormulaUsed for
Article 368 typeMajority of total membership and two-thirds of members present and voting, in each HouseConstitutional amendments (368(2)); removal of Supreme Court Judges (124(4)), and High Court Judges by the same procedure; approving a National Emergency (352(6)); a State Assembly's resolution to create or abolish a Legislative Council (169(1))
Article 61 typeTwo-thirds of the total membership of the HouseImpeachment of the President, in the House that prefers the charge and again in the House that investigates it
Article 249 typeTwo-thirds of members present and voting, Rajya Sabha onlyParliament legislating on a State List matter in the national interest (249(1)); creating new all-India services (312(1))

Some amendments under Article 368(2) also need ratification by half the State Legislatures; see the three ways the Constitution can be amended.

Take an imaginary House with 250 seats, of which 10 are vacant. On the day of the vote, 180 members are present and vote.

MajorityDenominatorVotes needed
Simple180 present and voting91
Absolute250 total membership126
Effective240 sitting members (250 − 10)121
Special, Article 368 typeBoth 250 and 180126 (higher of 126 and 120)
Special, Article 61 type250 total membership167 (two-thirds of 250 = 166.7)
Special, Article 249 type180 present and voting120

For the Article 368 type, work out both conditions and take whichever is higher. When attendance is low, the absolute-majority half decides; when attendance is high, the two-thirds half does.

The same House could not even meet unless 25 members were present, because the quorum is one-tenth of the total number of members.

A quick method for any such question in the exam hall:

  • Identify the decision and recall its Article.
  • Read the denominator from the formula: present and voting, all the then members, or total membership.
  • Compute each condition separately, rounding up to the next whole member.
  • If there are two conditions, the answer is the larger number.

At a joint sitting under Article 108, a Bill passes "by a majority of the total number of members of both Houses present and voting". That is a simple majority of the combined sitting, not a special majority.

  • ✘ "The Speaker is removed by a special majority." Article 94(c) needs only a majority of all the then members.
  • ✘ "Article 249 needs two-thirds of total membership." It needs two-thirds of members present and voting, in the Rajya Sabha alone.
  • ✘ "Impeachment of the President uses the Article 368 formula." Article 61 needs two-thirds of the total membership, which is stricter.

Q1. Removal of the Speaker of the Lok Sabha requires: medium

✅ Answer: (b) a majority of all the then members of the House

💡 Explanation: Article 94(c) needs a majority of 'all the then members of the House', which exams call an effective majority. Vacancies are left out of the count.

Q2. Which decision needs a resolution of the Rajya Sabha supported by two-thirds of members present and voting? medium

✅ Answer: (b) Parliament legislating on a State List subject in the national interest

💡 Explanation: Article 249(1) needs this Rajya Sabha resolution. Article 312 uses the same majority for new all-India services.

Q3. A House has 250 seats, 10 of them vacant. 180 members are present and vote. What is the minimum for a special majority under Article 368(2)? hard

✅ Answer: (b) 126

💡 Explanation: Both tests must pass: more than half the total membership of 250 (at least 126) and two-thirds of the 180 present and voting (120). The higher figure binds, so 126 votes are needed.

Q4. Impeachment of the President under Article 61 requires: medium

✅ Answer: (b) two-thirds of the total membership of the House

💡 Explanation: Article 61(2)(b) and (4) both say 'not less than two-thirds of the total membership of the House'. Members who are absent still count in the denominator.

Q5. Ordinary Bills and most motions in Parliament are passed by: easy

✅ Answer: (a) a majority of members present and voting

💡 Explanation: Article 100(1) sets the default: a majority of votes of the members present and voting, unless the Constitution provides otherwise.

Is 'effective majority' a term used in the Constitution?

No. The Constitution says 'a majority of all the then members of the House'. Effective majority, like simple, absolute and special majority, is the label textbooks and exams use for these formulas.

Does a joint sitting need a special majority?

No. Article 108(4) needs only a majority of the total number of members of both Houses present and voting, which is a simple majority of the combined sitting.
