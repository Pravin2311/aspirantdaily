Council of Ministers Under Articles 74 and 75: Binding Advice, the 91st Amendment Cap and Collective Responsibility

74(1) advice and proviso • 74(2) • 75(1A) 15% cap • 164(1A) minimum twelve • 75(1B) defectors • 75(3) collective responsibility • 75(5) six months • Article 78

UPSC SSC State PSC 📅 Last updated: 2026-11-06

The President is the head of the Union executive, but Article 74 places a Council of Ministers, led by the Prime Minister, beside the President to "aid and advise". Article 75 then deals with how Ministers are appointed, how many there can be, whom they answer to and how long a non-member can stay in office. Together these two Articles are the constitutional base of parliamentary government in India.

The current Article 74(1) says the President "shall, in the exercise of his functions, act in accordance with such advice". These words come from the 42nd Amendment (1976), which substituted clause (1) with effect from 3 January 1977.

The 44th Amendment (1978) then added a proviso. The President may require the Council of Ministers to reconsider its advice, "either generally or otherwise". After reconsideration, the President "shall act in accordance with the advice tendered after such reconsideration". So the President can send advice back once, but cannot reject it.

Article 74(2) adds a shield: "The question whether any, and if so what, advice was tendered by Ministers to the President shall not be inquired into in any court." The bar is on inquiring into the advice itself: whether it was given and what it said.

The "act in accordance with advice" rule is why most of the President's powers, including pardons and ordinances, are exercised on ministerial advice. The limits of the President's own judgement are discussed on veto powers of the President.

Article 75(1) sets an order: "The Prime Minister shall be appointed by the President and the other Ministers shall be appointed by the President on the advice of the Prime Minister." The text says nothing about how the President chooses the PM. That choice rests on the collective responsibility rule in clause (3): a PM who cannot keep the confidence of the Lok Sabha cannot run a Council that is answerable to it.

Article 75(2) says Ministers "hold office during the pleasure of the President". Read with Article 74(1), that pleasure is in practice exercised on the PM's advice, which is how a PM drops an individual Minister.

The 91st Amendment (2003), in force from 1 January 2004, inserted clauses (1A) and (1B). Clause (1A) says the total number of Ministers, including the Prime Minister, "shall not exceed fifteen per cent. of the total number of members of the House of the People".

Three details are tested:

  • The PM is counted inside the 15%.
  • The base is the total membership of the Lok Sabha, not of Parliament. Rajya Sabha numbers are not added.
  • For the Union there is no minimum number.

Worked example (Union). Take a Lok Sabha of 543 members, used here only to show the arithmetic. 15% of 543 = 81.45. The total cannot "exceed" 15%, and 82 would exceed 81.45, so the largest permitted Council is 81 Ministers, PM included.

Article 164(1A) applies the same cap to the States, with one difference: the number of Ministers, including the Chief Minister, "shall not be less than twelve".

For a small Assembly, 15% falls below twelve, so the floor of twelve is the binding rule. Exam options that state "15% in every State without exception" miss that proviso.

Clause (1B) links the Council of Ministers to the anti-defection law. A member of either House who belongs to a political party and is disqualified under paragraph 2 of the Tenth Schedule is also disqualified from appointment as a Minister. The bar runs from the date of disqualification until the date the member's term would have expired, or, if the person contests an election to either House before then, until the date of being declared elected, whichever is earlier.

The aim is to stop a defector from being rewarded with a ministerial post while the disqualification lasts. Article 164(1B) has the same rule for the States.

  • Collective responsibility (75(3)): "The Council of Ministers shall be collectively responsible to the House of the People." It answers to the Lok Sabha only. The Rajya Sabha cannot remove it; see special powers of the Rajya Sabha.
  • Oaths (75(4)): before entering office, a Minister takes the oaths of office and of secrecy, administered by the President in the forms in the Third Schedule.
  • The six-month rule (75(5)): a Minister who for six consecutive months is not a member of either House "shall at the expiration of that period cease to be a Minister". A non-member can therefore be appointed but must enter either House within six months.
  • Salaries (75(6)): fixed by Parliament by law and, until then, as in the Second Schedule.

Under Article 88, every Minister has the right to speak in and take part in the proceedings of either House, joint sittings and committees of which the Minister is named a member, but is not entitled by that Article to vote. A Minister votes only in the House of which the Minister is a member.

Article 77(1) says all executive action of the Government of India is expressed to be taken in the name of the President. Article 77(3) lets the President make rules for the convenient transaction of business and its allocation among Ministers.

Article 78 lists the Prime Minister's duties towards the President:

  1. (a) to communicate all decisions of the Council of Ministers on the administration of the Union and proposals for legislation;
  2. (b) to furnish information on those matters that the President calls for;
  3. (c) if the President requires, to submit for the Council's consideration any matter decided by a single Minister but not considered by the Council.

The State-level mirror, with the Governor and the Chief Minister, is covered on Union and State executive and Governor's powers.

  • 74(1): the President acts on advice (42nd Amendment) and may ask for reconsideration once (44th Amendment).
  • 74(2): courts cannot inquire into the advice tendered.
  • 75(1A): Ministers, PM included, not more than 15% of the Lok Sabha's total members (91st Amendment). States: 15% of the Assembly, but not fewer than twelve.
  • 75(1B): a defector disqualified under the Tenth Schedule cannot be made a Minister for the stated period.
  • 75(3): collective responsibility to the Lok Sabha only.
  • 75(5): a non-member must enter Parliament within six consecutive months.
  • 78: the PM keeps the President informed.

Q1. The 15% limit on the size of the Union Council of Ministers is calculated on: easy

✅ Answer: (b) Total members of the Lok Sabha

💡 Explanation: Article 75(1A) uses the total number of members of the House of the People, with the PM counted in.

Q2. In a State with a 60-member Assembly, the maximum number of Ministers including the CM is: medium

✅ Answer: (c) 12

💡 Explanation: 15% of 60 is 9, but the Article 164(1A) proviso sets a minimum of twelve, so twelve is allowed.

Q3. Under Article 74(1), after the Council of Ministers reconsiders its advice, the President: easy

✅ Answer: (c) Shall act in accordance with it

💡 Explanation: The proviso added by the 44th Amendment makes the advice after reconsideration binding.

Q4. A person who is not an MP is appointed a Minister. Under Article 75(5) they must become a member within: easy

✅ Answer: (b) Six consecutive months

💡 Explanation: A Minister who is not a member of either House for six consecutive months ceases to be a Minister.

Q5. The Council of Ministers is collectively responsible to: easy

✅ Answer: (c) The House of the People

💡 Explanation: Article 75(3): collectively responsible to the House of the People, that is the Lok Sabha.

Is the Prime Minister counted in the 15% cap?

Yes. Article 75(1A) says the total number of Ministers including the Prime Minister shall not exceed fifteen per cent of the Lok Sabha's total membership.

Can a member of the Rajya Sabha be a Minister?

Yes. Article 75(5) requires membership of either House, so a Rajya Sabha member qualifies, though the Council answers collectively to the Lok Sabha.
