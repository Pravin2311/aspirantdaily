Jurisdiction of the Supreme Court of India: Original, Writ, Appellate and Advisory

Articles 32, 129, 131-143 and 145(3) • Federal disputes • Special leave • Presidential references

UPSC SSC State PSC 📅 Last updated: 2026-11-01

"Jurisdiction" means the kinds of case a court can hear and how they reach it. The Supreme Court's jurisdiction is spread across Chapter IV of Part V of the Constitution, mostly Articles 129 to 143, with the writ power in Article 32.

Exams test two skills: matching each jurisdiction to its Article, and deciding which jurisdiction a given situation falls under. This page gives both.

Article 131 gives the Supreme Court power to hear certain disputes first, "to the exclusion of any other court". The disputes must be:

  • between the Government of India and one or more States;
  • between the Government of India and one or more States on one side and other States on the other; or
  • between two or more States.

The dispute must involve a question "on which the existence or extent of a legal right depends". A political disagreement with no legal right at stake is not enough.

The proviso excludes disputes arising from a treaty, agreement, covenant, engagement, sanad or similar instrument made before the Constitution began and still in operation. These can instead be referred to the Court for its opinion under Article 143(2).

This is the Supreme Court acting as a federal court. For the wider federal picture, see federalism and Centre-State relations.

Article 32(1) says "the right to move the Supreme Court by appropriate proceedings for the enforcement of the rights conferred by this Part is guaranteed". Article 32(2) lets the Court issue directions, orders or writs, "including writs in the nature of habeas corpus, mandamus, prohibition, quo warranto and certiorari".

Two features set it apart. First, the right to approach the Court is itself a Fundamental Right. Second, it is limited to enforcing Fundamental Rights. Article 139 allows Parliament to give the Court writ powers for other purposes by law. For the five writs and the High Court's parallel power, see the five writs under Articles 32 and 226.

Most cases reach the Supreme Court as appeals from High Courts. The route depends on the kind of case.

ArticleKind of caseCondition
132Civil, criminal or otherHigh Court certifies under Art. 134A a substantial question of law on interpreting the Constitution
133CivilHigh Court certifies a substantial question of law of general importance that needs to be decided by the Supreme Court
134(1)(a)CriminalHigh Court reversed an acquittal on appeal and sentenced the accused to death
134(1)(b)CriminalHigh Court withdrew a case to itself, convicted and sentenced the accused to death
134(1)(c)CriminalHigh Court certifies the case as fit for appeal

Article 134A sets how certificates are given: the High Court may act on its own motion, and must decide the question if the aggrieved party applies orally immediately after the judgment. Article 134(2) allows Parliament to widen criminal appeals by law.

Article 136 is the widest door into the Supreme Court. "Notwithstanding anything in this Chapter", the Court "may, in its discretion, grant special leave to appeal" from any judgment, decree, determination, sentence or order of any court or tribunal in India.

  • No High Court certificate is needed.
  • It is discretionary: no one has a right to special leave.
  • It does not apply to courts or tribunals under any law relating to the Armed Forces (Article 136(2)).

Under Article 143(1), if a question of law or fact of public importance "has arisen, or is likely to arise", the President may refer it to the Supreme Court. The Court "may, after such hearing as it thinks fit, report to the President its opinion".

Notice two words:

  • "may" in 143(1): the Court may decline to give an opinion.
  • "shall" in 143(2): for disputes excluded by the Article 131 proviso, the Court must report its opinion.

Under Article 145(3), at least five judges must hear an Article 143 reference.

A curative petition, which allows a final challenge after a review is dismissed, is not in the Constitution; it was created by the Court itself.

  • ✘ "Article 131 covers disputes between a State and a private company." It covers only disputes among the Union and States.
  • ✘ "The Supreme Court must answer every Presidential reference." Under 143(1) it "may" report its opinion.
  • ✘ "Special leave is a right." Article 136 says "in its discretion".
  • ✘ "Article 141 is the contempt power." Contempt is in Article 129; Article 141 makes the Court's law binding.

Q1. A dispute between two States over a legal right goes first to: easy

✅ Answer: (b) the Supreme Court under Article 131

💡 Explanation: Article 131 gives the Supreme Court original jurisdiction, 'to the exclusion of any other court', in disputes between two or more States involving a legal right.

Q2. Under Article 143(1), the Supreme Court: medium

✅ Answer: (b) may report its opinion

💡 Explanation: Article 143(1) says the Court 'may, after such hearing as it thinks fit, report to the President its opinion'. Under 143(2), for disputes excluded by the Article 131 proviso, it 'shall' report.

Q3. Special leave to appeal under Article 136 cannot be granted against: medium

✅ Answer: (c) a court or tribunal under a law relating to the Armed Forces

💡 Explanation: Article 136(2) excludes courts and tribunals constituted under any law relating to the Armed Forces.

Q4. The minimum number of judges to hear a Presidential reference under Article 143 is: easy

✅ Answer: (b) five

💡 Explanation: Article 145(3) fixes the minimum at five judges for Article 143 references and for substantial questions of constitutional interpretation.

Q5. Which Article makes the Supreme Court a court of record? easy

✅ Answer: (b) Article 129

💡 Explanation: Article 129 makes it a court of record with power to punish for contempt of itself. Article 141 makes its law binding; Article 142 is the complete-justice power.

Is Article 32 original jurisdiction?

A petitioner goes straight to the Supreme Court under Article 32, so it is original in that sense. But exams treat it separately as writ jurisdiction, because it is limited to enforcing Fundamental Rights and is not exclusive to the Supreme Court.

Is the advisory opinion binding on the President?

Article 143 says only that the Court may 'report to the President its opinion'. It does not make the opinion binding, and textbooks treat it as advisory.
