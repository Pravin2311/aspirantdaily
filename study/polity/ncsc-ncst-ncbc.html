National Commissions for SCs, STs and Backward Classes: Articles 338, 338A and 338B Compared

Article 338 NCSC • 338A NCST (89th Amendment) • 338B NCBC (102nd Amendment) • Civil court powers • Reports • 105th Amendment proviso • Articles 339 and 340

UPSC SSC State PSC 📅 Last updated: 2026-11-06

Part XVI of the Constitution creates three national commissions that watch over the safeguards for disadvantaged groups. They are built on almost the same text, so the best way to learn them is to learn the shared template once and then memorise the differences.

The footnotes to Article 338 record a history that exams test:

  1. 65th Amendment (1990), in force 12 March 1992, substituted clauses (1) and (2) of Article 338, which then covered Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes together.
  2. 89th Amendment (2003), in force 19 February 2004, split it in two. Article 338 now covers only Scheduled Castes, and the new Article 338A set up a separate National Commission for Scheduled Tribes. The words "and Scheduled Tribes" were omitted throughout Article 338.

The 102nd Amendment (2018), in force 15 August 2018, inserted Article 338B and gave the National Commission for Backward Classes constitutional status.

The same 102nd Amendment also changed Article 338's last clause. Before 2018, references to Scheduled Castes in Article 338 included backward classes notified by the President after a report under Article 340. Those words were omitted, because backward classes now have their own commission. Clause (10) still says references to Scheduled Castes include the Anglo-Indian community.

Each commission has the same six duties under clause (5), written for its own group:

  1. (a) Investigate and monitor all matters relating to the safeguards for the group under the Constitution, any other law or any government order, and evaluate how they work.
  2. (b) Inquire into specific complaints about deprivation of rights and safeguards.
  3. (c) Participate and advise on the socio-economic development of the group and evaluate progress under the Union and any State. For the NCSC and NCST the text says "the planning process of socio-economic development"; for the NCBC it says only "socio-economic development".
  4. (d) Report to the President annually, and at other times the commission thinks fit.
  5. (e) Recommend measures the Union or any State should take for effective implementation and for protection, welfare and development.
  6. (f) Other functions the President specifies by rule, subject to any law of Parliament.

Each commission also has the power to regulate its own procedure under clause (4).

Under clause (8), while investigating a matter under 5(a) or inquiring into a complaint under 5(b), each commission has "all the powers of a civil court trying a suit", in particular for:

  • summoning and enforcing the attendance of any person from any part of India and examining them on oath;
  • requiring the discovery and production of any document;
  • receiving evidence on affidavits;
  • requisitioning any public record or copy from any court or office;
  • issuing commissions to examine witnesses and documents;
  • any other matter the President determines by rule.

These powers apply only while investigating or inquiring. They are not general judicial powers to decide disputes.

Reports to Parliament (clause 6). The President lays every report before each House of Parliament with a memorandum explaining the action taken or proposed on the Union recommendations, and the reasons for not accepting any of them.

Reports to States (clause 7). Where a report concerns a State, a copy goes to the State with a similar memorandum to be laid before the State Legislature. Here the text differs:

  • For the NCSC and NCST, the copy goes to the Governor, who causes it to be laid.
  • For the NCBC, the copy goes to the State Government, which causes it to be laid.

Mandatory consultation (clause 9). The Union and every State Government "shall consult the Commission on all major policy matters" affecting the group. For the NCBC, a proviso added by the 105th Amendment (2021), in force 15 September 2021, says this duty does not apply for the purposes of Article 342A(3). That clause concerns the lists of backward classes that each State or Union territory prepares for its own purposes.

Two older provisions sit next to these Articles and are often confused with them:

  • Article 339(1): the President may at any time, and had to at the end of ten years from the commencement of the Constitution, appoint a Commission to report on the administration of Scheduled Areas and the welfare of Scheduled Tribes.
  • Article 340(1): the President may appoint a Commission to investigate the conditions of socially and educationally backward classes and recommend steps to improve them. This is an ad hoc inquiry commission, unlike the permanent NCBC under Article 338B.

The Scheduled Areas themselves are covered on Fifth and Sixth Schedules, and reservation provisions on Article 15. A list of other constitutional bodies is on constitutional and statutory bodies.

  • 338 NCSC and 338A NCST: split by the 89th Amendment, in force 19-2-2004.
  • 338B NCBC: constitutional status by the 102nd Amendment, in force 15-8-2018.
  • Each: Chairperson, Vice-Chairperson and three Members, appointed by the President by warrant.
  • Powers: those of a civil court while investigating or inquiring.
  • Reports: to the President, laid before Parliament with an action-taken memorandum. The State copy goes to the Governor (NCSC, NCST) or the State Government (NCBC).
  • Consultation: mandatory on all major policy matters, except the NCBC for Article 342A(3).

Q1. The separate National Commission for Scheduled Tribes was created by: easy

✅ Answer: (b) The 89th Amendment

💡 Explanation: The 89th Amendment (2003), in force 19-2-2004, inserted Article 338A and limited Article 338 to Scheduled Castes.

Q2. The National Commission for Backward Classes gets constitutional status under: easy

✅ Answer: (c) Article 338B

💡 Explanation: Article 338B was inserted by the 102nd Amendment (2018). Article 340 is an ad hoc inquiry commission.

Q3. Each of the three Commissions consists of: medium

✅ Answer: (b) A Chairperson, Vice-Chairperson and three other Members

💡 Explanation: Clause (2) of Articles 338, 338A and 338B uses the same composition, subject to any law of Parliament.

Q4. Where an NCBC report concerns a State, the copy is forwarded to: hard

✅ Answer: (b) The State Government

💡 Explanation: Article 338B(7) sends it to the State Government. For the NCSC and NCST it goes to the Governor.

Q5. The Commissions have the powers of a civil court: medium

✅ Answer: (b) Only while investigating a matter under 5(a) or inquiring into a complaint under 5(b)

💡 Explanation: Clause (8) grants civil court powers while investigating or inquiring under clause (5)(a) or (b).

Who appoints the members of the NCSC, NCST and NCBC?

The President appoints the Chairperson, Vice-Chairperson and other Members of each Commission by warrant under his hand and seal.

Is the NCBC under Article 338B the same as the Article 340 commission?

No. Article 340 lets the President appoint an ad hoc commission to investigate backward classes, while Article 338B creates a permanent constitutional body.
