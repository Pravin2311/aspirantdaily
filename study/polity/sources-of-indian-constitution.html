Sources of the Indian Constitution: Features Borrowed From Britain, the USA, Ireland, France, Canada and Others

NCERT's five-country table • Government of India Act, 1935 • Australia, Germany, USSR, Japan, South Africa • Memory hooks • Exam traps

UPSC SSC State PSC Railways 📅 Last updated: 2026-11-07

NCERT's Class 11 text Indian Constitution at Work (Chapter 1, "Constitution: Why and How?") says the framers did not hesitate to learn from the experiments and experiences of other countries. They borrowed a number of provisions from different countries.

NCERT also stresses that this borrowing was not slavish imitation. Each provision had to be defended on the grounds that it suited Indian problems and aspirations. Dr. B.R. Ambedkar made the same point in the Constituent Assembly on 4 November 1948. He argued that the only new things in a constitution framed so late in history are the changes made to remove faults and fit the country's needs.

Canadian ConstitutionA quasi-federal form of government (a federal system with a strong central government); the idea of residual powersResiduary powers with Parliament: Article 248 and Entry 97 of the Union List

NCERT adds that the Constituent Assembly itself was composed roughly along the lines of the Cabinet Mission plan. Its members were chosen by indirect election by the Provincial Legislative Assemblies established under the Government of India Act, 1935. Under that plan, the provinces were to elect 292 members and the princely states were allotted a minimum of 93 seats.

  • Rights from America, Principles from Ireland: Fundamental Rights and judicial review come from the USA, and the Directive Principles from Ireland. The DPSP and Fundamental Rights pair splits across these two countries.
  • Parliament from Britain: everything about how Parliament works — first past the post, the Speaker, law-making — is British.
  • "Strong Centre" from Canada: quasi-federalism and residuary powers with the Union.
  • Slogan from France: liberty, equality, fraternity in the Preamble.
  • Duties from the USSR: Fundamental Duties were added later, by the 42nd Amendment in 1976.
  • Liberty, equality and fraternity are French. Justice (social, economic and political) is usually attributed to the Soviet constitution.
  • Residuary powers are Canadian. The Concurrent List is Australian. Exams often swap the two.
  • Judicial review is American, but India's Constitution uses "procedure established by law", usually traced to Japan, rather than the US "due process of law".
  • The largest single source in terms of text is the Government of India Act, 1935, not any foreign constitution.

Is the Indian Constitution a borrowed constitution?

NCERT says the framers borrowed provisions from several countries but not in slavish imitation; each provision was defended as suited to Indian problems and aspirations.

Which countries does NCERT list as sources of the Constitution?

NCERT's table names five: the British, Irish, French, United States and Canadian constitutions, each with the specific provisions adapted from it.
