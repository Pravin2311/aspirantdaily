Veto Powers of the President of India: Absolute, Suspensive and Pocket Veto Under Article 111

Article 111 • Article 74 advice • Article 368(2) and the 24th Amendment • State Bills, Articles 200-201

UPSC SSC State PSC 📅 Last updated: 2026-11-01

A Bill passed by both Houses of Parliament becomes law only after the President's assent. Article 111 gives the President exactly three choices when the Bill is presented:

  • Assent to the Bill, which then becomes an Act;
  • Withhold assent, so the Bill does not become law; or
  • Return the Bill (if it is not a Money Bill) with a message asking the Houses to reconsider it or any provision, and suggesting amendments.

The words "absolute veto", "suspensive veto" and "pocket veto" do not appear in the Constitution. They are the names textbooks and exam papers give to the ways these three choices can be used. Knowing which Article clause sits behind each name is the key to most questions.

The proviso to Article 111 lets the President return a Bill "as soon as possible after the presentation". The message may ask the Houses to reconsider the whole Bill or "any specified provisions thereof", and to consider amendments he recommends.

Two limits make this veto only suspensive:

  • Once only. If the Houses pass the Bill again, "with or without amendment", the President "shall not withhold assent". He cannot return it a second time.
  • No special majority. The Houses re-pass the Bill by the same ordinary procedure. There is no requirement of a larger majority to override the President, which is why textbooks say India has no "qualified veto" of the kind used in the United States.

The main clause of Article 111 allows the President to "declare ... that he withholds assent". The Bill then ends there; it does not go back to the Houses.

The President does not use this power on his own judgment. Article 74(1) says there shall be a Council of Ministers "to aid and advise the President who shall, in the exercise of his functions, act in accordance with such advice". This mandatory wording was put into clause (1) by the 42nd Amendment, 1976. The 44th Amendment, 1978, then added a proviso: the President may ask the Council to reconsider its advice once, but "shall act in accordance with the advice tendered after such reconsideration".

So an absolute veto, in practice, is used when the government itself no longer wants the Bill to become law.

Article 111 says the President "shall declare" assent or withholding, but it sets no deadline. The Constitution also gives no time limit for returning a Bill beyond the words "as soon as possible".

If the President neither assents, withholds nor returns, the Bill simply remains pending. This is called the pocket veto. It is wider in India than in the United States, where the Constitution sets a fixed period for the President to act. Exams often ask about this contrast.

The rule for amendment Bills comes from the 24th Amendment, 1971. Article 368(2) now says the Bill "shall be presented to the President who shall give his assent to the Bill and thereupon the Constitution shall stand amended". The footnotes in the official edition show these words were substituted by section 3 of the Twenty-fourth Amendment. Since then, none of the three vetoes applies to an amendment Bill. For the full procedure, see Article 368.

For what makes a Bill a Money Bill, see Money Bill vs Financial Bill.

State Bills add a fourth option. Under Article 200, when a Bill passed by the State Legislature is presented, the Governor may assent, withhold assent, or reserve the Bill for the consideration of the President. Like the President, the Governor may return a non-Money Bill once, and must not withhold assent if it is passed again.

When a Bill is reserved, Article 201 applies:

  • The President declares that he assents or withholds assent.
  • For a non-Money Bill, he may direct the Governor to return it. The Legislature must reconsider it within six months.
  • If the Legislature passes it again, it is "presented again to the President for his consideration". Unlike Article 111, Article 201 does not say the President must then assent.

  • ✘ "The President can return a Money Bill." The proviso to Article 111 excludes Money Bills.
  • ✘ "The President can return a Bill twice." After re-passage he shall not withhold assent.
  • ✘ "The President can veto a constitutional amendment." Since the 24th Amendment, Article 368(2) says he shall give assent.
  • ✘ "Parliament needs a two-thirds majority to override a returned Bill." It simply passes the Bill again.

Q1. Which Bill can the President NOT return to Parliament for reconsideration? easy

✅ Answer: (b) A Money Bill

💡 Explanation: The proviso to Article 111 allows return only 'if it is not a Money Bill'.

Q2. If Parliament passes again a Bill returned by the President, the President: easy

✅ Answer: (c) shall not withhold assent

💡 Explanation: Article 111 proviso: when the Bill is passed again and presented, 'the President shall not withhold assent therefrom'.

Q3. The President is bound to give assent to a constitutional amendment Bill because of: medium

✅ Answer: (b) the 24th Amendment

💡 Explanation: Article 368(2) says the Bill 'shall be presented to the President who shall give his assent'; the footnote shows these words were substituted by the Twenty-fourth Amendment, 1971.

Q4. The 'pocket veto' is possible because Article 111: medium

✅ Answer: (b) fixes no time limit for the President to decide

💡 Explanation: Article 111 says the President 'shall declare' assent or withholding but sets no deadline. Keeping a Bill pending is what textbooks call the pocket veto.

Q5. Which option is available to a Governor but NOT to the President on a Bill passed by its own legislature? hard

✅ Answer: (c) Reserving the Bill for another authority's consideration

💡 Explanation: Article 200 lets the Governor reserve a Bill for the President's consideration. Article 111 gives the President only assent, withholding and return.

Are the words 'absolute', 'suspensive' and 'pocket' veto in the Constitution?

No. Article 111 only says the President may assent, withhold assent or return the Bill. The three names are textbook labels for withholding, returning, and keeping a Bill pending without a decision.

Can the President use the veto against the Cabinet's wishes?

Article 74(1) requires the President to act on the Council of Ministers' advice. He may ask for reconsideration once, but must then follow the advice tendered after reconsideration.
