74th Amendment Explained: Municipalities Under Part IXA, Reservation, Planning Committees and the Twelfth Schedule

Part IXA (243P-243ZG) • Nagar Panchayat, Council, Corporation • Wards Committees • 243T reservation • 243U term • DPC and MPC • Twelfth Schedule

UPSC SSC State PSC 📅 Last updated: 2026-11-06

The Constitution (Seventy-fourth Amendment) Act, 1992 inserted Part IXA, "The Municipalities", made up of Articles 243P to 243ZG, and added the Twelfth Schedule. Both came into force on 1 June 1993. It does for towns and cities what the 73rd Amendment did for villages: it gives urban local bodies a constitutional base, fixed five-year terms, elections run by a State Election Commission, reserved seats and a list of functions that States may devolve to them.

Every State must constitute three kinds of urban body:

  1. a Nagar Panchayat, by whatever name called, for a transitional area moving from rural to urban;
  2. a Municipal Council for a smaller urban area;
  3. a Municipal Corporation for a larger urban area.

The Constitution sets no population thresholds for these categories. Under 243Q(2), the Governor specifies each area by public notification, having regard to population, population density, revenue generated for local administration, the percentage of employment in non-agricultural activities, economic importance and other factors.

Industrial townships. Under the proviso to 243Q(1), no Municipality need be constituted in an area that the Governor notifies as an industrial township, considering its size and the municipal services an industrial establishment provides there.

Direct election (243R(1)). All seats are filled by direct election from territorial constituencies called wards.

Other representation (243R(2)). The State Legislature may by law provide for representation of:

  • persons with special knowledge or experience in municipal administration, who cannot vote in meetings;
  • Lok Sabha members and MLAs whose constituencies cover the municipal area wholly or partly;
  • Rajya Sabha members and MLCs registered as electors in the area;
  • chairpersons of committees constituted under 243S(5).

The State law also fixes the manner of electing the Chairperson of a Municipality.

Wards Committees (243S). These are compulsory in a Municipality with a population of three lakhs or more. A Wards Committee may cover one or more wards. If it covers one ward, the councillor for that ward chairs it. If it covers several, the Committee elects one of those wards' councillors as Chairperson.

Under 243T(5), the SC and ST reservation, and the SC and ST reservation of chairpersons, end when the period in Article 334 ends. Reservation for women is not tied to that period.

  • Five years (243U(1)). A Municipality continues for five years from the date appointed for its first meeting, unless dissolved sooner under law. It must get a reasonable opportunity of being heard before dissolution.
  • No dissolution by amending the law (243U(2)). An amendment to a law cannot cut short a functioning Municipality's term.
  • Six months (243U(3)). Elections must be completed before the term expires, or within six months of a dissolution. If less than six months of the term remain, no election is needed for that remainder.
  • Remainder only (243U(4)). A Municipality elected after an early dissolution serves only the rest of the original term.
  • Age 21 (243V(1)). Disqualifications for State Legislature elections apply, but no one is disqualified for being under 25 if they are 21 or older.
  • State Election Commission (243ZA). Superintendence, direction and control of electoral rolls and elections are vested in the State Election Commission under Article 243K, the same body that conducts Panchayat elections.

Article 243W lets the State Legislature, by law, endow Municipalities with the powers needed to function as institutions of self-government. This includes preparing plans for economic development and social justice and performing functions, including the matters in the Twelfth Schedule. The word is "may": devolution depends on State law.

The Twelfth Schedule lists 18 matters:

  1. Urban planning including town planning
  2. Regulation of land-use and construction of buildings
  3. Planning for economic and social development
  4. Roads and bridges
  5. Water supply for domestic, industrial and commercial purposes
  6. Public health, sanitation conservancy and solid waste management
  7. Fire services
  8. Urban forestry, protection of the environment and ecological aspects
  9. Safeguarding the interests of weaker sections, including persons with disabilities
  10. Slum improvement and upgradation
  11. Urban poverty alleviation
  12. Urban amenities such as parks, gardens and playgrounds
  13. Cultural, educational and aesthetic aspects
  14. Burials, cremations and electric crematoriums
  15. Cattle pounds; prevention of cruelty to animals
  16. Vital statistics including registration of births and deaths
  17. Public amenities including street lighting, parking lots, bus stops and public conveniences
  18. Regulation of slaughter houses and tanneries

Under 243X, the State Legislature may authorise Municipalities to levy taxes, duties, tolls and fees, assign them State-collected taxes, give grants-in-aid and set up funds. Under 243Y, the State Finance Commission constituted under Article 243I also reviews municipal finances and makes recommendations to the Governor, which are laid before the Legislature with an action-taken memorandum.

The four-fifths and two-thirds figures are a common exam pair. The elected seats in both are shared between rural and urban members in proportion to their populations.

Under Article 243ZC, Part IXA does not apply to the Scheduled Areas and tribal areas under Article 244, and it does not affect the Darjeeling Gorkha Hill Council. Parliament may extend it to Scheduled and tribal areas by law, and such a law is not an amendment under Article 368. Under 243ZB, Part IXA applies to Union territories, with the Administrator in place of the Governor.

The village-level counterpart is on the 73rd Amendment. A combined overview is on Panchayati Raj and Municipalities, and all Schedules are on schedules of the Constitution.

  • 74th Amendment: Part IXA (243P to 243ZG) and the Twelfth Schedule, in force 1-6-1993.
  • Three bodies: Nagar Panchayat, Municipal Council, Municipal Corporation, with areas notified by the Governor.
  • Wards Committees: compulsory at a population of three lakhs or more.
  • Women: at least one-third of directly elected seats.
  • Term: five years; elections within six months of dissolution.
  • Twelfth Schedule: 18 matters.
  • Planning committees: District (four-fifths elected), Metropolitan (two-thirds elected; ten lakh population).

Q1. Wards Committees are compulsory in a Municipality with a population of: easy

✅ Answer: (b) Three lakhs or more

💡 Explanation: Article 243S(1) requires Wards Committees where the population is three lakhs or more.

Q2. How many matters are listed in the Twelfth Schedule? easy

✅ Answer: (b) 18

💡 Explanation: The Twelfth Schedule lists 18 matters. The Eleventh Schedule, for Panchayats, lists 29.

Q3. A Metropolitan area under Article 243P has a population of at least: medium

✅ Answer: (c) Ten lakhs

💡 Explanation: Article 243P(c): ten lakhs or more, in one or more districts, notified by the Governor.

Q4. The elected share of a District Planning Committee must be at least: medium

✅ Answer: (d) Four-fifths

💡 Explanation: Article 243ZD(2) requires not less than four-fifths. For a Metropolitan Planning Committee it is two-thirds.

Q5. Who decides whether an area is a 'smaller urban area' for a Municipal Council? medium

✅ Answer: (b) The Governor by public notification

💡 Explanation: Article 243Q(2) lets the Governor specify transitional, smaller and larger urban areas by public notification.

When did the 74th Amendment come into force?

Part IXA and the Twelfth Schedule, inserted by the Constitution (Seventy-fourth Amendment) Act, 1992, came into force on 1 June 1993.

Who conducts municipal elections?

Under Article 243ZA, the State Election Commission referred to in Article 243K, the same body that conducts Panchayat elections in the State.
