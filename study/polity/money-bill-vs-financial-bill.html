Money Bill vs Financial Bill: How Articles 110, 109 and 117 Differ

Article 110 list • 'Only provisions' • Speaker's certificate • Article 109 procedure • Article 117(1) and 117(3) • Joint sitting

UPSC SSC State PSC 📅 Last updated: 2026-10-29

Students often treat "Money Bill" and "Financial Bill" as the same thing. The Constitution does not. It sets up three kinds of Bill that deal with government money, and they differ in where they can start, what the Rajya Sabha can do with them, and whether a deadlock can go to a joint sitting.

  • Money Bill, defined in Article 110 and passed under the special procedure of Article 109.
  • Financial Bill under Article 117(1), often called Category I in textbooks.
  • Financial Bill under Article 117(3), often called Category II.

The labels "Category I" and "Category II" are textbook usage, not words of the Constitution. They are convenient, but in an answer it is safer to name the clause.

Under Article 110(1), a Bill is a Money Bill if it contains only provisions dealing with all or any of these matters:

  • imposing, abolishing, remitting, altering or regulating any tax;
  • regulating the borrowing of money or the giving of guarantees by the Government of India;
  • custody of the Consolidated Fund or the Contingency Fund of India, and payments into or withdrawals from them;
  • appropriating money out of the Consolidated Fund of India;
  • declaring any expenditure to be charged on the Consolidated Fund, or increasing such expenditure;
  • receipt of money on account of the Consolidated Fund or the public account of India, its custody or issue, or the audit of Union or State accounts;
  • any matter incidental to the above.

The word "only" is the key. A Bill that deals with these matters and with something else is not a Money Bill. Clause (2) adds that a Bill does not become a Money Bill merely because it provides for fines or penalties, fees for licences or services, or taxes levied by a local authority for local purposes.

If there is doubt, the Speaker of the Lok Sabha decides, and that decision is final (clause 3). The Speaker endorses a signed certificate on the Bill when it is sent to the Rajya Sabha and again when it goes to the President (clause 4). Whether courts may review that certificate has been argued before the Supreme Court, notably in the Aadhaar judgment of 2018, and in Rojer Mathew (2019) the question was referred to a larger bench.

  • It cannot be introduced in the Rajya Sabha (Article 109(1)), and it needs the President's recommendation to be introduced at all (Article 117(1)).
  • After the Lok Sabha passes it, the Rajya Sabha has 14 days to return it with recommendations.
  • The Lok Sabha may accept or reject any or all of those recommendations. Either way, the Bill is deemed passed by both Houses.
  • If the Rajya Sabha does not return it within 14 days, it is deemed passed in the form the Lok Sabha passed it.
  • There is no joint sitting: Article 108 expressly does not apply to Money Bills, and none is needed because the Rajya Sabha cannot block the Bill.
  • The President may assent or withhold assent but cannot return it for reconsideration; the proviso to Article 111 applies only to Bills that are not Money Bills.

Article 117(1) Bills contain one or more of the Article 110 matters together with other provisions, so they fail the "only" test. Like Money Bills, they need the President's recommendation and can be introduced only in the Lok Sabha. After that, they follow the ordinary procedure. The Rajya Sabha can amend or reject them, and a deadlock can go to a joint sitting. One exception: no recommendation is needed to move an amendment that reduces or abolishes a tax.

Article 117(3) Bills contain none of the Article 110 matters, but passing them would involve spending from the Consolidated Fund of India. They can be introduced in either House without a recommendation, but neither House can pass them until the President has recommended that they be considered. Otherwise they are ordinary Bills, and joint sittings are available.

  • ✘ "Financial Bills can never go to a joint sitting." Only Money Bills are excluded.
  • ✘ "The Rajya Sabha can delay a Money Bill for six months." The six-month rule in Article 108 is for ordinary Bills; a Money Bill has 14 days.
  • ✘ "Any Bill with a fine in it is a Money Bill." Article 110(2) says otherwise.

Q1. Who decides finally whether a Bill is a Money Bill? easy

✅ Answer: (b) The Speaker of the Lok Sabha

💡 Explanation: Article 110(3) makes the Speaker's decision on this question final, and the Speaker endorses a certificate on the Bill.

Q2. Within how many days must the Rajya Sabha return a Money Bill to the Lok Sabha? easy

✅ Answer: (c) 14 days

💡 Explanation: Article 109(2) gives the Rajya Sabha fourteen days. If it does not return the Bill in time, the Bill is deemed passed by both Houses.

Q3. Which of the following can be resolved by a joint sitting of Parliament? medium

✅ Answer: (d) A Financial Bill under Article 117(1)

💡 Explanation: Article 108 excludes only Money Bills. An Article 117(1) Financial Bill follows ordinary procedure after introduction, so a deadlock over it can go to a joint sitting. Amendment Bills follow Article 368, which has no joint sitting.

Q4. A Bill that only imposes a fine for breaking a rule is: medium

✅ Answer: (a) not a Money Bill for that reason alone

💡 Explanation: Article 110(2) says a Bill is not a Money Bill merely because it provides for fines or other pecuniary penalties.

Q5. A Bill that involves spending from the Consolidated Fund but contains no Article 110 matters may be introduced in: hard

✅ Answer: (b) either House

💡 Explanation: This is an Article 117(3) Financial Bill. It can start in either House, but neither House can pass it until the President has recommended that it be considered.

Is every Finance Bill a Money Bill?

Not necessarily. The annual Finance Bill is usually certified as a Money Bill because it deals with taxes, but the label depends on its contents. If it also contains provisions outside the Article 110 list, it is a Financial Bill under Article 117(1), and the Rajya Sabha has full powers over it.

Can the Rajya Sabha reject a Money Bill?

No. It can only make recommendations within fourteen days. The Lok Sabha may accept or reject them, and if the Rajya Sabha does not return the Bill in time, it is deemed passed by both Houses in the form the Lok Sabha passed it.
