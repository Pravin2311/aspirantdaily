Question Hour and Zero Hour in Lok Sabha: Starred, Unstarred and Short Notice Questions, Limits, Supplementaries and Rotation of Ministries

Rule 32 • Notice and form • Starred vs unstarred vs short notice • Daily limits • Ministry rotation • Supplementaries • Zero Hour • Exam traps

UPSC State PSC SSC 📅 Last updated: 2026-11-07

NCERT's Class 11 text Indian Constitution at Work lists Question Hour among Parliament's main tools for controlling the executive. It is held every day during the sessions of Parliament, and in it ministers have to respond to searching questions raised by members. NCERT calls it "perhaps" the most effective method of keeping vigil on the executive and the administrative agencies. Members show great interest in it, and maximum attendance is recorded during this time.

Zero Hour is different. In NCERT's words, in Zero Hour members are free to raise any matter they think is important, though ministers are not bound to reply.

The detailed procedure below comes from the Rules of Procedure and Conduct of Business in Lok Sabha (Sixteenth Edition, 2019) and the Lok Sabha Secretariat's Handbook for Members.

RuleWhat it provides
32Unless the Speaker directs otherwise, the first hour of every sitting is for asking and answering questions
33Notice of a question: not less than fifteen clear days, unless the Speaker directs otherwise
34Notice is given in writing to the Secretary-General. It names the text, the Minister addressed and the date the answer is wanted. A notice signed by several members counts as given by the first signatory only
38Question time is allotted to Ministries in rotation
40A question can even go to a private member, if it relates to a Bill, resolution or other business that member is responsible for

Under the rotation, the Handbook says Ministries are divided into five groups, A to E, which come up on Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday and Friday respectively.

Under Rule 44, the Speaker may admit a starred notice as unstarred if a written reply would be more appropriate. Starred questions beyond the twenty are moved to the unstarred list for that day. A member can give no more than five question notices in all for any day.

The Handbook lists the kinds of question that, if admitted, are normally put down as unstarred. They include questions that:

  • ask for statistical information;
  • go into details, where the reply will obviously be long;
  • raise matters of local interest;
  • concern administrative details such as staff strength;
  • leave no prima facie scope for supplementaries, such as matters under correspondence, diplomatic negotiations or sub judice matters;
  • ask for statements to be laid on the Table; or
  • interest only a limited section of people.

Rule 50(1) lets the member who asked the question, or any other member called by the Speaker, ask a supplementary question to further elucidate a matter of fact on which an answer has been given. The Handbook adds that the first member named on a starred question can ask two supplementaries, while a member whose name is clubbed with it can ask one.

Under Rule 50(2), the Speaker holds a supplementary out of order if it:

  • does not arise from the main question or its answer;
  • gives information instead of seeking it;
  • involves more than one separate issue;
  • seeks confirmation or denial of an opinion; or
  • breaks any of the rules on questions.

Rule 54 covers urgent questions:

  1. A question on a matter of public importance may be asked with shorter notice. If the Speaker thinks it is urgent, the Speaker may have the Minister asked whether and when they can reply.
  2. If the Minister agrees, the question is answered on the day the Minister indicates, immediately after the oral-answer list is disposed of.
  3. If the Minister cannot answer at short notice, but the Speaker thinks the matter important enough, the Speaker may place it as the first question on the list for the day it would be due under Rule 33.

Rule 33 was amended in 2010 to require fifteen clear days for ordinary notice. Rule 54(1), as printed in the 2019 edition, still refers to notice "shorter than ten clear days".

Rule 39 says that if a question on the oral list is not reached within the time available, a written answer is deemed laid on the Table at the end of Question Hour. If Question Hour is dispensed with on a day, the answers for both lists are still deemed laid on the Table and form part of that day's proceedings.

Zero Hour does not appear in the Rules of Procedure (a text search of the 2019 edition finds no such term). The Handbook describes it as the time for "matters of urgent public importance raised after Question Hour" and sets out the practice:

  • Notices may be given in printed form at the Parliamentary Notice Office or online. The window runs from 1000 hours on one day to 0900 hours on the sitting day the member wants to raise the matter.
  • Twenty matters are allowed each day, in their order in the ballot. A few more of national or international importance may be allowed at the Speaker's discretion.
  • The Speaker decides the order in which matters are raised.
  • The matter must be within the Government of India's jurisdiction and must not contain allegations.

The contrast with Question Hour is the point exams test. Question Hour runs on notice, lists and binding answers. Zero Hour runs on a ballot and the Speaker's discretion, and ministers are not bound to reply.

  • Starred means an oral answer and carries an asterisk. Unstarred means a written answer, with no supplementaries.
  • The limits are 20 starred questions a day, one per member, and 230 unstarred.
  • The normal notice is fifteen clear days (Rule 33), not ten.
  • Zero Hour is a practice, not a rule-based device, and it follows Question Hour.
  • Question Hour is the first hour of the sitting, unless the Speaker directs otherwise.

Q1. A member who wants an oral answer to a question in Lok Sabha marks it with: easy

✅ Answer: (a) An asterisk

💡 Explanation: Rule 36 says a member desiring an oral answer distinguishes the question by an asterisk; without it the question goes on the written-answer list.

Q2. How many starred questions can be placed on the list for oral answer on one day? easy

✅ Answer: (b) 20

💡 Explanation: Rule 37(1) allows not more than twenty questions in all on the list for oral answer on any one day, and only one per member.

Q3. Unless the Speaker directs otherwise, how much notice must a member give for an ordinary question? medium

✅ Answer: (c) Not less than fifteen clear days

💡 Explanation: Rule 33 requires not less than fifteen clear days' notice of a question unless the Speaker otherwise directs.

Q4. Which of these can NOT be followed by supplementary questions? medium

✅ Answer: (d) Unstarred questions

💡 Explanation: Unstarred questions are answered in writing and are not called in the House, so no supplementary can be asked on them.

Q5. A question on a matter of urgent public importance asked on shorter notice is a: medium

✅ Answer: (a) Short notice question

💡 Explanation: Rule 54 lets a question on a matter of public importance be asked on shorter notice if the Speaker finds it urgent and the Minister agrees to reply.

Q6. NCERT says that in Zero Hour: easy

✅ Answer: (b) Members may raise any matter they think important, but ministers are not bound to reply

💡 Explanation: NCERT's Legislature chapter says in Zero Hour members are free to raise any important matter though ministers are not bound to reply.

Q7. What is the normal maximum number of questions in the unstarred list for a day? medium

✅ Answer: (c) 230

💡 Explanation: The Members' Handbook, citing Rule 45(2), puts the daily unstarred limit at 230, which may exceed by up to 25 questions on States under President's Rule.

Q8. Under Rule 50(2), a supplementary question is out of order if it: hard

✅ Answer: (d) Gives information instead of seeking it

💡 Explanation: Rule 50(2) lets the Speaker rule out a supplementary that gives information instead of seeking it, seeks confirmation of an opinion, or does not arise from the main question.

Is Zero Hour mentioned in the Rules of Procedure?

It does not appear among the rules on questions. The Lok Sabha Members' Handbook describes Zero Hour as matters of urgent public importance raised after Question Hour and sets out its notice and ballot practice.

What happens to starred questions that are not reached in Question Hour?

Rule 39 says a written answer to a listed oral question not called within the time is deemed laid on the Table at the end of Question Hour.
