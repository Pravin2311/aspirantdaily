Eighth Schedule of the Constitution: The 22 Languages and the Amendments That Added Them

Eighth Schedule • Articles 343, 344(1) and 351 • 21st, 71st, 92nd and 96th Amendments

UPSC SSC Railways State PSC 📅 Last updated: 2026-11-01

The Eighth Schedule is a list headed simply "Languages". Its heading cites Articles 344(1) and 351, the two Articles that use it. Today it has 22 entries.

The list does not make these languages "official languages of India". Article 343(1) says the official language of the Union "shall be Hindi in Devanagari script". The Schedule has narrower roles, explained below, but it is one of the most-asked Schedules in SSC, Railways and State PSC papers.

The official edition's footnotes record each change: Sindhi "Added by the Constitution (Twenty-first Amendment) Act, 1967"; Konkani, Manipuri and Nepali "Ins. by the Constitution (Seventy-first Amendment) Act, 1992"; and Bodo, Dogri, Maithili and Santhali "Ins. by the Constitution (Ninety-second Amendment) Act, 2003", in force from 7 January 2004.

The 96th Amendment, 2011 added no language. It replaced "Oriya" with "Odia".

Memory aid: 1-3-4. One language in 1967, three in 1992, four in 2003. For the 92nd, think B-D-M-S: Bodo, Dogri, Maithili, Santhali.

  • Article 344(1). The President was to constitute an Official Language Commission at the end of five years from the commencement of the Constitution and again at the end of ten years. Its members, besides the Chairman, represent "the different languages specified in the Eighth Schedule".
  • Article 351. The Union must develop Hindi by assimilating forms, style and expressions from "Hindustani and ... the other languages of India specified in the Eighth Schedule", and draw its vocabulary "primarily on Sanskrit and secondarily on other languages".

For the other Schedules, see the 12 Schedules of the Constitution.

  • ✘ "English is in the Eighth Schedule." It is not.
  • ✘ "The Eighth Schedule lists India's official languages." Hindi in Devanagari is the Union's official language under Article 343(1).
  • ✘ "The 96th Amendment added Odia." It only renamed Oriya, which was already in the list.
  • ✘ "Nepali came with the 92nd Amendment." Nepali, Konkani and Manipuri came with the 71st.

Q1. How many languages were in the Eighth Schedule when the Constitution came into force? easy

✅ Answer: (b) 14

💡 Explanation: Of today's 22, eight were added by amendments (1 by the 21st, 3 by the 71st, 4 by the 92nd), leaving 14 original languages.

Q2. Which amendment added Bodo, Dogri, Maithili and Santhali? easy

✅ Answer: (c) 92nd

💡 Explanation: The footnotes show these four were inserted by the Constitution (Ninety-second Amendment) Act, 2003.

Q3. Sindhi was added to the Eighth Schedule by the: medium

✅ Answer: (a) 21st Amendment, 1967

💡 Explanation: The footnote says Sindhi was 'Added by the Constitution (Twenty-first Amendment) Act, 1967'.

Q4. Which of these was NOT added by the 71st Amendment? medium

✅ Answer: (d) Maithili

💡 Explanation: The 71st Amendment (1992) added Konkani, Manipuri and Nepali. Maithili came with the 92nd Amendment (2003).

Q5. The 96th Amendment, 2011 relates to the Eighth Schedule because it: medium

✅ Answer: (b) changed 'Oriya' to 'Odia'

💡 Explanation: The footnote says 'Odia' was substituted for 'Oriya' by the Constitution (Ninety-sixth Amendment) Act, 2011. English is not in the Schedule.

Is English in the Eighth Schedule?

No. The Schedule's 22 entries run from Assamese to Urdu and do not include English. English is used for official purposes under Article 343 and laws made under it, which is a separate question.

Does inclusion make a language an official language of the Union?

No. Article 343(1) makes Hindi in Devanagari script the official language of the Union. The Eighth Schedule matters for Article 344 (representation on the Official Language Commission) and Article 351 (sources for developing Hindi).
