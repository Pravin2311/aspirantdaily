The Governor of a State: Appointment, Powers and Discretion Under the Constitution

Articles 153-158 • Article 163 discretion • Articles 174, 200, 213, 356 • Special responsibility, Articles 371-371H

UPSC SSC State PSC 📅 Last updated: 2026-11-01

Article 153 says "there shall be a Governor for each State". A proviso added in 1956 allows one person to be Governor of two or more States. Article 154(1) vests the executive power of the State in the Governor, to be exercised "either directly or through officers subordinate to him".

The Governor plays two roles at once. As constitutional head of the State, he normally acts on the advice of the State Council of Ministers. As the President's appointee, he is also the link between the Union and the State. Questions on the Governor usually test where these roles overlap, and above all, when the Governor may act without ministerial advice.

The Governor is appointed, not elected. Because tenure is "during the pleasure of the President", a Governor can be removed at any time, and the Constitution lays down no special removal procedure.

Article 163(1) says the Council of Ministers headed by the Chief Minister will "aid and advise the Governor in the exercise of his functions, except in so far as he is by or under this Constitution required to exercise his functions or any of them in his discretion".

Compare Article 74 for the President, which has no such exception. That single phrase is why the Governor has more independent room than the President.

Article 163(2) adds a strong protection: if a question arises whether a matter is within the Governor's discretion, "the decision of the Governor in his discretion shall be final", and nothing he does can be challenged on the ground that he ought or ought not to have acted in his discretion.

Some of these entries, such as reserving Bills and reporting under Article 356, are read as discretionary because the text asks for the Governor's own opinion or report. Exams usually list them together as "constitutional discretion".

Textbooks also describe situational discretion: decisions that arise from political circumstances rather than from any express words. The usual examples are:

  • choosing a Chief Minister when no party or alliance has a clear majority in the Assembly;
  • dismissing a Council of Ministers that has lost the confidence of the Assembly; and
  • deciding whether to dissolve the Assembly when the ministry has lost its majority.

The Constitution's text supports these only indirectly. Article 164(1) says the Governor appoints the Chief Minister and that Ministers hold office "during the pleasure of the Governor", and Article 174(2)(b) gives the power to dissolve the Assembly. How far the Governor may act without advice in these situations has been shaped by court rulings and conventions, which this page does not cover.

  • ✘ "The Governor is elected by the State Legislature." He is appointed by the President under Article 155.
  • ✘ "The Governor has a guaranteed five-year term." It is subject to the President's pleasure.
  • ✘ "The Governor's discretion is the same as the President's." Article 163 has an express discretion clause; Article 74 does not.
  • ✘ "Only the Nagaland Governor has special responsibility." Arunachal Pradesh, Sikkim, and by Presidential order Maharashtra, Gujarat and others also have such provisions.

Q1. The Governor of a State holds office: easy

✅ Answer: (b) during the pleasure of the President

💡 Explanation: Article 156(1) says the Governor holds office during the pleasure of the President. The five-year term in 156(3) is 'subject to' that.

Q2. Who finally decides whether a matter falls within the Governor's discretion? medium

✅ Answer: (c) The Governor, in his discretion

💡 Explanation: Article 163(2): the decision of the Governor in his discretion shall be final on that question.

Q3. The Governor must reserve a Bill for the President's consideration if it would: medium

✅ Answer: (b) endanger the position of the High Court

💡 Explanation: The second proviso to Article 200 makes reservation compulsory for a Bill that would so derogate from the High Court's powers as to endanger its position.

Q4. Which Governor has special responsibility for law and order under the Constitution? easy

✅ Answer: (b) Nagaland

💡 Explanation: Article 371A(1)(b) gives the Governor of Nagaland special responsibility for law and order. Article 371H(a) does the same for Arunachal Pradesh.

Q5. Minimum age for appointment as Governor is: easy

✅ Answer: (c) 35 years

💡 Explanation: Article 157: a citizen of India who has completed 35 years of age.

Is the Governor a nominal head like the President?

Mostly yes: Article 163(1) makes the Council of Ministers aid and advise the Governor. But unlike Article 74 for the President, Article 163 carves out matters where the Governor is required to act in his discretion, so the Governor has more independent room than the President.

Can the Governor be removed before five years?

Yes. Under Article 156(1) the Governor holds office during the pleasure of the President, and the five-year term in clause (3) is subject to that. The Constitution lays down no impeachment-style procedure for a Governor.
