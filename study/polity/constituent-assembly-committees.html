Constituent Assembly: Composition, Committees and Their Chairmen, and Key Dates

12 committees and chairmen • Drafting Committee and its members • How the Assembly was formed • Prasad, Nehru, Patel, Ambedkar, Rau • 1946-1950 dates

UPSC SSC State PSC 📅 Last updated: 2026-11-06

The Constituent Assembly framed India's Constitution between December 1946 and November 1949. It held eleven sessions, with sittings spread over 165 days. Between sessions, committees and sub-committees revised and refined the drafts. The Assembly adopted the Constitution on 26 November 1949, and it came into effect on 26 January 1950.

The committees most asked about in exams are the Drafting Committee (B. R. Ambedkar), the Union Powers and Union Constitution Committees (Jawaharlal Nehru), the Provincial Constitution and Advisory Committees (Vallabhbhai Patel) and the Rules of Procedure and Steering Committees (Rajendra Prasad).

Memory hook by chairman

  • Nehru (3): Union Powers, Union Constitution, States.
  • Patel (2): Provincial Constitution, Advisory.
  • Rajendra Prasad (2): Rules of Procedure, Steering.
  • Kripalani (2): Flag, Fundamental Rights Sub-Committee.
  • One each: Ambedkar (Drafting), Varadachariar (Supreme Court), Mookerjee (Minorities).

Exam trap: NCERT's table also lists a Constitution Review Commission under M. N. Venkatachaliah. That body was not a committee of the Constituent Assembly, so do not count it among them.

  • Members were not elected by universal franchise. Provincial elections were held in the winter of 1945-46, and the Provincial Legislatures then chose the representatives.
  • The Congress swept the general seats. The Muslim League won most reserved Muslim seats but boycotted the Assembly, pressing its demand for Pakistan.
  • The Socialists were also unwilling at first, seeing the Assembly as a British creation.
  • As a result, 82 per cent of the members also belonged to the Congress. The Congress was not one voice, though: some members were socialists and others defended landlordism.
  • NCERT puts the Assembly's strength at 300 members.

Ambedkar had been a political opponent of the Congress under British rule. On Mahatma Gandhi's advice he was asked to join the Union Cabinet as law minister at Independence. Guiding the draft through the Assembly took three years, and the printed debates fill eleven bulky volumes.

The committees did more than draft text: they brokered compromises on issues that split the Assembly. Language was the hardest. In one of the earliest sessions, R. V. Dhulekar, a Congressman from the United Provinces, demanded that Hindi be used for constitution-making, and the House broke up in commotion until Nehru intervened.

By September 1949, the Language Committee had produced its report with a compromise formula:

  • Hindi in the Devanagari script would be the official language, but the transition to Hindi would be gradual.
  • For the first fifteen years, English would continue for all official purposes.
  • Each province could choose one of the regional languages for official work within the province.

When Dhulekar spoke on 13 September 1949, his demand that Hindi be declared the national language, not just the official language, again set off a storm.

Debate outside the committees

NCERT also records dissent on the Assembly itself. The Communist member Somnath Lahiri argued that the Assembly was British-made and was "working the British plans". In the winter of 1946-47, the interim administration headed by Nehru could operate only under the directions of the Viceroy and London.

On 13 December 1946, Nehru introduced the Objectives Resolution. NCERT calls it a momentous resolution that outlined the defining ideals of the Constitution of independent India and gave the framework within which the work of constitution-making was to proceed.

The Preamble records the Assembly's resolve to constitute India into a sovereign socialist secular democratic republic and to secure justice, liberty, equality and fraternity for all citizens.

  • Drafting Committee: B. R. Ambedkar, with K. M. Munshi and Alladi Krishnaswamy Aiyar among its members.
  • President of the Assembly: Rajendra Prasad. Constitutional Advisor: B. N. Rau.
  • Eleven sessions over 165 days, December 1946 to November 1949.
  • Adopted 26 November 1949; in force 26 January 1950.

Who were the members of the Drafting Committee named by NCERT?

NCERT names B. R. Ambedkar as Chairman, with two other lawyers, K. M. Munshi from Gujarat and Alladi Krishnaswamy Aiyar from Madras, who gave crucial inputs.

When was the Constitution adopted and when did it come into force?

The Preamble records adoption on 26 November 1949, and NCERT notes that the Constitution came into effect on 26 January 1950.
