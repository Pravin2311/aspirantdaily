Article 371 to 371J: Special Provisions for 12 States, Article by Article, with the Amendments That Added Them

371 Maharashtra and Gujarat • 371A Nagaland • 371B Assam • 371C Manipur • 371D-E Andhra Pradesh and Telangana • 371F Sikkim • 371G Mizoram • 371H Arunachal • 371-I Goa • 371J Karnataka

UPSC SSC State PSC 📅 Last updated: 2026-11-04

Articles 371 to 371J sit in Part XXI of the Constitution, "Temporary, Transitional and Special Provisions". Each gives particular States special arrangements: development boards for backward regions, protection of tribal customary law, minimum Assembly sizes, or special responsibilities for the Governor. Exams test which Article belongs to which State and which Amendment inserted it, so the table below is the core of the topic.

The Article after 371H is written "371-I" in the official text, with a hyphen, so it cannot be misread as 371l or 3711.

Under Article 371(2), the President may by order give the Governor of Maharashtra or Gujarat "special responsibility" for three things:

  • setting up separate development boards for the named regions, with a report on each board placed before the State Assembly every year;
  • equitable allocation of development funds over those areas, subject to the needs of the State as a whole;
  • equitable arrangements for technical education, vocational training and employment in State services.

Article 371J applies the same model to Karnataka. It provides a separate development board for the Hyderabad-Karnataka region, equitable allocation of funds, and equitable opportunities in public employment, education and vocational training for people of that region. It also allows seats and posts to be reserved for people who belong to the region by birth or by domicile.

Articles 371A and 371G are the strongest of these provisions. Notwithstanding anything else in the Constitution, no Act of Parliament on the following four subjects applies to Nagaland or Mizoram unless the State Assembly decides by resolution that it should:

  1. religious or social practices of the Nagas (or Mizos);
  2. their customary law and procedure;
  3. the administration of civil and criminal justice involving decisions according to customary law;
  4. ownership and transfer of land (for Nagaland, "land and its resources").

Article 371A also gives the Governor of Nagaland special responsibility for law and order, a point covered on the Governor's powers and discretion. For Mizoram, the protection does not cover Central Acts already in force in the Union territory of Mizoram before the 53rd Amendment.

  • Assam (371B) and Manipur (371C): the President may provide for a committee of the State Assembly made up of members elected from the tribal areas (Assam) or the Hill Areas (Manipur). The rules of business and procedure can be modified so that the committee works properly.
  • Sikkim, Arunachal Pradesh and Goa: Articles 371F, 371H and 371-I each say the Legislative Assembly shall have "not less than thirty members". Article 170 normally requires at least sixty, so these are exceptions for small States.
  • Andhra Pradesh and Telangana (371D): the President may provide for equitable opportunities in public employment and education for people of different parts of each State.

Other special arrangements for tribal areas are in the Fifth and Sixth Schedules. See Fifth and Sixth Schedules and the Schedules of the Constitution.

Q1. Article 371A contains special provisions for: easy

✅ Answer: (b) Nagaland

💡 Explanation: 371A is Nagaland, 371B Assam, 371C Manipur and 371G Mizoram.

Q2. The development board for the Hyderabad-Karnataka region is provided by: easy

✅ Answer: (c) Article 371J

💡 Explanation: Article 371J, inserted by the 98th Amendment (2012), deals with Karnataka. Article 371 covers Maharashtra and Gujarat.

Q3. Which States have a constitutional minimum of thirty members in their Legislative Assembly under Article 371 series? medium

✅ Answer: (a) Sikkim, Arunachal Pradesh and Goa

💡 Explanation: Articles 371F (Sikkim), 371H (Arunachal Pradesh) and 371-I (Goa) each say 'not less than thirty members'. Article 170 normally requires sixty.

Q4. Under Article 371G, which subject is NOT protected from Acts of Parliament unless the Mizoram Assembly agrees? medium

✅ Answer: (d) Inter-State trade

💡 Explanation: 371G protects religious or social practices, customary law and procedure, justice by customary law, and ownership and transfer of land. Trade is not among them.

Q5. Article 371E provides for: medium

✅ Answer: (b) A University in Andhra Pradesh

💡 Explanation: Article 371E, 'Establishment of Central University in Andhra Pradesh', lets Parliament by law provide for a University in the State. It was added with 371D by the 32nd Amendment.

In which Part of the Constitution are Articles 371 to 371J?

Part XXI, 'Temporary, Transitional and Special Provisions', which runs from Article 369 to Article 392.

Is Article 371 the same as Article 370?

No. Article 370 dealt with Jammu and Kashmir. Articles 371 to 371J are separate provisions for twelve other States and are all still part of the text.
