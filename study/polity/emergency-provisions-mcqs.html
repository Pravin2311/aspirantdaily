Emergency Provisions MCQs: 20 Questions on Articles 352 to 360 With Article-Wise Answers

Article 352 grounds and approval • 358 vs 359 • 353 to 355 • 356 three-year limit • 357 • 360 salaries and Money Bills

These 20 original questions test Part XVIII of the Constitution, Articles 352 to 360. Each answer names the Article and clause it comes from, so you can check the text yourself. Try all 20 before opening the answers, then revise any Article you got wrong using the linked guides.

The questions are grouped by the three kinds of emergency, followed by the provisions on the effects of an emergency on Fundamental Rights. About a third are numerical (time limits, majorities, maximum durations) because those are what most exam questions on this Part turn on.

The majority for approving President's Rule and a Financial Emergency is not set out as a special majority in Articles 356 and 360, so it is read as an ordinary majority. "Until revoked" for a Financial Emergency follows from Article 360(2), which, unlike Article 356, sets no further period once the Proclamation is approved.

  1. Armed rebellion vs internal disturbance. Since the 44th Amendment (1978), the third ground for a National Emergency is "armed rebellion". "Internal disturbance" is no longer a ground under Article 352, but it still appears in Article 355 as a danger the Union must protect States against.
  2. Article 358 vs Article 359. Article 19 is suspended automatically only when the emergency is declared on the ground of war or external aggression. Under Article 359, the President's order can suspend enforcement of other rights, but never Articles 20 and 21.
  3. Who decides. A National Emergency can be proclaimed only after the Union Cabinet's decision is communicated to the President in writing. A Lok Sabha resolution of disapproval forces revocation.

Q1. Which is NOT a ground for a National Emergency under Article 352 today? easy

✅ Answer: (a) Internal disturbance

💡 Explanation: Since the 44th Amendment (1978), the grounds are war, external aggression and armed rebellion. 'Internal disturbance' was replaced.

Q2. The President can proclaim a National Emergency only after: easy

✅ Answer: (b) The Union Cabinet's decision is communicated in writing

💡 Explanation: Article 352(3) requires the decision of the Union Cabinet to be communicated to the President in writing.

Q3. A National Emergency must be approved by both Houses within: easy

✅ Answer: (c) One month

💡 Explanation: Article 352(4): it ceases after one month unless approved by resolutions of both Houses.

Q4. The majority needed in each House to approve a National Emergency is: medium

✅ Answer: (d) Majority of total membership and two-thirds of those present and voting

💡 Explanation: Article 352(6) requires this special majority in each House of Parliament.

Q5. Once approved, a National Emergency remains in force for: medium

✅ Answer: (a) Six months, renewable by fresh approval

💡 Explanation: Article 352(5): six months from the second approving resolution, extendable by further six-month approvals.

Q6. A special sitting of the Lok Sabha to consider disapproving an emergency can be demanded by notice of at least: medium

✅ Answer: (b) One-tenth of members

💡 Explanation: Article 352(8): notice in writing signed by not less than one-tenth of the total members of the House of the People.

Q7. A National Emergency may be proclaimed before actual war if: medium

✅ Answer: (c) The President is satisfied there is imminent danger

💡 Explanation: The Explanation to Article 352(1) allows a Proclamation if there is imminent danger of war, aggression or armed rebellion.

Q8. A Proclamation under Article 352 can apply to: easy

✅ Answer: (d) The whole of India or any part of it

💡 Explanation: Article 352(1) covers the whole of India or such part of its territory as specified.

Q9. Article 19 is automatically suspended under Article 358 when the emergency is declared on the ground of: hard

✅ Answer: (a) War or external aggression

💡 Explanation: Article 358 applies only where security is threatened by war or external aggression, not armed rebellion.

Q10. Under Article 359, the President cannot suspend enforcement of: easy

✅ Answer: (b) Articles 20 and 21

💡 Explanation: Article 359(1), as amended by the 44th Amendment, excludes Articles 20 and 21.

Q11. During a National Emergency, the Union's executive power extends to giving directions to States under: medium

✅ Answer: (c) Article 353

💡 Explanation: Article 353(a) extends the Union's executive power to directions on how a State exercises its executive power.

Q12. The Union's duty to protect States against external aggression and internal disturbance is in: medium

✅ Answer: (d) Article 355

💡 Explanation: Article 355 states this duty. 'Internal disturbance' still appears here though it is no longer a 352 ground.

Q13. President's Rule must be approved by Parliament within: easy

✅ Answer: (a) Two months

💡 Explanation: Article 356(3): the Proclamation ceases after two months unless approved by both Houses.

Q14. The maximum period for President's Rule under Article 356 is: easy

✅ Answer: (b) Three years

💡 Explanation: The first proviso to Article 356(4): no such Proclamation shall in any case remain in force for more than three years.

Q15. Under Article 356, the President may NOT assume: medium

✅ Answer: (c) The powers of the High Court

💡 Explanation: The proviso to Article 356(1) bars assuming any power vested in or exercisable by a High Court.

Q16. When a State is under President's Rule, Parliament may confer the State Legislature's law-making power on the President under: medium

✅ Answer: (d) Article 357

💡 Explanation: Article 357(1)(a) lets Parliament confer on the President the power of the State Legislature to make laws.

Q17. A Financial Emergency must be approved by Parliament within: easy

✅ Answer: (a) Two months

💡 Explanation: Article 360(2)(c): it ceases after two months unless approved by both Houses.

Q18. During a Financial Emergency, salary reductions can extend to: medium

✅ Answer: (b) Judges of the Supreme Court and High Courts as well

💡 Explanation: Article 360(4)(b) covers all persons serving the Union, including Supreme Court and High Court Judges.

Q19. During a Financial Emergency, State Money Bills may be required to be: hard

✅ Answer: (c) Reserved for the President's consideration after passage

💡 Explanation: Article 360(4)(a)(ii) allows a direction that Money Bills be reserved for the President after the State Legislature passes them.

Q20. Distribution of revenues between Union and States can be modified by the President during a National Emergency under: hard

✅ Answer: (d) Article 354

💡 Explanation: Article 354 lets the President modify Articles 268 to 279 by order during a National Emergency; the order is laid before Parliament.

How many times has the ground for National Emergency changed?

Once. The 44th Amendment (1978) replaced 'internal disturbance' with 'armed rebellion' in Article 352(1), in force from 20 June 1979.

Which emergency has no maximum duration?

A National Emergency can be continued by six-monthly approvals without a maximum, and a Financial Emergency continues until revoked once approved; President's Rule is capped at three years.
