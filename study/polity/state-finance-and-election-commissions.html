State Finance Commission (Article 243I) and State Election Commission (Article 243K): Who Appoints, What They Do, How They Compare

Article 243I and 243Y • Article 243K and 243ZA • Governor appoints • Every fifth year • Removal like a High Court Judge • Link to Article 280 • Comparison tables

UPSC State PSC SSC 📅 Last updated: 2026-11-07

The 73rd (Panchayats) and 74th (Municipalities) Amendments gave every State two constitutional bodies to make local self-government work:

  • a State Finance Commission (SFC) to fix how money flows to local bodies;
  • a State Election Commission (SEC) to conduct their elections.

The Constitution's footnotes record that the 73rd Amendment took effect from 24 April 1993. Both bodies are appointed by the Governor, not the President, which is the most common exam trap.

Who and when: the Governor must constitute a Finance Commission within one year of the 73rd Amendment's commencement, and then at the expiration of every fifth year. The State Legislature decides by law its composition, members' qualifications and how they are selected.

What it recommends: it reviews the finances of the Panchayats and advises the Governor on:

  1. Principles for:
    • dividing the net proceeds of State taxes, duties, tolls and fees between the State and the Panchayats, and among Panchayats at all levels;
    • which taxes, duties, tolls and fees may be assigned to or appropriated by Panchayats;
    • grants-in-aid to Panchayats from the State's Consolidated Fund.
  2. Measures needed to improve the Panchayats' financial position.
  3. Any other matter the Governor refers in the interest of sound Panchayat finance.

Accountability: the Governor lays every recommendation before the State Legislature, together with an explanatory memorandum on the action taken.

Municipalities too (Article 243Y): the 74th Amendment did not create a second commission. The same Finance Commission under 243I also reviews Municipal finances on the same three heads.

The Union Finance Commission is constituted by the President every fifth year under Article 280. The 73rd and 74th Amendments added two clauses that tie it to the SFCs:

  • Article 280(3)(bb): measures to augment a State's Consolidated Fund to supplement Panchayat resources;
  • Article 280(3)(c): the same for Municipalities.

In both cases the measures are based on the recommendations of the State's own Finance Commission. That makes the SFC report the starting point for central support to local bodies.

  • Mandate: the superintendence, direction and control of electoral rolls and of all Panchayat elections lie with a State Election Commission. It consists of a State Election Commissioner appointed by the Governor (Art. 243K(1)). Article 243ZA adds all Municipal elections.
  • Tenure: conditions of service and tenure are set by the Governor's rules, subject to any State law.
  • Safeguards: the State Election Commissioner can be removed only in like manner and on the like grounds as a High Court Judge. His service conditions cannot be varied to his disadvantage after appointment.
  • Staff: the Governor must provide staff when the SEC asks for it.
  • Law: the State Legislature may legislate on all matters relating to Panchayat elections.

Two related rules matter for SEC questions:

  • Timing (Article 243E): a Panchayat lasts five years from its first meeting. A fresh election must be completed before that term ends or, if the Panchayat is dissolved early, within six months of dissolution.
  • Disputes (Article 243O): a Panchayat election can be questioned only by an election petition.

Article by article, here is how a single cycle runs:

  1. Constitution of the SFC: the Governor sets up the State Finance Commission, under a State law that fixes its members and their qualifications (Art. 243I(1)-(2)).
  2. Review: the Commission examines the finances of the Panchayats and, under Article 243Y, of the Municipalities. It settles its own procedure and uses whatever powers the State Legislature gives it (Art. 243I(3)).
  3. Report: its recommendations go to the Governor, covering the sharing of State taxes, the taxes assigned to local bodies and grants-in-aid.
  4. Accountability: the Governor places the report before the State Legislature, along with an explanatory memorandum on the action taken.
  5. Central link: the Union Finance Commission uses the SFC recommendations when it suggests how to augment the State's Consolidated Fund for local bodies (Art. 280(3)(bb) and (c)). Its own report goes to each House of Parliament, also with an action-taken memorandum (Art. 281).

Running alongside, the State Election Commission keeps the local bodies elected:

  • it conducts each Panchayat election before the five-year term ends;
  • if a Panchayat is dissolved early, it holds the election within six months;
  • it handles Municipal elections in the same way.

Disputes over those elections go only through election petitions under State law, not through ordinary suits.

  • The Governor, not the President, constitutes the SFC and appoints the State Election Commissioner. Removal, though, is not at the Governor's pleasure: it follows the High Court Judge procedure.
  • There is no separate "Municipal Finance Commission". The 243I commission covers both Panchayats and Municipalities.
  • The SEC is independent of the ECI. The ECI does not conduct local body elections.
  • Article 280(3)(bb) and (c) link the Union Finance Commission to SFC recommendations, a frequent statement-type question.

Q1. Which Article provides for the State Finance Commission? easy

✅ Answer: (a) Article 243I

💡 Explanation: Article 243I requires the Governor to constitute a Finance Commission every fifth year to review Panchayat finances.

Q2. Who constitutes the State Finance Commission? easy

✅ Answer: (b) The Governor

💡 Explanation: Article 243I(1) says the Governor of a State shall constitute the Finance Commission within one year of the 73rd Amendment and every fifth year.

Q3. The State Election Commissioner can be removed only: medium

✅ Answer: (c) Like a High Court Judge

💡 Explanation: The proviso to Article 243K(2) says the State Election Commissioner can be removed only in the manner and on the grounds of a High Court Judge.

Q4. Which Article vests Municipal elections in the State Election Commission? medium

✅ Answer: (d) Article 243ZA

💡 Explanation: Article 243ZA(1) vests Municipal elections in the State Election Commission referred to in Article 243K.

Q5. Under Article 280(3)(bb), the Union Finance Commission's measures for Panchayats are based on recommendations of the: hard

✅ Answer: (a) State Finance Commission

💡 Explanation: Article 280(3)(bb) ties the Union Finance Commission's Panchayat measures to the State Finance Commission's recommendations.

Q6. If a Panchayat is dissolved early, elections must be completed within: medium

✅ Answer: (b) Six months

💡 Explanation: Article 243E(3)(b) requires an election before six months expire from the date of dissolution, unless under six months of term remain.

Q7. Which body reviews the finances of Municipalities? medium

✅ Answer: (c) The same State Finance Commission under 243I

💡 Explanation: Article 243Y says the Finance Commission constituted under Article 243I shall also review the financial position of Municipalities.

Q8. Who decides the composition and qualifications of State Finance Commission members? hard

✅ Answer: (d) The State Legislature, by law

💡 Explanation: Article 243I(2) lets the State Legislature provide by law for composition, qualifications and manner of selection.

Is the State Election Commission under the Election Commission of India?

No. Article 243K creates a separate State Election Commission appointed by the Governor; it conducts Panchayat and Municipal elections independently of the ECI.

How often is a State Finance Commission set up?

Article 243I requires the Governor to constitute one within a year of the 73rd Amendment's commencement and then at the expiration of every fifth year.
