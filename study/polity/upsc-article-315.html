UPSC and State Public Service Commissions Under Articles 315 to 323: Appointment, Removal, Functions and Post-Office Bar

Article 315 Joint Commissions • 316 term 65/62 • 317 removal • 318 regulations • 319 post-office bar • 320 functions • 322 Consolidated Fund • 323 reports

UPSC SSC State PSC 📅 Last updated: 2026-11-06

The Union Public Service Commission is a constitutional body. Chapter II of Part XIV, Articles 315 to 323, sets out who appoints its members, how long they serve, how they can be removed, what they must do and what they may never do after leaving office. The same Articles cover State Public Service Commissions and Joint Commissions, so most exam questions are about the differences between the three.

Article 315(1) says there shall be a Public Service Commission for the Union and one for each State. Two further arrangements are possible:

  • Joint State Public Service Commission (315(2)). Two or more States may agree to share a Commission. Each State's Legislature, or each House where there are two, must pass a resolution. Parliament may then provide for the Joint Commission by law. The States ask, but only Parliament can create it.
  • UPSC serving a State (315(4)). If the Governor of a State requests it, the UPSC may, with the President's approval, agree to serve all or any of that State's needs.

Further rules in Article 316:

  • Experience rule: as nearly as may be, one-half of the members of every Commission must have held office for at least ten years under the Government of India or of a State.
  • Acting Chairman (316(1A)): if the Chairman's office is vacant or the Chairman cannot act, one of the other members appointed by the President (Union or Joint) or the Governor (State) performs the duties. This clause was added by the 15th Amendment (1963).
  • State age limit: the 41st Amendment (1976) raised the State and Joint Commission age limit from sixty to sixty-two.
  • No re-appointment (316(3)): on the expiry of the term, a member is ineligible for re-appointment to that office.

The Constitution fixes no number of members. Under Article 318, the President (Union or Joint) or the Governor (State) decides the number of members and their conditions of service by regulations. Conditions of service cannot be varied to a member's disadvantage after appointment.

The Governor appoints State PSC members but cannot remove them. Article 317 gives removal to the President alone, by two routes.

Route 1: misbehaviour (317(1)). The President refers the case to the Supreme Court. The Court holds an inquiry under the procedure prescribed under Article 145. If it reports that the member ought to be removed, the President may remove the member by order. While the reference is pending, the President (Union or Joint) or the Governor (State) may suspend the member under 317(2).

Under 317(4), a member who is concerned or interested in any contract or agreement made by or on behalf of the Government of India or a State, or shares in its profit, is deemed guilty of misbehaviour. Holding shares as an ordinary member of an incorporated company, in common with other members, is excluded.

Route 2: direct removal (317(3)). The President may remove a member by order, without a Supreme Court inquiry, if the member:

  1. (a) is adjudged an insolvent;
  2. (b) engages in any paid employment outside the duties of the office during the term; or
  3. (c) is, in the President's opinion, unfit to continue because of infirmity of mind or body.

Under Article 320(1), the UPSC and State PSCs must conduct examinations for appointments to the Union and State services respectively. Under 320(2), if two or more States request it, the UPSC must help them frame and operate schemes of joint recruitment for services needing special qualifications.

Article 320(3) lists matters on which a Commission "shall be consulted":

  1. methods of recruitment to civil services and posts;
  2. principles for appointments, promotions and transfers, and the suitability of candidates;
  3. disciplinary matters affecting civil servants, including memorials and petitions;
  4. claims for legal costs incurred by a civil servant in defending proceedings over official acts;
  5. claims for a pension for injuries sustained while in civil service.

The proviso lets the President or the Governor make regulations listing matters on which consultation is not necessary. Those regulations must be laid before the legislature for at least fourteen days, and the legislature may modify them (320(5)). Under 320(4), a Commission need not be consulted on how reservations under Article 16(4) are made, or how effect is given to Article 335.

Article 321 lets Parliament or a State Legislature give a Commission additional functions by an Act, including for local authorities and public institutions.

Expenses (322): the Commission's expenses, including members' and staff salaries, allowances and pensions, are charged on the Consolidated Fund of India or of the State. Charged expenditure is not put to the vote of the legislature, which protects the Commission's independence.

Reports (323): the UPSC presents an annual report to the President. The President lays it before each House of Parliament with a memorandum explaining the reasons for not accepting the Commission's advice in any case. State PSCs report to the Governor, who lays the report before the State Legislature with a similar memorandum. The advice is therefore not binding, but rejecting it has to be explained publicly.

Other constitutional bodies are compared on constitutional and statutory bodies. All-India services, which the UPSC recruits for, are covered under Article 312 on special powers of the Rajya Sabha.

  • 315: UPSC, State PSCs and Joint Commissions (created by Parliament on State resolutions).
  • 316: six years or 65 (UPSC) / 62 (State and Joint); no re-appointment.
  • 317: only the President removes, after a Supreme Court inquiry for misbehaviour, or directly for insolvency, outside paid work or infirmity.
  • 319: UPSC Chairman cannot take any further government job.
  • 320: conducts examinations; must be consulted on recruitment, promotions, discipline and certain claims.

Q1. Who can remove a member of a State Public Service Commission? medium

✅ Answer: (c) The President

💡 Explanation: Article 317 gives removal to the President alone, even though the Governor appoints State PSC members.

Q2. A member of the UPSC holds office for six years or until the age of: easy

✅ Answer: (c) 65

💡 Explanation: Article 316(2): 65 for the Union Commission; 62 for State and Joint Commissions, whichever is earlier with six years.

Q3. After ceasing to hold office, the Chairman of the UPSC: medium

✅ Answer: (c) Is ineligible for any further employment under the Union or a State

💡 Explanation: Article 319(a) bars all further employment under the Government of India or a State.

Q4. A Joint State Public Service Commission is created by: medium

✅ Answer: (b) Parliament by law on resolutions of the States

💡 Explanation: Article 315(2): the States' Legislatures pass resolutions and Parliament provides for the Joint Commission by law.

Q5. The expenses of the UPSC are: easy

✅ Answer: (b) Charged on the Consolidated Fund of India

💡 Explanation: Article 322 charges them on the Consolidated Fund of India, so they are not submitted to the vote of Parliament.

Is the advice of the UPSC binding on the government?

The Constitution does not make it binding, but Article 323 requires reasons for any non-acceptance to be laid before Parliament with the annual report.

How many members does the UPSC have?

The Constitution fixes no number. Article 318 lets the President decide the number of members and their conditions of service by regulations.
