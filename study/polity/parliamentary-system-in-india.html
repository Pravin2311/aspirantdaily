Parliamentary System of Government in India: Features, Why It Was Chosen, Articles 74 and 75 and Collective Responsibility

Three types of executive • Why India chose it • Articles 74-75 • 15% cap • Collective responsibility • President vs PM • State level • Exam traps

UPSC SSC State PSC Railways 📅 Last updated: 2026-11-07

NCERT's Class 11 text Indian Constitution at Work (Chapter 4, "Executive") describes the executive as the branch of government responsible for implementing the laws and policies adopted by the legislature. Countries organise that branch in three main ways: presidential, parliamentary and semi-presidential.

In the parliamentary model, NCERT says, the executive and the legislature are interdependent. The legislature controls the executive and is in turn controlled by it. The chapter's key line is that the most important feature of a parliamentary executive is that it is routinely under the control and supervision of the legislature.

NCERT adds some country details:

  • Canada: a parliamentary democracy with a constitutional monarchy.
  • Japan: the Emperor is head of state.
  • Germany: the chancellor is head of government and the president is ceremonial.
  • France: the president appoints the prime minister and ministers but cannot dismiss them, because they are responsible to parliament.
  • Russia: the president appoints the prime minister.

NCERT gives three reasons:

  1. Experience: India had already run a parliamentary system under the Acts of 1919 and 1935. That experience showed that the executive can be effectively controlled by the representatives of the people.
  2. Accountability: the framers wanted a government sensitive to public expectations, and responsible and accountable.
  3. Avoiding a personality cult: a presidential executive puts heavy emphasis on the president as the source of all executive power, and there is always a danger of personality cult. The framers wanted a strong executive with safeguards against this.

The Constitution therefore adopted the parliamentary executive for both the national and State governments.

NCERT also records a dissenting view from the Constituent Assembly. Begum Aizaz Rasul argued for the Swiss system, in which the legislature elects the executive.

NCERT explains why the 15 per cent cap was needed. Before the 91st Amendment, Councils of Ministers grew very large. When no party had a clear majority, there was a temptation to win over MPs with ministerial posts.

Because the Council of Ministers is collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha, a ministry that loses the Lok Sabha's confidence must resign. NCERT draws out what this principle means:

  • The ministry is an executive committee of Parliament that collectively governs on its behalf.
  • It rests on the solidarity of the cabinet. A vote of no confidence even against a single minister leads to the resignation of the entire Council of Ministers.
  • A minister who disagrees with a cabinet decision must either accept it or resign.

The President is the formal head with wide executive, legislative, judicial and emergency powers. In a parliamentary system these are used only on the advice of the Council of Ministers.

NCERT explains why a President is still needed. The Council of Ministers depends on a majority and may be removed at any time. That calls for a head of state with a fixed term who can appoint the Prime Minister and represent the whole country. When no party has a clear majority, the President must also choose whom to appoint as Prime Minister.

The Prime Minister must have the support of the majority in the Lok Sabha, and loses office the moment that support is lost. The PM picks the ministers, allocates ranks and portfolios, and links the Council with the President and Parliament. Nehru called the Prime Minister "the linchpin of Government".

The Council of Ministers exists only once the Prime Minister has taken the oath. The PM's death or resignation dissolves the whole Council, whereas the exit of any other minister only creates a vacancy. Since 1989, NCERT notes, there have been many occasions when no party had a majority, and coalition Prime Ministers have had to act more as negotiators.

States follow the same parliamentary model. The Chief Minister, like the Prime Minister, leads the majority in the Assembly. The main variation NCERT notes is that the Governor is appointed by the President on the central government's advice, and has more discretionary powers than the President.

  • The Council of Ministers is collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha only, not to the Rajya Sabha (Art. 75(3) says "House of the People").
  • The 15 per cent cap counts the Prime Minister and is measured against the Lok Sabha's total membership.
  • France, Russia and Sri Lanka are NCERT's semi-presidential examples. Germany, with its chancellor, is parliamentary.
  • The binding-advice wording in Art. 74(1) came with the 42nd Amendment (1976). The reconsideration proviso came with the 44th (1978).

Q1. The Council of Ministers is collectively responsible to: easy

✅ Answer: (a) The Lok Sabha

💡 Explanation: Article 75(3) says the Council of Ministers shall be collectively responsible to the House of the People.

Q2. Which Amendment capped the Council of Ministers at 15 per cent of the Lok Sabha? medium

✅ Answer: (b) 91st Amendment, 2003

💡 Explanation: Article 75(1A), inserted by the 91st Amendment (2003), caps ministers including the PM at 15 per cent of the Lok Sabha.

Q3. Which country does NCERT list as having a semi-presidential system? medium

✅ Answer: (c) France

💡 Explanation: NCERT lists France, Russia and Sri Lanka as semi-presidential; Germany, Japan and Italy are parliamentary.

Q4. A minister who is not an MP must become one within how long or cease to be a minister? easy

✅ Answer: (d) Six consecutive months

💡 Explanation: Article 75(5) says a minister not in either House for six consecutive months ceases to be a minister.

Q5. The proviso letting the President send advice back once for reconsideration was added by the: hard

✅ Answer: (a) 44th Amendment

💡 Explanation: The footnote to Article 74 says the reconsideration proviso was inserted by the 44th Amendment Act, 1978.

Q6. One reason NCERT gives for India rejecting the presidential system was the danger of: medium

✅ Answer: (b) A personality cult

💡 Explanation: NCERT says the presidential executive carries a danger of personality cult, which the framers wanted to guard against.

Q7. Under collective responsibility, a no-confidence vote against one minister leads to: medium

✅ Answer: (c) Resignation of the entire Council

💡 Explanation: NCERT says a vote of no confidence even against a single minister leads to the resignation of the entire Council of Ministers.

Q8. Who described the Prime Minister as 'the linchpin of Government'? easy

✅ Answer: (d) Jawaharlal Nehru

💡 Explanation: NCERT says the PM's role as a link between the Council, the President and Parliament led Nehru to call the office the linchpin of Government.

Why did India choose a parliamentary rather than a presidential system?

NCERT gives three reasons: experience under the 1919 and 1935 Acts, the wish for an accountable government, and the danger of a personality cult in a presidential executive.

What is the difference between parliamentary and semi-presidential systems?

In a parliamentary system the president or monarch is a nominal head; in a semi-presidential system the president may hold significant day-to-day powers alongside the prime minister.
