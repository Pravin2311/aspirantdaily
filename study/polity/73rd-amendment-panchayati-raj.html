73rd Amendment and Part IX: Compulsory and Voluntary Features of Panchayati Raj

Constitution (73rd Amendment) Act, 1992 • Articles 243-243O • Eleventh Schedule • Article 280(3)(bb)

UPSC SSC Railways State PSC 📅 Last updated: 2026-11-01

Village panchayats existed long before 1992, but they depended entirely on State laws. Elections could be postponed for years and powers could be taken away at will. The Constitution (Seventy-third Amendment) Act, 1992 changed this by inserting Part IX, "The Panchayats" (Articles 243 to 243O) and the Eleventh Schedule. Both came into force on 24 April 1993.

Part IX gives Panchayats constitutional status. Some rules bind every State; others are left to State Legislatures. Exams test exactly this split, so this page sorts every feature into compulsory (the Constitution says "shall") and voluntary (it says the State "may").

The voluntary list explains why Panchayats are strong in some States and weak in others: the Constitution guarantees their existence and elections, but how much real power they get is still up to each State.

Article 243(b) defines the Gram Sabha as "a body consisting of persons registered in the electoral rolls relating to a village" within a village-level Panchayat area. In simple terms, it is all the adult voters of the village. It is not elected; every voter is a member.

Above it sit the three elected tiers:

  • Village level (often called Gram Panchayat);
  • Intermediate level (often called Panchayat Samiti or Block Panchayat), which the Governor specifies by notification and which may be skipped in a small State;
  • District level (often called Zila Parishad).

The Constitution does not use the names Gram Panchayat, Panchayat Samiti or Zila Parishad. Article 243(d) says a Panchayat is an institution of self-government "by whatever name called", so each State chooses its own names.

  • SCs and STs: seats in each Panchayat in proportion to their share of the area's population (243D(1)). Of these, at least one-third go to SC or ST women (243D(2)).
  • Women: at least one-third of all directly elected seats, counting the SC/ST women's seats (243D(3)).
  • Chairpersons: SC/ST chairperson offices at each level in proportion to their population in the State, and at least one-third of chairperson offices for women (243D(4)).
  • Rotation: reserved seats and offices rotate among constituencies and Panchayats.
  • Backward classes: optional, at the State's choice (243D(6)).

Article 243M(3A), added by the 83rd Amendment, says the SC reservation in Article 243D does not apply in Arunachal Pradesh.

Article 243M(1)-(2) excludes:

  • the States of Nagaland, Meghalaya and Mizoram;
  • the hill areas of Manipur that have District Councils;
  • Scheduled Areas under the Fifth Schedule; and
  • tribal areas under the Sixth Schedule.

Parliament may extend Part IX to Scheduled Areas and tribal areas by law, with modifications, under Article 243M(4)(b). For these areas, see Fifth and Sixth Schedules.

Under Article 243I, the Governor constitutes a State Finance Commission every five years to review Panchayat finances and recommend how State taxes should be shared with them. The same Amendment inserted clause (bb) into Article 280(3), so the Union Finance Commission also recommends measures to supplement Panchayat resources, based on the State Finance Commission's work.

For municipalities and the 74th Amendment, see Panchayati Raj and Municipalities.

Two further Articles protect the new system. Article 243O bars courts from questioning laws on delimitation of Panchayat constituencies or allotment of seats, and says a Panchayat election can be challenged only by an election petition before the authority set by State law. Article 243N gave States one year from the Amendment's commencement to bring old Panchayat laws into line with Part IX; inconsistent provisions lapsed after that unless amended or repealed earlier.

  • ✘ "The Election Commission of India conducts Panchayat elections." A State Election Commission does (243K).
  • ✘ "OBC reservation in Panchayats is compulsory." It is optional (243D(6)).
  • ✘ "The Gram Sabha is an elected body." It is all registered voters of the village.
  • ✘ "Every State must have three tiers." States with 20 lakh or fewer people may skip the intermediate tier.

Q1. Panchayats at the intermediate level need not be constituted in a State with a population not exceeding: easy

✅ Answer: (b) 20 lakh

💡 Explanation: Article 243B(2): a State with population not exceeding twenty lakhs may skip the intermediate level.

Q2. The minimum age to contest a Panchayat election is: easy

✅ Answer: (b) 21

💡 Explanation: Article 243F(1)(a) proviso: a person is not disqualified for being under 25 if he has attained 21.

Q3. Which of these is a VOLUNTARY provision of Part IX? medium

✅ Answer: (c) Reservation for backward classes

💡 Explanation: Article 243D(6) only says nothing prevents a State from reserving seats for backward classes. The other three are mandatory.

Q4. How many subjects are listed in the Eleventh Schedule? easy

✅ Answer: (c) 29

💡 Explanation: The Eleventh Schedule runs from 1 (Agriculture) to 29 (Maintenance of community assets). The Twelfth, for Municipalities, has 18.

Q5. After a Panchayat is dissolved early, elections must be completed within: medium

✅ Answer: (b) six months

💡 Explanation: Article 243E(3)(b): within six months of dissolution, unless less than six months of the term remained.

Does the 73rd Amendment apply everywhere in India?

No. Article 243M excludes Nagaland, Meghalaya and Mizoram, the hill areas of Manipur with District Councils, Fifth Schedule Scheduled Areas and Sixth Schedule tribal areas. Parliament may extend it to Scheduled Areas by law.

Who conducts Panchayat elections: the Election Commission of India?

No. Article 243K vests them in a State Election Commission, headed by a State Election Commissioner appointed by the Governor.
