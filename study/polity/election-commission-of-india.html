Election Commission of India: Article 324, Appointment Under the 2023 Act, Term, Removal and Functions

Article 324 clause by clause • Search and Selection Committees • Six years or 65 • Removal rules • Functions • Articles 325-329 • State Election Commissions

UPSC SSC Railways State PSC 📅 Last updated: 2026-11-07

Part XV of the Constitution (Articles 324 to 329) deals with elections. Article 324 sets up the Election Commission of India (ECI) and gives it the "superintendence, direction and control" of electoral rolls and of the conduct of elections to:

  • Parliament;
  • the Legislature of every State;
  • the offices of President and Vice-President.

The original text also covered election tribunals for disputes. The Nineteenth Amendment (1966) omitted those words, and election disputes now go to the courts through election petitions.

ClauseWhat it provides
324(1)Superintendence, direction and control of rolls and elections vested in the Election Commission
324(2)The Chief Election Commissioner (CEC) plus such number of other Election Commissioners (ECs), if any, as the President fixes. Appointment is by the President, subject to any law made by Parliament
324(3)When other ECs are appointed, the CEC acts as Chairman
324(4)Before general elections the President may appoint Regional Commissioners after consulting the Commission
324(5)Service conditions are set by Parliament's law or presidential rules. Removal safeguards apply (see below)
324(6)The President or a Governor must provide staff when the Commission asks

Article 324 allows either a single-member or a multi-member body. NCERT's Class 11 text (Indian Constitution at Work, Chapter 3) traces the history:

  • Till 1989 the Commission was single member.
  • Just before the 1989 general elections, two Election Commissioners were appointed. Soon after the elections the Commission went back to a single member.
  • In 1993 two Election Commissioners were appointed again, and the Commission has stayed multi-member since.

There was early friction over who held how much power, and the matter had to be settled by the Supreme Court. NCERT is clear on the result: the CEC presides over the Commission but does not have more powers than the other Commissioners. The three have equal powers to take all decisions as a collective body. The 2023 Act writes this into law: business should be transacted unanimously as far as possible, and any difference is decided by the majority opinion.

Article 324(2) left the appointment process "subject to the provisions of any law made in that behalf by Parliament". That law is the Chief Election Commissioner and other Election Commissioners (Appointment, Conditions of Service and Term of Office) Act, 2023 (Act 49 of 2023). It received the President's assent on 28 December 2023 and repealed the Election Commission Act of 1991.

  1. Eligibility: persons who hold or have held a post equivalent to Secretary to the Government of India, who have integrity, and who know about and have experience in managing and conducting elections.
  2. Search Committee: headed by the Minister of Law and Justice, with two members not below Secretary rank. It prepares a panel of five names.
  3. Selection Committee: recommends the appointment. Its members are:
    • the Prime Minister, as Chairperson;
    • the Leader of Opposition in the Lok Sabha;
    • a Union Cabinet Minister nominated by the Prime Minister.
    If no Leader of Opposition is recognised, the leader of the single largest opposition party counts as one. The committee may also consider names outside the panel.
  4. Appointment: the President appoints by warrant under his hand and seal.

A vacancy or defect in the Selection Committee does not make an appointment invalid.

NCERT notes a long-standing worry: if the government of the day alone picks Commissioners, a ruling party could appoint someone partisan. Many people had suggested that consulting the Leader of Opposition and the Chief Justice of India be made necessary.

Exam-guide context, not from the sources cited here: in March 2023 the Supreme Court, in the Anoop Baranwal case, directed that until Parliament made a law, appointments be made on the advice of a committee of the Prime Minister, the Leader of Opposition and the Chief Justice of India. The 2023 Act replaced the Chief Justice with a Union Cabinet Minister nominated by the Prime Minister, which drew criticism over independence.

Removing the CEC "like a Supreme Court Judge" means Parliament must pass the address with a special majority in both Houses. NCERT explains why: so that a ruling party cannot remove a CEC who refuses to favour it. The Act also bars any civil or criminal case against a current or former CEC or EC for acts done in their official duty.

NCERT lists the main functions:

  • Voters' list: supervising the preparation of an up-to-date, error-free voters' list.
  • Schedule: it decides the timing of elections and prepares the schedule. This runs from notification and nominations through scrutiny and withdrawal to polling, counting and results.
  • Free and fair poll: it can postpone or cancel the election in the whole country, a specific State or a constituency, enforce the model code of conduct for parties and candidates, order a re-poll in a constituency and order a recount.
  • Parties: it recognises political parties and allots them symbols.

The Commission has very limited staff of its own. A Chief Electoral Officer in every State assists it, and it relies on government officers. During the election process those officers are under its control: the EC can transfer them, stop their transfers, and act against anyone who is partisan.

ArticleProvision
325One general electoral roll for every territorial constituency; no one excluded only on grounds of religion, race, caste or sex
326Lok Sabha and Assembly elections on adult suffrage. The voting age is 18, substituted for 21 by the 61st Amendment (1988), effective 28 March 1989
327Parliament may legislate on elections, including electoral rolls and delimitation of constituencies
328A State Legislature may legislate on its own elections where Parliament has not
329Courts cannot question delimitation laws made under Articles 327 or 328. An election can be challenged only by an election petition

The ECI does not conduct local body elections. Under Article 243K a separate State Election Commission handles Panchayat elections, and Article 243ZA gives it Municipal elections too. Its State Election Commissioner is appointed by the Governor and can be removed only like a High Court Judge. NCERT stresses that State Election Commissioners work independently of the ECI.

  • The Constitution fixes neither the number of Election Commissioners nor their term. The President fixes the number (Art. 324(2)), and the term comes from the 2023 Act.
  • Only the CEC has Supreme Court Judge-style removal protection. Other ECs can be removed on the CEC's recommendation.
  • The Selection Committee under the 2023 Act does not include the Chief Justice of India.
  • The ECI conducts the President and Vice-President elections but not Panchayat or Municipal ones.
  • No reappointment is allowed, and an EC promoted to CEC gets six years in total.

Q1. Which Article of the Constitution provides for the Election Commission? easy

✅ Answer: (a) Article 324

💡 Explanation: Article 324(1) vests superintendence, direction and control of elections in a Commission called the Election Commission.

Q2. Under the 2023 Act, who chairs the Selection Committee for the CEC and Election Commissioners? easy

✅ Answer: (b) The Prime Minister

💡 Explanation: Section 7(1) names the Prime Minister as Chairperson, with the Leader of Opposition and a nominated Union Cabinet Minister as members.

Q3. Who heads the Search Committee that prepares the panel of five names? medium

✅ Answer: (c) Minister of Law and Justice

💡 Explanation: Section 6 says a Search Committee headed by the Minister of Law and Justice, with two Secretary-rank members, prepares a panel of five persons.

Q4. The term of the CEC under the 2023 Act is: easy

✅ Answer: (d) Six years or till age 65, whichever is earlier

💡 Explanation: Section 9(1) fixes a term of six years from assuming office or until the age of sixty-five years, whichever is earlier.

Q5. How can an Election Commissioner other than the CEC be removed? medium

✅ Answer: (a) Only on the recommendation of the CEC

💡 Explanation: Article 324(5) and Section 11(3) both say other Election Commissioners can be removed only on the recommendation of the CEC.

Q6. The voting age was lowered from 21 to 18 by which Constitutional Amendment? medium

✅ Answer: (b) 61st Amendment

💡 Explanation: A footnote to Article 326 records that the Sixty-first Amendment Act, 1988 substituted eighteen years for twenty-one years.

Q7. Who conducts elections to Panchayats and Municipalities? medium

✅ Answer: (c) State Election Commission

💡 Explanation: Articles 243K and 243ZA vest Panchayat and Municipal elections in the State Election Commission appointed by the Governor.

Q8. If the CEC and Election Commissioners differ on a matter, it is decided by: hard

✅ Answer: (d) Majority opinion

💡 Explanation: Section 18(2) of the 2023 Act says business should be unanimous where possible, otherwise decided by the majority opinion.

How is the Chief Election Commissioner appointed now?

Under the 2023 Act the President appoints the CEC on the recommendation of a Selection Committee of the Prime Minister, the Leader of Opposition and a Cabinet Minister.

Does the CEC have more power than other Election Commissioners?

No. NCERT says the CEC presides but the three members have equal powers as a collective body, and the 2023 Act decides disagreements by majority.
