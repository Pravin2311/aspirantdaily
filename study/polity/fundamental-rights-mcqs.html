Fundamental Rights MCQ: 30 Questions on Articles 12 to 35, Each Explained with the Article Text

Definition of State • Equality 14-18 • Freedoms 19-22 • Exploitation 23-24 • Religion 25-28 • Minorities 29-30 • Property • Writs 32 • Articles 33-35 • Emergency

UPSC SSC State PSC Railway 📅 Last updated: 2026-11-04

These 30 questions cover Part III of the Constitution, Articles 12 to 35, plus the Emergency rule in Article 359 that limits them. Every question was written for this page, and every explanation names the Article and says what its text actually provides, so a wrong answer tells you exactly which provision to reread. Give yourself about 40 seconds a question. Prelims papers often test the clause, not just the Article number, so read the explanation even when you get one right.

If you want to revise first, the full notes are on Fundamental Rights, DPSP and Duties. The six freedoms have their own page on Article 19, the criminal-law protections are on Article 20, and the writs are on Articles 32 and 226.

The Constitution groups Articles 12 to 35 under headings. Learning which Article sits under which heading answers about a third of the questions on its own.

GroupArticlesWhat they cover
General12, 13Definition of "the State"; laws inconsistent with fundamental rights are void
Right to Equality14 to 18Equality before law, no discrimination, equal opportunity in public jobs, untouchability abolished, titles abolished
Right to Freedom19 to 22Six freedoms, protection in conviction, life and personal liberty, education, arrest and detention
Right against Exploitation23, 24Trafficking and forced labour; child labour below 14
Right to Freedom of Religion25 to 28Conscience and practice, denominations, religious taxes, religious instruction
Cultural and Educational Rights29, 30Language, script and culture; minority educational institutions
Right to Constitutional Remedies32Moving the Supreme Court; the five writs
Limits and enforcement33 to 35Armed forces, martial law, Parliament's exclusive power to legislate

Article 31, the old right to property, was omitted by the 44th Amendment in 1978, so the "Right to Property" heading no longer exists. Articles 31A to 31C remain under "Saving of Certain Laws".

  • Person or citizen. Articles 14, 20, 21 and 25 say "any person", so they protect foreigners too. Articles 15, 16 and 19 say "citizen".
  • Article 15 or 16. Descent and residence are grounds only in Article 16(2), the public employment clause. Article 15(1) lists religion, race, caste, sex and place of birth.
  • The 24-hour rule. Article 22(2) excludes the time taken to travel to the magistrate. Clause (3) keeps enemy aliens and preventive detainees outside it.
  • Emergency. Articles 20 and 21 cannot be suspended under Article 359. Article 19 is suspended automatically by Article 358 during an Emergency declared on grounds of war or external aggression.
  • Article 35. Only Parliament can make the laws it lists. State Legislatures do not have that power.

Questions 1 to 2 are on the general Articles, 3 to 7 on equality, 8 to 14 on freedom, 15 and 16 on exploitation, 17 to 20 on religion, 21 and 22 on minorities, 23 and 24 on property, 25 and 26 on writs, and 27 to 30 on Articles 33 to 35 and the Emergency.

Q1. For the purposes of Part III, which Article defines 'the State'? easy

✅ Answer: (a) Article 12

💡 Explanation: Article 12 defines 'the State' to include the Government and Parliament of India, the Government and Legislature of each State, and all local or other authorities. Article 36 applies the same definition to Part IV.

Q2. Which Article makes laws inconsistent with fundamental rights void to the extent of the inconsistency? easy

✅ Answer: (b) Article 13

💡 Explanation: Article 13 is headed 'Laws inconsistent with or in derogation of the fundamental rights'. It is the textual basis for judicial review of laws under Part III.

Q3. 'Equality before the law' and 'equal protection of the laws' are both guaranteed by: easy

✅ Answer: (c) Article 14

💡 Explanation: Article 14 contains both phrases and applies to 'any person', so it protects foreigners too, not only citizens.

Q4. Which ground is NOT listed in Article 15(1)? medium

✅ Answer: (d) Descent

💡 Explanation: Article 15(1) lists religion, race, caste, sex and place of birth. Descent and residence appear in Article 16(2), which deals with public employment.

Q5. Article 16 deals with: easy

✅ Answer: (a) Equality of opportunity in public employment

💡 Explanation: Article 16 is headed 'Equality of opportunity in matters of public employment'. Clause (4) allows reservation for backward classes not adequately represented in State services.

Q6. Which Article abolishes 'Untouchability' and makes its practice an offence? easy

✅ Answer: (b) Article 17

💡 Explanation: Article 17 abolishes untouchability, forbids its practice in any form and makes enforcing any disability arising from it an offence punishable by law.

Q7. Under Article 18, the State may confer which kind of title? medium

✅ Answer: (c) A military or academic distinction

💡 Explanation: Article 18(1) bars titles, 'not being a military or academic distinction'. Those two kinds are the only exception.

Q8. How many freedoms does Article 19(1) now guarantee? easy

✅ Answer: (d) Six

💡 Explanation: Clauses (a) to (e) and (g) remain, which makes six. Clause (f), the right to property, was omitted by the 44th Amendment.

Q9. The right to 'reside and settle in any part of the territory of India' is in: medium

✅ Answer: (a) Article 19(1)(e)

💡 Explanation: Clause (d) is moving freely throughout India; clause (e) is residing and settling; clause (g) is practising any profession or carrying on any occupation, trade or business.

Q10. Which Amendment added 'co-operative societies' to Article 19(1)(c)? hard

✅ Answer: (b) 97th

💡 Explanation: Article 19(1)(c) now reads 'to form associations or unions or co-operative societies'; the words in brackets were added by the 97th Amendment (2011), which also inserted Part IXB.

Q11. Protection against double jeopardy is found in: medium

✅ Answer: (c) Article 20(2)

💡 Explanation: Article 20(2) says no person shall be prosecuted and punished for the same offence more than once. Clause (1) bars ex post facto criminal laws and clause (3) bars self-incrimination.

Q12. 'No person accused of any offence shall be compelled to be a witness against himself.' This is: easy

✅ Answer: (d) Article 20(3)

💡 Explanation: Article 20(3) is the protection against self-incrimination. It applies only to a person accused of an offence.

Q13. Article 21A guarantees free and compulsory education to children aged: easy

✅ Answer: (a) 6 to 14 years

💡 Explanation: Article 21A covers children 'of the age of six to fourteen years', in the manner the State determines by law. It was inserted by the 86th Amendment (2002).

Q14. Under Article 22(2), an arrested person must be produced before the nearest magistrate within: medium

✅ Answer: (b) 24 hours, excluding travel time

💡 Explanation: Article 22(2) gives twenty-four hours, excluding the time needed for the journey from the place of arrest to the magistrate's court. Clause (3) excludes enemy aliens and preventive detainees from this protection.

Q15. Traffic in human beings, begar and other forced labour are prohibited by: easy

✅ Answer: (c) Article 23

💡 Explanation: Article 23 is headed 'Prohibition of traffic in human beings and forced labour'. Contravening it is an offence punishable by law.

Q16. Article 24 bars employing children below what age in factories, mines or hazardous work? easy

✅ Answer: (d) 14 years

💡 Explanation: Article 24 says no child below the age of fourteen years shall work in any factory or mine or any other hazardous employment.

Q17. Freedom of conscience and the right freely to profess, practise and propagate religion is in: easy

✅ Answer: (a) Article 25

💡 Explanation: Article 25 gives this freedom to all persons, subject to public order, morality, health and the other provisions of Part III.

Q18. The freedom of religious denominations to manage their own religious affairs is under: medium

✅ Answer: (b) Article 26

💡 Explanation: Article 26 lets every religious denomination establish institutions, manage its own affairs in matters of religion, and own and administer property in accordance with law.

Q19. No person can be compelled to pay taxes whose proceeds promote a particular religion. Which Article says so? medium

✅ Answer: (c) Article 27

💡 Explanation: Article 27 is 'Freedom as to payment of taxes for promotion of any particular religion'. Article 265 is the general rule that taxes need the authority of law.

Q20. Article 28(1) bars religious instruction in an educational institution that is: hard

✅ Answer: (d) Wholly maintained out of State funds

💡 Explanation: Only institutions wholly maintained out of State funds are covered by the bar. Under Article 28(3), a State-recognised or aided institution cannot force anyone to attend religious instruction without consent.

Q21. A section of citizens with a distinct language, script or culture has the right to conserve it under: medium

✅ Answer: (a) Article 29(1)

💡 Explanation: Article 29(1) protects any section of citizens with a distinct language, script or culture of its own. It is not limited to minorities.

Q22. Which Article gives minorities based on religion or language the right to establish and administer educational institutions of their choice? easy

✅ Answer: (b) Article 30

💡 Explanation: Article 30(1) gives this right to all minorities, whether based on religion or language. Article 30(2) bars the State from discriminating against them when giving aid.

Q23. The right to property was removed from Part III by which Amendment? medium

✅ Answer: (c) 44th

💡 Explanation: The 44th Amendment (1978) omitted Article 31 and Article 19(1)(f). Property is now protected by Article 300A, which is a constitutional right but not a fundamental right.

Q24. After the 44th Amendment, where is the right to property found? easy

✅ Answer: (d) Article 300A

💡 Explanation: Article 300A in Part XII says no person shall be deprived of his property save by authority of law.

Q25. Which writ is NOT named in Article 32(2)? easy

✅ Answer: (a) Injunction

💡 Explanation: Article 32(2) names habeas corpus, mandamus, prohibition, quo warranto and certiorari. An injunction is a civil court remedy, not one of the five writs.

Q26. Who can be empowered by law to issue writs for fundamental rights, besides the Supreme Court, under Article 32(3)? hard

✅ Answer: (b) Any other court, by law of Parliament

💡 Explanation: Article 32(3) lets Parliament by law empower any other court to exercise the Supreme Court's writ powers within its local limits. High Courts already have their own writ power under Article 226.

Q27. Under Article 33, Parliament may restrict or abrogate fundamental rights in their application to: medium

✅ Answer: (c) Members of the Armed Forces

💡 Explanation: Article 33 covers the Armed Forces, forces charged with maintaining public order, intelligence bodies and telecommunication staff of such forces, to ensure discipline and the proper discharge of duties.

Q28. Restrictions on fundamental rights while martial law is in force in an area are dealt with in: hard

✅ Answer: (d) Article 34

💡 Explanation: Article 34 lets Parliament indemnify officers for acts done to maintain or restore order in an area under martial law. Article 359 deals with suspension during a proclaimed Emergency.

Q29. Under Article 35, the power to make laws on the matters it lists (for example, punishments for offences under Part III) belongs to: hard

✅ Answer: (a) Parliament only

💡 Explanation: Article 35 vests this power in Parliament and says the Legislature of a State shall not have it, so the punishment for an offence such as practising untouchability is uniform across India.

Q30. During a proclamation of Emergency, the President cannot suspend the enforcement of which Articles? medium

✅ Answer: (b) Articles 20 and 21

💡 Explanation: Article 359, as amended by the 44th Amendment, excludes Articles 20 and 21 from any order suspending enforcement of fundamental rights.

Which fundamental rights are available to non-citizens too?

Rights worded for 'any person', such as Articles 14, 20, 21, 21A, 22, 23, 25 and 27, apply to foreigners as well. Articles 15, 16, 19, 29 and 30 are limited to citizens (Article 30 to minorities).

Which fundamental rights survive an Emergency?

Articles 20 and 21. Article 359 lets the President suspend the enforcement of other rights during an Emergency, but since the 44th Amendment it cannot be applied to these two.
