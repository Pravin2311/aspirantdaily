Pardoning Power of the President and the Governor: Article 72 vs Article 161

Article 72 (President) • Article 161 (Governor) • Article 74 advice • Pardon, commutation, remission, respite, reprieve

UPSC SSC State PSC 📅 Last updated: 2026-11-01

Courts decide guilt and punishment. The executive keeps a separate power to show mercy after conviction. The Constitution gives this power to the President in Article 72 and to the Governor in Article 161.

Both Articles use the same list of powers: to "grant pardons, reprieves, respites or remissions of punishment or to suspend, remit or commute the sentence of any person convicted of any offence". The difference lies in which cases each authority can act in. Exam questions test the five forms of clemency and the three differences in scope.

The Constitution names these powers but does not define them. The meanings below are the standard ones used in textbooks and exam answers.

FormMeaningExample
PardonRemoves both the sentence and the conviction; the person is freed from all punishmentA convict is released and the conviction is wiped out
CommutationSubstitutes a lighter form of punishmentDeath to life imprisonment; rigorous to simple imprisonment
RemissionReduces the length of the sentence without changing its natureTwo years' imprisonment reduced to one year
RespiteAwards a lesser sentence because of a special fact about the convictPregnancy or physical disability of the convict
ReprieveTemporarily stays the execution of a sentence, especially a death sentenceExecution stayed while a mercy petition is decided

A memory aid: pardon wipes out, commutation changes the type, remission cuts the length, respite softens for a special reason, reprieve pauses.

Article 72(1) gives the President power in three kinds of case:

  • Court Martial. "In all cases where the punishment or sentence is by a Court Martial."
  • Matters of Union executive power. "In all cases where the punishment or sentence is for an offence against any law relating to a matter to which the executive power of the Union extends." Under Article 73(1)(a), Union executive power extends to matters on which Parliament can make laws.
  • Death sentences. "In all cases where the sentence is a sentence of death", whatever law the offence was under.

Two savings clauses follow. Article 72(2) keeps the power that a law gives to Armed Forces officers to suspend, remit or commute a Court Martial sentence. Article 72(3) keeps the Governor's power to "suspend, remit or commute a sentence of death".

Article 161 is shorter. The Governor has the same list of powers for "any offence against any law relating to a matter to which the executive power of the State extends".

There is no Court Martial clause and no separate death-sentence clause. The Governor's power is tied entirely to State executive power.

So the question to ask in any Governor case is simple: does the law the person was convicted under deal with a matter to which the State's executive power extends? If yes, Article 161 applies. If the matter lies only within Union executive power, the Governor has no role, and the petition must go to the President under Article 72, unless the sentence is death, where both may be approached.

PointPresident (Art. 72)Governor (Art. 161)
Forms of clemencyAll fiveAll five
Offences on matters within Union executive powerYesNo
Offences on matters within State executive powerOnly if the sentence is deathYes
Court MartialYesNo
Death sentenceCan pardon, commute, remit, suspendArticle 72(3) preserves power to suspend, remit or commute; textbooks say the Governor cannot pardon a death sentence
Acts onAdvice of the Union Council of Ministers (Art. 74)Advice of the State Council of Ministers

The last row matters. Under Article 74(1), the President "shall, in the exercise of his functions, act in accordance with" the advice of the Council of Ministers. Mercy petitions are therefore decided on the government's advice, not by the President alone.

  • A soldier sentenced by a Court Martial petitions for mercy. Only the President can act under Article 72(1)(a). Under Article 72(2), an Armed Forces officer empowered by law may also suspend, remit or commute.
  • A person sentenced to death for murder files mercy petitions. The President can act under Article 72(1)(c). The Governor can act under Article 161, and Article 72(3) confirms the Governor's power to suspend, remit or commute the death sentence.
  • A person convicted under a State excise law and sentenced to imprisonment. This is within State executive power, so the Governor acts under Article 161. The President has no power, because the sentence is not death and the matter is not within Union executive power.
  • A person convicted under a law on a Union List subject and sentenced to imprisonment. That matter is within Union executive power, so the President acts under Article 72(1)(b).
  • ✘ "The Governor can pardon a Court Martial sentence." Article 161 has no Court Martial clause.
  • ✘ "Only the President can deal with death sentences." Article 72(3) keeps the Governor's power to suspend, remit or commute them.
  • ✘ "Respite and reprieve mean the same thing." A reprieve pauses execution; a respite awards a lesser sentence for a special reason.
  • ✘ "The President decides mercy petitions personally." Article 74(1) requires him to act on the Council of Ministers' advice.

For the President's other powers, see veto powers, ordinances and Union and State executive.

Q1. Who can pardon a sentence passed by a Court Martial? easy

✅ Answer: (b) The President

💡 Explanation: Article 72(1)(a) gives the President power in all Court Martial cases. Article 161 has no such clause, so the Governor has none.

Q2. The Governor's pardoning power under Article 161 extends to offences against laws on matters to which: easy

✅ Answer: (b) the executive power of the State extends

💡 Explanation: Article 161 is limited to 'any law relating to a matter to which the executive power of the State extends'.

Q3. Article 72(3) preserves which power of the Governor regarding death sentences? medium

✅ Answer: (b) To suspend, remit or commute

💡 Explanation: Article 72(3) says clause (1)(c) does not affect the power 'to suspend, remit or commute a sentence of death exercisable by the Governor'.

Q4. Reducing a sentence of rigorous imprisonment to simple imprisonment is an example of: medium

✅ Answer: (b) commutation

💡 Explanation: Commutation substitutes a lighter form of punishment. The sentence remains, but in a lighter form.

Q5. In exercising the pardoning power, the President: medium

✅ Answer: (b) acts on the advice of the Council of Ministers

💡 Explanation: Article 74(1) requires the President to act in accordance with the Council of Ministers' advice in exercising his functions, including under Article 72.

Can both the President and the Governor act in the same case?

Their fields overlap where a law falls under both Union and State executive power, and for death sentences Article 72(3) expressly keeps the Governor's power to suspend, remit or commute. A petitioner can therefore approach either authority in such cases.

Are the five forms of clemency defined in the Constitution?

No. Articles 72 and 161 name them but do not define them. The meanings on this page are the standard explanations used in textbooks.
