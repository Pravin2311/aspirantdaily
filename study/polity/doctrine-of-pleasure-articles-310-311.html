Doctrine of Pleasure Under Articles 310 and 311: Tenure, Civil Servant Protections and the Three Exceptions

Article 309 service rules • 310(1) pleasure of President or Governor • 310(2) contracts • 311(1) appointing authority • 311(2) inquiry • exceptions (a), (b), (c) • 311(3)

UPSC State PSC 📅 Last updated: 2026-11-06

Article 310 says that government servants hold office "during the pleasure" of the President (for the Union) or the Governor (for a State). Article 311 then limits that pleasure for civil servants. No civil servant can be dismissed or removed by an authority below the one that appointed them, and none can be dismissed, removed or reduced in rank without an inquiry in which they hear the charges and get a reasonable opportunity to answer them. The second proviso to Article 311(2) lists three cases where that inquiry may be skipped.

Both Articles sit in Chapter I of Part XIV, "Services under the Union and the States", alongside Article 309 on recruitment and conditions of service. This page reads the three Articles clause by clause from the official text. It does not cover the Supreme Court's case law on them.

Article 309 lets "Acts of the appropriate Legislature" regulate recruitment and conditions of service for posts under the Union or a State. Parliament legislates for Union posts and a State Legislature for State posts.

Its proviso fills the gap until such an Act exists. The President, or a person the President directs, may make rules for Union services, and the Governor, or a person the Governor directs, may make rules for State services. Those rules give way to any Act later passed on the subject. Much of the day-to-day law on government service therefore sits in rules made under this proviso, not in Acts.

Article 310(1) states the rule. "Except as expressly provided by this Constitution", the following hold office during the pleasure of the President:

  • members of a defence service;
  • members of a civil service of the Union;
  • members of an all-India service;
  • holders of any post connected with defence or any civil post under the Union.

Members of a civil service of a State, and holders of civil posts under a State, hold office during the pleasure of the Governor.

Note that members of an all-India service, such as the IAS and IPS, are listed with the President's group even when they serve a State. The Article names them separately from State civil services.

Article 310(2) deals with contracts. Where a person who is not already a member of a regular service is appointed on contract for special qualifications, the contract may provide compensation if the post is abolished before the agreed period ends, or if the person has to vacate it for reasons not connected with misconduct. The pleasure rule still applies to such a person, but the contract can make early termination cost the government money.

The words "except as expressly provided by this Constitution" matter. Several offices have their own removal procedure, which overrides pleasure:

Supreme Court JudgeOrder of the President after an address by each House of Parliament, on proved misbehaviour or incapacity124(4)

These holders are not "at pleasure" in the ordinary sense. The Constitution itself fixes when and how they can go.

Article 311 protects a narrower group than Article 310. It covers members of a civil service of the Union, an all-India service or a civil service of a State, and holders of civil posts under the Union or a State. It does not mention defence services, so members of the armed forces are outside Article 311.

Clause (1) says no such person "shall be dismissed or removed by an authority subordinate to that by which he was appointed". A higher or equal authority may act; a lower one may not. Clause (1) speaks of dismissal and removal only, not reduction in rank.

Clause (2) covers three penalties: dismissal, removal and reduction in rank. None of them may be imposed except after an inquiry in which the person:

  1. has been informed of the charges against them, and
  2. has been given a reasonable opportunity of being heard in respect of those charges.

The present clauses (2) and (3) were substituted by the Fifteenth Amendment (1963). The 42nd Amendment (1976) then omitted certain words and substituted the first proviso. Under that first proviso, once the inquiry is over, the penalty may be imposed on the basis of the evidence from the inquiry. The person need not be given a further chance to make a representation on the proposed penalty.

The second proviso to Article 311(2) says the inquiry rule does not apply in three cases:

ClauseSituationWho decides
(a)The person is dismissed, removed or reduced in rank for conduct that led to conviction on a criminal chargeThe disciplinary authority, relying on the conviction
(b)It is not reasonably practicable to hold the inquiry, for a reason recorded in writingThe authority empowered to dismiss, remove or reduce in rank
(c)Holding the inquiry is not expedient in the interest of the security of the StateThe President or the Governor

Clause (b) requires the reason to be recorded in writing. Article 311(3) adds that if a question arises whether holding an inquiry is reasonably practicable, the decision of the authority empowered to impose the penalty "shall be final".

  • Article 309 sets the rules of service: recruitment, pay, conduct and discipline.
  • Article 310 says tenure is at pleasure, unless the Constitution provides otherwise.
  • Article 311 limits that pleasure for civil servants with two procedural safeguards and three exceptions.

A common exam trap is to treat Article 311 as covering everyone in government. It covers civil services and civil posts only. Another trap is the first proviso. Since 1976 there is no second opportunity to answer the proposed penalty, but the opportunity to answer the charges during the inquiry remains.

Q1. Under Article 310(1), a member of an all-India service holds office during the pleasure of: medium

✅ Answer: (b) The President

💡 Explanation: Article 310(1) lists all-India service members in the President's pleasure group, not the Governor's.

Q2. Article 311 does NOT protect: medium

✅ Answer: (c) Members of a defence service

💡 Explanation: Article 311 covers civil services and civil posts only; defence services are not mentioned in it.

Q3. Under Article 311(1), a civil servant cannot be dismissed by: easy

✅ Answer: (b) An authority subordinate to the appointing authority

💡 Explanation: Article 311(1) bars dismissal or removal by an authority subordinate to the one that made the appointment.

Q4. Which is NOT an exception to the inquiry rule in Article 311(2)? medium

✅ Answer: (d) Loss of confidence of the Minister

💡 Explanation: The second proviso lists only conviction, impracticability recorded in writing, and security of the State.

Q5. Whether holding an inquiry is reasonably practicable is finally decided under Article 311(3) by: hard

✅ Answer: (b) The authority empowered to impose the penalty

💡 Explanation: Article 311(3) makes the decision of the authority empowered to dismiss, remove or reduce in rank final.

Does Article 311 apply to members of the armed forces?

No. Article 311 names civil services and civil posts only. Defence service members are under the pleasure rule in Article 310(1) but outside the Article 311 safeguards.

Is a second hearing on the penalty still required?

No. Since the 42nd Amendment, the first proviso to Article 311(2) lets the penalty rest on the inquiry evidence without a further representation on the proposed penalty.
