Finance Commission Under Article 280: Composition, Functions and the 16th Finance Commission (2026-31)

Articles 270, 275, 280 and 281 • 16th Finance Commission (2026-31) • Vertical and horizontal devolution

UPSC SSC Banking State PSC 📅 Last updated: 2026-11-01

The Union collects most of the large taxes, but States carry much of the spending on health, police, agriculture and local services. The Constitution handles this imbalance in two ways. Under Article 270, many Union taxes are "levied and collected by the Government of India" and then "distributed between the Union and the States". Under Article 275, Parliament can give grants-in-aid to States "in need of assistance".

How much each side gets, and how the States' part is divided among them, is decided on the advice of an independent body: the Finance Commission under Article 280.

PointRule
Who constitutes itThe President, by order
WhenWithin two years of the Constitution's commencement, then at the end of every fifth year, or earlier if the President considers it necessary
MembersA Chairman and four other members, appointed by the President
QualificationsAs Parliament may determine by law (Article 280(2))
Procedure and powersThe Commission determines its own procedure; powers as Parliament confers by law (Article 280(4))

So the Commission is a constitutional body, but a temporary one: a new Commission is set up for each five-year cycle.

The Commission makes recommendations to the President on:

  • Vertical devolution: the distribution of the net proceeds of shareable taxes between the Union and the States.
  • Horizontal devolution: the allocation of the States' share among the States.
  • Grants-in-aid: the principles that should govern grants to States out of the Consolidated Fund of India.
  • Panchayats (clause bb): measures to augment a State's Consolidated Fund to supplement Panchayat resources, based on the State Finance Commission's recommendations. Inserted by the 73rd Amendment.
  • Municipalities (clause c): the same for Municipalities. Inserted by the 74th Amendment.
  • Any other matter the President refers "in the interests of sound finance" (clause d).

Under Article 281, the President must lay every recommendation before each House of Parliament, together with an explanatory memorandum on the action taken.

The Government of India constituted the Sixteenth Finance Commission on 31 December 2023, by Notification S.O. 5533(E), "in pursuance to Article 280(1)". Dr Arvind Panagariya, former Vice-Chairman of NITI Aayog and Professor at Columbia University, is its Chairman. Shri Ritvik Ranjanam Pandey was appointed Secretary.

It was asked to report by 31 October 2025, for the five years from 1 April 2026 to 31 March 2031.

According to the report, the members appointed in January 2024 were Ajay Narayan Jha, Annie George Mathew, Niranjan Rajadhyaksha and Soumya Kanti Ghosh (part-time). Manoj Panda replaced Dr Rajadhyaksha, who did not assume office. After Shri Jha resigned in April 2025, T. Rabi Sankar was appointed a part-time member.

Its terms of reference covered the three standard Article 280(3) items, plus a review of how disaster management is financed under the Disaster Management Act, 2005.

Two terms run through every Finance Commission report. Vertical devolution is the split of the divisible pool of shareable Union taxes between the Union on one side and all the States together on the other. Horizontal devolution is how the States' combined share is then divided among the individual States, using a formula with weighted criteria.

Article 280(3)(a) covers both in one sentence: "the distribution between the Union and the States of the net proceeds of taxes" is vertical, and "the allocation between the States of the respective shares of such proceeds" is horizontal. Grants-in-aid under Article 275 are a third channel, separate from tax sharing.

The Sixteenth Commission described its method for the vertical share as a comparison of the revenue the Union has available for devolution against the additional revenue the States require, adjusted for scope to raise taxes and cut wasteful spending. It also recommended that the Union disclose every year the net proceeds of taxes as certified by the CAG, to make the divisible pool more transparent.

Vertical devolution: 41 per cent. The report recommends "that the States' share in the divisible pool is retained at 41 per cent". The history behind that number is often asked:

The Union therefore keeps the remaining 59 per cent of the divisible pool.

Under this formula, Uttar Pradesh gets the largest share of the States' pool (17.619 per cent), followed by Bihar (9.948). Sikkim gets the smallest (0.335).

Grants. The Commission does not recommend revenue deficit grants, and does not recommend any sector-specific or State-specific grants.

  • ✘ "The Finance Commission is a permanent body." A new one is constituted every five years or earlier.
  • ✘ "The Finance Minister chairs it." The President appoints an independent Chairman.
  • ✘ "FC-16 raised the States' share to 42%." It retained 41%; 42% was FC-14.
  • ✘ "Population is the biggest criterion." Per capita GSDP distance has the highest weight (42.5).

Are the Finance Commission's recommendations binding?

Article 281 requires the President to lay every recommendation before Parliament with an explanatory memorandum on the action taken. The Constitution does not say the recommendations are binding; it only requires this disclosure.

What is 'contribution to GDP' in the 16th Commission's formula?

It is a new criterion with a 10 per cent weight in Table 8.8, along with population, demographic performance, area, forest and per capita GSDP distance.
