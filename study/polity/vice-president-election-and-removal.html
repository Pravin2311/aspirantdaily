Vice-President of India: Election by Both Houses, Role as Rajya Sabha Chairman, Term and Removal (Articles 63 to 71)

Ex officio Chairman of Rajya Sabha • Acting President • Electoral college of all MPs • Age 35 • Five-year term • Removal by Rajya Sabha majority • Disputes to Supreme Court

UPSC SSC State PSC Railway 📅 Last updated: 2026-11-04

Article 63 says simply: "There shall be a Vice-President of India." The office is defined by the two jobs the Constitution gives it:

  • Chairman of the Rajya Sabha. Under Article 64, the Vice-President is ex officio Chairman of the Council of States and may not hold any other office of profit.
  • Stand-in for the President. Under Article 65, the Vice-President acts as President when the office falls vacant, and discharges the President's functions when the President is absent, ill or otherwise unable to act.

The two roles do not run together. While acting as President, the Vice-President does not perform the duties of Chairman of the Rajya Sabha and is not paid the Chairman's salary (proviso to Article 64). During that period the Vice-President has all the powers and immunities of the President (Article 65(3)).

The Vice-President is elected by an electoral college made up of the members of both Houses of Parliament. The election uses proportional representation by means of the single transferable vote, and the voting is by secret ballot.

Three details are commonly tested:

  • All MPs, including nominated members, are in the electoral college. The President's electoral college under Article 54 has only elected MPs and elected MLAs.
  • State legislators have no part in the Vice-President's election.
  • The wording changed in 1961. Originally the Constitution said "members of both Houses of Parliament assembled at a joint meeting". The 11th Amendment replaced this with "an electoral college", so no joint sitting is needed.

Under Article 66(3), a candidate must:

  1. be a citizen of India;
  2. have completed thirty-five years of age; and
  3. be qualified for election as a member of the Council of States.

Article 66(4) disqualifies anyone holding an office of profit under the Union, a State or an authority controlled by either. Sitting as President, Vice-President, Governor or a Minister does not count as an office of profit for this purpose. Under Article 66(2), the Vice-President cannot be a member of Parliament or of a State Legislature. A member who is elected is deemed to have vacated the seat on entering office.

The term is five years from the date of entering office. The Vice-President continues in office after the term ends until a successor takes over. The term can end early in two ways.

  • Resignation: by writing addressed to the President.
  • Removal: by a resolution of the Council of States "passed by a majority of all the then members of the Council and agreed to by the House of the People". The resolution can be moved only after at least fourteen days' notice.

The removal procedure differs from the President's impeachment in four ways. It starts only in the Rajya Sabha. It needs only a majority of all the then members, not two-thirds. The Lok Sabha merely agrees to it. And Article 67 states no ground for removal. Compare impeachment of the President under Article 61.

An election to fill a vacancy when the term expires must be completed before the term ends (Article 68(1)). If the office falls vacant through death, resignation, removal or otherwise, the election must be held "as soon as possible", and the person elected serves a full five-year term (Article 68(2)). Unlike the President's case, there is no six-month outer limit in the text.

Before taking office, the Vice-President makes an oath or affirmation before the President, or a person the President appoints, under Article 69. The oath is to bear true faith and allegiance to the Constitution and to faithfully discharge the duties of the office.

All doubts and disputes about the election are decided by the Supreme Court, whose decision is final (Article 71(1)). Acts done before the Court declares an election void stay valid (Article 71(2)). For the Rajya Sabha side of the role, see Parliament of India. For the President's election, see election of the President.

Two Articles in the Parliament chapter complete the picture. Under Article 91(1), when the office of Chairman is vacant, or while the Vice-President is acting as President, the Chairman's duties are performed by the Deputy Chairman. If the Deputy Chairman's office is also vacant, they are performed by a member of the Rajya Sabha whom the President appoints.

Article 92 deals with a removal resolution against the Vice-President. While such a resolution is under consideration, the Chairman "shall not, though he is present, preside". The Chairman may still speak in and take part in the proceedings. But under Article 92(2), notwithstanding the usual voting rule in Article 100, the Chairman "shall not be entitled to vote at all on such resolution or on any other matter during such proceedings". The same rule applies to the Deputy Chairman when a resolution for the Deputy Chairman's own removal is being considered.

In normal sittings the Chairman, like the Speaker, does not vote in the first instance but has a casting vote in case of a tie, under Article 100(1). The full working of the Upper House is on Parliament of India.

Q1. Who elects the Vice-President of India? easy

✅ Answer: (b) Members of both Houses of Parliament

💡 Explanation: Article 66(1): an electoral college consisting of the members of both Houses of Parliament, including nominated members. MLAs do not take part.

Q2. A Vice-President candidate must be qualified for election as a member of: medium

✅ Answer: (c) The Rajya Sabha

💡 Explanation: Article 66(3)(c) requires qualification for the Council of States. A presidential candidate must be qualified for the Lok Sabha (Article 58).

Q3. A resolution to remove the Vice-President can be moved in: easy

✅ Answer: (d) The Rajya Sabha only

💡 Explanation: Article 67(b): a resolution of the Council of States passed by a majority of all its then members, which the House of the People then agrees to.

Q4. While acting as President, the Vice-President: medium

✅ Answer: (a) Does not perform the duties of Rajya Sabha Chairman

💡 Explanation: The proviso to Article 64 says that while acting as President the Vice-President does not perform the Chairman's duties and is not paid the Chairman's salary.

Q5. Which Amendment changed the Vice-President's election from a joint meeting of both Houses to an electoral college? hard

✅ Answer: (b) 11th

💡 Explanation: The footnote to Article 66 records that the 11th Amendment (1961) replaced 'members of both Houses of Parliament assembled at a joint meeting'.

Do nominated members of Parliament vote in the Vice-President's election?

Yes. Article 66 gives the vote to the members of both Houses, without limiting it to elected members, so nominated members of both Houses take part. They do not vote in the President's election.

To whom does the Vice-President resign?

To the President, by writing under his hand (Article 67(a)). The President resigns to the Vice-President (Article 56(1)(a)).
