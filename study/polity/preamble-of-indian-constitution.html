Preamble of the Indian Constitution: Full Text and Every Key Word Explained

Full text • Nature of the State • Justice, Liberty, Equality, Fraternity • 26 November 1949 • 42nd Amendment, 1976

UPSC SSC State PSC 📅 Last updated: 2026-10-31

The Preamble is the Constitution's opening statement. In under a hundred words it says where the Constitution's authority comes from, what kind of State India is, and what that State is meant to achieve. Here is the text as it stands today:

WE, THE PEOPLE OF INDIA, having solemnly resolved to constitute India into a SOVEREIGN SOCIALIST SECULAR DEMOCRATIC REPUBLIC and to secure to all its citizens:

JUSTICE, social, economic and political;

LIBERTY of thought, expression, belief, faith and worship;

EQUALITY of status and of opportunity;

and to promote among them all

FRATERNITY assuring the dignity of the individual and the unity and integrity of the Nation;

IN OUR CONSTITUENT ASSEMBLY this twenty-sixth day of November, 1949, do HEREBY ADOPT, ENACT AND GIVE TO OURSELVES THIS CONSTITUTION.

A useful way to read it is in four parts: the source of authority ("We, the people of India"), the nature of the State (the five adjectives), the objectives (justice, liberty, equality, fraternity) and the date of adoption.

The opening words place the source of the Constitution's authority in the people of India, not in a king, the British Parliament or any other outside power. The closing words make the same point from the other end: the people "give to ourselves" this Constitution.

The meanings below are the standard textbook readings that exams expect. The Constitution does not define these words.

WordWhat it meansExam point
SovereignIndia decides its internal and external affairs and is not subject to any outside authority.Membership of the Commonwealth or the UN does not limit this sovereignty.
SocialistThe State aims to reduce inequality of income, status and opportunity.Added in 1976. Read it with the Directive Principles, such as Articles 38 and 39.
SecularThe State has no religion of its own and treats all religions equally.Added in 1976. Articles 25 to 28 give it practical effect.
DemocraticGovernment is chosen by the people through periodic elections on universal adult franchise.Covers political democracy, and the objectives point to social and economic democracy too.
RepublicThe head of State is elected, not hereditary.The President is elected indirectly for a fixed term.
  • Justice: social, economic and political.
  • Liberty: of thought, expression, belief, faith and worship.
  • Equality: of status and of opportunity.
  • Fraternity: assuring the dignity of the individual and the unity and integrity of the Nation.

Notice the exact wording that exams test. Justice has three kinds, liberty has five objects, and equality is "of status and of opportunity", not of income or outcome. Fraternity is the only objective the State is to "promote among them all" rather than "secure".

The Preamble's promises are not left as words. Each objective is carried into specific Parts of the Constitution, and linking them is a common way to frame a mains answer or to eliminate options in an MCQ.

ObjectiveWhere the Constitution gives it effect
Social and economic justiceDirective Principles, especially Articles 38 and 39, which ask the State to reduce inequality and distribute resources for the common good
Political justiceUniversal adult franchise and elected legislatures at every level
Liberty of thought and expressionArticle 19(1)(a), freedom of speech and expression
Liberty of belief, faith and worshipArticles 25 to 28, freedom of religion
Equality of statusArticle 14, equality before the law for every person; Articles 15, 17 and 18 against discrimination, untouchability and titles
Equality of opportunityArticle 16, equal opportunity for all citizens in public employment
FraternityArticle 51A(e), the duty to promote harmony and the spirit of common brotherhood

Read this way, the Preamble works as a summary of the whole document. Part III protects liberty and equality as enforceable rights, Part IV directs the State towards social and economic justice, and Part IVA asks citizens to keep up fraternity. That is also why courts turn to the Preamble when a provision is unclear: it tells them what the provision was meant to achieve.

For the rights side, see Article 19's six freedoms; for the State's goals, see the classification of Directive Principles.

The Preamble has been amended once. The Constitution (Forty-second) Amendment Act, 1976, with effect from 3 January 1977:

  • replaced "SOVEREIGN DEMOCRATIC REPUBLIC" with "SOVEREIGN SOCIALIST SECULAR DEMOCRATIC REPUBLIC", adding Socialist and Secular; and
  • replaced "unity of the Nation" with "unity and integrity of the Nation", adding Integrity.
  • ✘ "Sovereign" or "Republic" was added in 1976. Both were there from the start.
  • ✘ The 44th Amendment changed the Preamble. It did not; only the 42nd did.

This is a classic exam question. In In re Berubari Union (1960) the Supreme Court said the Preamble was not part of the Constitution. In Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala (1973) the Court took the opposite view and treated the Preamble as part of the Constitution, which is the position exams expect today. Because it is part of the Constitution, it can be amended under Article 368, but not in a way that damages the basic structure.

The Preamble is still not enforceable on its own: no one can go to court claiming a right under the Preamble alone. For how it fits into the Constitution as a whole, see the Constitution making and salient features notes.

Q1. Which words were added to the Preamble by the 42nd Amendment Act, 1976? easy

✅ Answer: (b) Socialist, Secular and Integrity

💡 Explanation: The amendment replaced 'Sovereign Democratic Republic' with 'Sovereign Socialist Secular Democratic Republic' and 'unity of the Nation' with 'unity and integrity of the Nation'.

Q2. The date mentioned in the Preamble is: easy

✅ Answer: (c) 26 November 1949

💡 Explanation: The Preamble says the Constituent Assembly adopted, enacted and gave the Constitution to itself on 26 November 1949. Most of the Constitution came into force on 26 January 1950.

Q3. According to the Preamble, which kinds of justice are to be secured? easy

✅ Answer: (a) Social, economic and political

💡 Explanation: The Preamble lists justice as social, economic and political. Legal or moral justice are not among the three words used.

Q4. In the Preamble, 'equality' is of: medium

✅ Answer: (b) status and of opportunity

💡 Explanation: The text reads 'EQUALITY of status and of opportunity'. It does not promise equality of income or of outcome.

Q5. Fraternity in the Preamble assures: medium

✅ Answer: (c) the dignity of the individual and the unity and integrity of the Nation

💡 Explanation: The Preamble ties fraternity to the dignity of the individual and the unity and integrity of the Nation.

Is the Preamble enforceable in court?

No. It does not by itself grant rights or powers that a court can enforce. Courts use it as a guide when interpreting the rest of the Constitution, especially where a provision is ambiguous.

Why does the Preamble say 26 November 1949 and not 26 January 1950?

The Constituent Assembly adopted the Constitution on 26 November 1949, and the Preamble records that act. Most provisions came into force on 26 January 1950, which is celebrated as Republic Day.
