🏘️ Panchayati Raj & Urban Local Bodies

Committees • 73rd Amendment • 74th Amendment • Gram Sabha • PESA • Urban Bodies

High Yield Polity 📅 Last updated: Oct 2026
  • Local self-government is a State List subject, but the 73rd and 74th Amendments (1992) gave panchayats and municipalities constitutional status.
  • The first panchayat under the new three-tier system was inaugurated by Jawaharlal Nehru at Nagaur, Rajasthan, on October 2, 1959.
  • The 73rd Amendment came into force on April 24, 1993, now celebrated as National Panchayati Raj Day.

Committees That Shaped Panchayati Raj

CommitteeYearKey recommendation
Balwantrai Mehta1957Three-tier system — village, block and district — called "democratic decentralisation"
Ashok Mehta1977–78Two-tier system (district and mandal) and participation of political parties
G.V.K. Rao1985The district as the main unit of planning; called the system "grass without roots"
L.M. Singhvi1986Constitutional status for panchayats

The 64th Amendment Bill (1989) under Rajiv Gandhi failed in the Rajya Sabha; the 73rd Amendment was passed in 1992 under P.V. Narasimha Rao. Article 40 (a Directive Principle) already asked states to organise village panchayats.

More than 20 states, including Bihar (the first, in 2006), Madhya Pradesh and Rajasthan, have raised women's reservation in panchayats to 50%.

PESA Act, 1996

The Panchayats (Extension to Scheduled Areas) Act extends Part IX to Fifth Schedule areas in ten states, with special powers for the Gram Sabha — to protect tribal customs, manage community resources, be consulted before land acquisition and control minor forest produce. It was based on the Dilip Singh Bhuria Committee (1995). Sixth Schedule areas in the north-east have their own Autonomous District Councils and are outside Part IX.

Where the 73rd Amendment Does Not Apply

Nagaland, Meghalaya and Mizoram; hill areas of Manipur with district councils; and the Darjeeling hill area, which has the Gorkhaland Territorial Administration. Parliament can extend it to these areas by law.

  1. Committee ↔ recommendation: Balwantrai Mehta (three-tier), Ashok Mehta (two-tier), L.M. Singhvi (constitutional status).
  2. Numbers: 29 subjects (11th Schedule), 18 subjects (12th), one-third women, 21 years.
  3. Compulsory vs voluntary statement questions — OBC reservation is voluntary; SC/ST reservation is compulsory.

Q1. The 11th Schedule of the Constitution contains how many subjects for panchayats?

✅ Answer: (b) 29

💡 Explanation: The 11th Schedule lists 29 subjects; the 12th Schedule lists 18 for municipalities.

Q2. Which committee recommended a three-tier Panchayati Raj system?

✅ Answer: (a) Balwantrai Mehta Committee

💡 Explanation: The Balwantrai Mehta Committee (1957) proposed the village–block–district structure.

Q3. Which of these is a VOLUNTARY provision of the 73rd Amendment?

✅ Answer: (d) Reservation for backward classes

💡 Explanation: States may choose whether to reserve seats for backward classes; the others are compulsory.

Q4. National Panchayati Raj Day is observed on:

✅ Answer: (c) April 24

💡 Explanation: The 73rd Amendment came into force on April 24, 1993.

Q5. Ward committees are mandatory in municipalities with a population of at least:

✅ Answer: (a) 3 lakh

💡 Explanation: Article 243S requires ward committees where the population is 3 lakh or more.

🔁 Revise in 60 Seconds

  • 73rd: Part IX, 11th Schedule (29), in force Apr 24, 1993. 74th: Part IXA, 12th Schedule (18), June 1, 1993.
  • Compulsory: Gram Sabha, 5-yr term, 1/3 women, SC/ST quota, SEC, SFC, age 21.
  • PESA 1996 for Fifth Schedule areas (Bhuria Committee).

📌 Prelims Traps to Avoid

  • ✘ Reservation for OBCs in panchayats is voluntary, not compulsory.
  • ✘ The State Election Commissioner is appointed by the Governor, not the Election Commission of India.
  • ✘ PESA covers Fifth Schedule areas; Sixth Schedule areas have their own councils.

What is the difference between the 73rd and 74th Amendments?

The 73rd Amendment (Part IX, 11th Schedule with 29 subjects) gave constitutional status to rural panchayats. The 74th Amendment (Part IXA, 12th Schedule with 18 subjects) did the same for urban local bodies — nagar panchayats, municipal councils and municipal corporations.

Which committee recommended constitutional status for panchayats?

The L.M. Singhvi Committee (1986). Its recommendation was eventually implemented through the 73rd Amendment in 1992.
