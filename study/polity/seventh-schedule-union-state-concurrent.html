Seventh Schedule: Union List, State List and Concurrent List With Key Subjects

Article 246 • Residuary power, Article 248 • Repugnancy, Article 254 • 42nd Amendment shifts

UPSC SSC Railways State PSC 📅 Last updated: 2026-11-01

Article 246 divides law-making power using three lists in the Seventh Schedule:

ListWho legislatesArticleLast entry number
List I: Union ListParliament alone246(1)97
List II: State ListState Legislature alone, subject to (1) and (2)246(3)66
List III: Concurrent ListBoth Parliament and the States246(2)47

Residuary power belongs to Parliament. Article 248 gives it exclusive power over any matter "not enumerated in the Concurrent List or State List", including any tax not mentioned in those Lists. Entry 97 of the Union List says the same thing.

A useful rule of thumb: the Union List holds subjects that need one national law, the State List holds local and day-to-day governance, and the Concurrent List holds subjects where national uniformity helps but States need room to act.

The 42nd Amendment, 1976, shifted several subjects from the State List to the Concurrent List. The official edition shows the State List entries as omitted and the Concurrent List entries as inserted or substituted by section 57 of that Amendment.

SubjectState List entry omittedConcurrent List entry now
EducationEntry 11Entry 25 (substituted)
ForestsEntries 19-20Entry 17A (inserted)
Protection of wild animals and birdsEntries 19-20Entry 17B (inserted)
Weights and measuresEntry 29Entry 33A (inserted)
Administration of justiceWords omitted from entry 3Entry 11A (inserted)

Population control and family planning (entry 20A) was also added to the Concurrent List by the same Amendment. Memory aid: EFWWJ for Education, Forests, Wild animals, Weights and measures, Justice.

On a Concurrent subject, both Parliament and a State may legislate, so conflicts happen. Article 254(1) says the law made by Parliament, "whether passed before or after" the State law, prevails, and the State law "shall, to the extent of the repugnancy, be void".

There is one way out for the State. Under Article 254(2), if the State law was reserved for the President's consideration and received his assent, it prevails in that State. Even then, Parliament can later amend or repeal it.

For more on Centre-State relations, see federalism and Centre-State relations and the 12 Schedules.

  • ✘ "Residuary powers lie with the States, as in the USA." In India they lie with Parliament (Article 248).
  • ✘ "Police is a Concurrent subject." It is entry 2 of the State List.
  • ✘ "Education has always been Concurrent." It moved there by the 42nd Amendment, 1976.
  • ✘ "A Central law prevails over a State law only if passed later." Article 254(1) says before or after.

Q1. Residuary powers of legislation in India lie with: easy

✅ Answer: (b) Parliament

💡 Explanation: Article 248 and entry 97 of List I give Parliament exclusive power over matters not in the State or Concurrent List.

Q2. 'Forests' is now in the: easy

✅ Answer: (c) Concurrent List

💡 Explanation: Entry 17A of the Concurrent List, inserted by the 42nd Amendment, 1976.

Q3. 'Police' falls in the: easy

✅ Answer: (b) State List

💡 Explanation: Entry 2 of List II, subject to entry 2A of List I on deployment of Union forces.

Q4. Which of these was NOT inserted into the Concurrent List by the 42nd Amendment? medium

✅ Answer: (c) Criminal law

💡 Explanation: Criminal law has been entry 1 of the Concurrent List from the start. Entries 17A, 17B and 20A were inserted in 1976.

Q5. A State law on a Concurrent subject conflicts with an earlier Central law. It can prevail in that State if: medium

✅ Answer: (c) it was reserved for and received the President's assent

💡 Explanation: Article 254(2). Parliament can still later amend or repeal it under the proviso.

How many entries does each List have?

The last entries are numbered 97 (Union), 66 (State) and 47 (Concurrent), but some entries have been omitted and others inserted with letters such as 11A or 17B. So different books give slightly different counts; always check whether a question asks about numbering or the actual count.
