  • ISRO was founded on August 15, 1969; its founding vision came from Vikram Sarabhai. India's first rocket was launched from Thumba (Kerala) on November 21, 1963.
  • India's first satellite, Aryabhata, was launched on April 19, 1975 on a Soviet rocket. Most launches now take place from the Satish Dhawan Space Centre, Sriharikota.
  • This page covers the lasting concepts and milestones; for the latest updates see the current affairs notes.

ISRO's Launch Vehicles

VehicleBest known for
SLV-3India's first launch vehicle; placed Rohini in orbit in 1980 (project led by A.P.J. Abdul Kalam)
PSLVISRO's "workhorse" for polar and sun-synchronous orbits; launched Chandrayaan-1 (2008) and Mangalyaan (2013); put a record 104 satellites into orbit in one launch on February 15, 2017
GSLVUses an indigenous cryogenic upper stage (liquid hydrogen and liquid oxygen) for heavier satellites and higher orbits; launched NISAR in 2025
LVM3 (formerly GSLV Mk III)India's heaviest rocket; launched Chandrayaan-2, Chandrayaan-3 and OneWeb satellites, and is the vehicle for Gaganyaan
SSLVSmall Satellite Launch Vehicle for low-cost, quick launches of small satellites

Types of Orbits

OrbitHeightUsed for
Low Earth Orbit (LEO)About 160–2,000 kmEarth observation, space stations (the ISS)
Sun-synchronous (SSO)A polar LEO, roughly 600–800 kmRemote sensing — passes over a place at the same local time each day
Medium Earth Orbit (MEO)2,000–35,786 kmNavigation constellations such as GPS
Geostationary (GEO)35,786 km above the equatorCommunication and weather satellites (GSAT, INSAT) — period of 24 hours, so it seems fixed over one spot

NavIC (formerly IRNSS) is India's regional navigation system, using satellites in geostationary and inclined geosynchronous orbits.

India plans its own space station, the Bharatiya Antariksh Station, with the first module targeted for 2028. The Gaganyaan human spaceflight programme is covered in our Gaganyaan and Chandrayaan-4 notes.

India's Missile Programme

The Integrated Guided Missile Development Programme (IGMDP) began in 1983 under A.P.J. Abdul Kalam at DRDO (founded 1958). It produced five missiles:

MissileType
PrithviShort-range surface-to-surface ballistic missile
AgniBallistic missile family — Agni-V has a range of over 5,000 km; its first flight with MIRV technology (several warheads, each with its own target) was Mission Divyastra on March 11, 2024
AkashMedium-range surface-to-air missile
NagThird-generation "fire and forget" anti-tank guided missile
TrishulShort-range surface-to-air missile (programme closed)

BrahMos, a supersonic cruise missile, is a joint venture of India and Russia; its name combines the Brahmaputra and Moskva rivers.

Navy and the Nuclear Triad

  • INS Vikrant, India's first indigenously built aircraft carrier, was commissioned on September 2, 2022.
  • INS Arihant, the first indigenous nuclear-powered ballistic missile submarine, completed its first deterrence patrol in 2018, completing India's nuclear triad — the ability to launch nuclear weapons from land, air and sea.
  • Tejas is India's indigenous light combat aircraft, built by HAL.

Q1. Chandrayaan-3 landed near the Moon's south pole on:

✅ Answer: (b) August 23, 2023

💡 Explanation: The Vikram lander touched down on August 23, 2023, now National Space Day.

Q2. A geostationary satellite orbits at a height of about:

✅ Answer: (a) 35,786 km

💡 Explanation: At 35,786 km above the equator, the orbital period matches Earth's rotation (24 hours).

Q3. Which Indian launch vehicle placed a record 104 satellites in orbit in 2017?

✅ Answer: (d) PSLV

💡 Explanation: PSLV-C37 launched 104 satellites on February 15, 2017.

Q4. Akash is a:

✅ Answer: (a) Surface-to-air missile

💡 Explanation: Akash is a medium-range surface-to-air missile developed under the IGMDP.

Q5. With SpaDeX in January 2025, India became the ___ country to dock two spacecraft in orbit.

✅ Answer: (c) Fourth

💡 Explanation: India followed the USA, Russia and China.

What is the difference between PSLV and GSLV?

PSLV is mainly used to place satellites in polar and sun-synchronous low Earth orbits. GSLV uses a cryogenic upper stage to carry heavier satellites to geosynchronous transfer orbit. LVM3 is the heaviest variant.

When did Chandrayaan-3 land on the Moon?

On August 23, 2023, near the lunar south pole. India was the first country to land in that region, and August 23 is now celebrated as National Space Day.
