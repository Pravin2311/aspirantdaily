Plant and Animal Tissues: Meristems, Permanent Tissues, Xylem vs Phloem, Four Animal Tissues and Joints

A tissue is a group of similar cells working together on one function. NCERT's Class 9 science textbook "Exploration" (chapter 3, "Tissues in Action") classifies them as follows:

GroupTypesMain job
Plant: meristematicApical, lateral, intercalaryGrowth by continuous cell division
Plant: simple permanentParenchyma, collenchyma, sclerenchymaStorage, flexible support, hard support
Plant: complex permanentXylem, phloemTransport of water and minerals; transport of food
Plant: protectiveEpidermisOuter covering; reduces water loss
AnimalEpithelial, connective, muscular, nervousCovering, support and connection, movement, control

The difference starts with how plants and animals live. Most plants stay in one place and need rigid support, which their cell walls provide. Most animals move, and their cells have no rigid wall, so they can change shape.

MeristemLocationGrowth it causes
ApicalTips of roots and shootsLength
LateralA ring along the circumference of the stemGirth (thickness)
IntercalaryBase of the internode, just above the nodeRegrowth after cutting or grazing, as in grasses

NCERT's onion experiment shows the apical meristem at work. Roots with their tips cut stop growing, while uncut roots keep growing. The tips hold the dividing cells. Lateral meristem activity leaves annual growth rings in tree trunks. Counting the rings estimates a tree's age, and their widths reflect good and bad growing years.

Meristem cells are small and thin-walled, with a large nucleus and dense cytoplasm. They are tightly packed and usually have no vacuoles, which suits rapid division. Some new cells lose the ability to divide and specialise into permanent tissue. This process is called differentiation.

TissueCellsWallFunction and examples
ParenchymaLiving, loosely packed with intercellular spacesThinStores food; photosynthesis in green parts; air spaces help aquatic plants float
CollenchymaLivingUnevenly thickened at the corners by pectinSupport with flexibility, so stems and tendrils bend without breaking
SclerenchymaMostly deadThick, with ligninHardness and strength: stems, leaf veins, coconut husk, walnut shell

This explains NCERT's twig question. A fresh twig bends because of living, flexible tissue, while a dry twig snaps. Coconut husk fibres are hard because sclerenchyma has lignin, while coriander stalks stay soft.

The epidermis is a single, tightly packed layer of flat cells covering the whole plant. A waxy cuticle of cutin covers it, and desert plants have a thicker cuticle to cut water loss. Root epidermis grows root hairs that increase the surface for absorbing water. Leaf epidermis has stomata: pores for gas exchange and transpiration. Transpiration creates the pull that draws water up the xylem and also removes wastes.

In older stems, cells below the epidermis form the cork cambium, which produces cork cells. These are dead, compact and impermeable to water and gases, and they form the bark.

Both are complex permanent tissues because each contains more than one type of cell.

1. Epithelial tissue covers the body and lines organs. Its cells are closely packed, which keeps out germs and reduces water loss.

FunctionStructureWhere
Exchange (diffusion)Single layer of thin, flat cellsLining of blood vessels and lungs
ProtectionMany layers; outer cells flatSkin, mouth, oesophagus
SecretionCuboidal or columnar gland cellsSalivary and sweat glands, stomach lining
AbsorptionSingle layer of tall, pillar-like cellsLining of the small intestine

2. Connective tissue connects and supports. Its matrix decides its nature: watery in blood and hard in bone.

  • Blood: plasma is about 55% of its volume and the formed elements about 45%. Red cells carry haemoglobin, an iron-rich protein, and live about 4 months. Platelets help clotting, and white cells gather at infections.
  • Bone: a rigid matrix of calcium and phosphorus compounds.
  • Cartilage: soft and flexible, as in the ear and nose; it cushions the ends of bones.
  • Tendon: joins muscle to bone. Ligament: joins bone to bone and limits movement.

4. Nervous tissue is made of neurons. Dendrites receive signals, the cell body holds the nucleus, and the axon carries messages to axon terminals, which pass them to other cells.

The skeleton makes up about 12 to 15% of adult body weight. Humans have 12 pairs of ribs, joined to the backbone at the back and to the sternum in front by flexible cartilage, so the rib cage can expand during breathing. Cartilage discs between the vertebrae let the spine bend safely.

In 1958, F. C. Steward grew complete carrot plants from single phloem cells in a nutrient medium. The cells first dedifferentiated, regaining the ability to divide, and then redifferentiated into roots and shoots. This ability is totipotency. In India, Sipra Guha Mukherjee and S. C. Maheshwari raised complete plants from anther culture, a breakthrough in plant tissue culture.

  • Apical meristem: length. Lateral: girth. Intercalary: regrowth in grasses.
  • Sclerenchyma has lignin and mostly dead cells; collenchyma has pectin and living cells.
  • Xylem carries water up; phloem carries food. Only xylem parenchyma is living in xylem.
  • Animal tissues: epithelial, connective, muscular, nervous.
  • Tendon: muscle to bone. Ligament: bone to bone.
  • Cardiac muscle is branched and involuntary; skeletal is voluntary and multinucleate.

Q1. Grass regrows after grazing because of the: easy

✅ Answer: (a) Intercalary meristem

💡 Explanation: The intercalary meristem at the base of the internode lets grasses regenerate after cutting.

Q2. Increase in girth of a stem is caused by the: easy

✅ Answer: (b) Lateral meristem

💡 Explanation: A ring of dividing cells, the lateral meristem, adds cells inside and outside to widen the stem.

Q3. Which tissue has thick, lignified walls and mostly dead cells? easy

✅ Answer: (c) Sclerenchyma

💡 Explanation: Lignin deposition makes sclerenchyma hard; it forms coconut husk and walnut shells.

Q4. The only living component of xylem is: medium

✅ Answer: (d) Xylem parenchyma

💡 Explanation: NCERT: tracheids, vessels and fibres are mainly sclerenchymatous; only xylem parenchyma is living.

Q5. A tendon connects: easy

✅ Answer: (a) Muscle to bone

💡 Explanation: Tendons join muscles to bones; ligaments join bone to bone.

Q6. Cardiac muscle cells are: medium

✅ Answer: (b) Branched with one nucleus

💡 Explanation: Cardiac fibres are cylindrical and branched, with one nucleus and faint striations.

Q7. The elbow is an example of a: easy

✅ Answer: (c) Hinge joint

💡 Explanation: The elbow bends and straightens in one direction only, like a door hinge.

Q8. The ability of a single plant cell to grow into a whole plant is called: medium

✅ Answer: (d) Totipotency

💡 Explanation: F. C. Steward showed carrot phloem cells can dedifferentiate and grow into whole plants.

What is the difference between a tendon and a ligament?

A tendon joins a muscle to a bone and transmits its pull. A ligament joins one bone to another, gives stability and stops excessive movement.

Why do meristem cells lack vacuoles?

Meristem cells are built for rapid, continuous division, with dense cytoplasm and a large nucleus. Large storage vacuoles belong to mature, specialised cells instead.
