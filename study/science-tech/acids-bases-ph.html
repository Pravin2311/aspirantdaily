Acids, Bases and the pH Scale: Indicators, pH in Daily Life, Natural Acids and Important Salts

pH 7 neutral, below 7 acidic, above 7 basic • Body works at pH 7.0-7.8 • Acid rain below 5.6 • Tooth decay below 5.5 • Baking soda NaHCO₃, washing soda Na₂CO₃·10H₂O, Plaster of Paris CaSO₄·½H₂O

The pH scale measures hydrogen ion concentration. The "p" comes from the German potenz, meaning power. NCERT's scale generally runs from 0 (very acidic) to 14 (very alkaline), with 7 as neutral. The more hydronium ions (H₃O⁺) a solution has, the lower its pH. From 7 up to 14, hydroxide ion (OH⁻) concentration and alkali strength rise. Universal indicator, a mix of several indicators, shows a different colour at each pH.

Strong vs weak: strength depends on how many H⁺ or OH⁻ ions are produced. At the same concentration, hydrochloric acid gives more H⁺ ions than acetic acid, so HCl is a strong acid and acetic acid a weak one.

  • Acid + metal → salt + hydrogen gas.
  • Acid + metal carbonate or hydrogencarbonate → salt + carbon dioxide + water. The CO₂ turns lime water milky.
  • Acid + base → salt + water (neutralisation): H⁺(aq) + OH⁻(aq) → H₂O.
  • Metal oxides react like bases (basic oxides); non-metal oxides are acidic.
  • Acids give H⁺ only in water, so dry HCl gas does not change dry litmus. Glucose and alcohol contain hydrogen but do not release H⁺ ions, so they do not conduct.
  • Water-soluble bases are alkalis. Always add acid to water, slowly and with stirring, never water to acid.
  • Digestion: the stomach makes hydrochloric acid. Excess acid causes pain, relieved by antacids such as milk of magnesia (magnesium hydroxide) or baking soda.
  • Teeth: enamel (calcium hydroxyapatite) is the hardest substance in the body but corrodes below pH 5.5. Basic toothpaste neutralises the acid.
  • Stings: a bee sting leaves an acid, relieved by baking soda. Nettle hairs inject methanoic acid; the traditional remedy is a dock leaf.
  • Soil and rivers: plants need a specific soil pH, and acid rain makes rivers hard for aquatic life. Venus has thick clouds of sulphuric acid.

Salts of a strong acid and strong base are neutral (pH 7). A strong acid with a weak base gives an acidic salt (pH below 7), and a strong base with a weak acid gives a basic salt (pH above 7). Common salt (NaCl) is the raw material for several of the chemicals below.

Water of crystallisation is the fixed number of water molecules in one formula unit of a salt. That is why washing soda crystals look dry despite holding ten water molecules. For other everyday chemistry, see chemistry in everyday life.

Q1. A solution turns red litmus blue. Its pH is likely to be: easy

✅ Answer: (d) 10

💡 Explanation: Turning red litmus blue means the solution is basic, and basic solutions have pH above 7. Of the options, only 10 fits. This is NCERT exercise 1.

Q2. Rain is called acid rain when its pH falls below: easy

✅ Answer: (a) 5.6

💡 Explanation: NCERT defines acid rain as rain water with pH less than 5.6. Such rain lowers river pH and makes survival hard for aquatic life.

Q3. Which acid is found in tamarind, according to NCERT? medium

✅ Answer: (b) Tartaric acid

💡 Explanation: NCERT's Table 2.3 pairs tamarind with tartaric acid. Citric acid is in orange and lemon, oxalic in tomato, and lactic in curd.

Q4. Solutions A to E show pH 4, 1, 11, 7 and 9. Which is strongly alkaline? medium

✅ Answer: (c) C

💡 Explanation: The highest pH is the most alkaline, so C at pH 11 is strongly alkaline. B at pH 1 is strongly acidic and D at pH 7 is neutral.

Q5. What is the chemical formula of Plaster of Paris? easy

✅ Answer: (d) CaSO₄·½H₂O

💡 Explanation: Plaster of Paris is calcium sulphate hemihydrate, CaSO₄·½H₂O, made by heating gypsum (CaSO₄·2H₂O) at 373 K. Two formula units share one water molecule.

What does a pH of 7 mean?

pH 7 is neutral. Values below 7 are acidic, and the lower the number the more hydronium ions there are. Values from 7 up to 14 are basic, with rising hydroxide ion concentration.

Why does tooth decay start after eating sweets?

Bacteria in the mouth break down sugar and food particles into acids. Once the mouth's pH falls below 5.5, the enamel starts to corrode. Basic toothpaste neutralises the excess acid.

What is the difference between baking soda and washing soda?

Baking soda is sodium hydrogencarbonate, NaHCO₃, a mild base used in cooking, antacids and fire extinguishers. Washing soda is Na₂CO₃·10H₂O, made by recrystallising sodium carbonate, and is used for cleaning and in glass, soap and paper industries.
