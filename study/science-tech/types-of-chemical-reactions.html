Types of Chemical Reactions: Combination, Decomposition, Displacement, Double Displacement and Redox, with Everyday Examples

Combination: A + B → AB • Decomposition: AB → A + B (heat, light or electricity) • Displacement: A + BC → AC + B • Double displacement: ions swap • Redox: oxidation and reduction together

SSC Railway 📅 Last updated: 2026-11-02

A chemical reaction breaks and makes bonds between atoms to give new substances. Atoms are not created, destroyed or turned into other elements. NCERT lists four signs that a reaction has taken place: a change in state, a change in colour, evolution of a gas and a change in temperature.

A word equation such as magnesium + oxygen → magnesium oxide puts the reactants on the left and the products on the right of an arrow. Written with formulas, Mg + O₂ → MgO is a skeletal equation: it has two oxygen atoms on the left and one on the right, so it is unbalanced.

Equations must be balanced because of the law of conservation of mass: mass is neither created nor destroyed, so each element must have the same number of atoms on both sides. NCERT's hit-and-trial method, using the smallest whole-number coefficients, works like this for iron and steam:

  1. Start with the compound that has the most atoms (Fe₃O₄) and its biggest element (oxygen). Four oxygens on the right need 4 H₂O on the left.
  2. Now there are 8 hydrogen atoms on the left, so write 4 H₂ on the right.
  3. Fe₃O₄ has three iron atoms, so write 3 Fe on the left.
  4. Check every element: 3Fe + 4H₂O → Fe₃O₄ + 4H₂.

Never change a formula to balance: write 4H₂O, not H₂O₄. Add state symbols where useful: (s) solid, (l) liquid, (g) gas and (aq) dissolved in water. Conditions such as temperature, pressure, a catalyst or sunlight go above or below the arrow.

Combination: two or more substances form a single product. Quick lime (CaO) reacts vigorously with water to give slaked lime, Ca(OH)₂, releasing a lot of heat. Other examples are burning coal (C + O₂ → CO₂) and forming water (2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O).

Whitewash chemistry: slaked lime on a wall reacts slowly with carbon dioxide in the air. After two to three days it forms a thin, shiny layer of calcium carbonate, CaCO₃, the same formula as marble.

Decomposition is the opposite: one reactant breaks into simpler products. It needs energy, so NCERT's decomposition examples are endothermic. Sort them by the energy source:

In a displacement reaction a more reactive element pushes a less reactive one out of its compound. Iron nails in blue copper sulphate solution turn brownish as copper coats them, and the blue colour fades because iron sulphate forms. Zinc and lead also displace copper because they are more reactive. The metals' activity series tells you which displacements can happen.

In a double displacement reaction two compounds exchange ions. Sodium sulphate and barium chloride solutions give a white, insoluble precipitate of barium sulphate, while sodium chloride stays dissolved. Any reaction that forms a precipitate is a precipitation reaction. Mixing lead nitrate and potassium iodide is another: it gives a yellow precipitate of lead iodide.

How to tell them apart: displacement has a lone element among the reactants (Fe + CuSO₄); double displacement has two compounds on each side.

Heating copper in air gives black copper(II) oxide: copper gains oxygen and is oxidised. Passing hydrogen over the hot oxide turns it brown again: copper oxide loses oxygen and is reduced, while hydrogen gains oxygen and is oxidised. Because oxidation and reduction happen together, these are redox reactions.

  • Oxidation: gain of oxygen or loss of hydrogen.
  • Reduction: loss of oxygen or gain of hydrogen.
  • In ZnO + C → Zn + CO, carbon is oxidised and zinc oxide reduced. In MnO₂ + 4HCl → MnCl₂ + 2H₂O + Cl₂, HCl is oxidised and MnO₂ reduced.

Exothermic reactions give out heat: quick lime with water, burning natural gas, respiration (glucose + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water + energy) and vegetable matter rotting into compost. Endothermic reactions absorb energy, as decomposition does.

Oxidation in daily life:

  • Corrosion: iron rusts to a reddish-brown coating, silver turns black and copper green. It damages car bodies, bridges, railings and ships.
  • Rancidity: fats and oils that oxidise change smell and taste. Antioxidants, airtight containers and flushing chip packets with nitrogen slow it down.

Q1. Fe₂O₃ + 2Al → Al₂O₃ + 2Fe is an example of a: medium

✅ Answer: (d) Displacement reaction

💡 Explanation: Aluminium, the more reactive metal, takes oxygen from iron oxide and sets iron free: a displacement reaction. It is also redox. This is NCERT exercise 2.

Q2. White silver chloride turning grey in sunlight is an example of: easy

✅ Answer: (a) Photochemical decomposition

💡 Explanation: Light breaks silver chloride into silver and chlorine. A single reactant giving simpler products is decomposition; here light supplies the energy.

Q3. In 2PbO + C → 2Pb + CO₂, which substance is oxidised? medium

✅ Answer: (c) Carbon

💡 Explanation: Carbon gains oxygen to become CO₂, so carbon is oxidised. Lead oxide loses oxygen, so it is reduced. This is adapted from NCERT exercise 1.

Q4. Which of these is an endothermic process? easy

✅ Answer: (b) Thermal decomposition of limestone

💡 Explanation: Decomposing limestone needs heat to be supplied, so it absorbs energy. The other three release heat and are exothermic, NCERT says.

Q5. Why do chip manufacturers flush packets with nitrogen? easy

✅ Answer: (c) To prevent oxidation and rancidity

💡 Explanation: Nitrogen keeps oxygen away from the fats and oils in chips, so they do not oxidise and turn rancid. NCERT gives this as a way to slow rancidity.

What are the five kinds of chemical reaction in NCERT?

NCERT Class 10 describes combination, decomposition, displacement, double displacement, and oxidation-reduction (redox) reactions. A reaction can belong to more than one type: the thermit reaction is both displacement and redox.

What is the difference between displacement and double displacement?

In displacement, a more reactive element pushes a less reactive one out of its compound, such as iron freeing copper from copper sulphate. In double displacement, two compounds swap ions, such as sodium sulphate and barium chloride forming barium sulphate and sodium chloride.

Is every decomposition reaction endothermic?

NCERT says decomposition needs energy as heat, light or electricity to break the reactant down, and reactions that absorb energy are endothermic. So the examples in NCERT are all endothermic.
