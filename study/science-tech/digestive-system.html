Human Digestive System: Digestive Enzymes and Their Functions, Organ by Organ

Mouth: salivary amylase • Stomach: HCl, pepsin, mucus • Small intestine: bile, trypsin, lipase, intestinal juice • Villi and absorption • Large intestine • Dental caries

SSC Railway State PSC 📅 Last updated: 2026-11-04

The alimentary canal is a single long tube from the mouth to the anus, and each part of it does a different job. Food passes through it in this order:

  1. Mouth: teeth crush food, saliva wets it and starts digesting starch.
  2. Oesophagus (food pipe): carries food to the stomach by muscular waves.
  3. Stomach: mixes food with gastric juice; protein digestion begins in an acidic medium.
  4. Small intestine: the longest part of the canal, where carbohydrates, proteins and fats are completely digested and the products absorbed.
  5. Large intestine: absorbs more water from what is left.
  6. Anus: removes the waste, regulated by the anal sphincter.

Two organs outside the canal pour their secretions into the small intestine: the liver (bile) and the pancreas (pancreatic juice). This page follows the NCERT Class X Science chapter Life Processes, which is the base text for most competitive-exam questions on the topic.

Food has to be broken into small molecules before it can be absorbed. NCERT calls the enzymes that do this "biological catalysts". This table is the one to revise before an exam: for each juice, where it comes from, what it contains and what it acts on.

A common trap: bile contains no digestive enzyme. It helps digestion by emulsifying fats and neutralising acid, so the enzymes can work.

Digestion starts before you swallow. Chewing crushes food into small particles of a similar texture, and saliva wets it so it slides through the soft lining of the canal. The "watering" of the mouth when we see food we like is saliva, secreted by the salivary glands. Saliva contains salivary amylase, which breaks starch down to simple sugar. The muscular tongue mixes food with saliva while we chew.

A simple test shows this. NCERT's Activity 5.3 adds saliva to one test tube of starch solution and not the other. After 20 to 30 minutes, iodine turns the starch-only tube blue-black but not the one with saliva, because the amylase has digested the starch.

Food is moved along the whole gut by peristalsis: the muscles of the canal's lining contract rhythmically and push it forward. The oesophagus carries it from the mouth to the stomach this way.

The stomach is a large organ that expands as food enters. Its muscular walls churn food with digestive juice from the gastric glands in its wall. These glands release three things:

  • Hydrochloric acid, which makes the stomach acidic. Pepsin only works in an acidic medium.
  • Pepsin, a protein-digesting enzyme.
  • Mucus, which protects the stomach's inner lining from the acid under normal conditions. "Acidity" complaints relate to this balance.

Food leaves the stomach through a sphincter muscle that releases it into the small intestine a little at a time.

The small intestine is the longest part of the alimentary canal. It fits into the abdomen only because it is extensively coiled. NCERT calls it the site of the complete digestion of carbohydrates, proteins and fats. Three secretions act here, in this order.

  1. Bile from the liver. Food arriving from the stomach is acidic, but pancreatic enzymes need an alkaline medium. Bile makes it alkaline. Bile salts also break large fat globules into smaller ones (emulsification), which gives the enzymes more surface to act on. NCERT compares this to the way soap breaks up dirt.
  2. Pancreatic juice from the pancreas. Contains trypsin for digesting proteins and lipase for breaking down emulsified fats.
  3. Intestinal juice from glands in the intestinal wall. Its enzymes complete the job: proteins to amino acids, complex carbohydrates to glucose, and fats to fatty acids and glycerol.

Intestine length depends on diet. Grass-eating herbivores need a longer small intestine to digest cellulose. Carnivores such as tigers have a shorter one, because meat is easier to digest.

The small intestine's inner lining has many finger-like projections called villi. They greatly increase the surface area for absorption. Villi are richly supplied with blood vessels, which carry the absorbed food to every cell of the body. There it is used to release energy, build new tissue and repair old tissue. How blood carries it is covered on the circulatory system.

Whatever is not absorbed passes to the large intestine, whose wall absorbs more water from it. The remaining waste leaves through the anus, controlled by the anal sphincter.

Dental caries, or tooth decay, gradually softens the enamel and dentine. It starts when bacteria acting on sugars produce acids that demineralise the enamel. Bacteria and food particles stick together on the teeth as dental plaque. Because plaque covers the tooth, saliva cannot reach the surface to neutralise the acid. Brushing after eating removes plaque before the acid forms. Left untreated, microorganisms can reach the pulp and cause inflammation and infection.

  • Starch digestion begins in the mouth (salivary amylase). Protein digestion begins in the stomach (pepsin). Fat digestion happens in the small intestine (lipase, after bile emulsifies the fat).
  • Complete digestion of all three nutrients happens in the small intestine, which is also where most absorption takes place.
  • The end products are glucose, amino acids, and fatty acids with glycerol.
  • The liver's bile has no enzyme. The pancreas supplies both trypsin and lipase.
  • Hydrochloric acid does not digest food itself. It provides the acidic medium for pepsin.
  • Villi increase the surface area for absorption. The large intestine mainly absorbs water.

Q1. Which enzyme begins the digestion of starch in the mouth? easy

✅ Answer: (b) Salivary amylase

💡 Explanation: Saliva contains salivary amylase, which breaks starch down to simple sugar. Pepsin and trypsin act on proteins and lipase on fats.

Q2. What is the role of hydrochloric acid in the stomach? easy

✅ Answer: (c) It creates an acidic medium for pepsin

💡 Explanation: NCERT says hydrochloric acid creates an acidic medium which facilitates the action of pepsin, the protein-digesting enzyme.

Q3. Bile helps in digestion mainly by: medium

✅ Answer: (a) Breaking fat globules into smaller globules

💡 Explanation: Bile salts emulsify fats, breaking large globules into smaller ones so that lipase can act. Bile also makes the acidic food alkaline. It contains no digestive enzyme.

Q4. Which pair of enzymes is found in pancreatic juice? medium

✅ Answer: (d) Trypsin and lipase

💡 Explanation: The pancreas secretes pancreatic juice with trypsin for proteins and lipase for emulsified fats. Pepsin is a stomach enzyme.

Q5. Why do herbivores have a longer small intestine than carnivores? easy

✅ Answer: (b) To digest cellulose

💡 Explanation: Grass-eating herbivores need a longer small intestine to digest cellulose. Meat is easier to digest, so carnivores such as tigers have a shorter one.

Where does the digestion of fats take place?

In the small intestine. Bile from the liver first breaks fat into small globules, then lipase from the pancreas breaks the emulsified fat down, and intestinal enzymes finish the job, producing fatty acids and glycerol.

What are the end products of digestion?

Proteins end as amino acids, complex carbohydrates as glucose, and fats as fatty acids and glycerol. These are absorbed through the villi of the small intestine into the blood.
