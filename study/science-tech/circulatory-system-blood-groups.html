Circulatory System and Blood Groups: Blood Components, ABO and Rh Groups, the Heart, the Cardiac Cycle and Double Circulation

Plasma 55%, formed elements 45% • RBC, WBC, platelets • ABO table • O universal donor, AB universal recipient • Rh and erythroblastosis foetalis • Four-chambered heart • SAN pacemaker • 72 beats, 0.8 s cycle, 5 L output

SSC Railway 📅 Last updated: 2026-11-04

Blood is a fluid connective tissue. NCERT's Class XI Biology splits it into two parts.

  • Plasma (about 55 per cent): a straw-coloured, viscous fluid that is 90-92 per cent water and 6-8 per cent protein. Its main proteins are fibrinogen (for clotting), globulins (for defence) and albumins (for osmotic balance). It also carries minerals, glucose, amino acids and lipids. Plasma without the clotting factors is called serum.
  • Formed elements (about 45 per cent): erythrocytes, leucocytes and platelets.

Neutrophils and monocytes are phagocytic and destroy invading organisms. Basophils release histamine, serotonin and heparin in inflammation, and eosinophils resist infections and are linked to allergies.

The ABO system rests on two surface antigens, A and B, on the RBCs, and two natural antibodies in the plasma. A donor's blood must be matched to the recipient's, or the RBCs clump and are destroyed. NCERT's Table 15.1 sets out the four groups:

Group O blood can be given to anyone, so O individuals are universal donors. Group AB persons can accept blood from all groups, so they are universal recipients. Exam questions on blood groups and the universal donor usually turn on this table: read the antigen column for donation and the antibody column for receiving.

A second antigen, the Rh antigen, is named after a similar one in Rhesus monkeys. Nearly 80 per cent of humans carry it and are Rh positive; the rest are Rh negative. An Rh-negative person exposed to Rh-positive blood makes antibodies against it, so the Rh group must also be matched before a transfusion.

A special case arises in pregnancy. If an Rh-negative mother carries an Rh-positive foetus, the placenta keeps the two bloods apart in the first pregnancy, but at delivery the mother may be exposed to a little foetal blood and start making Rh antibodies. In later pregnancies these antibodies can leak into an Rh-positive foetus and destroy its RBCs, causing severe anaemia and jaundice or even death. This is erythroblastosis foetalis. NCERT notes it can be prevented by giving the mother anti-Rh antibodies right after the first delivery.

When we are injured, blood coagulates to prevent heavy loss. The clot is a network of fibrin threads trapping dead and damaged formed elements. In a cascade of reactions, the enzyme complex thrombokinase helps convert prothrombin into thrombin, and thrombin converts inactive fibrinogen into fibrin. Calcium ions play a very important role.

Some water and small dissolved substances leave the capillaries as tissue fluid. The lymphatic system collects it and returns it to the major veins; the fluid inside is lymph, colourless and rich in lymphocytes. Fats are absorbed through lymph in the lacteals of the intestinal villi.

Arthropods and molluscs have an open circulatory system; annelids and chordates have a closed one. Among vertebrates, fishes have a 2-chambered heart, amphibians and reptiles (except crocodiles) a 3-chambered heart, and crocodiles, birds and mammals a 4-chambered heart.

  • The human heart lies in the thoracic cavity between the lungs, slightly tilted to the left, about the size of a clenched fist, inside a double-walled pericardium.
  • Two small upper atria and two larger lower ventricles; ventricle walls are much thicker.
  • Valves: tricuspid (right atrium to right ventricle), bicuspid or mitral (left side), and semilunar valves at the pulmonary artery and aorta. They allow flow in one direction only.
  • Nodal tissue: the sino-atrial node (SAN) in the right atrium generates 70-75 action potentials a minute and is the pacemaker. The impulse passes through the AV node, the AV bundle (bundle of His) and the Purkinje fibres. Because the heart's beat starts in its own muscle, it is called myogenic.

One round of contraction (systole) and relaxation (diastole) of the atria and ventricles is a cardiac cycle. The heart beats 72 times a minute, so each cycle takes 0.8 seconds. Each ventricle pumps about 70 mL per beat (the stroke volume). Stroke volume × heart rate = cardiac output, which averages about 5 litres a minute; for example, 70 mL × 72 = 5,040 mL. The first heart sound, lub, is the closing of the tricuspid and bicuspid valves; the second, dub, is the closing of the semilunar valves. The ECG records the heart's electrical activity.

Humans have double circulation. In pulmonary circulation, the right ventricle sends deoxygenated blood to the lungs, and oxygenated blood returns to the left atrium. In systemic circulation, the left ventricle sends oxygenated blood through the aorta to the tissues, and deoxygenated blood returns through the vena cava to the right atrium. The hepatic portal vein carries blood from the intestine to the liver first.

Disorders to know: hypertension (repeated readings of 140/90 or more, against a normal 120/80 mm Hg), coronary artery disease (atherosclerosis), angina and heart failure. For related topics see human body and health and vaccines and immunity.

Q1. Why is a person with blood group O called a universal donor? easy

✅ Answer: (b) Their RBCs carry neither antigen A nor antigen B

💡 Explanation: Group O RBCs have no A or B antigen, so the recipient's antibodies have nothing to attack; NCERT's table shows O can be given to A, B, AB and O.

Q2. Which blood group's plasma carries neither anti-A nor anti-B antibodies? medium

✅ Answer: (c) AB

💡 Explanation: Group AB has both antigens on its RBCs and no antibodies in plasma, which is why it can receive blood from all four groups.

Q3. Erythroblastosis foetalis can occur when: medium

✅ Answer: (d) Mother is Rh-negative and foetus is Rh-positive, in a later pregnancy

💡 Explanation: The mother may be exposed to Rh+ blood at the first delivery and make antibodies, which can destroy the RBCs of an Rh+ foetus in later pregnancies.

Q4. Which part of the heart is called the pacemaker? easy

✅ Answer: (a) Sino-atrial node

💡 Explanation: The SAN generates the most action potentials per minute (70-75) and so sets the pace of the heart's activity.

Q5. If the stroke volume is 70 mL and the heart rate is 72 beats per minute, the cardiac output is about: medium

✅ Answer: (c) 5 litres per minute

💡 Explanation: Cardiac output = stroke volume × heart rate = 70 × 72 = 5,040 mL, about 5 litres per minute, the average NCERT gives.

Which is the universal donor and which is the universal recipient?

Group O is the universal donor because its RBCs carry no A or B antigen. Group AB is the universal recipient because its plasma has no anti-A or anti-B antibodies. The Rh group must still be matched before a transfusion.

What is the difference between plasma and serum?

Plasma is the fluid part of blood, about 55 per cent of it. Serum is plasma without the clotting factors.

Why is human circulation called double circulation?

Blood passes through the heart twice in each round: the right ventricle pumps it to the lungs (pulmonary circulation) and the left ventricle pumps it to the body (systemic circulation), without oxygenated and deoxygenated blood mixing.
