General Science Chemistry MCQs: 30 Questions on Mixtures, the Atom and Chemical Bonding With Explained Answers

These 30 original questions cover three chemistry chapters of NCERT's 2026-27 Class 9 science textbook "Exploration": mixtures and their separation (chapter 5), the structure of the atom (chapter 8), and atoms, bonding and formulae (chapter 9). Questions 1 to 10 are on mixtures, 11 to 20 on the atom, and 21 to 30 on bonding and formulae.

A few questions need simple arithmetic: a weighted average, a mass number or a formula mass. Use these atomic masses: H = 1, C = 12, N = 14, O = 16, Na = 23, Mg = 24, Cl = 35.5 and Ca = 40 (all in u).

Q1. A mixture of sand and water is: easy

✅ Answer: (b) Heterogeneous

💡 Explanation: The sand particles stay visible and settle, so the mixture is not uniform throughout.

Q2. Particle size in a true solution is: easy

✅ Answer: (a) Less than 1 nm

💡 Explanation: NCERT gives solute particles under 1 nm, colloids 1 to 1000 nm and suspensions above 1000 nm.

Q3. Which of these shows the Tyndall effect? easy

✅ Answer: (d) Milk

💡 Explanation: Milk is a colloid and scatters a light beam; true solutions do not scatter light.

Q4. Acetone (about 56 °C) and water (100 °C) are separated by: medium

✅ Answer: (c) Distillation

💡 Explanation: They are miscible and their boiling points differ by more than 25 °C, so simple distillation works.

Q5. Crude petroleum is separated into fractions by: medium

✅ Answer: (b) Fractional distillation

💡 Explanation: Its components have boiling points less than 25 °C apart, which needs fractional distillation.

Q6. Camphor can be separated from sand by: easy

✅ Answer: (a) Sublimation

💡 Explanation: Camphor turns straight into vapour on heating while sand stays behind in the dish.

Q7. Alum is added to muddy water to: easy

✅ Answer: (d) Make fine particles clump and settle

💡 Explanation: Alum acts as a coagulant, so the clumps settle and can be decanted or filtered.

Q8. 10 g of salt dissolved in 90 g of water gives a solution of: medium

✅ Answer: (c) 10% m/m

💡 Explanation: Mass percent uses the solution's mass: 10 ÷ (10 + 90) × 100 gives 10 per cent.

Q9. Butter is an example of a: medium

✅ Answer: (b) Water-in-oil emulsion

💡 Explanation: NCERT lists butter, body lotion and cold cream as water-in-oil emulsions.

Q10. Brass is an alloy of copper and: easy

✅ Answer: (a) Zinc

💡 Explanation: NCERT gives brass as about 80 per cent copper and 20 per cent zinc.

Q11. The electron was discovered by: easy

✅ Answer: (d) J. J. Thomson

💡 Explanation: Thomson's 1897 cathode ray experiments revealed negatively charged particles, later called electrons.

Q12. The plum pudding model of the atom was given by: easy

✅ Answer: (c) J. J. Thomson

💡 Explanation: Thomson pictured electrons spread through a sphere of positive charge.

Q13. In the gold foil experiment, most alpha particles passed straight through. This showed: medium

✅ Answer: (b) Most of the atom is empty space

💡 Explanation: Only a tiny dense nucleus could deflect some particles, so most of the atom is empty.

Q14. The neutron was discovered by: easy

✅ Answer: (a) James Chadwick in 1932

💡 Explanation: Chadwick, a student of Rutherford, found the uncharged particle in 1932.

Q15. The maximum number of electrons in the L shell is: easy

✅ Answer: (d) 8

💡 Explanation: Using 2n² with n = 2 gives 8 electrons for the L shell.

Q16. Rutherford's model could not explain: medium

✅ Answer: (c) The stability of the atom

💡 Explanation: An orbiting electron should lose energy and fall into the nucleus, which real atoms do not do.

Q17. An atom with 17 protons and 18 neutrons has mass number: easy

✅ Answer: (b) 35

💡 Explanation: Mass number is protons plus neutrons: 17 + 18 = 35.

Q18. Atoms with the same atomic number but different mass numbers are: easy

✅ Answer: (a) Isotopes

💡 Explanation: Isotopes share the proton count; isobars share the mass number instead.

Q19. Calcium-40 and argon-40 are: medium

✅ Answer: (d) Isobars

💡 Explanation: They have the same mass number 40 but different atomic numbers, 20 and 18.

Q20. Which isotope is used in radiation treatment for cancer? medium

✅ Answer: (c) Cobalt-60

💡 Explanation: NCERT names cobalt-60 for cancer radiotherapy; carbon-14 is used for dating.

Q21. The law of conservation of mass was proposed by: easy

✅ Answer: (b) Antoine Lavoisier

💡 Explanation: Lavoisier, called the Father of Modern Chemistry, proposed that mass is conserved in reactions.

Q22. Water always has hydrogen and oxygen in the mass ratio: medium

✅ Answer: (a) 1:8

💡 Explanation: By Proust's law of constant proportions, 9 g of water gives 1 g hydrogen and 8 g oxygen.

Q23. An ionic bond forms by: easy

✅ Answer: (d) Transfer of electrons

💡 Explanation: One atom loses electrons, another gains them, and the ions attract each other.

Q24. The bond in an oxygen molecule (O₂) is a: medium

✅ Answer: (c) Double covalent bond

💡 Explanation: Each oxygen atom shares two electrons, giving two shared pairs.

Q25. Sugar solution does not conduct electricity because sugar: medium

✅ Answer: (b) Gives no ions in solution

💡 Explanation: Sugar is covalent; it dissolves but releases no free ions to carry current.

Q26. The correct name of N₂O₅ is: medium

✅ Answer: (a) Dinitrogen pentoxide

💡 Explanation: Di and penta give the counts; the final a is dropped before oxide.

Q27. Using valencies, aluminium (3) and oxygen (2) form: hard

✅ Answer: (d) Al₂O₃

💡 Explanation: Criss-crossing the valencies 3 and 2 gives two aluminium and three oxygen atoms.

Q28. The molecular mass of water (H₂O) is: easy

✅ Answer: (c) 18 u

💡 Explanation: Two hydrogens give 2 u and one oxygen gives 16 u, so 18 u in total.

Q29. The formula unit mass of MgCl₂ is: hard

✅ Answer: (b) 95 u

💡 Explanation: One magnesium (24 u) plus two chlorine atoms (2 × 35.5 u) gives 95 u.

Q30. Which element exists only as single atoms because its atoms are already stable? medium

✅ Answer: (a) Helium

💡 Explanation: Helium has a full K-shell of two electrons, so it does not form molecules.

Which chapters does this set cover?

What atomic masses do the calculation questions use?
