Ionic vs Covalent Bonds: Formation, Properties, Naming, Formulae and the Laws of Chemical Combination

Conservation of mass • Constant proportions • Dalton's postulates • Single and double covalent bonds • Cations and anions • Naming with prefixes • Valency table • Criss-cross formulae • Molecular and formula unit mass

SSC Railways 📅 Last updated: 2026-11-06

Atoms bond to reach a stable outer shell, an octet of 8 electrons, or 2 if the K-shell is outermost. They do it in one of two ways. NCERT's Class 9 science textbook "Exploration" (chapter 9, "Atomic Foundations of Matter") compares them:

FeatureIonic bondCovalent bond
How it formsTransfer of electrons from one atom to anotherSharing of electron pairs between atoms
What holds itElectrostatic attraction between cations and anionsThe shared pair attracting both nuclei
Typical partnersMetal with non-metalAtoms of one element (H₂, Cl₂, O₂) or of different elements (HCl, H₂O)
Structure3-D crystals, not moleculesDiscrete molecules
SolubilityGenerally soluble in water, insoluble in kerosene and petrolMostly insoluble in water, soluble in kerosene and petrol
Electrical conductivityConducts when dissolved in water, not as a solidDoes not conduct, even when dissolved (sugar)
Melting and boiling pointsGenerally highUsually low
ExamplesSodium chloride, copper sulfateWater, hydrogen chloride, camphor, naphthalene

This answers NCERT's opening puzzle: salt solution conducts electricity because it has free ions, while sugar solution does not, because sugar gives no ions.

The baking soda and vinegar activity shows why a closed container matters. In an open flask, the carbon dioxide escapes and the final reading drops. With a balloon sealing the flask, the mass stays the same.

Using the laws: 12 g of carbon gives 44 g of carbon dioxide, so 2.4 g of carbon gives 44 × 2.4 ÷ 12 = 8.8 g. Sodium chloride has sodium and chlorine in the ratio 23:35.5, so 46 g of sodium needs 35.5 × 46 ÷ 23 = 71 g of chlorine.

Dalton presented his atomic theory in 1808 to explain both laws. His postulates:

  • All matter is made of tiny atoms, which take part in chemical reactions.
  • Atoms are indivisible and cannot be created or destroyed in a chemical reaction.
  • Atoms of one element are identical in mass and chemical properties; atoms of different elements differ.
  • Atoms combine in simple whole-number ratios to form compounds.
  • The relative number and kinds of atoms are constant in a given compound.

Later discoveries of subatomic particles and isotopes changed parts of this picture; see atomic structure.

A molecule is an electrically neutral group of more than one atom that can exist independently and shows the properties of the substance. Helium is an exception that exists as single atoms because its atoms are already stable.

Naming covalent compounds uses prefixes: mono (1), di (2), tri (3), tetra (4), penta (5), hexa (6). The second element ends in -ide, and mono is dropped for the first element.

  • CO is carbon monoxide; CO₂ is carbon dioxide, not monocarbon dioxide.
  • PCl₃ is phosphorus trichloride; SF₆ is sulfur hexafluoride.
  • N₂O₄ is dinitrogen tetroxide, not tetraoxide, because the final "a" is dropped before a vowel.
  • No prefix goes before hydrogen: H₂S is hydrogen sulfide.
  • Some keep common names: H₂O is water and NH₃ is ammonia.

Sodium (2, 8, 1) loses its one valence electron and becomes Na⁺: 11 protons, 10 electrons. Chlorine (2, 8, 7) gains one electron and becomes the chloride anion Cl⁻. The electrostatic attraction between these oppositely charged ions is the ionic bond. In a sodium chloride crystal, each Na⁺ is surrounded by six Cl⁻ ions and each Cl⁻ by six Na⁺ ions.

Atoms with fewer than four valence electrons generally give them up; those with more than four usually gain or share. Sulfur, with six, gains two to become S²⁻.

Naming: the cation comes first, then the anion. Simple anions end in -ide (chloride, oxide, sulfide). Polyatomic ions, made of two or more elements, usually do not.

Criss-cross method: write the symbols, write their valencies, then swap the valencies to become subscripts. A subscript of 1 is not written. Hydrogen (1) with chlorine (1) gives HCl. Hydrogen (1) with sulfur (2) gives H₂S.

A covalent formula shows the actual atoms in a molecule. An ionic formula shows only the simplest whole-number ratio of ions, called a formula unit, because ionic compounds form crystals rather than molecules.

  • Lavoisier: conservation of mass. Proust: constant proportions (H:O in water = 1:8 by mass).
  • Ionic bond: electron transfer; covalent bond: electron sharing.
  • O₂ has a double bond; H₂, Cl₂ and HCl have single bonds.
  • Ionic compounds conduct electricity in solution, not as solids; sugar solution does not conduct.
  • Ionic compounds have formula unit mass, not molecular mass.

Q1. An ionic bond is formed by: easy

✅ Answer: (b) Transfer of electrons

💡 Explanation: One atom loses electrons and another gains them, and the ions attract each other.

Q2. The law of conservation of mass was proposed by: easy

✅ Answer: (a) Lavoisier

💡 Explanation: Antoine Lavoisier, called the Father of Modern Chemistry, proposed this law.

Q3. Hydrogen and oxygen in pure water are in the mass ratio: medium

✅ Answer: (d) 1:8

💡 Explanation: NCERT: water from any source always has hydrogen and oxygen in the mass ratio 1:8.

Q4. The oxygen molecule O₂ has: medium

✅ Answer: (c) A double bond

💡 Explanation: Each oxygen atom shares two electrons, giving two shared pairs, a double bond.

Q5. Sugar solution does not conduct electricity because: medium

✅ Answer: (b) Sugar gives no ions in solution

💡 Explanation: Sugar is covalent; it dissolves in water but provides no free ions to carry current.

Q6. The correct name of N₂O₄ is: medium

✅ Answer: (c) Dinitrogen tetroxide

💡 Explanation: Prefixes give di and tetra; the final a is dropped before oxide, so tetroxide.

Q7. The formula unit mass of Na₂O (Na = 23 u, O = 16 u) is: easy

✅ Answer: (a) 62 u

💡 Explanation: Two sodium atoms give 46 u and one oxygen gives 16 u, totalling 62 u.

Q8. Using criss-cross valencies, hydrogen and sulfur form: easy

✅ Answer: (d) H₂S

💡 Explanation: Hydrogen has valency 1 and sulfur 2; swapping them gives H₂S.

Why does salt water conduct electricity but sugar water does not?

Salt is ionic and releases free sodium and chloride ions in water, which carry current. Sugar is covalent and gives no ions in solution.

Why do ionic compounds have formula unit mass instead of molecular mass?

Ionic compounds form three-dimensional crystals rather than separate molecules, so their formula only shows the simplest whole-number ratio of ions.
