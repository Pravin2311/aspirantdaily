Human Eye Defects and Their Correction: Myopia, Hypermetropia, Presbyopia and Cataract

Myopia: image before retina, concave lens • Hypermetropia: image behind retina, convex lens • Presbyopia: ageing, often bifocals • Near point 25 cm, far point infinity

SSC Railway 📅 Last updated: 2026-11-02

NCERT names three common refractive defects of vision: myopia, hypermetropia and presbyopia. Each is corrected with a suitable spherical lens. The quickest way to answer any question is to ask where the image falls: in front of the retina, behind it, or out of reach because the lens can no longer adjust.

Bifocals: the common type has a concave upper part for distant vision and a convex lower part for near vision. Today contact lenses and surgery can also correct refractive defects.

Memory hook: myopia means seeing only what is near "my" face, and it needs the lens that spreads light out (concave, negative power). Hypermetropia needs the lens that adds focusing power (convex, positive power).

The eyeball is roughly spherical, about 2.3 cm across. Accommodation is the lens's ability to change its focal length. For distant objects the ciliary muscles relax, the lens becomes thin and the focal length increases. For near objects they contract, the lens thickens and the focal length decreases. It cannot shrink below a limit, which is why a page held too close looks blurred.

A normal eye sees clearly from its near point, about 25 cm (the least distance of distinct vision) to its far point at infinity.

Lens power is P = 1/f, with f in metres, in dioptres. Concave lenses have negative power and convex lenses positive power, so a prescription's sign tells you the defect.

  • Myopia: choose a concave lens whose focal length equals the far point. Far point 80 cm gives f = −0.8 m and P = −1.25 D.
  • Distant vision −5.5 D: f = 1/(−5.5) ≈ −0.18 m, a concave lens, so the person is myopic.
  • Near vision +1.5 D: f = 1/1.5 ≈ +0.67 m, a convex lens for reading.

Q1. Myopia is corrected using a: easy

✅ Answer: (a) Concave lens

💡 Explanation: In myopia the image of a distant object forms in front of the retina. A diverging concave lens of suitable power moves it back onto the retina.

Q2. The change in focal length of the eye lens is caused by the: easy

✅ Answer: (d) Ciliary muscles

💡 Explanation: NCERT says the ciliary muscles change the curvature of the eye lens. Relaxed muscles make the lens thin for distant vision; contracted muscles make it thick for near vision.

Q3. Which is NOT a cause of hypermetropia? medium

✅ Answer: (c) Elongation of the eyeball

💡 Explanation: Elongation of the eyeball is a cause of myopia. NCERT gives hypermetropia's causes as a too-long focal length or a too-small eyeball.

Q4. The far point of a myopic eye is 80 cm. What lens power corrects it? hard

✅ Answer: (b) −1.25 D

💡 Explanation: A concave lens must image distant objects at 80 cm, so f = −0.8 m and P = 1/f = −1.25 dioptre. Myopia always needs a negative (concave) power.

Q5. Why does the sky look dark to an astronaut at very high altitude? medium

✅ Answer: (c) There is almost no air to scatter sunlight

💡 Explanation: NCERT explains that without the atmosphere there would be no scattering, and scattering is not prominent at great heights, so the sky appears dark.

What is the difference between myopia and hypermetropia?

Myopia (near-sightedness) blurs distant objects because their image forms in front of the retina; it is corrected with a concave lens. Hypermetropia (far-sightedness) blurs near objects because their image forms behind the retina; it is corrected with a convex lens.

Is presbyopia the same as hypermetropia?

No. Both make near objects hard to see, but presbyopia comes with age as the ciliary muscles weaken and the lens stiffens. Hypermetropia is caused by a too-long focal length or a small eyeball. People with both near and far problems often use bifocals.

What are the near point and far point of a normal eye?

The near point, or least distance of distinct vision, is about 25 cm for a young adult. The far point is infinity, so a normal eye sees clearly anything from 25 cm to infinity.
