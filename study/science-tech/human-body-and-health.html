🫀 Human Body, Nutrition & Diseases

Body Systems • Blood Groups • Vitamins • Diseases by Pathogen • Vaccines

High Yield Science & Tech 📅 Last updated: Oct 2026
  • Questions on the human body appear in nearly every SSC, RRB and State PSC paper, mostly as one-line facts and matching pairs.
  • An adult human has 206 bones; normal body temperature is about 37°C (98.6°F) and normal blood pressure about 120/80 mm Hg.
  • Learn diseases by pathogen type — bacteria, viruses, protozoa, fungi — because exams ask "which of these is NOT a viral disease?".

Blood Groups

The ABO system was discovered by Karl Landsteiner (1900–01). Group O negative is the universal donor (no A or B antigens on its red cells) and AB positive the universal recipient (no anti-A or anti-B antibodies in its plasma). If an Rh-negative mother carries an Rh-positive baby, her antibodies can harm a later Rh-positive pregnancy — a condition called erythroblastosis fetalis.

Vaccines and Immunisation

  • Edward Jenner developed the first vaccine, against smallpox, in 1796; smallpox was declared eradicated in 1980.
  • Louis Pasteur made the rabies vaccine (1885) and developed pasteurisation.
  • BCG protects against tuberculosis. Jonas Salk developed the injected polio vaccine and Albert Sabin the oral one.
  • WHO certified India polio-free on March 27, 2014. Mission Indradhanush (2014) aims at full immunisation of children and pregnant women.
  • Alexander Fleming discovered penicillin, the first antibiotic, in 1928. Antibiotics work against bacteria, not viruses.
  1. Odd one out: "Which is not a viral disease?" — typhoid, cholera or TB are the usual answers.
  2. Vitamin ↔ disease matching, often with chemical names (thiamine, ascorbic acid, calciferol).
  3. Vector questions: Anopheles → malaria, Aedes → dengue, Culex → filariasis, sandfly → kala-azar.

Q1. Scurvy is caused by a deficiency of:

✅ Answer: (c) Vitamin C

💡 Explanation: Vitamin C (ascorbic acid) deficiency causes scurvy, with bleeding gums.

Q2. Which of these diseases is caused by bacteria?

✅ Answer: (a) Typhoid

💡 Explanation: Typhoid is caused by Salmonella Typhi; the others are viral.

Q3. The universal donor blood group is:

✅ Answer: (b) O negative

💡 Explanation: O-negative red cells carry no A, B or Rh antigens.

Q4. Kala-azar is transmitted by:

✅ Answer: (d) Sandfly

💡 Explanation: Kala-azar (visceral leishmaniasis) is spread by sandflies.

Q5. The smallest bone in the human body is the:

✅ Answer: (a) Stapes

💡 Explanation: The stapes in the middle ear is the smallest bone.

🔁 Revise in 60 Seconds

  • 206 bones; 37°C; 120/80; RBC life ~120 days.
  • A night blindness, B1 beriberi, B3 pellagra, C scurvy, D rickets, K clotting.
  • Bacterial: TB, cholera, typhoid, tetanus. Viral: dengue, polio, rabies, measles.
  • Jenner smallpox (1796), Pasteur rabies (1885), Fleming penicillin (1928); India polio-free 2014.

📌 Prelims Traps to Avoid

  • ✘ Malaria is caused by a protozoan, not a virus or bacterium.
  • ✘ Antibiotics do not work against viral diseases such as flu or COVID-19.
  • ✘ Vitamin D is made in the skin with sunlight; vitamin B12 comes almost only from animal foods.

Which vitamin deficiency causes night blindness?

Vitamin A (retinol). Severe deficiency can also cause xerophthalmia, a drying of the eye.

Which diseases are caused by protozoa?

Malaria (Plasmodium), kala-azar (Leishmania), amoebic dysentery (Entamoeba) and sleeping sickness (Trypanosoma).
