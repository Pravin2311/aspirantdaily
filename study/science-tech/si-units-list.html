SI Units List: The 7 Base Units, the 22 Derived Units with Special Names, and the SI Prefixes

Second, metre, kilogram, ampere, kelvin, mole, candela • 2019 definitions • Newton to katal • Common units without special names • Prefixes 10⁻³⁰ to 10³⁰

The International System of Units (SI) is maintained by the International Bureau of Weights and Measures (BIPM). Its official text is the SI Brochure, 9th edition. Every unit in physics, chemistry and engineering can be built from seven base units. Exams ask for the unit of a quantity, its symbol, or which quantity a unit measures, so this table is the place to start.

The last column is how the units are defined today. Since the 2019 revision, each unit comes from a fixed value of a constant of nature, not from a physical object. The kilogram used to be fixed as the mass of the international prototype of the kilogram, a physical object. It is now defined through the Planck constant. The SI Brochure states that the numerical values of these seven defining constants "have no uncertainty".

Two traps: the base unit of mass is the kilogram, not the gram, and the unit of temperature is the kelvin, not the "degree kelvin".

The degree Celsius is the same size as the kelvin. A temperature difference of 1 °C equals a difference of 1 K, so only the starting point differs.

Torque and energy share the same base units, kg m² s⁻². The SI Brochure lists torque as the newton metre and keeps the special name joule for energy, which stops the two being confused.

Case matters. M (mega) and m (milli) differ by a factor of 10⁹, and P (peta) and p (pico) by 10²⁷. Prefixes are attached to "gram", not "kilogram": a millionth of a kilogram is a milligram (mg), not a "microkilogram". Prefixes also mean strict powers of 10, so a kilobit is 1000 bits, not 1024.

Q1. Which of these is NOT an SI base unit? easy

✅ Answer: (c) Newton

💡 Explanation: The newton (kg m s⁻²) is a derived unit with a special name. The seven base units are the second, metre, kilogram, ampere, kelvin, mole and candela.

Q2. The SI unit of luminous intensity is the: easy

✅ Answer: (b) Candela

💡 Explanation: Luminous intensity is a base quantity measured in candela (cd). The lumen (cd sr) measures luminous flux and the lux (lm/m²) measures illuminance.

Q3. One tesla, the unit of magnetic flux density, is equal to: medium

✅ Answer: (a) Wb/m²

💡 Explanation: Table 4 of the SI Brochure gives the tesla as Wb/m². Wb/A is the henry, V/A the ohm and N m the joule (or torque).

Q4. Which prefix stands for 10³⁰? medium

✅ Answer: (d) Quetta

💡 Explanation: Quetta (Q) is 10³⁰ and quecto (q) is 10⁻³⁰. Ronna is 10²⁷, yotta 10²⁴ and zetta 10²¹.

Q5. The kilogram is now defined by fixing the value of the: hard

✅ Answer: (a) Planck constant

💡 Explanation: Since the 2019 revision the kilogram is defined through the Planck constant, h = 6.626 070 15 × 10⁻³⁴ J s. The Avogadro constant defines the mole and the Boltzmann constant the kelvin.

How many SI base units are there?

Seven: second (time), metre (length), kilogram (mass), ampere (electric current), kelvin (temperature), mole (amount of substance) and candela (luminous intensity).

Is the degree Celsius an SI unit?

Yes. It is one of the 22 derived units with a special name, and it is the same size as the kelvin. A temperature in kelvin is the Celsius value plus 273.15.
