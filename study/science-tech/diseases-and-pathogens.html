Diseases Caused by Bacteria, Viruses, Protozoa, Worms and Fungi: Pathogen, Spread, Symptoms and Prevention

Bacteria: typhoid, pneumonia • Virus: common cold, AIDS (HIV) • Protozoa: malaria, amoebiasis • Helminths: ascariasis, filariasis • Fungi: ringworm

SSC Railway State PSC 📅 Last updated: 2026-11-01

NCERT calls disease-causing organisms pathogens. They include bacteria, viruses, fungi, protozoans and helminths (parasitic worms). Infectious diseases spread from person to person. Among non-infectious diseases, cancer is the major cause of death.

NCERT also names dysentery, plague and diphtheria as other bacterial diseases.

  1. An infected female Anopheles mosquito bites and injects sporozoites, the infectious form.
  2. The parasites multiply first in liver cells, then attack and rupture red blood cells.
  3. Each rupture releases haemozoin, which causes chills and high fever every three to four days.
  4. A mosquito biting an infected person takes up the parasites, which develop in its body and gather as sporozoites in its salivary glands.

The parasite needs two hosts, humans and mosquitoes. The female Anopheles is also the vector, or transmitting agent.

AIDS stands for Acquired Immuno Deficiency Syndrome. It was first reported in 1981. HIV first enters macrophages, where the enzyme reverse transcriptase copies its RNA genome into DNA, so the macrophage becomes an "HIV factory". The virus then enters helper T-lymphocytes and multiplies, and their numbers keep falling. There is a time lag between infection and symptoms, usually 5 to 10 years. HIV spreads only through body fluids, so infected people should not be isolated from family and society.

  • Food- and water-borne diseases (typhoid, amoebiasis, ascariasis): personal hygiene, clean drinking water and food, proper disposal of waste and excreta, and regular cleaning of water reservoirs.
  • Air-borne diseases (pneumonia, common cold): also avoid close contact with infected people and their belongings.
  • Vector-borne diseases (malaria, filariasis): control mosquitoes and their breeding places. Do not let water stagnate, clean coolers, use nets and wire mesh, spray insecticides, and put Gambusia fish in ponds to eat larvae. NCERT stresses this because of outbreaks of dengue and chikungunya, spread by Aedes mosquitoes.
  • Vaccines: they eradicated smallpox and have largely controlled polio, diphtheria, pneumonia and tetanus.

Q1. The Widal test is used to confirm: easy

✅ Answer: (b) Typhoid

💡 Explanation: NCERT says typhoid fever, caused by Salmonella typhi, could be confirmed by the Widal test.

Q2. Which disease is caused by a protozoan? easy

✅ Answer: (d) Amoebiasis

💡 Explanation: Entamoeba histolytica, a protozoan, causes amoebiasis. Ringworm is fungal, the common cold is viral and ascariasis is caused by a worm.

Q3. The chills and fever of malaria are linked to the release of: medium

✅ Answer: (a) Haemozoin

💡 Explanation: When Plasmodium ruptures red blood cells, the toxic substance haemozoin is released, causing chills and high fever every three to four days.

Q4. Elephantiasis is caused by: medium

✅ Answer: (c) Wuchereria

💡 Explanation: Wuchereria (W. bancrofti and W. malayi), the filarial worms, cause filariasis or elephantiasis, spread by female mosquitoes.

Q5. HIV mainly destroys which cells, weakening immunity? medium

✅ Answer: (b) Helper T-lymphocytes

💡 Explanation: HIV multiplies in macrophages and then in helper T-lymphocytes. Their numbers fall progressively, so the immune system weakens.

Which diseases are caused by bacteria and viruses according to NCERT?

Bacterial: typhoid, pneumonia, dysentery, plague and diphtheria. Viral: the common cold (rhinoviruses) and AIDS (HIV). NCERT adds dengue and chikungunya as Aedes-borne diseases without naming the pathogen type.

Why does malaria fever come back every few days?

Plasmodium multiplies inside red blood cells and bursts them in cycles. Each burst releases haemozoin, which brings on chills and high fever, recurring every three to four days.

Which mosquito spreads which disease?

The female Anopheles spreads malaria. Female mosquitoes also carry the filarial worms of elephantiasis. Aedes mosquitoes spread dengue and chikungunya.
