Mendel's Laws and Inheritance Ratios: Monohybrid 3:1, Dihybrid 9:3:3:1, Test Cross, Incomplete Dominance and ABO Blood Groups

Monohybrid F2: 3:1 phenotype, 1:2:1 genotype • Dihybrid F2: 9:3:3:1 • Test cross with recessive parent • Snapdragon 1:2:1 • ABO: three alleles, six genotypes

SSC Railway State PSC 📅 Last updated: 2026-11-01

Gregor Mendel crossed garden peas for seven years, from 1856 to 1863. NCERT notes that this was the first time statistical analysis and mathematical logic were applied to a problem in biology. He chose 14 true-breeding varieties in pairs that differed in just one character. A true-breeding line keeps showing the same trait after generations of self-pollination.

Mendel published in 1865, but his work stayed unrecognised until 1900. That year, de Vries, Correns and von Tschermak independently rediscovered it.

  • Gene: Mendel's "factor", the unit of inheritance. Alleles are slightly different forms of the same gene, such as T and t.
  • Genotype is the genetic makeup (TT, Tt, tt). Phenotype is the visible trait (tall, dwarf).
  • Homozygous: identical alleles (TT or tt). Heterozygous: different alleles (Tt).
LawWhat it saysWhat it explains
Law of Dominance (first law)Factors occur in pairs; in a dissimilar pair, one dominates the otherOnly one parental trait shows in F1; the 3:1 ratio in F2
Law of Segregation (second law)The two alleles separate during gamete formation, so each gamete gets only oneThe recessive trait reappears unchanged in F2
Law of Independent AssortmentWhen two pairs of traits combine in a hybrid, one pair segregates independently of the otherThe 9:3:3:1 ratio of the dihybrid F2
  1. Assign symbols: a capital letter for the dominant allele and the same small letter for the recessive one (T and t, not T and d).
  2. Write the parents' genotypes.
  3. List each parent's gametes. Each gamete gets one allele of each gene. A plant heterozygous for n genes makes 2n gamete types: Tt makes 2, RrYy makes 4, and AaBbCc makes 8.
  4. Draw a Punnett square, the grid devised by Reginald C. Punnett. Put one parent's gametes along the top and the other's down the side, then fill each box.
  5. Count genotypes first, then group them into phenotypes using the dominance rule.

Parents: TT (tall) × tt (dwarf). All gametes are T and t respectively, so every F1 plant is Tt, and tall.

F1 selfed: Tt × Tt. Each parent gives T or t with a 50 per cent chance.

  • Genotypes: 1 TT : 2 Tt : 1 tt, a 1:2:1 ratio
  • Phenotypes: TT and Tt are both tall, so 1 + 2 = 3 tall : 1 dwarf, a 3:1 ratio

Shortcut: (½T + ½t)² = ¼TT + ½Tt + ¼tt, which is how NCERT writes it as a binomial.

Test cross: to tell a TT plant from a Tt plant, cross it with the recessive tt. If all offspring are tall, the plant was TT. If half are tall and half dwarf (1:1), it was Tt.

Parents: RRYY (round, yellow) × rryy (wrinkled, green). The F1 is RrYy, with round yellow seeds.

F1 selfed: each F1 plant makes four gamete types, RY, Ry, rY and ry, each ¼. A 4 × 4 Punnett square has 16 boxes.

Fast route: multiply the two 3:1 ratios, (3 round : 1 wrinkled) × (3 yellow : 1 green):

Genotypes: there are 3 × 3 = 9 genotypes, in the ratio 1:2:1:2:4:2:1:2:1. The 9 round yellow plants alone comprise 1 RRYY + 2 RRYy + 2 RrYY + 4 RrYy = 9. So the genotypic ratio is not 9:3:3:1.

Practice: in an F2 of 160 plants, wrinkled green = 1/16 × 160 = 10. In a monohybrid F2 of 400 plants, tall = 3/4 × 400 = 300.

Incomplete dominance: in snapdragon (Antirrhinum), red RR × white rr gives a pink F1 (Rr). The F2 is 1 red : 2 pink : 1 white. The genotypic ratio is the usual 1:2:1, but now the phenotypes match it, because Rr can be told apart.

Co-dominance: the F1 resembles both parents. In human ABO blood groups, gene I has three alleles: IA, IB and i. IA and IB are each dominant over i, but together they both express, giving group AB.

Three alleles give 3 + 2 + 1 = 6 genotypes and 4 phenotypes. Because more than two alleles govern one character, ABO is also an example of multiple alleles.

One gene, two views: in pea seeds, Bb seeds are round like BB, so B looks dominant. But their starch grains are intermediate in size, so for grain size the alleles show incomplete dominance. NCERT's point is that dominance depends on which phenotype you look at.

Q1. In Mendel's monohybrid cross, the F2 genotypic ratio is: easy

✅ Answer: (b) 1:2:1

💡 Explanation: F2 genotypes are 1/4 TT, 1/2 Tt and 1/4 tt, so 1:2:1. The phenotypic ratio is 3:1 because TT and Tt both look tall.

Q2. A tall plant (Tt) is test-crossed with a dwarf plant (tt). The expected ratio of tall to dwarf is: medium

✅ Answer: (d) 1:1

💡 Explanation: Tt gives T and t gametes in equal numbers; tt gives only t. Offspring are half Tt (tall) and half tt (dwarf), a 1:1 ratio.

Q3. In a dihybrid F2 of 160 plants, how many are expected to be wrinkled and green? medium

✅ Answer: (a) 10

💡 Explanation: Wrinkled green is the double recessive, 1 of 16 parts. 1/16 × 160 = 10.

Q4. Pink snapdragon flowers from red × white parents show: easy

✅ Answer: (c) Incomplete dominance

💡 Explanation: The F1 Rr is in between the parents, so R is not completely dominant. NCERT uses snapdragon as its example of incomplete dominance.

Q5. Parents with blood groups A (IAi) and B (IBi) can have children with: hard

✅ Answer: (d) A, B, AB or O

💡 Explanation: Gametes IA or i from one parent and IB or i from the other give IAIB (AB), IAi (A), IBi (B) and ii (O), each one quarter.

What is the mendel monohybrid ratio?

In the F2 of a monohybrid cross, the phenotypic ratio is 3:1 (dominant to recessive) and the genotypic ratio is 1:2:1 (homozygous dominant, heterozygous, homozygous recessive).

Why does a dihybrid cross give 9:3:3:1?

Each trait segregates 3:1 independently of the other. Multiplying (3:1) by (3:1) gives 9:3:3:1, which is the law of independent assortment in action.

How is co-dominance different from incomplete dominance?

In incomplete dominance the hybrid is in between the parents, as pink snapdragons are. In co-dominance both parental traits show fully, as in blood group AB, where both A and B sugars are present.
