General Science Physics MCQs: 30 Questions on Motion, Work and Energy, and Sound With Explained Answers

These 30 original questions cover three physics chapters of NCERT's 2026-27 Class 9 science textbook "Exploration": motion (chapter 4), work, energy and simple machines (chapter 7), and sound (chapter 10). Questions 1 to 10 are on motion, 11 to 20 on work and energy, and 21 to 30 on sound. Each explanation gives the rule or formula behind the answer.

About a third of the questions need a short calculation. Most use g = 10 m/s² or a speed of sound in air of 340 m/s, as NCERT's own examples do. Learn the table below first.

Q1. Which equation of motion is independent of time? easy

✅ Answer: (a) v² = u² + 2as

💡 Explanation: Eliminating t from the two primary equations gives v² = u² + 2as.

Q2. A car goes from 10 m/s to 15 m/s in 10 s. Its acceleration is: easy

✅ Answer: (b) 0.5 m/s²

💡 Explanation: Acceleration is change in velocity over time: (15 − 10) ÷ 10 = 0.5 m/s².

Q3. 72 km/h expressed in m/s is: easy

✅ Answer: (c) 20 m/s

💡 Explanation: Divide km/h by 3.6 to get m/s: 72 ÷ 3.6 = 20 m/s.

Q4. The slope of a position-time graph gives: easy

✅ Answer: (d) Velocity

💡 Explanation: Slope is rate of change of position with time, which is velocity.

Q5. The area under a velocity-time graph gives: medium

✅ Answer: (a) Displacement

💡 Explanation: NCERT derives s = ut + ½at² from the area under the velocity-time line.

Q6. A body moving in a circle at constant speed is: medium

✅ Answer: (b) Accelerated, as its direction changes

💡 Explanation: Velocity direction changes at every point, so uniform circular motion is accelerated.

Q7. After one complete revolution of radius R, the displacement is: easy

✅ Answer: (c) Zero

💡 Explanation: The body returns to its starting point, so its net change in position is zero.

Q8. With the same braking force, a car at 108 km/h needs how many times the stopping distance it needs at 54 km/h? hard

✅ Answer: (d) 4

💡 Explanation: Stopping distance is u² ÷ 2a, so doubling speed makes it four times longer.

Q9. A ball dropped from rest reaches 39.2 m/s after 4 s. The acceleration is: medium

✅ Answer: (a) 9.8 m/s²

💡 Explanation: Acceleration is 39.2 ÷ 4 = 9.8 m/s², the acceleration due to gravity.

Q10. A runner covers 160 m but ends 40 m from the start. Distance and displacement are: easy

✅ Answer: (b) 160 m and 40 m

💡 Explanation: Distance is the full path; displacement is the net change in position, here 40 m.

Q11. The SI unit of power is the: easy

✅ Answer: (c) Watt

💡 Explanation: Power is work per unit time; 1 watt equals 1 joule per second.

Q12. Work done when a 200 N force moves an object 5 m in the direction of the force is: easy

✅ Answer: (d) 1000 J

💡 Explanation: Work equals force times displacement along it: 200 × 5 = 1000 J.

Q13. No work is done by a force when the displacement is: medium

✅ Answer: (a) Perpendicular to the force

💡 Explanation: A force perpendicular to the displacement does zero work, as when carrying a box on level ground.

Q14. A goalkeeper stopping a moving ball does: medium

✅ Answer: (b) Negative work on the ball

💡 Explanation: Her force is opposite to the ball's displacement, so the work on the ball is negative.

Q15. If the speed of a body is doubled, its kinetic energy becomes: easy

✅ Answer: (c) Four times

💡 Explanation: Kinetic energy is ½mv², so doubling v multiplies it by four.

Q16. Potential energy of a 5 kg mass at 4 m height (g = 10 m/s²) is: easy

✅ Answer: (d) 200 J

💡 Explanation: Using mgh: 5 × 10 × 4 = 200 J of gravitational potential energy.

Q17. 1 horsepower equals: easy

✅ Answer: (a) 746 W

💡 Explanation: NCERT gives one horsepower as 746 watt, a unit once used for engines.

Q18. A 60 kg person climbs 5 m in 10 s (g = 10 m/s²). The power is: hard

✅ Answer: (b) 300 W

💡 Explanation: Work is mgh = 60 × 10 × 5 = 3000 J; divided by 10 s gives 300 W.

Q19. The mechanical advantage of a fixed pulley is: medium

✅ Answer: (c) 1

💡 Explanation: A fixed pulley only changes the direction of effort, so load equals effort.

Q20. In a wheelbarrow, which lies between the fulcrum and the effort? medium

✅ Answer: (d) Load

💡 Explanation: With the load in the middle, a wheelbarrow is a Class II lever.

Q21. Sound cannot travel through: easy

✅ Answer: (a) Vacuum

💡 Explanation: Sound is a mechanical wave and needs a material medium; the bell jar experiment shows this.

Q22. Sound waves in air are: easy

✅ Answer: (b) Longitudinal

💡 Explanation: Air particles vibrate parallel to the direction the sound travels.

Q23. At 15 °C, sound travels fastest in: easy

✅ Answer: (c) Steel

💡 Explanation: NCERT's table gives about 5000 m/s in steel, 1500 in water and 340 in air.

Q24. The human audible range is: easy

✅ Answer: (d) 20 Hz to 20 kHz

💡 Explanation: NCERT: 20 Hz to 20,000 Hz, varying by person and narrowing with age.

Q25. Sound of frequency 50 kHz is: easy

✅ Answer: (a) Ultrasonic

💡 Explanation: Frequencies above 20 kHz are ultrasonic; bats and dolphins can detect them.

Q26. A sound wave has frequency 170 Hz in air at 340 m/s. Its wavelength is: medium

✅ Answer: (b) 2 m

💡 Explanation: Wavelength is speed divided by frequency: 340 ÷ 170 = 2 m.

Q27. To hear a distinct echo in air at 340 m/s, the reflector must be at least: medium

✅ Answer: (c) 17 m away

💡 Explanation: Sound needs 0.1 s: 34 m there and back, so the reflector is 17 m away.

Q28. An echo from a cliff is heard after 2 s (sound at 340 m/s). The cliff is: hard

✅ Answer: (d) 340 m away

💡 Explanation: Sound covers 680 m there and back, so the cliff is half of that: 340 m.

Q29. Pitch of a sound depends on its: easy

✅ Answer: (a) Frequency

💡 Explanation: Pitch is how humans perceive frequency; loudness depends on amplitude.

Q30. SONAR uses: easy

✅ Answer: (b) Ultrasonic waves

💡 Explanation: SONAR sends ultrasonic waves into water and analyses the reflections.

Which chapters does this set cover?

Chapters 4, 7 and 10 of the 2026-27 NCERT Class 9 book: motion, work and energy with simple machines, and sound. Ten questions come from each chapter.

What value of g should I use in these questions?

Use g = 10 m/s² unless a question says otherwise, as NCERT's worked examples do. The free-fall question uses the measured value of 9.8 m/s².
