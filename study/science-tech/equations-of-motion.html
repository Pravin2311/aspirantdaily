Equations of Motion: v = u + at, s = ut + ½at², v² = u² + 2as, Motion Graphs and Circular Motion

Distance vs displacement • Speed vs velocity • Acceleration and g • Position-time and velocity-time graphs • v = u + at • s = ut + ½at² • v² = u² + 2as • Braking distance • Uniform circular motion

SSC Railways 📅 Last updated: 2026-11-06

For motion in a straight line with constant acceleration, three kinematic equations link initial velocity u, final velocity v, acceleration a, time t and displacement s. NCERT's Class 9 science textbook "Exploration" (chapter 4, "Describing Motion Around Us") derives them from the velocity-time graph:

v = u + atsYou know the time and want the velocity
s = ut + ½at²vYou want the displacement after a given time
v² = u² + 2astTime is not given, as in braking-distance problems

The equations hold only when acceleration is constant. When the motion is in both directions along the line, the signs of u, v, a and s show the direction of each quantity.

Position is the distance and direction of an object from a chosen reference point. An object is in motion if its position changes with time.

Distance and the magnitude of displacement are equal only if the object moves in one direction without turning back. NCERT's athlete runs 160 m in total but ends 40 m from the start, so her displacement is 40 m. A swimmer who swims a 25 m pool there and back in 50 s covers 50 m, an average speed of 1 m/s. He ends where he started, so his displacement and average velocity are both zero.

When an object speeds up, its acceleration is in the direction of its velocity. When it slows down, the acceleration points the opposite way. Motion is uniform when equal distances are covered in equal time intervals, which means constant speed.

  • Bus speeding up: from 36 km/h (10 m/s) to 54 km/h (15 m/s) in 10 s, a = (15 − 10) ÷ 10 = 0.5 m/s².
  • Bus braking: from 15 m/s to rest in 5 s, a = (0 − 15) ÷ 5 = −3 m/s². The minus sign means the acceleration is opposite to the motion.
  • Free fall: a dropped object reaches 39.2 m/s after 4 s, so a = 39.2 ÷ 4 = 9.8 m/s². This is the acceleration due to the Earth's gravity, g.

To convert km/h to m/s, multiply by 1000/3600, which is the same as dividing by 3.6. So 54 km/h is 15 m/s and 108 km/h is 30 m/s.

The equations come from the velocity-time graph. Its slope is a, which gives v = u + at. The area under the line, a rectangle ut plus a triangle ½at², gives s = ut + ½at². Removing t between the two gives v² = u² + 2as. NCERT calls the first two the primary equations and derives the third from them.

NCERT's braking example: a car's brakes give an acceleration of −4 m/s². Using v² = u² + 2as with v = 0, the stopping distance is s = u² ÷ (2 × 4).

Speed when brakingIn m/sStopping distance

Doubling the speed makes the braking distance four times as long, because it depends on u². NCERT adds that the real distance also depends on the road surface, the brakes and the driver's reaction time. This is why a safe gap from the vehicle ahead must grow with speed.

Motion in a plane, such as a kicked ball or a satellite in orbit, is two-dimensional. When an object moves in a circle at constant speed, its motion is uniform circular motion.

  • In one revolution of radius R, the distance is the circumference 2πR, but the displacement is zero.
  • Average speed for one revolution is 2πR ÷ T, where T is the time for one revolution. The average velocity over that time is zero.
  • The speed is constant, but the direction of velocity keeps changing. At each point the velocity is along the tangent to the circle.
  • So uniform circular motion is accelerated motion, even though the speed never changes.

NCERT's marble activity shows the tangent rule. A marble running round the inside of a ring moves off in a straight line the moment the ring is lifted. NCERT treats uniform circular motion as an idealised model for planets circling the Sun and vehicles taking a curve.

NCERT notes that speed as distance divided by time appears in the Aryabhatiya (5th century CE). It quotes a problem from the Ganitakaumudi (14th century CE): two postmen start 210 yojanas apart and walk towards each other at 9 and 5 yojanas a day. Together they close 14 yojanas a day, so they meet after 210 ÷ 14 = 15 days.

  • v = u + at, s = ut + ½at², v² = u² + 2as; valid only for constant acceleration.
  • The slope of a position-time graph is velocity; the slope of a velocity-time graph is acceleration; the area under a velocity-time graph is displacement.
  • Distance is never less than the magnitude of displacement.
  • g ≈ 9.8 m/s².
  • Uniform circular motion has constant speed but changing velocity, so it is accelerated.
  • Braking distance grows with the square of speed.

For the forces behind these motions, see laws of motion. For energy in motion, see work, energy and power.

Q1. Which equation of motion does not contain time? easy

✅ Answer: (b) v² = u² + 2as

💡 Explanation: Eliminating t from the two primary equations gives v² = u² + 2as.

Q2. The slope of a velocity-time graph gives: easy

✅ Answer: (c) Acceleration

💡 Explanation: Slope is the rate of change of velocity with time, which is acceleration.

Q3. The area under a velocity-time graph gives: medium

✅ Answer: (a) Displacement

💡 Explanation: NCERT derives s = ut + ½at² from the area under the velocity-time line.

Q4. A body completes one full circle of radius R. Its displacement is: easy

✅ Answer: (d) Zero

💡 Explanation: It returns to its starting point, so the net change in position is zero.

Q5. Uniform circular motion is accelerated because: medium

✅ Answer: (b) Direction of velocity changes

💡 Explanation: Speed stays constant but the direction of velocity changes at every point.

Q6. If braking acceleration stays the same, doubling the speed makes braking distance: medium

✅ Answer: (c) Four times

💡 Explanation: From v² = u² + 2as, stopping distance is proportional to the square of u.

Q7. 54 km/h in m/s is: easy

✅ Answer: (a) 15 m/s

💡 Explanation: Divide by 3.6 to convert km/h to m/s: 54 ÷ 3.6 gives 15 m/s.

Q8. A horizontal line on a position-time graph shows the object is: easy

✅ Answer: (d) At rest

💡 Explanation: Position does not change with time, so the object is stationary.

Can distance be less than displacement?

No. Distance is the full path length, so it is at least the magnitude of displacement. They are equal only for motion in one direction without turning back.

Why is braking distance so much longer at high speed?

From v² = u² + 2as with v = 0, stopping distance is u² ÷ 2a. Doubling speed from 54 to 108 km/h raises it from about 28 m to 112.5 m.
