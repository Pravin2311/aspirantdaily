Endocrine Glands and Hormones: Gland-by-Gland Table of Hormones, Functions and Disorders

Hypothalamus: releasing and inhibiting hormones • Pituitary: GH, TSH, ACTH, LH, FSH, ADH, oxytocin • Thyroid: T3, T4 • Pancreas: insulin, glucagon • Adrenal: adrenaline, cortisol, aldosterone

SSC Railway State PSC 📅 Last updated: 2026-11-01

Endocrine glands have no ducts, so they are called ductless glands. They release hormones: non-nutrient chemicals made in trace amounts that act as messengers between cells. The nervous system gives fast, short-lived, point-to-point control. Hormones give the continuous regulation that nerves cannot reach.

  • Hypothalamus–pituitary link: hypothalamic hormones reach the anterior pituitary through a portal circulation. The posterior pituitary is under direct neural control of the hypothalamus.
  • Pituitary location: a bony cavity called the sella tursica, attached to the hypothalamus by a stalk. Its pars intermedia secretes only MSH, which regulates skin pigmentation.
  • Gonadotrophins: LH and FSH. In females, LH induces ovulation and maintains the corpus luteum.
  • Thyroid: two lobes on either side of the trachea joined by the isthmus. Iodine is essential for its hormones.
  • Calcium balance: PTH raises blood calcium by acting on bone, kidney and gut. Thyrocalcitonin from the thyroid lowers it.
  • Adrenal layers: the cortex has three zones: zona glomerulosa (outer), zona fasciculata (middle) and zona reticularis (inner).
  • Pancreas: a composite gland, both exocrine and endocrine. Its 1 to 2 million islets form only 1 to 2 per cent of the tissue.

How hormones act: each hormone binds a specific receptor in its target tissue. Protein hormones such as insulin bind receptors on the cell membrane and work through second messengers such as cyclic AMP. Steroid and thyroid hormones bind receptors inside the cell and regulate genes.

Q1. Diabetes insipidus is caused by a defect in: medium

✅ Answer: (c) Antidiuretic hormone (ADH)

💡 Explanation: NCERT says impaired synthesis or release of ADH (vasopressin) reduces the kidney's ability to conserve water, causing diabetes insipidus.

Q2. Which hormone is called the emergency or fight-or-flight hormone? easy

✅ Answer: (a) Adrenaline

💡 Explanation: Adrenaline and noradrenaline from the adrenal medulla are secreted rapidly under stress; NCERT calls them emergency hormones.

Q3. Goitre in adults is mainly linked to a deficiency of: easy

✅ Answer: (d) Iodine

💡 Explanation: Iodine is needed to make thyroid hormones. Its deficiency causes hypothyroidism and an enlarged thyroid, which is goitre.

Q4. Which hormone increases blood calcium levels? medium

✅ Answer: (b) Parathyroid hormone

💡 Explanation: NCERT calls PTH a hypercalcemic hormone. Thyrocalcitonin works the other way and decreases blood calcium.

Q5. Erythropoietin, which stimulates RBC formation, is produced by the: medium

✅ Answer: (c) Kidney

💡 Explanation: The juxtaglomerular cells of the kidney produce erythropoietin. The heart's atrial wall makes ANF instead.

Which gland is called the master gland?

The pituitary is commonly called the master gland because its hormones such as TSH, ACTH, LH and FSH control other glands. NCERT shows it is itself controlled by the hypothalamus.

What is the difference between diabetes mellitus and diabetes insipidus?

Diabetes mellitus is prolonged high blood sugar from insulin deficiency or resistance, with glucose lost in urine. Diabetes insipidus comes from a lack of ADH, so the kidneys lose too much water.

Which hormones are not made by endocrine glands?

NCERT lists ANF from the heart's atrial wall, erythropoietin from the kidney, and gastrin, secretin, CCK and GIP from cells in the gut, besides growth factors from other tissues.
