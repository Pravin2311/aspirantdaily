DNA vs RNA: Sugar, Bases, Strands, Stability and Function Compared

Deoxyribose vs ribose • Thymine vs uracil • A=T two H-bonds, G≡C three • Pitch 3.4 nm, 10 bp per turn, 0.34 nm per bp • RNA world • mRNA, tRNA, rRNA

SSC Railway State PSC 📅 Last updated: 2026-11-01

Deoxyribonucleic acid (DNA) and ribonucleic acid (RNA) are the two nucleic acids found in living things. DNA is the genetic material of most organisms. RNA is the genetic material in some viruses, but mostly it works as a messenger, and it also acts as an adapter, a structural molecule and sometimes a catalyst.

NCERT reduces the chemical difference to two points: the sugar, and thymine versus uracil. Thymine is simply 5-methyl uracil.

Every nucleotide has three parts: a nitrogenous base, a pentose sugar and a phosphate group. A base joined to the sugar through an N-glycosidic link makes a nucleoside. Adding a phosphate at the 5' carbon makes a nucleotide. Nucleotides join through their sugar and phosphate to form the backbone of a chain, with a free phosphate at the 5' end and a free –OH at the 3' end.

A quick memory aid: the "pure" bases are A and G (purines), and the rest are pyrimidines.

Friedrich Miescher (spelt Meischer in NCERT) first identified DNA in 1869 and called it "Nuclein". In 1953 James Watson and Francis Crick proposed the double helix, using X-ray diffraction data from Maurice Wilkins and Rosalind Franklin. Their base pairing also rested on Erwin Chargaff's finding that in double-stranded DNA the ratio of A to T, and of G to C, is one.

  • Backbone: sugar and phosphate on the outside, with the bases projecting inward.
  • Anti-parallel: one chain runs 5'→3' and the other 3'→5'.
  • Pairing: A pairs with T by two hydrogen bonds and G pairs with C by three, so a purine always faces a pyrimidine. This keeps the two strands an even distance apart.
  • Dimensions: a right-handed coil with a pitch of 3.4 nm and about 10 base pairs per turn, so each base pair adds about 0.34 nm.
  • Stability: hydrogen bonds plus the stacking of base pairs on top of one another.

Worked example: how many hydrogen bonds hold a stretch of 4 A–T pairs and 6 G–C pairs? The answer is 4 × 2 + 6 × 3 = 26. Because G–C pairs have three bonds, DNA rich in G and C holds its strands together more tightly.

NCERT's genome sizes are also asked: phage φX174 has 5,386 nucleotides, phage lambda has 48,502 bp, E. coli has 4.6 × 106 bp, and haploid human DNA has 3.3 × 109 bp.

NCERT lists four tests that a genetic material must pass:

  1. It must make copies of itself (replication).
  2. It must be chemically and structurally stable.
  3. It must allow slow changes (mutation) for evolution.
  4. It must express itself as Mendelian characters.

Both nucleic acids can copy themselves through base pairing; proteins fail this first test. On stability, DNA wins. The 2'-OH group on every RNA nucleotide is reactive and makes RNA easy to break down, and RNA's catalytic ability makes it reactive too. Thymine in place of uracil adds stability to DNA. RNA does have advantages: it mutates faster and can direct protein synthesis directly, while DNA depends on RNA to make proteins.

NCERT's conclusion: DNA is preferred for storing genetic information, and RNA is better for transmitting it.

RNA world: NCERT states that RNA was the first genetic material. Early life processes such as metabolism, translation and splicing evolved around it, and it acted both as genetic material and as a catalyst. Being reactive, it was unstable, so DNA evolved from RNA with chemical changes that make it more stable. Its double strand also allows repair.

Q1. Which base is found in RNA but not in DNA? easy

✅ Answer: (b) Uracil

💡 Explanation: Uracil takes the place of thymine in RNA. Cytosine, adenine and guanine are common to both, and thymine (5-methyl uracil) is found only in DNA.

Q2. The sugar in DNA differs from the sugar in RNA because DNA's sugar lacks: medium

✅ Answer: (c) The –OH group at the 2' position

💡 Explanation: RNA has ribose with an –OH group at the 2' carbon. DNA has deoxyribose, which lacks it, and this missing 2'-OH is a main reason DNA is more stable.

Q3. How many hydrogen bonds join guanine and cytosine in DNA? easy

✅ Answer: (a) Three

💡 Explanation: Guanine pairs with cytosine through three hydrogen bonds, and adenine pairs with thymine through two.

Q4. Who gave the unequivocal proof that DNA is the genetic material? medium

✅ Answer: (d) Hershey and Chase

💡 Explanation: NCERT credits Alfred Hershey and Martha Chase (1952) with the unequivocal proof. Griffith found the transforming principle, Watson and Crick proposed the double helix, and Meselson and Stahl proved semiconservative replication.

Q5. Tobacco Mosaic Virus is an example of an organism whose genetic material is: easy

✅ Answer: (b) RNA

💡 Explanation: NCERT names Tobacco Mosaic Virus and QB bacteriophage as viruses whose genetic material is RNA.

What are the two chemical differences between DNA and RNA?

DNA's sugar is deoxyribose while RNA's is ribose, which carries an extra –OH group at the 2' carbon. DNA has thymine where RNA has uracil.

Why is DNA more stable than RNA?

RNA's 2'-OH group is reactive and makes it easy to break down, and RNA can act as a catalyst. DNA lacks that group, is double stranded with a complementary strand, and its thymine adds further stability.

Is RNA always single stranded?

Not always, but usually. Exam questions treat DNA as the double helix and RNA as normally a single chain. NCERT stresses the double strand as one reason DNA resists change and can be repaired.
