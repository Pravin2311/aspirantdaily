Photosynthesis and Respiration in Plants: Equation, Light Reaction, Calvin Cycle, C3 vs C4, Glycolysis and ATP Yield

6CO2 + 12H2O → C6H12O6 + 6H2O + 6O2 • P680 and P700 • 3 ATP + 2 NADPH per CO2 • Kranz anatomy • Blackman's law • 38 ATP • RQ 1, 0.7, 0.9

SSC Railway State PSC 📅 Last updated: 2026-11-01

Green plants make their own food by trapping light energy and storing it as chemical energy in sugars. NCERT Class 11 Biology writes the overall process as:

6CO2 + 12H2O → C6H12O6 + 6H2O + 6O2 (in the presence of light)

This is a summary of many steps, not a single reaction. Three points come up again and again in exams:

  • The oxygen comes from water. Cornelius van Niel studied purple and green sulphur bacteria, which use H2S instead of water and release sulphur rather than oxygen. From this he inferred that the O2 released by green plants comes from H2O, not from CO2. Radioisotope experiments later proved it.
  • Why 12 water molecules? Six O2 contain 12 oxygen atoms, and every one of them comes from water.
  • Where it happens: in the chloroplasts, mostly in the mesophyll cells of leaves, but also in other green parts. The membrane system (grana and stroma lamellae) traps light and makes ATP and NADPH. The stroma's enzymes then make sugar, which forms starch.

The light reactions take place on the thylakoid membranes. They cover light absorption, water splitting, oxygen release, and the formation of ATP and NADPH.

  • Non-cyclic photophosphorylation uses both photosystems in the Z scheme and gives ATP and NADPH.
  • Cyclic photophosphorylation uses only PS I and gives ATP but no NADPH. It takes place in the stroma lamellae.
  • Chemiosmosis: protons build up inside the thylakoid lumen, and their flow back through ATP synthase makes ATP. NCERT lists four requirements: a membrane, a proton pump, a proton gradient and ATP synthase.

Of the three products, O2 leaves the chloroplast. ATP and NADPH drive sugar synthesis in the stroma.

Melvin Calvin used radioactive 14C in algae to show that the first product of CO2 fixation is a 3-carbon acid, 3-phosphoglyceric acid (PGA). The CO2 acceptor turned out to be a 5-carbon sugar, ribulose bisphosphate (RuBP). The Calvin cycle runs in all photosynthetic plants, C3 and C4 alike.

  1. Carboxylation: RuBisCO fixes CO2 onto RuBP, giving two molecules of 3-PGA. The full name, RuBP carboxylase-oxygenase, reflects that the enzyme can also react with oxygen.
  2. Reduction: 2 ATP and 2 NADPH are used per CO2 fixed to form sugar.
  3. Regeneration: 1 more ATP per CO2 regenerates RuBP so the cycle can continue.

Kranz means "wreath". It describes the ring of large bundle sheath cells around the vascular bundles. These cells have many chloroplasts and thick walls that block gas exchange, and they have no intercellular spaces. In this Hatch and Slack pathway, OAA formed in the mesophyll is turned into malic or aspartic acid and moved to the bundle sheath. There it releases CO2 into the Calvin cycle.

Photorespiration: RuBisCO's active site can bind O2 as well as CO2. In C3 plants some O2 does bind, and RuBP then forms phosphoglycerate and phosphoglycolate instead of two PGA. This pathway makes no sugar, no ATP and no NADPH, and it releases CO2 while using ATP. NCERT says its biological function is not yet known. C4 plants raise the CO2 level at the enzyme, so they avoid photorespiration, which is why their productivity is higher.

Blackman's Law of Limiting Factors (1905): when several factors affect a process, its rate is set by the factor nearest to its minimum value. A leaf with good light and CO2 still will not photosynthesise if the temperature is very low.

  • Light: the rate rises in step with light at low intensity, but saturation occurs at 10 per cent of full sunlight. Light is rarely limiting in nature, except for plants in shade or dense forest. Very strong light breaks down chlorophyll.
  • Carbon dioxide: the major limiting factor. Air holds only 0.03 to 0.04 per cent CO2. C3 plants respond to extra CO2, so greenhouse tomatoes and bell peppers are grown in CO2-enriched air for higher yields.
  • Temperature: the enzyme-driven dark reactions are far more sensitive than the light reactions. C4 plants have a higher temperature optimum than C3 plants.
  • Water: acts mainly through the plant. Water stress closes stomata, which cuts the CO2 supply, and it makes leaves wilt.

Respiration releases the energy stored in food. For glucose: C6H12O6 + 6O2 → 6CO2 + 6H2O + energy, which is broadly photosynthesis run in reverse.

Fermentation: without oxygen, yeast turns pyruvic acid into CO2 and ethanol, and muscles short of oxygen make lactic acid. Both release less than 7 per cent of glucose's energy, and the net gain is only 2 ATP per glucose. On NCERT's assumptions, aerobic respiration can give a net gain of 38 ATP.

Respiratory quotient (RQ) = volume of CO2 evolved ÷ volume of O2 consumed.

  • Carbohydrates: 6CO2 ÷ 6O2 = 1.
  • Fats (tripalmitin): 102 ÷ 145 ≈ 0.7.
  • Proteins: about 0.9.

Q1. In photosynthesis, the oxygen released by green plants comes from: easy

✅ Answer: (c) Water

💡 Explanation: NCERT states that the O2 released comes from water, which was proved with radioisotope techniques. Van Niel's study of sulphur bacteria first suggested this.

Q2. The first stable product of CO2 fixation in C4 plants is: medium

✅ Answer: (a) Oxaloacetic acid (OAA)

💡 Explanation: C4 plants fix CO2 first as the 4-carbon oxaloacetic acid. PGA is the first product in C3 plants, and PEP is the acceptor, not the product.

Q3. How many ATP and NADPH molecules does the Calvin cycle use to make one glucose? medium

✅ Answer: (c) 18 ATP and 12 NADPH

💡 Explanation: Each CO2 needs 3 ATP and 2 NADPH, and one glucose needs six CO2, so the cycle uses 18 ATP and 12 NADPH.

Q4. Which statement about photorespiration is correct? medium

✅ Answer: (b) It does not occur in C4 plants

💡 Explanation: C4 plants concentrate CO2 at RuBisCO in bundle sheath cells, so its oxygenase activity is minimised and photorespiration does not occur. Photorespiration makes no sugar, ATP or NADPH.

Q5. When fats such as tripalmitin are the respiratory substrate, the respiratory quotient is about: easy

✅ Answer: (d) 0.7

💡 Explanation: For tripalmitin, 102 CO2 are released for 145 O2 used, which gives an RQ of about 0.7. Carbohydrates give 1 and proteins about 0.9.

Why does the photosynthesis equation use 12 water molecules and not 6?

All the oxygen released comes from water. Six O2 molecules contain 12 oxygen atoms, so 12 water molecules must be split. Six new water molecules are formed again on the product side.

Is the dark reaction really dark?

No. The biosynthetic phase does not use light directly, but it depends on the ATP and NADPH made by the light reactions. NCERT asks whether calling it the dark reaction is a misnomer for this reason.

Is 38 ATP per glucose the exact figure?

NCERT treats 38 as a theoretical net gain. It assumes that pathways run one after another, that only glucose is respired and that no intermediates are drawn off, which does not happen in a living cell.
