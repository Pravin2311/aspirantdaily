Work, Energy and Power: Formulas, Work-Energy Theorem, Conservation and Simple Machines

W = Fs • Positive, negative and zero work • Work-energy theorem • ½mv² and mgh • Conservation of mechanical energy • Power, watt and horsepower • Pulley, inclined plane, lever classes

Work, energy and power come down to four formulas, all from NCERT's Class 9 science textbook "Exploration" (chapter 7, "Work, Energy, and Simple Machines"):

QuantityFormulaSI unitMeaning
WorkW = F × sjoule (J)Force times displacement in the direction of the force
Kinetic energyK = ½ mv²joule (J)Energy due to motion
Gravitational potential energyU = mghjoule (J)Energy due to height above the ground (near the Earth's surface)
PowerP = W ÷ twatt (W)Rate of doing work

One joule is the work done when a force of 1 newton moves an object 1 metre in the direction of the force, so 1 J = 1 kg m² s⁻². One watt is one joule per second. The units are named after James Prescott Joule and James Watt.

Work needs both a force and a displacement along it. Lifting three 5 kg bags takes three times the work of lifting one, and lifting one bag 3 m takes three times the work of lifting it 1 m. Work has no direction, but it carries a sign:

CaseWorkExample
Displacement along the forcePositivePushing a wheelchair; lifting a dumbbell
Displacement opposite to the forceNegativeA goalkeeper stopping a ball; slowly lowering a dumbbell
No displacementZeroPushing a rigid wall, however tired you feel
Force perpendicular to displacementZeroThe upward force on a box carried while walking on level ground

Worked example: a goalkeeper applies 200 N and her hands move back 15 cm while stopping the ball. The ball moves against her force, so W = 200 × (−0.15) = −30 J.

On a force–displacement graph, work is the area under the line. This works even when the force is not constant.

Energy is the capacity to do work. A thrown cricket ball can knock over the wickets because the fielder did work on it. NCERT states the link as the work-energy theorem: the work done on an object equals the change in its energy. It holds for systems of objects and for forces that are not constant.

Energy takes many forms, including mechanical, thermal, chemical, electrical, sound and nuclear, and can change from one to another. A bulb turns electrical energy into light; food's chemical energy powers muscles.

Kinetic energy is the energy of motion: K = ½ mv². Because velocity is squared, doubling the speed makes kinetic energy four times as large.

  • Fast delivery: a 0.2 kg cricket ball at 154.8 km/h (43 m/s) has K = ½ × 0.2 × 43² = 184.9 J.
  • Carrier landing: a 15,000 kg jet is stopped within 100 m by a wire pulling back with 367,500 N. The wire does −36,750,000 J of work, which removes all the kinetic energy: ½ × 15,000 × v² = 36,750,000, so v² = 4900 and v = 70 m/s, or 252 km/h.

Potential energy is stored by deformation, as in a stretched slingshot band, a bent bow or a compressed spring. It is also stored by the relative positions of objects that attract or repel, such as magnets, charges, or a ball and the Earth. Raising mass m by height h takes work mgh, so U = mgh. A 200 g ball thrown 10 m up has U = 0.2 × 10 × 10 = 20 J (taking g = 10 m/s²).

Mechanical energy is kinetic plus potential energy. For a falling object, potential energy lost equals kinetic energy gained, so the total stays at mgh. This is the conservation of mechanical energy, which holds when no other external forces act. A pendulum shows it: all potential at the ends of its swing, all kinetic at the bottom. Friction makes a real pendulum slow down and stop.

A useful result: on a frictionless slide of height h, the speed at the bottom comes from mgh = ½ mv², so v = √(2gh). It does not depend on the child's mass or the shape of the slide. For a 5 m slide with g = 10 m/s², v = 10 m/s.

NCERT's Himalayan example is the gharat or panchakki, a water mill in which falling water turns potential energy into kinetic energy to grind grain. Hydroelectric dams use the same conversion to generate electricity.

Running up the stairs in one minute and walking up in five do the same work, but running needs more power. Power is work done per unit time.

  • Weightlifter: lifting 75 kg by 2 m takes 75 × 10 × 2 = 1500 J. Doing it in 5 s needs 1500 ÷ 5 = 300 W.
  • Car start: a 1000 kg car reaching 72 km/h (20 m/s) from rest gains ½ × 1000 × 20² = 200,000 J. Doing it in 10 s needs 20,000 W.
  • Horsepower: 1 hp = 746 W, a unit from the days when engines were compared with horses.

Simple machines make a task easier by changing the size or direction of the force needed. They do not reduce the total work and they do not create energy. The force you apply is the effort, the force overcome is the load, and mechanical advantage = load ÷ effort.

MachineMechanical advantageWhat it gives
Fixed pulley1Only a change of direction: pulling down is easier than lifting
Movable pulley or pulley systemCan exceed 1Heavier loads with smaller effort, as in elevators and cranes
Inclined planeL ÷ h (length ÷ height)Smaller force over a longer distance
LeverEffort arm ÷ load armSmaller force over a larger distance

Ramp example: a step 30 cm high reached by a ramp 40 cm wide gives a ramp length of 50 cm (a 3-4-5 triangle). The mechanical advantage is 50 ÷ 30 ≈ 1.67.

Lever balance: effort × effort arm = load × load arm. A 15 kg child 2 m from a seesaw's fulcrum balances a 30 kg child sitting 1 m from it on the other side.

  • W = Fs; work is zero if there is no displacement or the force is perpendicular to it.
  • Kinetic energy is ½ mv²; doubling velocity quadruples it.
  • Potential energy near the Earth is mgh.
  • Work done on an object equals its change in energy.
  • Power is work ÷ time; 1 W = 1 J/s and 1 hp = 746 W.
  • Mechanical advantage = load ÷ effort; a fixed pulley has 1.

Q1. The SI unit of work is: easy

✅ Answer: (a) Joule

💡 Explanation: One joule is the work done when 1 N moves an object 1 m along the force.

Q2. If the velocity of a body is doubled, its kinetic energy becomes: easy

✅ Answer: (b) Four times

💡 Explanation: Kinetic energy is half m v squared, so doubling v multiplies it by four.

Q3. A person pushing a rigid wall does: easy

✅ Answer: (c) Zero work

💡 Explanation: There is no displacement of the wall, so the work done on it is zero.

Q4. 1 horsepower is equal to: easy

✅ Answer: (d) 746 W

💡 Explanation: NCERT gives one horsepower as 746 watt, a unit once used to compare engines with horses.

Q5. The potential energy of a 2 kg mass at 5 m height (g = 10 m/s²) is: medium

✅ Answer: (b) 100 J

💡 Explanation: Using mgh: 2 kg × 10 m/s² × 5 m gives 100 J of potential energy.

Q6. The mechanical advantage of a fixed pulley is: medium

✅ Answer: (d) 1

💡 Explanation: A fixed pulley changes only the direction of the effort, so load equals effort.

Q7. A wheelbarrow is a lever of: medium

✅ Answer: (c) Class II

💡 Explanation: In a wheelbarrow the load lies between the fulcrum and the effort, making it Class II.

Q8. Power is defined as: easy

✅ Answer: (a) Rate of doing work

💡 Explanation: Power is work done divided by the time taken, measured in watt.

Does a simple machine reduce the work needed?

No. A pulley, ramp or lever changes the size or direction of the force, but the total work stays the same, ignoring friction.

Why does a child reach the same speed on any frictionless slide of equal height?

Potential energy mgh turns fully into kinetic energy ½mv², so v = √(2gh). Mass cancels and the shape of the slide does not enter.
