Transmission Media: Twisted Pair, Coaxial, Optical Fibre and Wireless

Guided vs unguided • Twisted pair • Coaxial • Optical fibre • Radio, microwave, infrared • Bluetooth and Wi-Fi

SSC Banking 📅 Last updated: 2026-10-29

A transmission medium is whatever carries a signal from the sender to the receiver. It is also called the channel or link. Media fall into two groups:

  • Guided (wired) media use a physical cable that guides the signal from node to node: twisted pair, coaxial cable and optical fibre.
  • Unguided (wireless) media send electromagnetic waves through the air using an antenna: radio waves, microwaves and infrared.

Two measures describe a channel's capacity. Bandwidth is the range of frequencies the channel can carry, measured in hertz (Hz). Data transfer rate is the number of bits sent per second (bps). The higher the bandwidth, the higher the data rate.

Twisted pair is two insulated copper wires twisted round each other like a DNA helix, usually with several pairs inside one outer jacket. The twisting reduces electrical interference from nearby pairs. It is cheap and is the cable of telephone lines and most LANs. It comes unshielded (UTP) or shielded with a metal layer (STP).

Coaxial cable has a copper core, a layer of insulation, an outer conductor (usually copper mesh) and a plastic cover. That shielding makes it better protected and higher in bandwidth than twisted pair, so it carries higher-frequency signals over longer distances.

Optical fibre carries data as light through a thin glass core. The core is covered with cladding made of less dense glass, and the whole is wrapped in an outer jacket of PVC or Teflon. Fibre is light, has the highest bandwidth of the three, carries signals over long distances and is not affected by electromagnetic noise, which is why it is used in backbone networks. It is expensive, and a fibre carries light in one direction, so full duplex needs two fibres.

Wireless media use the electromagnetic spectrum. The textbook classification divides the band from 3 kHz to 900 THz into radio waves, microwaves, infrared and visible light, and uses the first three for communication. Other references draw the band edges slightly differently, so learn the properties more than the exact boundaries.

Microwave antennas are dish-shaped and mounted on tall buildings or towers to stay in line of sight. Waves weaken with distance, so repeaters along the route regenerate the signal.

Bluetooth connects phones, headphones, keyboards and similar devices over a short distance. It uses the unlicensed 2.4 GHz band, reaches about 10 metres and runs at 1 to 2 Mbps. Devices in range form a piconet, in which one master communicates with up to 7 active slaves at once. Up to 255 devices can join, but only 8 are active at a time.

Wi-Fi is the popular name for wireless LAN, IEEE standard 802.11. Devices connect to an access point, which joins the wired network.

  • ✘ Saying optical fibre carries electric signals. It carries light.
  • ✘ Calling microwaves omni-directional. Radio waves are; microwaves need line of sight.
  • ✘ Saying one fibre is enough for full duplex. It takes two.
  • ✘ Counting 8 active slaves in a piconet. It is 7 slaves plus the master.

These media link the networks in PAN, LAN, MAN and WAN. The devices at each end are covered in networking devices.

Q1. Which of these is an unguided transmission medium? easy

✅ Answer: (d) Microwave

💡 Explanation: Unguided media carry electromagnetic waves through the air with no physical link. Microwaves are unguided; the three cables are guided media.

Q2. What does an optical fibre cable carry? easy

✅ Answer: (a) Light signals

💡 Explanation: Twisted pair and coaxial cable carry electric signals. Optical fibre carries data as light through a thin strand of glass.

Q3. Why are the wires in a twisted pair cable twisted? easy

✅ Answer: (b) To reduce electrical interference

💡 Explanation: Twisting the two insulated copper wires minimises the effect of electrical interference from neighbouring pairs.

Q4. Which wireless medium is unidirectional, needs line-of-sight between antennas and is used for satellite links? medium

✅ Answer: (c) Microwaves

💡 Explanation: Microwaves are unidirectional and cannot pass through walls or hills, so the two antennas must face each other. Radio waves are omni-directional.

Q5. How many active slave devices can a Bluetooth master communicate with at the same time? medium

✅ Answer: (a) 7

💡 Explanation: In a piconet the master can talk to up to 7 active slaves at once, which makes 8 active devices in total. Up to 255 devices can belong to the network, most of them inactive.

Q6. Which statement about optical fibre is correct? hard

✅ Answer: (d) It has higher bandwidth than copper cables

💡 Explanation: Optical fibre has higher bandwidth and is not affected by electromagnetic noise. It is more expensive, and a fibre carries light one way, so full duplex needs two cables.

What is the difference between bandwidth and data transfer rate?

Bandwidth is the range of frequencies a channel can carry and is measured in hertz. Data transfer rate is the number of bits sent per second and is measured in bps. A channel with higher bandwidth can carry a higher data rate.

Is Wi-Fi a guided or an unguided medium?

Unguided. Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, satellite links and infrared all send electromagnetic waves through the air with an antenna instead of a cable, so they are wireless or unguided media.
