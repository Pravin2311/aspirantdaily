Memory Units: Bit, Nibble, Byte and KB to YB with Conversions

Bit, nibble, byte • KB to YB table • Powers of 2 • KB vs KiB • Bits vs bytes in speeds

SSC Banking Railway 📅 Last updated: 2026-10-29

A computer stores everything as the binary digits 0 and 1. Each digit is a bit, the smallest unit of memory. Bits are handled in groups called words: a group of 4 bits is a nibble (for example 1010), and a group of 8 bits, or two nibbles, is a byte (for example 01000110).

The byte is the base for every larger unit of storage.

From the byte upwards, each unit in the textbook table is 1024 times the one before it, because 1024 = 2¹⁰ is the power of 2 closest to a thousand.

UnitEqual toIn bytes (power of 2)
1 Kilobyte (KB)1024 bytes2¹⁰
1 Megabyte (MB)1024 KB2²⁰
1 Gigabyte (GB)1024 MB2³⁰
1 Terabyte (TB)1024 GB2⁴⁰
1 Petabyte (PB)1024 TB2⁵⁰
1 Exabyte (EB)1024 PB2⁶⁰
1 Zettabyte (ZB)1024 EB2⁷⁰
1 Yottabyte (YB)1024 ZB2⁸⁰

The power goes up by 10 at each step, so you can rebuild the last column from the first row. To remember the order, say the first letters as a chain: K-M-G-T-P-E-Z-Y.

Going to a smaller unit, multiply by 1024 for each step. Going to a larger unit, divide by 1024 for each step. Count the steps before you calculate.

Worked example 1: GB to KB

2 GB to KB is two steps down (GB → MB → KB): 2 × 1024 × 1024 = 2,097,152 KB.

Worked example 2: MB to GB

4096 MB to GB is one step up: 4096 ÷ 1024 = 4 GB.

Worked example 3: bytes to bits

Each byte is 8 bits, so 1 KB = 1024 × 8 = 8192 bits.

In the International System of Units (SI), kilo means exactly 1000 and mega means exactly 1,000,000. To avoid the clash with 1024, the International Electrotechnical Commission approved separate binary prefixes in December 1998: kibi (Ki) for 2¹⁰, mebi (Mi) for 2²⁰, gibi (Gi) for 2³⁰, and so on. Under that scheme 1 MiB = 1,048,576 bytes while 1 MB = 1,000,000 bytes. NIST notes that these binary prefixes are not part of the SI.

Worked example 4: how far apart are MB and MiB?

2²⁰ − 10⁶ = 1,048,576 − 1,000,000 = 48,576 bytes, close to 5% of a megabyte. The gap grows at each larger unit.

For exam questions, use the 1024 table unless the question names the SI or KiB convention.

Network speeds are given in bits per second: Kbps, Mbps, Gbps. File sizes are given in bytes. A lower-case b means bits and an upper-case B means bytes, so 1 MBps is eight times 1 Mbps.

Worked example 5: download time

A 100 MB file on an 80 Mbps line: 100 × 8 = 800 megabits, and 800 ÷ 80 = 10 seconds, ignoring overheads.

  • ✘ Putting EB after ZB. The order is PB, EB, ZB, YB.
  • ✘ Multiplying by 1000 in an exam that expects 1024.
  • ✘ Mixing up bits and bytes in speed questions: convert megabytes to megabits (× 8) before dividing by the speed.
  • ✘ Calling a nibble 8 bits. A nibble is 4 bits; a byte is 8.

For the binary numbers behind these units, see number system conversion. Where each unit is stored is covered in types of computer memory, and the computer awareness cheat code has a one-page revision list.

Q1. How many bits make one nibble? easy

✅ Answer: (b) 4

💡 Explanation: A nibble is a 4-bit word. Two nibbles make a byte, which is 8 bits.

Q2. 1 YB (yottabyte) is equal to: easy

✅ Answer: (d) 1024 ZB

💡 Explanation: In the 1024 table the order is KB, MB, GB, TB, PB, EB, ZB, YB. The yottabyte comes straight after the zettabyte, so 1 YB = 1024 ZB.

Q3. Which of these is the largest? easy

✅ Answer: (b) 1 EB

💡 Explanation: 1024 GB is just 1 TB. A petabyte is 1024 TB and an exabyte is 1024 PB, so 1 EB is the largest.

Q4. How many KB are there in 3 MB (using 1 MB = 1024 KB)? easy

✅ Answer: (b) 3072

💡 Explanation: Moving down one unit multiplies by 1024, so 3 MB = 3 × 1024 = 3072 KB.

Q5. The symbol MiB stands for: medium

✅ Answer: (a) a mebibyte, exactly 2^20 bytes

💡 Explanation: The IEC prefix mebi (Mi) means 2^20, so 1 MiB = 1,048,576 bytes. The SI megabyte (MB) is exactly 1,000,000 bytes.

Q6. An internet plan offers 80 Mbps. Ignoring overheads, how long does a 100 MB file take to download? medium

✅ Answer: (c) 10 seconds

💡 Explanation: Mbps counts bits. 100 MB is 100 × 8 = 800 megabits, and 800 ÷ 80 = 10 seconds. Forgetting the factor of 8 gives the 1.25 trap.

Is 1 KB equal to 1000 bytes or 1024 bytes?

Both conventions exist. Computer awareness questions and the NCERT textbook use 1 KB = 1024 bytes. Under SI rules, kilo means exactly 1000, and the IEC name for 1024 bytes is the kibibyte (KiB). Unless a question says otherwise, answer with 1024.

What comes after the yottabyte?

Exam tables, including NCERT's, stop at the yottabyte, so YB is the safe answer for the largest unit. Learn the eight-step order KB, MB, GB, TB, PB, EB, ZB, YB and its powers of 2 rather than names beyond it.
