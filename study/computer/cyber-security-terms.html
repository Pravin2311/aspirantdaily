Cyber Security Terms for Exams: Phishing (and Its Phone and SMS Forms), Hacking, DoS and DDoS, Snooping, Firewall, HTTPS and Cookies

Phishing by email, call or SMS • Identity theft • White, black and grey hats • DoS vs DDoS and botnets • Snooping vs eavesdropping • Firewall types • HTTP vs HTTPS • Cookies • IT Act 2000

SSC Banking 📅 Last updated: 2026-11-04

Phishing means showing the user a fake website or email that looks genuine, to collect usernames, passwords and bank or card details. NCERT says the most common method is email spoofing: the sender's address is forged so that the mail seems to come from your bank or college. The logo may be copied exactly, but the web address will be fake.

NCERT adds that phishing by phone call or text message is now common too. These forms have their own names in everyday use: vishing (voice phishing) and smishing (SMS phishing). Questions on phishing, vishing and smishing test exactly this: the channel changes, the trick does not.

Identity theft is one type of phishing attack. Stolen personal data, such as PAN, Aadhaar, passport or banking details, is misused in the victim's name. NCERT lists three kinds: financial (for money), criminal (to hide the criminal's real identity) and medical (to get drugs or treatment).

Hackers know systems, networks and programming well enough to find loopholes. NCERT sorts them by intent:

  • White hats are ethical hackers. They find security flaws and help fix them, and organisations hire them.
  • Black hats, also called crackers, exploit flaws unlawfully.
  • Grey hats sit in between. They break into systems for the challenge, not for money or politics.

A Denial of Service (DoS) attack overloads a website or server with so many requests that genuine users cannot get through. In a Distributed DoS (DDoS), the requests come from many compromised computers, called zombies, on which the attacker has installed a bot. Together they form a botnet. Because the attack comes from many places at once, DDoS is far harder to stop than a DoS from a single source.

Snooping, also called sniffing, captures network traffic so it can be analysed, sometimes later. Network administrators also use it to troubleshoot. Eavesdropping is real-time listening to private communication such as calls, messages or video conferences. The key difference: eavesdropping happens in real time and snooping need not.

A firewall protects a trusted private network from unauthorised traffic coming from an untrusted network such as the Internet. It can be software, hardware or both, and it filters incoming and outgoing traffic by set rules. A network firewall sits between networks; a host-based firewall sits on one computer.

HTTP sends data as it is, so it suits public pages such as news. HTTPS encrypts data before sending it and needs an SSL digital certificate, so use it for banking and passwords. For the protocols themselves, see computer networks.

A cookie is a small file a website stores on your computer, for example to remember your cart. Session cookies end a session after a time-out, and authentication cookies remember that you have logged in. A zombie cookie comes back after it is deleted, and some third-party cookies share your data for advertising.

In India, cyber crime is covered by the Information Technology Act, 2000, amended in 2008. A Cyber Appellate Tribunal resolves disputes arising from cyber crime.

Q1. A caller pretending to be from your bank asks for your card details. This is phishing carried out by: easy

✅ Answer: (c) A phone call (often called vishing)

💡 Explanation: NCERT notes that phishing attempts by phone call or text message are common. The phone form is widely called vishing and the SMS form smishing.

Q2. What makes a DDoS attack different from a simple DoS attack? medium

✅ Answer: (a) The requests come from many compromised computers in a botnet

💡 Explanation: In DDoS the flood comes from many 'zombie' machines controlled through bots. That makes it much harder to block than a DoS from one source.

Q3. Which statement about snooping and eavesdropping is correct? medium

✅ Answer: (d) Eavesdropping is real-time interception; snooping captures traffic for analysis

💡 Explanation: NCERT says eavesdropping happens in real time, while snooping (sniffing) can capture traffic and analyse it later.

Q4. A hacker who finds security flaws and helps the owner fix them is called a: easy

✅ Answer: (b) White hat

💡 Explanation: White hats are ethical hackers that organisations hire to find and fix flaws. Black hats, also called crackers, exploit flaws unlawfully.

Q5. Websites that use HTTPS need which of the following? easy

✅ Answer: (c) An SSL digital certificate

💡 Explanation: HTTPS encrypts data before sending it, and NCERT notes that HTTPS-based websites require an SSL digital certificate.

What are vishing and smishing?

They are the common names for phishing by voice call (vishing) and by SMS (smishing). The trick is the same as email phishing: a message that looks genuine asks for passwords, OTPs or bank details.

Is a cookie a virus?

No. Cookies are small data files that websites store to remember things like a cart or a login. NCERT notes that some malware can disguise itself as cookies, and that third-party cookies can share data for advertising.

Which law deals with cyber crime in India?

The Information Technology Act, 2000, amended in 2008. It recognises electronic records and digital signatures and sets out cyber offences and penalties.
