History of Computers and Pioneers: From the Abacus to VLSI, with the Inventors Exams Ask About

Abacus • Pascaline 1642 • Analytical Engine 1834 • Tabulating Machine 1890 • Turing Machine 1937 • Stored program 1945 • Transistor 1947 • Moore's Law 1965 • IBM PC 1981

SSC Banking Railways 📅 Last updated: 2026-11-01

Exams expect Charles Babbage. NCERT's Class XI Computer Science textbook explains why: his Analytical Engine (1834) was a mechanical computing device for inputting, processing, storing and displaying output, and it is considered to form the basis of modern computers. Those four functions are still the basic jobs of every computer.

Babbage is only one name in a long chain, though. NCERT's timeline runs from the abacus to the microchip, and questions draw on all of it.

Year (NCERT timeline)InventionPerson or placeWhy it matters

1890Tabulating MachineHerman HollerithSummarised data on punched cards; a step towards programming

NCERT spells the inventor "Blaize Pascal"; the usual spelling is Blaise. The years are the markers on NCERT's Figure 1.4. Where an exam asks for a different year, check the question's own source.

John von Neumann's stored program idea is that a computer keeps both its data and its instructions in memory, instead of being rewired for each job. NCERT describes the resulting von Neumann architecture as five parts:

  • a Central Processing Unit (CPU) for arithmetic and logical instructions,
  • a memory to store data and programs,
  • input devices,
  • output devices, and
  • communication channels to send and receive data.

NCERT calls the Electronic Numerical Integrator and Computer (ENIAC) the first binary programmable computer based on this architecture. EDVAC is the other machine NCERT links to the stored program concept.

After the transistor and the IC, the next steps were about fitting ever more components on one chip:

PeriodMilestoneWhat NCERT says
1965Moore's LawIntel co-founder Gordon Moore predicted transistors per chip would double every two years while costs halved
1970sLarge Scale Integration (LSI)A complete CPU on a single chip: the microprocessor
1980sVery Large Scale Integration (VLSI)Around 3 million components on a small chip
LaterSuper Large Scale Integration (SLSI)Even higher density of transistors and components on one IC

For how memory sizes grew alongside, see memory units from bit to yottabyte and types of computer memory.

  • 1981: IBM introduced its first personal computer (PC) for home users.
  • 1984: Apple introduced the Macintosh.
  • GUI operating systems from Microsoft and others replaced command-line systems such as UNIX and DOS, and PC use surged.
  • Around the 1990s: the World Wide Web accelerated mass use of computers.
  • Afterwards: laptops, then smartphones and tablets, and now wearables and smart appliances in the Internet of Things, using artificial intelligence.

Many exams group this history into "generations" of computers by their main switching technology. NCERT does not number generations, but its milestones map onto the usual grouping:

TechnologyNCERT milestone

TransistorsBell Labs transistor (1947) replaces vacuum tubes
Integrated circuitsIC: a whole circuit on one silicon chip
Microprocessors (LSI, VLSI)CPU on one chip in the 1970s; about 3 million components in the 1980s
  • Pascal vs Babbage: Pascal built a calculator (Pascaline); Babbage designed a general computing engine. "Father of the computer" is Babbage.
  • Hollerith = punched cards for tabulating data, not the stored program.
  • Von Neumann = stored program. Turing = the theoretical general-purpose machine.
  • Transistors replaced vacuum tubes, and ICs then put whole circuits on a chip. Do not swap the order.
  • Moore's Law is a prediction about doubling every two years, not a law of physics.

Q1. Whose invention does NCERT say is considered to form the basis of modern computers? easy

✅ Answer: (b) Charles Babbage's Analytical Engine

💡 Explanation: NCERT describes the Analytical Engine (1834) as a mechanical device for input, processing, storage and output that is considered the basis of modern computers. This is why Babbage is called the father of the computer.

Q2. Who designed a tabulating machine to summarise data stored on punched cards? easy

✅ Answer: (c) Herman Hollerith

💡 Explanation: Herman Hollerith's tabulating machine (1890 on the NCERT timeline) worked on punched cards and is described as a step towards programming.

Q3. The stored program concept, in which data and program are both kept in memory, was introduced by: medium

✅ Answer: (b) John von Neumann

💡 Explanation: NCERT credits John von Neumann with the stored program computer concept, which gave the von Neumann architecture of CPU, memory, input/output and communication channels.

Q4. Transistors replaced which component in early computers? easy

✅ Answer: (b) Vacuum tubes

💡 Explanation: NCERT's timeline says vacuum tubes were replaced by transistors developed at Bell Labs using semiconductor materials (1947).

Q5. Moore's Law predicts that the number of transistors on a chip will double roughly every: easy

✅ Answer: (c) Two years

💡 Explanation: Gordon Moore's 1965 prediction, as NCERT states it, was doubling every two years while costs halve.

Who is called the father of the computer?

Charles Babbage. NCERT says his Analytical Engine of 1834, which could take input, process, store and display output, is considered to form the basis of modern computers.

Which was the first computer?

It depends on the definition. NCERT calls ENIAC the first binary programmable computer based on the von Neumann architecture, while mechanical calculators such as the Pascaline (1642) came centuries earlier.

What did Herman Hollerith invent?

A tabulating machine that summarised data stored on punched cards, shown on NCERT's timeline at 1890 as a step towards programming.
