NEFT is a nation-wide centralised payment system owned and operated by the Reserve Bank of India. It is available round the clock on all days, but it settles in batches at half-hourly intervals. That batch processing is the key difference from RTGS.

  • Limits: RBI sets no limit, though a bank may set its own based on its risk perception. A person without a bank account can send cash through NEFT, up to Rs 50,000 per transaction.
  • Charges: online NEFT from a savings account has been free since 1 January 2020. For other outward transfers RBI caps the charge, from Rs 2.50 (up to Rs 10,000) to Rs 25 (above Rs 2 lakh), plus GST.
  • Timing: credit is expected within two hours of batch settlement. Delays attract penal interest at the RBI repo rate plus two percent.
  • Abroad: outbound NEFT remittances are allowed only to Nepal, under the Indo-Nepal Remittance Facility Scheme.

RBI defines RTGS as continuous, real-time settlement, transaction by transaction, without netting.

  • "Real time" means instructions are processed when received.
  • "Gross" means each transfer settles individually.
  • Funds settle in RBI's books, so payments are final and irrevocable.

RTGS is meant mainly for large values: the minimum is Rs 2 lakh and there is no ceiling. It has run 24x7x365 since 14 December 2020. Inward RTGS is free. Outward charges are capped at Rs 25 for Rs 2 to 5 lakh and Rs 50 above Rs 5 lakh. The beneficiary bank must credit the account within 30 minutes. RTGS does not accept future-dated transactions.

An RBI Bulletin article (June 2019) describes IMPS as an instant inter-bank electronic funds transfer system that customers can operate through mobile phones. It was launched on 22 November 2010 and works through mobile, internet, ATM, SMS, branch and USSD channels.

UPI, developed by the National Payments Corporation of India (NPCI), is, in RBI's words, an immediate money transfer system that enables round-the-clock interbank transfers throughout the year. Its milestones, from a PIB explainer of August 2026:

  • pilot in April 2016 with 21 member banks, and public rollout in August 2016;
  • BHIM app launched in December 2016;
  • one billion transactions in a month crossed in October 2019;
  • UPI 123PAY brought feature-phone users into digital payments, and UPI Lite enabled small PIN-less payments;
  • the tax-payment limit was raised from Rs 1 lakh to Rs 5 lakh, and the UPI Lite wallet limit to Rs 5,000.

The scale is now large: in July 2026 UPI had 741 live banks and processed 2,365.8 crore transactions. It is accepted in 11 countries. As early as 2019, RBI noted that UPI had superseded IMPS in transaction volumes.

The IFSC (Indian Financial System Code) is an 11-character code:

  • the first 4 letters identify the bank;
  • the 5th character is always 0 (zero);
  • the last 6 characters identify the branch.

Both NEFT and RTGS are credit-push systems: only the payer can start a transfer. In RTGS, credit is given solely on the account number, so a wrong number sends money to the wrong account.

  • NEFT is 24x7 but still settles in batches. "24x7" does not mean "real time".
  • RTGS has a minimum (Rs 2 lakh), not a maximum. NEFT has neither from RBI.
  • NEFT and RTGS are run by RBI; UPI is NPCI's.
  • UPI's pilot was in April 2016, its public launch in August 2016, and BHIM in December 2016.

What is the main difference between NEFT and RTGS?

NEFT settles in half-hourly batches with no RBI minimum, while RTGS settles each transaction individually in real time and needs at least Rs 2 lakh.

Who runs UPI and IMPS?

Both are run by the National Payments Corporation of India (NPCI). NEFT and RTGS are owned and operated by the Reserve Bank of India.
