Difference Between AI and Machine Learning, With NLP, Virtual and Augmented Reality, Robotics and the Five Vs of Big Data

AI imitates human intelligence • ML is a subsystem of AI that learns from data • Training and testing data • NLP • VR vs AR • Robots, humanoids, drones • Big data: volume, velocity, variety, veracity, value

SSC Banking UPSC 📅 Last updated: 2026-11-04

Artificial Intelligence (AI) tries to copy human intelligence in machines so that they behave intelligently. NCERT lists the human abilities an intelligent machine should imitate: learning, decision-making and problem solving.

To work with little human help, an AI system is programmed to build a knowledge base (a store of facts, assumptions and rules) and to make decisions from it. It can also learn from past outcomes to make better decisions next time.

NCERT's everyday examples are easy to remember:

  • map apps that pick the fastest route by reading live traffic data;
  • social networks that recognise and tag friends in an uploaded photo;
  • personal assistants such as Siri, Google Now, Cortana and Alexa.

Machine Learning (ML) is a subsystem of AI. In ML, computers learn from data using statistical techniques without being explicitly programmed by a person. That phrase is what separates the two: AI is the goal of intelligent behaviour, and ML is one way to reach it, by learning from examples instead of following hand-written rules.

ML algorithms are called models. A model is first trained on training data and then checked on separate testing data. After repeated training, once its results reach an acceptable accuracy, it is used to make predictions on new, unseen data. A common exam trap is to swap the two: training data teaches the model, testing data checks it.

Natural Language Processing (NLP) deals with how humans and computers interact in human languages such as Hindi and English. NCERT's examples come from daily life: the search box that predicts the next word as you type, and spell checking.

An NLP system can convert text to speech and speech to text, which is how we can search the web or control devices by voice. Two fast-growing uses are machine translation between languages and automated customer service, where software answers customers' questions and complaints.

Both create immersive experiences that stimulate the senses, and both are used in training, for example driving and flight simulators. They differ in one key way.

A robot is a machine that carries out tasks automatically with accuracy. Unlike other machines, a robot is programmable, and sensors are one of its main parts. Robots can be wheeled, legged, manipulators or humanoids. Robots that look like humans are called humanoids; NCERT's example is Sophia, which uses AI, facial recognition and imitates human expressions.

Two other examples NCERT gives are NASA's Mars Exploration Rover mission and the drone, an unmanned aircraft that is either remotely controlled or flies on its own using onboard sensors and GPS. Drones are used in filming, delivery, disaster management, agriculture and border patrolling.

AI and ML need large amounts of data. Data sets so large and complex that ordinary tools cannot process them are called big data. NCERT lists five characteristics:

  1. Volume: enormous size, too large for traditional database tools.
  2. Velocity: the speed at which data is generated and stored.
  3. Variety: structured, semi-structured and unstructured data such as text, images and video.
  4. Veracity: trustworthiness. Noisy or biased data gives wrong results.
  5. Value: hidden patterns and useful knowledge worth the cost of processing.

The five Vs often appear as a matching question: a definition on one side and the five words on the other. Volume and velocity are easy to confuse, so tie velocity to speed and volume to size. Variety is about kinds of data, veracity about whether the data can be trusted, and value about whether processing it is worth the cost.

Computer awareness papers usually test these topics with short, example-based questions. A question describes something familiar, such as a voice assistant, a word suggestion while typing, a VR headset or a robot that looks human, and asks which technology it is. The fastest way to answer is to match the example to its definition: imitating human thinking is AI, learning from data is ML, handling human language is NLP, a computer-made world is VR, extra information over the real world is AR, and a human-like robot is a humanoid.

Q1. According to NCERT, what is the relationship between machine learning and artificial intelligence? easy

✅ Answer: (a) ML is a subsystem of AI

💡 Explanation: NCERT calls machine learning a subsystem of AI, in which computers learn from data without being explicitly programmed.

Q2. The suggestion of the next word while you type in a search box is an example of: easy

✅ Answer: (c) Natural language processing

💡 Explanation: NCERT gives predictive typing and spell checking as examples of NLP, which handles human languages.

Q3. Pointing your phone camera at a monument and seeing its history laid over the live view is an example of: medium

✅ Answer: (d) Augmented reality

💡 Explanation: AR adds digital information on top of the real surroundings. VR replaces the surroundings with a computer-generated world.

Q4. Which 'V' of big data refers to the trustworthiness of the data? medium

✅ Answer: (b) Veracity

💡 Explanation: NCERT says veracity refers to trustworthiness, because noisy or biased data can give wrong results.

Q5. In machine learning, a model is checked for accuracy on which data before it is used on new data? medium

✅ Answer: (c) Testing data

💡 Explanation: Models are first trained on training data and then tested on testing data. Only when accuracy is acceptable are they used to predict on new data.

In one line, what is the difference between AI and machine learning?

AI is the broad goal of making machines behave intelligently; machine learning is one way of reaching it, in which the machine learns patterns from data instead of following rules written by a person.

Are deep learning and generative AI covered on this page?

No. The NCERT chapter this page is based on does not cover them, so they are left out rather than explained from an unchecked source. Both are usually described as later developments within machine learning.

Is a voice assistant like Alexa AI or NLP?

Both. NCERT gives these assistants as examples of AI, and understanding speech and replying in human language is the NLP part of what they do.
