Mirror and Water Images: Letters, Numbers, Words and the Mirror Image of Clock Time

Mirror flips left-right • Water flips top-bottom • Letters that stay the same • Clock in a mirror: 11:60 − time • Clock in water: 18:30 − time

SSC Railway 📅 Last updated: 2026-11-02

Both images are reflections, but along different lines:

  • A mirror image (mirror held to the side) flips the object left to right. Top and bottom stay in place.
  • A water image (object reflected in water below it) flips it top to bottom. Left and right stay in place.

So a mirror reverses the order of letters in a word and turns each one round; water turns each letter upside down but keeps the order. If an option looks right except that it is upside down, it is the water image, not the mirror image.

In option-based questions, do not try to picture the whole image at once. Pick one distinctive feature, such as the letter at one end of the word, a slanted stroke, or the hand nearest 12, and work out where it must end up. Usually two or three options fail that single test, and a second feature settles the rest. This is faster and far less error-prone than redrawing the full figure.

In a plain capital font, a letter looks unchanged in a mirror if it has a vertical line of symmetry, and unchanged in water if it has a horizontal one.

Vertical symmetryA, H, I, M, O, T, U, V, W, X, YMirror
Horizontal symmetryB, C, D, E, H, I, K, O, XWater
BothH, I, O, XMirror and water

Digits behave similarly: in a seven-segment style, 0 and 8 look the same both ways, and 3 looks the same in water. Small letters vary by font, so exams use capitals.

For a word, check two things. In a mirror, the letter order reverses, so MOM stays MOM but TOM becomes MOT, mirrored. In water, the order stays the same, so a word made only of B, C, D, E, H, I, K, O and X letters, such as BOX, looks unchanged.

A side mirror makes each hand travel anticlockwise by the same angle, so the mirrored time is the time remaining until 12:00. To avoid borrowing, write 12:00 as 11:60 and subtract.

Mirror time = 11:60 − actual time (for times from 1:00 to 11:59)

For times from 12:00 to 12:59, subtract from 23:60 instead, or treat 12 as 0.

Worked example 1: 3:40

11:60 − 3:40 = 8:20.

Worked example 2: 10:15

11:60 − 10:15 = 1:45.

Worked example 3: 12:20

Treat 12:20 as 0:20. 11:60 − 0:20 = 11:40.

The rule also works backwards: if the mirror shows a time, subtract it from 11:60 to get the real time.

Worked example 4: from the image to the real time

A mirror shows 4:50. Actual time = 11:60 − 4:50 = 7:10.

Quick check: 3:00 and 9:00 swap, 6:00 and 12:00 stay put, and a minute hand on 2 (10 minutes) appears on 10 (50 minutes).

Exams use a second rule for water images: subtract the time from 18:30 and convert to the 12-hour clock if needed. If the result is above 12, subtract 12.

Worked example 5: 2:10

18:30 − 2:10 = 16:20, which is 4:20.

The minute hand is placed exactly by this rule (a minute hand at 2, or 10 minutes, is reflected to 4, or 20 minutes). The hour hand is only approximate, because a real clock can never show a true upside-down reflection. Use 18:30 because that is the convention the options are built on.

  • Using the mirror rule for water. Mirror is 11:60; water is 18:30.
  • Forgetting to reverse word order in a mirror. Each letter flips and the sequence also runs backwards.
  • Applying 11:60 to times after 12:00. Treat 12:xx as 0:xx first.
  • Trusting small letters. b, d, p and q change into each other in mirrors and water, and fonts differ.
  • Picking the water image for a mirror question. If the option is upside down, it is the wrong reflection.

Q1. A clock shows 4.25. What time does its mirror image show? (Times are written as hours.minutes.) easy

✅ Answer: (a) 7.35

💡 Explanation: Subtract the time from 11.60: 11.60 − 4.25 = 7.35, so the mirror shows 7.35.

Q2. What is the mirror image of 9.05 on a clock? easy

✅ Answer: (b) 2.55

💡 Explanation: 11.60 − 9.05 = 2.55. Check: the minute hand at 1 (5 minutes) appears at 11 (55 minutes) in the mirror.

Q3. By the usual exam rule, what is the water image of 1.20 on a clock? medium

✅ Answer: (d) 5.10

💡 Explanation: Subtract from 18.30: 18.30 − 1.20 = 17.10, which is 5.10 on a 12-hour clock. 10.40 is the mirror image, not the water image.

Q4. In a mirror, a clock appears to show 2.30. What is the actual time? medium

✅ Answer: (c) 9.30

💡 Explanation: The mirror rule works both ways: 11.60 − 2.30 = 9.30. A clock at 9.30 looks like 2.30 in the mirror.

Q5. How many of the letters A, B, C, D, E, H look exactly the same in a plane mirror held to their right? medium

✅ Answer: (c) 2

💡 Explanation: Only letters with a vertical line of symmetry look the same in a side mirror. Of these six, only A and H have one; B, C, D and E have horizontal symmetry instead, so they look the same in water.

Why does the 11:60 rule work?

A side mirror sends each hand to the same angle measured anticlockwise instead of clockwise. That is the time left until 12:00, which is 12:00 − time, written as 11:60 − time to make the subtraction easy.

Is the water image of a clock an exact time?

Not exactly. A top-to-bottom flip puts the minute hand exactly where 18:30 − time says, but the hour hand of a real clock would be about half an hour's worth off. Exams use the 18:30 rule as a convention, so apply it as given.

Does a mirror reverse a whole word?

Yes. A mirror on the right reverses the order of the letters and flips each letter left to right. A word looks unchanged only if every letter is symmetrical about a vertical line and the word reads the same backwards, such as TOOT.
