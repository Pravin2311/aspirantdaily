Inferences from a Passage: The Five-Option Method

The five options • A four-step method • One passage worked through • Numbers in the passage • Traps

Banking 📅 Last updated: 2026-10-29

In this question type you get a short passage, often about the economy, a policy or a social trend, followed by several statements. For each statement you decide how far the passage supports it. A common format gives five fixed options:

  • Definitely true: the statement follows directly from the passage, or can be calculated from its figures.
  • Probably true: the passage points towards it, but does not prove it.
  • Data inadequate: the passage gives no basis to judge it either way.
  • Probably false: the passage points against it, but does not rule it out.
  • Definitely false: the statement contradicts the passage.

The skill being tested is precision. You are not asked whether the statement is true in the real world, only how strongly the passage supports it. Treat the passage as the whole universe of facts for the question.

Work through these steps in order for every statement. The order matters, because the certain verdicts are the easiest to prove and the hardest to argue with.

  1. Look for certainty. Is the statement stated in the passage, a restatement of it, or a calculation from its figures? Then it is definitely true. Does it contradict a stated fact or figure? Then it is definitely false.
  2. Look for a lean. If it is not certain, does something in the passage make it more likely, such as a trend, a reason the passage gives or a claim by someone in it? Then it is probably true. Does the passage make it less likely? Then it is probably false.
  3. No link means data inadequate. If the statement is about something the passage does not discuss at all, the answer is data inadequate.
  4. Re-read the qualifiers. Words such as only, all, most, always and never often turn a probable statement into a definitely false one.

Statements that try to make you leap from "this happened after that" to "that caused this" usually belong in the probable categories. A passage rarely proves a cause unless it says so outright.

The passage below is invented for practice; Rivana is a fictional state and the figures are made up.

Practice passage

In 2025, the number of digital payment transactions in the state of Rivana rose 25% to 500 crore. Rural areas accounted for 40% of these transactions, up from 30% the year before. The state government attributes the growth mainly to cheaper smartphones and an awareness campaign it ran in rural districts. Cash withdrawals from ATMs in the state fell over the same period.

Now classify each statement.

Statement 1: 'Rivana had 400 crore digital transactions in 2024.'

A 25% rise led to 500 crore, so the earlier figure was 500 ÷ 1.25 = 400 crore. It is calculated from the passage: definitely true.

Statement 2: 'Rural digital transactions grew faster than total transactions.'

Rural transactions were 30% of 400 = 120 crore in 2024 and 40% of 500 = 200 crore in 2025, a rise of about 66.7%. Total transactions rose 25%. So rural growth was faster: definitely true.

Statement 3: 'Urban digital transactions fell in 2025.'

Urban transactions were 400 − 120 = 280 crore in 2024 and 500 − 200 = 300 crore in 2025. They rose by 20 crore, even though their share fell. The statement contradicts the figures: definitely false. Confusing a falling share with a falling number is the trap here.

Statement 4: 'Most of Rivana's digital transactions in 2025 took place in rural areas.'

Rural areas had 40% of transactions, which is 200 crore of 500. That is less than half, so the statement is definitely false.

Statement 5: 'People in Rivana used less cash in 2025 than in 2024.'

ATM withdrawals fell while digital payments rose, which points that way. But people can get cash in other ways, such as bank counters, so the passage does not prove it: probably true.

Statement 6: 'The rural awareness campaign had no effect on digital payments.'

The government credits the campaign as a main reason for the growth, and rural transactions grew fastest. The passage leans against the statement without proving the campaign worked: probably false.

Statement 7: 'Rivana's banks charge a fee on ATM withdrawals.'

The passage mentions ATM withdrawals falling but says nothing about fees, or anything that makes a fee more or less likely: data inadequate.

When a passage gives figures, a statement built from them is usually certain one way or the other, so check the arithmetic before reaching for a probable answer. Three habits help:

  • Recover the base before comparing. A 25% rise to 500 means the base was 400, not 375.
  • Separate shares from amounts. A share can fall while the amount behind it rises, as with urban transactions above.
  • Compare growth rates on their own bases, not the raw differences.

For example, if 40% of every 100 transactions were rural, that is 40 rural transactions per hundred, and a statement saying "four in ten" is the same fact restated, so it is definitely true.

  • ✘ Using real-world knowledge. A statement can be true in life and still be data inadequate here.
  • ✘ Treating "happened after" as "was caused by". That is probably true at best.
  • ✘ Marking every unfamiliar statement as data inadequate. First check whether the passage leans one way.
  • ✘ Missing an extreme word such as only or all that makes the statement definitely false.

The same discipline of judging only from what is given runs through statement and conclusions and statement and assumptions. To practise telling a fact from an opinion, see fact, inference and judgement.

Q1. Passage: 'A city library extended its opening hours from 8 to 12 hours a day in March. Average daily visitors rose from 600 in February to 900 in April. A survey found that 70% of the additional visitors were students.' Statement: 'Average daily visitors rose by 50% between February and April.' easy

✅ Answer: (a) Definitely true

💡 Explanation: The rise is 300 on a base of 600, which is exactly 50%. It is computed from stated figures, so it is definitely true.

Q2. Same library passage. Statement: 'The library charges a membership fee.' easy

✅ Answer: (c) Data inadequate

💡 Explanation: The passage says nothing about fees, and nothing in it makes a fee more or less likely. With no basis either way, the answer is data inadequate.

Q3. Same library passage. Statement: 'The longer opening hours helped attract more visitors.' medium

✅ Answer: (b) Probably true

💡 Explanation: Visitors rose soon after the hours were extended, which strongly suggests a link. But the passage never says the hours caused the rise, so it is probably, not definitely, true.

Q4. Same library passage. Statement: 'Only students use the library.' medium

✅ Answer: (d) Definitely false

💡 Explanation: The survey found that 70% of the additional visitors were students, so 30% of them were not. The statement contradicts the passage and is definitely false.

Q5. Same library passage. Statement: 'More than 200 of the additional daily visitors were students.' hard

✅ Answer: (a) Definitely true

💡 Explanation: The additional visitors number 900 − 600 = 300, and 70% of 300 is 210, which is more than 200. Computed from stated figures, so definitely true.

Q6. Same library passage. Statement: 'Extending the opening hours made no difference to the number of students using the library.' hard

✅ Answer: (b) Probably false

💡 Explanation: Most of the extra visitors after the change were students, so the passage leans against 'no difference'. It does not prove the hours were the cause, so the answer is probably false.

When should I choose 'data inadequate' rather than 'probably true'?

Choose probably true only when something in the passage makes the statement more likely, such as a trend, a cause the passage suggests, or an official's claim. If the statement brings in a topic the passage never touches, there is nothing to lean on, so the answer is data inadequate.

Can I use general knowledge in inference questions?

Use it only to understand what the words mean, never to supply facts. The passage is the only evidence. A statement that is true in the real world but has no support in the passage is still data inadequate.
