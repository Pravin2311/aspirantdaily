Letter Series and Alphanumeric Series: Finding the Pattern Fast

Letters to numbers • Fixed and growing gaps • Backward series • Two interleaved series • Letter groups • Letters with numbers

SSC Banking Railway 📅 Last updated: 2026-10-20

A letter series is a number series in disguise. The quickest way to find any pattern is to write each letter's position in the alphabet under it, A = 1 to Z = 26, and then look at the gaps between the numbers. Most patterns jump out once you can see the numbers.

To find positions instantly, use the anchor letters E, J, O, T and Y, which sit at 5, 10, 15, 20 and 25. Every other letter is within two places of one of them. The EJOTY trick and reverse positions page covers this in detail, and it is worth learning before you practise series.

When a position goes above 26, subtract 26 to wrap round to the start of the alphabet. When it goes below 1, add 26.

The simplest series add or subtract the same number each time. Check the first two gaps; if they match, test the rest.

Worked example 1: a fixed gap

C, F, I, L, ? The positions are 3, 6, 9, 12, rising by 3. The next is 15, which is O.

If the gaps are not equal, look at the gaps themselves. They often rise by one each time, double, or follow the squares.

Worked example 2: a growing gap

B, C, E, H, L, ? The gaps are +1, +2, +3, +4, so the next is +5. L is 12, and 12 + 5 = 17, which is Q.

Worked example 3: a backward series

Z, W, T, Q, ? The positions fall by 3: 26, 23, 20, 17. The next is 14, which is N.

Watch for wrap-round near the end of the alphabet. In S, V, Y, ?, the gap is +3, so the next position is 25 + 3 = 28. Subtract 26 to get 2, which is B.

If neither the gaps nor the gaps between gaps make sense, the series may be two separate series woven together. Read only the odd-numbered terms, then only the even-numbered terms. Each half usually has a simple pattern of its own.

Worked example 4: alternate terms

A, Z, C, X, E, V, ? The odd terms are A, C, E, rising by 2. The even terms are Z, X, V, falling by 2. The missing term is the next odd one: G.

A clue that two series are interleaved is a jump back and forth between the two ends of the alphabet, as in this example.

Many questions use groups of two, three or four letters. Look at two things separately: the pattern inside a group, and the step from the first letter of one group to the first letter of the next.

Worked example 5: consecutive letters

ABC, EFG, IJK, ? Each group holds three consecutive letters, and each group starts 4 places after the previous one: A, E, I. The next group starts at M, giving MNO.

Some groups pair a letter with its opposite in the alphabet, the letter the same distance from the other end. Opposites always add up to 27 in position: A (1) and Z (26), B (2) and Y (25), and so on.

Worked example 6: opposite pairs

AZ, BY, CX, ? The first letters run A, B, C, so the next is D. Its opposite is at 27 − 4 = 23, which is W. The answer is DW.

Alphanumeric series combine letters and numbers in each term. Treat them as two series running side by side. Often the number is linked to the letter, for example its position, its position doubled, or its reverse position.

Worked example 7: letter and position

A1, C3, E5, G7, ? The letters skip one each time, and every number is the position of its letter. The next letter is I, at position 9, so the term is I9.

Another common style is the blank-filling series, such as a b _ a b _ a b _, where a short block repeats. Find the length of the repeating block first, then fill each blank from the matching place in the block.

  • ✘ Counting positions in your head without writing them down. One slip ruins the pattern.
  • ✘ Forgetting to wrap round past Z or before A.
  • ✘ Treating an interleaved series as a single series.
  • ✘ In letter groups, comparing the wrong letters. Compare first with first, second with second.

Q1. Find the next term: D, H, L, P, ? easy

✅ Answer: (b) T

💡 Explanation: The positions are 4, 8, 12 and 16, rising by 4 each time. The next position is 20, which is T.

Q2. Find the next term: A, C, F, J, O, ? medium

✅ Answer: (d) U

💡 Explanation: The gaps grow by one each time: +2, +3, +4, +5. The next gap is +6, and O (15) + 6 = 21, which is U.

Q3. Find the next term: Y, V, S, P, ? easy

✅ Answer: (a) M

💡 Explanation: The series moves backwards by 3: 25, 22, 19, 16. The next position is 13, which is M.

Q4. Find the next group: BDF, HJL, NPR, ? medium

✅ Answer: (c) TVX

💡 Explanation: Within each group the letters skip one (B, D, F). Each group starts 6 places after the previous one: B, H, N, so the next starts at T and reads TVX.

Q5. Find the next term: B2, E5, H8, K11, ? easy

✅ Answer: (a) N14

💡 Explanation: Each letter moves forward 3 places and each number is the position of its letter. After K (11) comes N, at position 14, so the term is N14.

Q6. Find the next term: A, Z, B, Y, C, X, ? medium

✅ Answer: (d) D

💡 Explanation: Two series alternate. The odd terms A, B, C move forward and the even terms Z, Y, X move backward. The next term is an odd one, so it is D.

What should I try first when a letter series looks random?

Write the position number under every letter and look at the differences. If the differences are not constant, check the alternate terms separately, then check whether the differences themselves form a pattern such as 1, 2, 3 or 2, 4, 8.

What happens when a series runs past Z?

It wraps round to A. Position 27 is A again, 28 is B and so on, so subtract 26 from any position above 26. Backward series wrap from A to Z in the same way.
