Syllogism with 'Only a Few' and 'Only': Rules, Diagrams and Solved Questions

Only a few = some + some not • Diagram with a split circle • 'Only A are B' reversed • Solved combinations • Common traps

Banking SSC Railway 📅 Last updated: 2026-11-10

Newer syllogism questions in bank and SSC exams use restrictive words such as only a few and only. They look like the familiar "some", but they say more, and missing that extra meaning is the most common reason students lose these marks.

"Only a few A are B" is really two statements joined together:

  1. Some A are B, and
  2. Some A are not B.

Because of the second part, A can never lie wholly inside B. Because of the first part, A and B can never be fully apart. Everything else in this topic follows from those two facts.

Conclusion from "Only a few A are B"Verdict
Some A are BDefinitely true
Some A are not BDefinitely true
Some B are ADefinitely true
All A are B / All A being B is a possibilityFalse: never possible
No A is B / No A being B is a possibilityFalse: never possible
All B being A is a possibilityFollows

The last row surprises many students. "Only a few" limits how many A are B. It says nothing about how many B are A, so the whole B circle may sit inside the overlapping part of A.

Draw A and B as two overlapping circles, and mark the part of A outside B with a cross or a shaded strip to show it must contain members. That marked strip is what stops you from later drawing A inside B when you test a possibility.

Compare the four basic statement types you already know from the syllogism Venn diagram method. "Only a few" behaves like a "some" statement and a "some not" statement drawn on the same pair of circles.

  • "A few A are B" (without "only") is read as plain "some A are B". It still allows all A to be B.
  • "Only a few A are not B" gives the same two facts in the other order: some A are not B and some A are B.

"Only A are B" means that nothing except A can be B. In other words, all B are A. Draw the B circle inside A.

So "Only doctors are surgeons" is the same as "All surgeons are doctors". It does not say all doctors are surgeons; that is only a possibility.

Conclusion from "Only A are B"Verdict
All B are ADefinitely true
Some A are B / Some B are ADefinitely true
All A are BNot definite; follows only as a possibility
Some A are not BNot definite; follows only as a possibility

The trap is to read "only A are B" as "all A are B". The direction is reversed: the word after "only" is the bigger circle.

With three terms there are seven regions in the diagram. You do not need to draw them all; just place the circles one statement at a time and keep the "must contain members" marks.

Case 1: Only a few A are B. All B are C.

The A members inside B are also inside C, so some A are C is definite. The A members outside B have no restriction, so all A being C is a possibility. "No A is C" is impossible.

Case 2: Only a few A are B. No B is C.

The A members inside B can never be C, so some A are not C is definite and all A being C is impossible. "Some A are C" and "No A is C" are each possible, and form an either-or pair if both are offered.

Case 3: All A are B. Only a few B are C.

The only definite new fact is some B are not C, which comes straight from the second statement. Whether A touches C is open: "some A are C", "no A is C" and "all A are C" are all possible.

Case 4: Only A are B. Some B are C.

Every B is an A, and some B are C, so those B members are A and C at once: some A are C is definite. "All C are A" is only a possibility.

Case 5: Only a few A are B. Only a few B are C.

A and C share no definite link. "Some A are C", "No A is C" and "All A are C" are all possible. If a question offers "Some A are C" and "No A is C" together, answer either I or II follows.

  1. Rewrite the restrictive statements. "Only a few A are B" becomes "some A are B + some A are not B". "Only A are B" becomes "all B are A".
  2. Draw the minimum diagram, marking every region the statements say must contain members.
  3. Test definite conclusions by asking whether any allowed diagram makes them false.
  4. Test possibilities by trying to draw them without breaking a marked region or any statement.
  5. Look for either-or pairs on the same two terms: some/no, or some not/all.

For practice, take the three definite facts and three impossible conclusions in the first table and say each one aloud before you start a mock test. That is six quick checks, and they cover most of what examiners ask about "only a few".

  • ✘ Treating "only a few" as plain "some". It also says some A are not B, so "all A are B" can never be true, even as a possibility.
  • ✘ Reading "only A are B" as "all A are B". It means all B are A.
  • ✘ Rejecting "all B being A is a possibility" after "only a few A are B". That possibility is allowed.
  • ✘ Using everyday sense. Treat each statement as true, however odd it sounds.
  • ✘ Marking an either-or answer when the two conclusions are about different pairs of terms.

For the four basic statement types and possibility rules, start with the syllogism Venn diagram method. Diagram-choice questions are covered in Venn diagram reasoning.

Q1. Statement: Only a few books are novels. Conclusions: I. Some books are not novels. II. All books being novels is a possibility. easy

✅ Answer: (a) Only I follows

💡 Explanation: 'Only a few' means some books are novels and some are not. So I is definitely true, and all books being novels is ruled out by the statement itself, so II does not follow.

Q2. Statements: Only a few pens are blue. All blue are inks. Conclusions: I. Some pens are inks. II. All pens being inks is a possibility. medium

✅ Answer: (a) Both follow

💡 Explanation: The pens that are blue sit inside inks, so some pens are inks. The pens that are not blue may still lie inside the ink circle, because no statement keeps them out, so all pens being inks is possible.

Q3. Statements: Only a few cups are plates. No plate is a bowl. Conclusions: I. Some cups are not bowls. II. All cups being bowls is a possibility. medium

✅ Answer: (a) Only I follows

💡 Explanation: The cups that are plates can never be bowls, so some cups are not bowls. For the same reason all cups can never be bowls, so II is impossible.

Q4. Statements: Only doctors are surgeons. Some surgeons are teachers. Conclusions: I. Some doctors are teachers. II. All doctors being surgeons is a possibility. medium

✅ Answer: (a) Both follow

💡 Explanation: 'Only doctors are surgeons' means all surgeons are doctors. The surgeons who are teachers are therefore doctors, so I follows. Nothing stops every doctor from being a surgeon, so II is possible and follows.

Q5. Statements: All rings are gold. Only a few gold are costly. Conclusions: I. Some gold are not costly. II. Some rings are costly. medium

✅ Answer: (a) Only I follows

💡 Explanation: I comes straight from 'only a few'. The costly part of gold may or may not touch the rings, so II is only a possibility, not a definite conclusion.

Q6. Statements: Only a few tables are chairs. Only a few chairs are sofas. Conclusions: I. Some tables are sofas. II. No table is a sofa. hard

✅ Answer: (a) Either I or II follows

💡 Explanation: Tables and sofas have no definite relation: they may overlap or not. I and II are about the same two terms, each is possible, and exactly one of them must be true, so this is an either-or pair.

What does 'only a few A are B' mean in syllogism?

It means two things at once: some A are B, and some A are not B. So 'all A are B' is false, 'no A is B' is false, and 'some B are A' is definitely true.

Is 'a few' the same as 'only a few'?

No. 'A few A are B' is treated like 'some A are B', which still allows all A to be B. 'Only a few' adds the second part, that some A are not B, so all A being B is no longer possible.

How do I read 'only A are B'?

Reverse it. 'Only A are B' means every B is an A, so draw the B circle inside A. It does not say that all A are B; that remains a possibility unless another statement rules it out.

Can all B be A when only a few A are B?

Yes, it is a possibility. The statement limits how many A are B, not how many B are A, so the whole B circle may sit inside the part of A that overlaps it.
