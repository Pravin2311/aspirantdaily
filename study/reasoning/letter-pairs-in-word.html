How Many Pairs of Letters in the Word Have as Many Letters Between Them as in the Alphabet?

What counts as a pair • Write positions under the word • Check each letter against all later letters • Forward-only vs both directions • Repeated letters

SSC Banking 📅 Last updated: 2026-11-02

The question asks: how many pairs of letters in a word have as many letters between them in the word as they have between them in the alphabet?

Put simply, take any two letters of the word. Count the gap between them in the word, and the gap between them in the alphabet. If the two gaps are equal, the pair counts.

  • Forward pair: the later letter in the word is also later in the alphabet, as in G…H or L…N.
  • Backward pair: the later letter in the word comes earlier in the alphabet, as in T…S or I…E.

Unless the question says "forward only" or "in alphabetical order", count both. The gap can be zero: two neighbouring letters in the word that are neighbours in the alphabet (such as S and T) form a pair.

Write each letter's alphabet position under it. Then the rule becomes simple arithmetic: for letters at word positions i and j, the pair counts if

|alphabet position difference| = j − i

Work letter by letter from the left. For each letter, compare it with every letter to its right, stopping once the word-gap is larger than any alphabet difference could be. This systematic sweep is what stops you missing pairs.

Worked example 1: FIGHTER

Positions: F 6, I 9, G 7, H 8, T 20, E 5, R 18.

  • F (word place 1): no letter to its right is the right distance away in the alphabet.
  • I (place 2): E is 4 places later in the word and 4 letters earlier in the alphabet (9 − 5). Pair I-E (backward).
  • G (place 3): H is next in the word and next in the alphabet. Pair G-H (forward).
  • T (place 5): R is 2 places later and 2 letters earlier (20 − 18). Pair T-R (backward).

Total: 3 pairs.

Worked example 2: forward only

If the question asks only for pairs in alphabetical order, FIGHTER has just 1: G-H. The other two run backwards.

One letter can belong to several pairs. Keep going after you find the first match for a letter.

Worked example 3: BRIGHTEN

Positions: B 2, R 18, I 9, G 7, H 8, T 20, E 5, N 14. The I at place 3 pairs with E at place 7 (gap 4, and 9 − 5 = 4) and with N at place 8 (gap 5, and 14 − 9 = 5). G-H is a neighbouring pair. Total: 3 pairs, two of which share the letter I.

Worked example 4: CHARGED

Positions: C 3, H 8, A 1, R 18, G 7, E 5, D 4. C pairs with A (gap 2, 3 − 1 = 2) and with G (gap 4, 7 − 3 = 4), and E-D are neighbours in both. Total: 3 pairs.

Worked example 5: a word with a single pair

STRANGE: positions S 19, T 20, R 18, A 1, N 14, G 7, E 5. S and T sit side by side in both the word and the alphabet, so they form a pair. Checking every other combination finds nothing: T and R are next to each other in the word but two apart in the alphabet, and S and R are neighbours in the alphabet but two places apart in the word. Total: 1 pair. Words with letters spread widely across the alphabet, like this one, rarely have more than one or two.

For a quick pre-check, look for letters close together in the alphabet: if every letter in a word is far from the others in the alphabet, there can be only a few pairs.

Each occurrence of a repeated letter is a separate letter at its own position. In DEFINITE, the first I (place 4) and the second I (place 6) pair with different letters, so treat them as two different starting points.

  • Counting the letters between, not the gap. Using j − i on word places and the difference of alphabet positions keeps both sides consistent.
  • Forgetting backward pairs. They are often half the answer.
  • Stopping at one pair per letter. A letter can pair with several others.
  • Double counting. Each pair is counted once, from its left letter.
  • Misreading "forward only". Check the wording before you start counting.

Use the EJOTY trick to write alphabet positions quickly, and see letter series for related gap-based questions.

Q1. How many pairs of letters in the word PLANETS have as many letters between them in the word as in the English alphabet (counting both directions)? easy

✅ Answer: (c) 2

💡 Explanation: L-N (one letter between in both the word and the alphabet) and T-S (adjacent in both, read backwards). That makes 2 pairs.

Q2. How many such pairs are there in the word MONKEY? easy

✅ Answer: (a) 1

💡 Explanation: Only O-N qualifies: they are next to each other in the word and next to each other in the alphabet (read backwards). No other pair matches.

Q3. How many such pairs are there in the word HIJACK? medium

✅ Answer: (d) 3

💡 Explanation: H-I, I-J and H-J (one letter between in both) all qualify. A, C and K do not pair with anything, so the answer is 3.

Q4. How many such pairs are there in the word GLIMPSE? medium

✅ Answer: (b) 2

💡 Explanation: G-I has one letter between in both. I-E has three letters between in the word (M, P, S) and three in the alphabet (F, G, H), read backwards. That makes 2.

Q5. How many such pairs are there in the word DEFINITE? hard

✅ Answer: (c) 7

💡 Explanation: D-E, D-F, E-F, D-I, E-I, F-I (all forwards from the first I) and I-E backwards from the second I. That makes 7 pairs.

Do backward pairs count?

Usually yes: most questions mean 'forward and backward', and many say so. If a question says 'in the same order as in the alphabet' or 'forward only', count only pairs whose second letter comes later in the alphabet.

What if a letter repeats in the word?

Treat each occurrence as a separate letter at its own position. In DEFINITE, the two I's pair with different letters because their positions in the word differ.

Do adjacent letters like ST count?

Yes. Two letters next to each other in the word with nothing between them form a pair if they are also next to each other in the alphabet, such as S-T or O-N.
