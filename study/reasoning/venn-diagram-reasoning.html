Venn Diagram Reasoning: Choose the Diagram That Fits Three Classes, and Count Regions in a Figure

Subset, overlap or separate • Testing each pair • Nine standard diagrams • Reading numbers in regions • Only, exactly two, at least two

SSC Railway 📅 Last updated: 2026-11-02

Every Venn diagram question is built from pairs of classes, and any two classes are related in only three ways:

RelationTest questionDrawingExample
SubsetIs every member of A also a B?A's circle inside B'sMango, Fruit
OverlapCan something be both, without all being both?Two crossing circlesWomen, Teachers
SeparateIs nothing ever both?Two circles that do not touchCats, Dogs

With three classes, there are three pairs. Decide each pair's relation, then pick the one diagram that shows all three at once. These questions use real-world facts, unlike syllogisms, which accept the given statements as true.

Exam options are almost always drawn from this set. Learn to name each one in words.

A chain: A overlaps B, B overlaps C, A and C separateMiddle class links the other twoChildren, Students, Retired people

In the last row, some children are students and some students are retired people, but no child is retired. That is why the middle circle touches both outer circles, which do not touch each other.

  1. Look for an obvious subset first (a city inside a state, a mango inside fruit). It removes most options at once.
  2. Check the pair you are least sure of with the test question: can one thing be both?
  3. Check for a common region. With three overlapping classes, ask whether one item can belong to all three. A female Indian doctor shows that Doctors, Women and Indians share a centre.
  4. Match your three pair relations to the diagram list, and reject any option that shows a relation you have ruled out.

Two common slips are drawing "Doctors" and "Lawyers" as separate (a person can hold both degrees) and drawing "Hockey" inside "Cricket" because both are sports (they are separate classes inside "Sports").

The second type of question shows numbers written in the regions of a Venn figure, or gives totals, and asks how many items fit a description. Read the description as a set of conditions, then add the matching regions.

Worked example 1: two sets

40 students like tea, 30 like coffee and 12 like both. Total who like at least one = 40 + 30 − 12 = 58. The overlap is subtracted once because it was counted in both totals.

Worked example 2: only one set

From the same data, tea only = 40 − 12 = 28.

For three sets, label the seven regions. Suppose a figure shows: A only 8, B only 6, C only 5, A and B only 3, B and C only 4, A and C only 2, and all three 1.

Worked example 3: exactly two

Exactly two sets means the three two-way regions without the centre: 3 + 4 + 2 = 9.

Worked example 4: at least two

At least two adds the centre: 9 + 1 = 10.

Worked example 5: in A but not C

Regions inside A and outside C are A only and A-and-B only: 8 + 3 = 11.

Worked example 6: none of the three

If the whole group has 40 members, those outside every circle number 40 − 29 = 11.

A useful habit for every counting question is to fill in the centre first and work outwards. Given totals rather than region numbers, start from "all three", subtract it from each two-way total to get the "exactly two" regions, then subtract those from each set total to get the "only" regions. Finally, add all seven regions and compare with the stated total as a check before answering.

Q1. Which diagram best represents Kolkata, West Bengal and India? easy

✅ Answer: (a) Three circles, one inside the next

💡 Explanation: Kolkata is part of West Bengal, which is part of India, so each class lies wholly inside the next: three nested circles.

Q2. Which diagram best represents Doctors, Women and Singers? easy

✅ Answer: (c) Three mutually overlapping circles

💡 Explanation: Some doctors are women, some women are singers, some singers are doctors, and one person can be all three. Every pair overlaps and there is a common region.

Q3. In a class, 50 students play cricket, 35 play football and 15 play both. How many play exactly one of the two games? medium

✅ Answer: (d) 55

💡 Explanation: Cricket only = 50 − 15 = 35 and football only = 35 − 15 = 20, so exactly one game = 55. Equivalently 50 + 35 − 2 × 15.

Q4. Set A has 20 members, set B has 25, and 8 belong to both. How many belong to B only? easy

✅ Answer: (b) 17

💡 Explanation: B only = all of B minus the shared region = 25 − 8 = 17.

Q5. Which diagram best represents Pens, Pencils and Stationery? easy

✅ Answer: (c) Two separate circles inside a larger circle

💡 Explanation: Pens and pencils are both stationery, but nothing is both a pen and a pencil. So two non-touching circles sit inside one big circle.

How do I decide whether two classes overlap?

Ask whether one thing can belong to both at once. A woman can be a doctor, so Women and Doctors overlap. A cat cannot be a dog, so Cats and Dogs are separate. If every member of one class is in the other, draw one inside the other.

Do Venn diagram questions depend on real-world facts?

Yes. Unlike syllogisms, these questions use what is true in the world, so Mango is always inside Fruit. Syllogism questions instead accept the given statements even if they are false.

What does 'exactly two' mean in a three-set figure?

It means belonging to two of the sets but not the third. Add the three regions where just two circles overlap, and leave out the centre where all three meet.
