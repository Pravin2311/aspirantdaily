Dice Problems: Finding Opposite Faces from Positions and Nets

Standard die: opposites add to 7 • Two views, two common faces • One common face • Four adjacent faces • Unfolded nets

SSC Banking Railway 📅 Last updated: 2026-10-20

A die is a cube with six faces. Each face touches four others, called its adjacent faces, and is directly opposite the remaining one. At any moment you can see at most three faces: the top, the front and one side. Dice questions show you one or more such positions and ask which faces are opposite each other.

Since each face touches 6 − 2 = 4 others, two faces that are ever seen together in one position can never be opposite. That simple fact is the basis of every rule below.

A die can rest in 6 × 4 = 24 different orientations, so do not rely on picturing the rotation in your head. Use the rules instead.

In this page every position is written as top, front and right face, in that order, as you would see them in a printed figure.

On a standard die, the numbers on opposite faces always add up to 7: 1 is opposite 6, 2 is opposite 5, and 3 is opposite 4. If the question says the die is standard, that is all you need.

Worked example 1: top and bottom

A standard die shows 2 on top. The bottom face is 7 − 2 = 5.

All six faces add up to 1 + 2 + 3 + 4 + 5 + 6 = 21, which helps with questions about sums of hidden or adjacent faces.

For example, the four faces adjacent to 3 are everything except 3 and its opposite 4, so they add up to 21 − 7 = 14.

If two positions share two faces, the two remaining faces, one from each position, are opposite. Each of them sits next to both common faces, but they never appear together, so they must be the pair of opposite faces.

Worked example 2: two common faces

Position 1: 1 on top, 2 in front, 3 on the right. Position 2: 2 on top, 1 in front, 6 on the right. The common faces are 1 and 2, so the other faces, 3 and 6, are opposite.

If two positions share one face and it appears in the same place, for example on top in both, read the other two faces in the same order in each position. Faces in matching places are opposite.

Worked example 3: one common face

Position 1: 1 on top, 2 in front, 3 on the right. Position 2: 1 on top, 5 in front, 6 on the right. With 1 on top in both, the front faces 2 and 5 are opposite, and the right faces 3 and 6 are opposite. The last pair is then 1 and 4.

If the common face is in a different place in each position, this shortcut does not apply directly. Mentally rotate one position until the common face is in the same place, or use the rule below.

When there are three or more positions, list every face seen together with the face you are asking about. Once you have found four different neighbours, the one face never seen with it is its opposite.

Worked example 4: collecting neighbours

Position 1: 1, 2, 3. Position 2: 1, 3, 5. Position 3: 6, 2, 1. Across these, 1 appears with 2, 3, 5 and 6. That is four neighbours, so the remaining face, 4, is opposite 1.

Some questions show the die flattened into a net of six squares. Two rules cover most nets.

  • In a straight row or column of squares, faces with exactly one square between them are opposite.
  • Squares that touch along an edge in the net are adjacent on the die, so they cannot be opposite.

Worked example 5: a cross-shaped net

A net has the row 3, 1, 4, 6, with 2 above the 1 and 5 below it. In the row, 3 and 4 have one square between them, so they are opposite, and so are 1 and 6. The squares above and below the row, 2 and 5, are opposite.

For an unusual net shape, fold it in your head one square at a time around a fixed base square, or sketch it quickly.

  • ✘ Assuming opposite faces add to 7 on a die that is not standard.
  • ✘ Using the one-common-face rule when the common face is in a different place.
  • ✘ Calling two faces opposite after seeing them together in one position.
  • ✘ In nets, treating diagonally touching squares as opposite.

Q1. On a standard die, 4 is on the top face. Which number is on the bottom face? easy

✅ Answer: (c) 3

💡 Explanation: On a standard die the numbers on opposite faces always add up to 7. The bottom face is therefore 7 − 4 = 3.

Q2. Two positions of a die: (1) A on top, B in front, C on the right. (2) B on top, A in front, F on the right. Which letter is opposite C? medium

✅ Answer: (a) F

💡 Explanation: The two positions share two faces, A and B. The remaining faces, C and F, can never be seen together with both A and B, so C and F are opposite.

Q3. Two positions of a die: (1) A on top, B in front, C on the right. (2) A on top, E in front, D on the right. Which letter is opposite B? medium

✅ Answer: (d) E

💡 Explanation: A is on top in both positions. Read the faces in the same order after A: B then C in the first, E then D in the second. Faces in matching places are opposite, so B is opposite E.

Q4. Three positions of a die: (1) A top, B front, C right. (2) A top, C front, E right. (3) F top, B front, A right. Which letter is opposite A? hard

✅ Answer: (b) D

💡 Explanation: A is seen next to B, C, E and F across the three positions. A face has only four neighbours, so the one letter never seen with A, D, must be opposite it.

Q5. On a standard die, what is the sum of the numbers on the four faces adjacent to the face showing 2? medium

✅ Answer: (c) 14

💡 Explanation: All six faces add up to 21. The faces not adjacent to 2 are 2 itself and its opposite face 5. The four adjacent faces add to 21 − 7 = 14.

Q6. An unfolded die has a row of four squares D, A, E, F, with B above A and C below A. Which letter is opposite A? medium

✅ Answer: (a) F

💡 Explanation: In a straight row of squares, faces with one square between them are opposite. A and F have E between them, so A is opposite F. Likewise D is opposite E, and B is opposite C.

Can I always assume opposite faces add to 7?

Only when the question says the die is a standard die, or the positions shown are consistent with it. Many exam dice use letters, symbols or non-standard numbering, so work from the positions given rather than assuming the sum of 7.

What if the two positions share no common face?

Two positions with no common face show all six faces between them, but they do not by themselves fix which faces are opposite. You will usually need a third position, or the question will give extra information such as a net.
