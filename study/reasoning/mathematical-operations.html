Mathematical Operations Reasoning: Symbol Substitution, Sign Interchange, Defined Operators and Missing Signs

Rewrite first, then BODMAS • Letter and symbol substitution • Interchanging signs • Defined operators • Missing operators • Common traps

SSC Railway 📅 Last updated: 2026-11-02

Every question in this topic has two steps, and most mistakes come from mixing them up:

  1. Rewrite the expression with the real operations, symbol by symbol, on paper.
  2. Evaluate using BODMAS: brackets, then orders (powers and roots), then division and multiplication from left to right, then addition and subtraction from left to right.

Never evaluate while substituting. A coded + that really means × must be done before a real + further along, and you can only see that once the whole line is rewritten. For a refresher on order of operations, see BODMAS simplification.

The question gives a table of meanings, such as "+ means ×". Copy the table once, then rewrite the expression.

Worked example 1: symbols

If + means ×, − means ÷, × means − and ÷ means +, find 8 + 6 − 3 × 4 ÷ 5. Rewritten: 8 × 6 ÷ 3 − 4 + 5. Left to right for × and ÷: 48 ÷ 3 = 16. Then 16 − 4 + 5 = 17.

Worked example 2: letters

If P means +, Q means −, R means × and S means ÷, find 18 S 3 R 4 P 6 Q 2. Rewritten: 18 ÷ 3 × 4 + 6 − 2 = 6 × 4 + 6 − 2 = 28.

Here the question asks which equation becomes correct after two signs (sometimes two numbers too) are swapped. Swap in each option, then test it.

Worked example 3: swap + and −

Which is correct if + and − are interchanged: (a) 10 − 4 + 3 = 11, (b) 8 + 2 − 5 = 1? Option (a) becomes 10 + 4 − 3 = 11, which is true. Option (b) becomes 8 − 2 + 5 = 11, not 1.

Worked example 4: swap × and ÷

If × and ÷ are interchanged, is 9 + 2 ÷ 3 = 15 correct? After the swap: 9 + 2 × 3 = 9 + 6 = 15. Yes, the equation holds.

Worked example 5: swapping numbers and signs together

If the numbers 4 and 8 are interchanged and the signs + and ÷ are interchanged, find 4 + 8 × 2. Make both swaps before calculating: the expression becomes 8 ÷ 4 × 2. Left to right, 8 ÷ 4 = 2 and 2 × 2 = 4. Swapping only the signs would give 4 ÷ 8 × 2 = 1, a wrong answer that often appears among the options. Always write the fully rewritten line.

Evaluate each side fully before comparing, and remember that the equals sign is never swapped. When an option has many terms, evaluate the shorter side first: if it already fails, there is no need to finish the longer side.

Some questions invent an operator with a formula, such as a # b = 2a + 3b or a @ b = a² − b. Substitute exactly as written; the order of a and b matters.

Worked example 6: a @ b = a² − b

5 @ 3 = 25 − 3 = 22. Note that 3 @ 5 = 9 − 5 = 4, a different answer, because the operator is not symmetric.

Worked example 7: nested operators

With the same rule, find (2 @ 1) @ 4. Inner bracket first: 2 @ 1 = 4 − 1 = 3. Then 3 @ 4 = 9 − 4 = 5.

In missing-sign questions, you choose the signs that make an equation true. Start with the operation that changes the numbers most (× or ÷), and check the size of the result before working it out fully.

Worked example 8: 12 ? 4 ? 2 = 14

Try + and ÷: 12 + 4 ÷ 2 = 12 + 2 = 14. That works. Trying × first gives 48 or 24, far too large, so it can be ruled out at a glance.

  • Working left to right without BODMAS. After substitution, × and ÷ still come before + and −.
  • Substituting only some symbols. Rewrite every sign before calculating.
  • Reversing a defined operator. a @ b and b @ a usually differ.
  • Swapping the equals sign. Only the named signs or numbers change.
  • Stopping at the first option that "looks right". Check both sides of the equation.

Q1. If × means +, + means ÷, − means × and ÷ means −, what is 20 + 4 × 3 − 5 ÷ 6? easy

✅ Answer: (a) 14

💡 Explanation: Rewrite as 20 ÷ 4 + 3 × 5 − 6. By BODMAS this is 5 + 15 − 6 = 14.

Q2. If a # b = 2a + 3b, what is 4 # 5? easy

✅ Answer: (b) 23

💡 Explanation: Substitute a = 4 and b = 5: 2 × 4 + 3 × 5 = 8 + 15 = 23.

Q3. If the signs + and × are interchanged, what is the value of 6 × 4 + 2? medium

✅ Answer: (d) 14

💡 Explanation: After the swap the expression is 6 + 4 × 2. Multiplication first: 6 + 8 = 14. 20 comes from working left to right without BODMAS.

Q4. If A $ B means A² + B², what is 3 $ 4? easy

✅ Answer: (b) 25

💡 Explanation: 3 $ 4 = 9 + 16 = 25. 49 is (3 + 4)², a different operation.

Q5. If P means +, Q means −, R means × and S means ÷, what is 36 S 4 R 3 Q 5 P 2? medium

✅ Answer: (a) 24

💡 Explanation: Rewrite as 36 ÷ 4 × 3 − 5 + 2 = 9 × 3 − 5 + 2 = 27 − 5 + 2 = 24.

Do I apply BODMAS before or after replacing the symbols?

After. First rewrite the whole expression with the real signs, then apply BODMAS: division and multiplication from left to right, then addition and subtraction from left to right.

How do I check options in sign-interchange questions quickly?

Swap the signs in each option, then evaluate the side with fewer terms first. As soon as the two sides disagree, move on to the next option.

What if the question also interchanges numbers?

Do the number swaps and the sign swaps together before evaluating. Writing the new expression out in full on paper prevents half-swapped mistakes.
