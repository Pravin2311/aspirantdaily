🌱 India's Net Zero Target 2070

Panchamrit Strategy • Renewable Expansion • Emission Reduction • Climate Justice

High Yield GS-3 + GS-2 📅 Last updated: Oct 2026
  • What is Net Zero? → Achieving balance between greenhouse gas emissions produced and removed from atmosphere.
  • Announcement: PM Narendra Modi announced the 2070 net-zero target and the five "Panchamrit" pledges in his national statement at COP26 Glasgow on 1 November 2021.
  • Panchamrit (Five Nectars): Five actionable commitments released alongside Net Zero target forming comprehensive climate strategy.
  • UPSC Angle: Tests understanding of environment policy, economic growth constraints, international diplomacy, and just transition principles.

📌 Panchamrit Strategy (Five Commitments)

  • #1 Non-Fossil Capacity: Reach 500 GW non-fossil energy capacity by 2030.
  • #2 50% from Renewables: Meet 50% of energy requirements from renewable energy by 2030. (The 2022 updated NDC framed this as about 50% of installed electric power capacity from non-fossil sources by 2030 — reached in June 2025, five years early.)
  • #3 One Billion Tonnes: Reduce total projected carbon emissions by 1 billion tonnes between 2021 and 2030.
  • #4 Emission Intensity Cut: Reduce the emission intensity of GDP by 45% by 2030 compared to 2005 levels.
  • #5 Net Zero: Achieve net zero emissions by 2070.
  • Not a Panchamrit pledge: The additional carbon sink of 2.5–3 billion tonnes CO₂ equivalent through forests and tree cover by 2030 comes from India's 2015 NDC (kept in the 2022 update).
  • Next NDC (2031-35): Cut emission intensity by 47% from 2005 levels by 2035 and reach 60% non-fossil installed capacity.

📌 Sectoral Decomposition

  • Power Sector: Largest contributor to the decarbonisation pathway; India backs a coal "phase-down" (not phase-out) while scaling renewables;
  • Transportation: An aspiration (not a mandate) of 30% electric vehicle sales by 2030 (EV30@30 campaign); fuel-cell and hydrogen trials for heavy vehicles;
  • Industry: Green steel production via hydrogen direct reduction; circular economy practices adopted in cement sector;
  • Agriculture: Biofuels (20% ethanol blending in petrol, E20, reached in 2025), reduced methane emissions from paddy fields (Alternative Wetting & Drying method).

✅ Quick Facts

  • Funding Requirements: India's first NDC (2015) estimated at least US$2.5 trillion needed for climate action between 2015 and 2030; India argues developed countries must provide finance and technology.
  • Carbon Market: The Carbon Credit Trading Scheme was notified in June 2023; binding emission-intensity targets were notified for aluminium, cement, chlor-alkali and pulp & paper (October 2025) and refining, petrochemicals and textiles (January 2026).
  • Per Capita Emissions: About 2 tonnes of CO₂ per person — well under half the world average and a fraction of the US or China — the basis of India's argument for differentiated responsibility.
💡 Prelims Trap: Note difference between "capacity" (installed MW) and "energy generation" (actual electricity produced). Also, "intensity reduction" doesn't mean absolute emission cut immediately but cleaner growth first followed by eventual peak-and-decline pattern.

🎯 Net Zero Strategy: Multi-Dimensional Analysis

🔹 Economic Trade-offs & Opportunities

  • Green Jobs Creation: The transition can create jobs in renewable manufacturing, installation and maintenance, though they may arise in different places from coal job losses;
  • Energy Security: Reduced oil imports enhances macroeconomic stability against volatile crude prices impacting inflation and current account deficit;

🔹 Social Equity Dimensions

  • Coal Worker Resettlement: Coal-dependent districts (e.g., in Jharkhand, Chhattisgarh, Odisha) need alternative livelihoods, coordinated between Centre and states — the "just transition" question;
  • Urban-Rural Divide: Metropolitan areas adopting EVs, rooftop solar faster than rural populations lacking financial literacy and institutional trust barriers slowing down broader participation rates.

🔹 Critical Challenges & Way Forward

  1. Financing Gap: Climate finance mobilised so far is far below what the transition needs; the COP29 finance goal ($300 billion a year by 2035) was criticised by India as inadequate;
  2. Way Forward: Strengthen institutional architecture, accelerate skill development initiatives, enhance transparency reporting systems, lead international negotiations strategically.

📌 Case 1: Rewa Ultra Mega Solar Park (Madhya Pradesh)

  • Context: 750 MW solar park in Rewa district; its 2017 auction (₹2.97 per unit) was the first to make solar cheaper than new coal power, and it supplies the Delhi Metro;
  • Impact: Showed how a state-backed solar park (land, transmission and payment security provided upfront) lowers risk and tariffs;

📌 Case 2: National Green Hydrogen Mission (approved January 2023)

  • Strategy: ₹19,744 crore mission targeting 5 million tonnes a year of green hydrogen production capacity by 2030, mainly through SIGHT incentives for electrolysers and production;
  • Application: Heavy-duty transport, steel manufacturing replacing grey variants currently sourced from natural gas reforming processes emitting significant GHGs;

Q1. With reference to India's Net Zero commitment, consider the following statements:
1. India announced its target of achieving Net Zero by 2070 at COP26 Glasgow.
2. The Panchamrit strategy includes reaching 500 GW non-fossil energy capacity by 2030.
3. Emission intensity reduction target is 45% by 2030 compared to 2010 baseline levels.

Which are correct?

✅ Answer: (a) 1 and 2 only

💡 Explanation: Statement 3 is incorrect: Emission intensity reduction target is 45% by 2030 compared to 2005 baseline levels not 2010.

Q2. The term "Panchamrit" refers to:

✅ Answer: (b) Five nectar-like climate pledges

💡 Explanation: "Panchamrit" literally translates to "five nectars" representing five specific climate action commitments announced by PM Modi at COP26 Glasgow.

Q3. Consider the following pairs (Panchamrit pledges, COP26):
Pledge | Target by 2030
1. Non-fossil energy capacity | 500 GW
2. Emission intensity of GDP | 45% below 2005 levels
3. Projected carbon emissions | Reduce by 1 billion tonnes

How many pairs are correctly matched?

✅ Answer: (c) All three

💡 Explanation: All three pairs are correctly matched. The other two Panchamrit pledges are 50% of energy requirements from renewables by 2030 and net zero by 2070. The 2.5-3 Gt carbon sink is a 2015 NDC target, not a Panchamrit pledge.

🔁 India's Net Zero in 10 Seconds

  • Target Year: 2070 (announced Nov 1, 2021 at COP26 Glasgow)
  • Panchamrit: 500 GW non-fossil capacity, 50% of energy from renewables, 1 billion tonne cut in projected emissions, 45% intensity cut (all by 2030), net zero by 2070
  • Progress: 50% non-fossil installed capacity reached June 2025; next NDC targets 47% intensity cut and 60% non-fossil capacity by 2035
  • Baseline Year: 2005 used for emission intensity calculations ensuring fair burden sharing
  • Funding Need: At least $2.5 trillion for 2015-2030 (2015 NDC estimate)

🧠 Mnemonic: "NET ZERO"

N → Net Zero by 2070 — Ultimate target year announcement made at COP26

E → Emission Intensity Cut — 45% reduction compared to 2005 baseline levels

T → Targets for 2030 — 500 GW, 50% renewables, 1 billion tonnes, 45% intensity

Z → Zero phase-out pledge — India backs a coal "phase-down", not a phase-out

O → Offsets Via Carbon Sinks — Afforestation programs creating carbon absorption capacity

R → Renewables Scale Up — 50% non-fossil capacity reached in June 2025; 500 GW by 2030
