🧱 BRICS Expansion

New Members 2024-25 • Partner Countries • De-dollarization • Global South Leadership

High Yield IR + Economy ⚡ Multipolar World Order 📅 Last updated: Oct 2026
  • What is BRICS? → Association of 5 major emerging economies: Brazil, Russia, India, China, South Africa.
  • Founded: 2006 (as BRIC); South Africa joined 2010 → BRICS.
  • Expansion: Egypt, Ethiopia, Iran and UAE joined on 1 January 2024; Indonesia joined as a full member in January 2025. Saudi Arabia was invited in 2023, but its status is still ambiguous.
  • Key Institutions: New Development Bank (NDB) and Contingent Reserve Arrangement (CRA). There is no common BRICS currency or operational common payment system.
  • Why important for UPSC? → Tests understanding of multipolar world order, de-dollarization, Global South representation, India's strategic autonomy, economic diplomacy.

📌 BRICS Structure & Evolution

  • Original 5 (2010): Brazil, Russia, India, China, South Africa
  • New Members: Egypt, Ethiopia, Iran, UAE (1 January 2024) and Indonesia (January 2025). Saudi Arabia was invited in 2023; India lists it as a member, but Saudi Arabia itself still attends as an invitee.
  • Partner countries (10): Belarus, Bolivia, Cuba, Kazakhstan, Malaysia, Nigeria, Thailand, Uganda, Uzbekistan and Vietnam. The partner category was created at the Kazan summit (2024).
  • Combined weight: Nearly half the world's population and roughly two-fifths of global GDP (PPP)
  • Decision-Making: Consensus-based; annual summits; no permanent secretariat

📌 Key Institutions

  • New Development Bank (NDB): Agreement signed 2014 (Fortaleza), HQ Shanghai; $100B authorised capital; funds infrastructure and sustainable development projects. Membership has widened beyond BRICS (e.g., Bangladesh, UAE, Egypt)
  • Contingent Reserve Arrangement (CRA): $100B pool for balance of payments support; India's contribution: $18B
  • Payments: A BRICS Payment Task Force works on making national payment and financial-messaging systems interoperable (backed again in the 2026 New Delhi Declaration); there is no operational common "BRICS Pay" system
  • Local Currency Settlement: Promoting trade in national currencies (e.g., INR-RUB, CNY-BRL) to bypass USD

📌 Strategic Objectives of Expansion

  • Geographic Diversification: From Eurasia-focused to pan-Global South representation (Africa, Middle East, Latin America)
  • Economic Weight: Enhance collective bargaining in WTO, IMF, World Bank reforms
  • Energy Security: UAE, Iran, Russia = major oil/gas producers; Egypt, Ethiopia = strategic corridors
  • De-dollarization: Reduce vulnerability to US sanctions, monetary policy spillovers
  • Counterbalance: Provide alternative to Western-dominated institutions (G7, IMF conditionality)

📌 India's Stance on BRICS Expansion

  • Supportive but Cautious: Backs expansion in principle; emphasizes consensus, institutional capacity, strategic coherence
  • Strategic Autonomy: Uses BRICS for economic diplomacy while maintaining Quad, G20, bilateral ties with West
  • Concerns: China's dominance in NDB; Iran's inclusion complicates West Asia diplomacy; cohesion challenges with diverse members
  • Opportunities: Market access for Indian goods/services; NDB funding for infrastructure; voice for Global South

✅ Quick Facts

  • Term Coined: Jim O'Neill (Goldman Sachs, 2001) for investment thesis
  • First Summit: Yekaterinburg, Russia (2009)
  • 15th Summit: Johannesburg, South Africa (Aug 2023) — invited six countries to join
  • 16th Summit: Kazan, Russia (Oct 2024) — first with new members; created the partner-country category
  • 17th Summit: Rio de Janeiro, Brazil (6–7 July 2025) — welcomed Indonesia and 10 partner countries
  • 18th Summit: New Delhi, India (12–13 September 2026), Bharat Mandapam — adopted the New Delhi Declaration; China chairs in 2027
  • NDB President: Dilma Rousseff (Brazil, former President) since 2023; re-elected in 2025

✅ Key Numbers

  • Members: 10 (Brazil, Russia, India, China, South Africa, Egypt, Ethiopia, Iran, UAE, Indonesia), plus Saudi Arabia with ambiguous status
  • Partner countries: 10 (Belarus, Bolivia, Cuba, Kazakhstan, Malaysia, Nigeria, Thailand, Uganda, Uzbekistan and Vietnam)
  • NDB voting: founders subscribed equal shares (20% each at founding); no member has a veto, and founders' combined voting power cannot fall below 55%
  • CRA Size: $100 billion; India's commitment: $18 billion
💡 Prelims Trap: BRICS has no treaty/charter — it's an informal grouping; decisions are non-binding; expansion requires unanimous consent of all members.

🎯 BRICS Expansion: Multi-Dimensional Analysis

🔹 Geopolitical Significance: Towards Multipolarity

  • Counter-Narrative: BRICS+ challenges Western-dominated global governance (G7, Bretton Woods); advocates for reformed UN, IMF quota changes.
  • Strategic Autonomy: Enables members to pursue independent foreign policies; India balances BRICS with Quad, US ties.
  • Regional Dynamics: Iran-UAE inclusion brings Gulf rivals together; Egypt-Ethiopia adds Nile Basin voices; potential for internal friction.

🔹 Economic Dimensions: De-dollarization & Development Finance

  • Local Currency Trade: India-Russia rupee-ruble mechanism; China-Brazil yuan-real swaps; reduces USD transaction costs, sanction risks.
  • NDB's Role: Alternative to World Bank/IMF; focuses on infrastructure, sustainability; but faces challenges: capital adequacy, project pipeline, governance.
  • Limitations: BRICS currencies lack convertibility and depth; the US dollar is on one side of nearly 9 in 10 forex trades (BIS survey); no common BRICS payment system or currency exists.

🔹 Challenges to Cohesion & Effectiveness

  • India-China Rivalry: Border tensions, trade imbalance (India's deficit with China was $99.2B in 2024-25), strategic competition in Indian Ocean limit deeper integration.
  • Diverse Interests: Democratic (India, Brazil) vs authoritarian (China, Russia, Iran); oil exporters vs importers; varying China-dependence.
  • Institutional Capacity: No permanent secretariat, limited staff, consensus rule → slow decision-making; NDB lending is small next to the World Bank's.
  • External Pressures: US sanctions on Russia/Iran complicate financial cooperation; Western criticism of "anti-Western" narrative.

🔹 India's Strategic Calculus & Way Forward

  • Opportunities: NDB funding for green infrastructure; market access for IT/pharma; platform for Global South advocacy (climate justice, SDGs).
  • Risks: Over-dependence on China-led initiatives; dilution of strategic autonomy; reputational risks from Iran/Russia association.

🔹 Mains Answer Framework

  1. Contextualize: BRICS as instrument of multipolarity; India's multi-alignment strategy.
  2. Analyze Expansion: Geographic/economic rationale; institutional implications (NDB, CRA); de-dollarization prospects.
  3. Critically Evaluate: Cohesion challenges (India-China, diverse regimes); implementation gaps; external constraints.
  4. Way Forward: Strengthen NDB governance; promote local currency mechanisms pragmatically; leverage BRICS for Global South agenda while safeguarding strategic autonomy.

📌 Case 1: India-Russia Trade in Local Currencies

  • Context: Post-2022 sanctions disrupted USD/EUR trade; India needed Russian oil, Russia needed Indian goods.
  • Mechanism: RBI allowed trade settlement in rupees (July 2022) through Special Rupee Vostro Accounts that Russian banks hold with Indian banks.
  • Outcome: India-Russia trade rose from about $13B (2021-22) to about $66B (2023-24), mostly Russian oil; Russia built up rupee balances it found hard to use.
  • UPSC Link: De-dollarization in practice + Strategic autonomy + Balance of payments management.

📌 Case 2: NDB Lending to India

  • Context: India's target of 500 GW non-fossil power capacity by 2030 and its infrastructure push need large, long-term finance.
  • NDB Support: As a founding member, India borrows from the NDB for projects such as metro rail, roads, water supply and renewable energy.
  • Advantage: Faster appraisal than World Bank; focus on country systems; local currency lending options.
  • UPSC Link: Climate finance + Multilateral development banks + Energy transition + India's net-zero pathway.

📌 Case 3: The Global South Voice at COP28

  • Context: Climate negotiations often reflect North-South divide on finance, responsibility.
  • Coordination: The BASIC group (Brazil, South Africa, India, China), all BRICS members, coordinates positions on climate finance, technology transfer and equity.
  • Outcome at COP28 (Dubai, 2023): The loss and damage fund was operationalised on day one; the UAE Consensus called for "transitioning away from fossil fuels in energy systems".
  • UPSC Link: Climate diplomacy + Global South solidarity + Multilateral negotiation tactics + SDG 13.

Q1. With reference to BRICS, consider the following statements:
1. The term 'BRIC' was coined by Goldman Sachs economist Jim O'Neill in 2001.
2. The New Development Bank (NDB) is headquartered in New Delhi.
3. BRICS expansion in 2024 added Egypt, Ethiopia, Iran, and UAE as members.

Which of the statements given above are correct?

✅ Answer: (b) 1 and 3 only

💡 Explanation: Jim O'Neill coined 'BRIC' in 2001 (✓). NDB HQ is in Shanghai, not New Delhi (✗). Egypt, Ethiopia, Iran, UAE joined in 2024 (✓).

Q2. The Contingent Reserve Arrangement (CRA) of BRICS is primarily designed to:

✅ Answer: (b) Provide balance of payments support during crises

💡 Explanation: CRA is a $100B pool for short-term liquidity support during balance of payments pressures, similar to IMF's role but regional.

Q3. Which of the following countries was NOT among the new BRICS members that joined in January 2024?

✅ Answer: (b) Indonesia

💡 Explanation: Indonesia joined as a full member in January 2025, a year later. Egypt, Ethiopia, Iran and UAE joined on 1 January 2024.

Q4. Consider the following pairs:
BRICS Institution | Primary Function
1. New Development Bank | Infrastructure and sustainable development financing
2. Contingent Reserve Arrangement | Balance of payments crisis support
3. BRICS Pay | Operational cross-border payment system

How many pairs are correctly matched?

✅ Answer: (b) Only two

💡 Explanation: NDB (✓) and CRA (✓) are correctly matched. There is no operational common BRICS payment system; a Payment Task Force works on linking national systems (✗).

Q5. At the founding of the New Development Bank (NDB), India's share of voting power was:

✅ Answer: (b) 20%

💡 Explanation: The 5 founding members subscribed equal shares of the NDB's initial capital, so each had 20% of the votes at founding. No member has a veto, and as new members join, the founders' combined voting power cannot fall below 55%.
