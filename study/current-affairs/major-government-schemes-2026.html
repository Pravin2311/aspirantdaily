📋 Major Government Schemes Approved in 2026

GOBARdhan • PM Surya Sarovar Yojana • Samudra Manthan — Cabinet Approvals Explained

High Yield Polity + Economy + Environment ⚡ Schemes in the News 📅 Last updated: Oct 2026
  • What is this topic? → A cluster of major Union Cabinet-approved schemes in mid-2026 spanning bioenergy, solar power, and offshore hydrocarbon exploration — classic "schemes in the news" Prelims material.
  • GOBARdhan: National Circular Bioenergy Scheme for compressed biogas (CBG), outlay ₹23,731 crore, approved 6 August 2026.
  • PM Surya Sarovar Yojana (PM-SSY): Floating solar photovoltaic scheme, outlay ₹5,070 crore, approved 31 July 2026.
  • Samudra Manthan: National Offshore Exploration Scheme, outlay ₹84,084 crore, approved 31 July 2026, for ultra-deepwater drilling and energy security.
  • Why important for UPSC? → "Schemes in the news" are a regular source of Prelims questions; these three also link directly to GS-3 energy security and environment themes.

📌 GOBARdhan — National Circular Bioenergy Scheme

  • Full form: Galvanising Organic Bio-Agro Resources Dhan.
  • Outlay & Approval: ₹23,731 crore, approved by the Union Cabinet on 6 August 2026.
  • Implementation Period: FY 2026-27 to FY 2035-36.
  • Objective: Convert agricultural residue, cattle dung, municipal organic waste, and press mud into Compressed Biogas (CBG) and organic manure, targeting nearly ten-fold growth in domestic CBG production.
  • Administering Ministry: Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas. It brings earlier CBG support (SATAT, MDA, BAM, DPI and CFA under the National Bioenergy Programme, which together saw over 200 CBG plants commissioned) onto one platform.
  • Six components: assured offtake by City Gas Distribution entities against the CBG Obligation (3% in FY 2026-27, 4% in FY 2027-28, 5% from FY 2028-29 in CNG-transport and PNG-domestic); an administered price of ₹2,110 per MMBTU with a minimum ten-year horizon; capital assistance of up to ₹2 crore per tonne per day of CBG capacity; pipeline connectivity; credit guarantees for MSME projects; and a CBG Ecosystem Challenge Fund for district-level work.
  • Significance: CBG is chemically equivalent to natural gas, so it can feed the existing gas network and replace imported fossil gas.

📌 Pradhan Mantri Surya Sarovar Yojana (PM-SSY)

  • Outlay & Approval: ₹5,070 crore, approved 31 July 2026.
  • Objective: Develop 5,000 MW of floating solar photovoltaic capacity along with 10,000 MWh of co-located energy storage (minimum 2-hour storage duration). Projects are sanctioned from FY 2026-27 to FY 2030-31, with disbursement up to FY 2032-33.
  • Incentive Structure: Central Financial Assistance (CFA) of ₹1 crore per MW after commissioning, plus up to ₹50 lakh per project for feasibility studies (bathymetry, hydrography, environmental studies).
  • Baseline: India's floating solar capacity is about 700 MW today; the National Institute of Solar Energy estimates a potential of about 102.18 GWp on reservoirs and inland water bodies.
  • Rationale: Floating solar on reservoirs/water bodies saves scarce land, reduces water evaporation, and pairs naturally with hydro/irrigation infrastructure.

📌 Samudra Manthan — National Offshore Exploration Scheme

  • Outlay & Approval: ₹84,084 crore, approved 31 July 2026 for implementation up to FY 2030-31. It is a Central Sector Scheme of the Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas, following the Prime Minister's Independence Day 2025 call for a "modern-day Samudra Manthan".
  • Objective: Large-scale seismic data acquisition, accelerated deepwater and ultra-deepwater exploratory drilling, scientific drilling in frontier basins and common offshore production and evacuation infrastructure, expected to catalyse reserve accretion of over 600 MMTOE (million metric tonnes of oil equivalent).
  • Additional Component: Establishes an integrated Oil & Gas Manufacturing and Services Zone to build domestic capacity in exploration equipment and services.
  • Strategic Rationale: India imports most of its crude oil; raising domestic offshore output improves energy security.

✅ Key Numbers

  • Combined outlay of all three schemes: ₹1,12,885 crore (about ₹1.13 lakh crore)
  • GOBARdhan targets ~10x growth in domestic CBG production
  • PM-SSY: 5,000 MW floating solar + 10,000 MWh storage, ₹1 crore/MW CFA
  • Samudra Manthan: ₹84,084 crore, targeting 600 MMTOE, valid through FY31
💡 Prelims Trap: All three schemes were approved within days of each other in late July–early August 2026 — PM-SSY and Samudra Manthan on 31 July, GOBARdhan on 6 August. Exams often test which scheme belongs to which ministry/sector — don't mix up the outlay figures across the three.

🎯 India's 2026 Energy-Security Scheme Cluster: Analysis

🔹 A Coordinated Energy Security Push

  • Three Pillars, One Goal: GOBARdhan (bioenergy), PM-SSY (solar), and Samudra Manthan (hydrocarbons) together address renewable, circular, and conventional energy sources — a diversified energy-security strategy rather than a single-technology bet.
  • Import Substitution: Samudra Manthan directly targets crude oil import dependence, while GOBARdhan targets substituting imported natural gas/LPG with domestic CBG.

🔹 Circular Economy and Rural Livelihoods (GOBARdhan)

  • Waste-to-Wealth Model: Converts agricultural residue and cattle dung — previously often burned (contributing to air pollution) or wasted — into fuel and organic manure.
  • Rural Income: Creates a market for biomass/dung collection, potentially supplementing farmer and rural household incomes alongside environmental benefits.
  • Link to Stubble Burning: A functioning CBG value chain gives farmers an economic incentive NOT to burn crop residue, directly relevant to North India's annual air-pollution crisis.

🔹 Land-Efficient Renewable Energy (PM-SSY)

  • Land Scarcity Solution: Floating solar avoids the land-acquisition conflicts that ground-mounted solar parks often face, using reservoirs and water bodies instead.
  • Co-benefits: Reduces water evaporation from reservoirs and can pair with existing hydropower infrastructure for grid balancing.

🔹 Way Forward

  • Implementation Risk: Large outlays (₹84,084 crore for Samudra Manthan alone) require sustained multi-year execution, and offshore exploration is capital-intensive with long gestation and uncertain discovery rates.
  • Way Forward: Ensure timely CFA disbursement under PM-SSY, build CBG off-take infrastructure (city gas distribution linkages) for GOBARdhan, and fast-track exploration blocks under Samudra Manthan.

🔹 Mains Answer Framework

  1. Contextualize: India's energy import dependence and the push for diversified domestic energy sources.
  2. Analyze Each Scheme: GOBARdhan (bioenergy/circular economy), PM-SSY (land-efficient solar), Samudra Manthan (hydrocarbon security).
  3. Critically Evaluate: Implementation and execution risk given the scale of outlays; need for coordinated infrastructure (CBG off-take, grid integration).
  4. Way Forward: Timely disbursement, infrastructure build-out, and monitoring mechanisms for each scheme.

📌 Case 1: GOBARdhan and the Stubble-Burning Problem

  • Context: Crop-residue burning in North India causes severe seasonal air pollution, especially around Delhi-NCR.
  • Link: GOBARdhan's CBG value chain gives farmers a market for residue instead of burning it — a market-based (not purely regulatory) solution to a persistent environmental problem.
  • UPSC Link: GS-3 environment + circular economy + Delhi air pollution current affairs (cross-reference our Environment Cheat Code).

📌 Case 2: PM-SSY and India's Land-Constrained Solar Expansion

  • Context: Ground-mounted solar parks often face land-acquisition delays and conflicts with agricultural use.
  • Link: Floating solar on reservoirs sidesteps land conflicts entirely while adding water-conservation co-benefits.
  • UPSC Link: GS-3 renewable energy + land-use policy + water resource management.

📌 Case 3: Samudra Manthan and Crude Oil Import Dependence

  • Context: India imports the large majority of its crude oil requirement, a major current-account and strategic vulnerability.
  • Link: Ultra-deepwater exploration under Samudra Manthan targets previously inaccessible offshore reserves to gradually reduce this dependence.
  • UPSC Link: GS-3 energy security + current account deficit + strategic resource policy.
