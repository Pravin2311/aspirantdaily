⚡ Electric Vehicle Policy India

FAME II • Battery Swapping Standards • Charging Infrastructure • PLI Schemes • 2030 EV Targets

High Yield GS-3 + GS-1 + Essay ⚡ Environment + Industry 📅 Last updated: Oct 2026
  • What is India's EV policy? → Not one law, but a set of schemes run mainly by the Ministry of Heavy Industries (MHI): demand incentives (FAME, then PM E-DRIVE), manufacturing incentives (PLI-Auto and the ACC battery PLI), and an import-for-investment scheme for electric cars (SPMEPCI).
  • FAME-II (2019–2024): ₹11,500 crore (raised from ₹10,000 crore in February 2024); supported 16,71,606 EVs.
  • PM E-DRIVE (from 2024): ₹10,900 crore; notified 29 September 2024, effective 1 April 2024; extended to 31 March 2028.
  • Result so far: EV share of vehicle sales rose from 0.7% in FY 2019-20 to 8.2% in FY 2025-26 (MHI, July 2026).
  • UPSC Angle: Energy security, urban air quality, industrial policy, critical minerals and fiscal design of subsidies.

📌 FAME India Scheme

  • FAME = Faster Adoption and Manufacturing of (Hybrid &) Electric Vehicles in India.
  • Phase I: 2015 to March 2019.
  • Phase II: 1 April 2019 to 31 March 2024; outlay ₹11,500 crore; 16,71,606 EVs supported; also funded e-buses and charging stations.
  • EMPS 2024: The Electric Mobility Promotion Scheme ran from 1 April to 30 September 2024 as a bridge, and was then subsumed into PM E-DRIVE.

📌 PM E-DRIVE (PM Electric Drive Revolution in Innovative Vehicle Enhancement)

  • Outlay: ₹10,900 crore; tenure extended within the same outlay from 31 March 2026 to 31 March 2028.
  • Demand incentives (from FY 2025-26): e-2W ₹2,500/kWh, capped at ₹5,000 per vehicle (e-2W support ended 31 July 2026); e-rickshaws and e-carts ₹2,500/kWh, capped at ₹12,500; e-2W and e-3W incentives also capped at 15% of ex-factory price.
  • New categories: e-trucks (₹5,000/kWh, capped at 10% of ex-factory price and by weight class) and e-ambulances (₹30,000/kWh, capped at 35%).
  • e-Buses: 14,000 e-buses allocated to cities and state transport undertakings, with incentives capped at ₹20–35 lakh per bus depending on length.
  • Charging: ₹2,000 crore for public charging stations; by 1 July 2026, ₹689 crore had been approved for 6,562 chargers through 3 oil marketing companies and 9 states.
  • Progress: 23,22,878 EVs incentivised (₹2,281.94 crore paid to manufacturers) as of 22 July 2026.

📌 Manufacturing Schemes

  • PLI for Automobile and Auto Components (₹25,938 crore): Approved 23 September 2021 for advanced automotive technology products, including EVs, with at least 50% domestic value addition. By 31 March 2026: ₹44,326 crore invested, 67,820 jobs, ₹2,386.36 crore of incentives paid.
  • PLI for Advanced Chemistry Cell (ACC) batteries (₹18,100 crore): Launched 12 May 2021 for 50 GWh of cell manufacturing capacity; 40 GWh awarded to four firms. As of July 2026 no firm had yet claimed an incentive, and most cells are still imported.
  • SPMEPCI (electric passenger cars): Notified 15 March 2024. Approved carmakers that commit at least ₹4,150 crore of investment may import a limited number of EVs at a reduced 15% customs duty, with duty forgone capped at ₹6,484 crore or the investment made, whichever is lower.
  • Phased Manufacturing Programme (PMP): EV makers must localise components to qualify for PM E-DRIVE incentives.

📌 Other Rules

  • Battery Waste Management Rules, 2022 (MoEFCC, 22 August 2022): Extended Producer Responsibility for collecting and recycling batteries, including EV batteries.
  • Battery swapping: NITI Aayog released a draft battery swapping policy in 2022; it has not been notified as a national policy.

✅ Quick Facts

  • EV penetration: 0.7% (FY 2019-20) → 8.2% (FY 2025-26).
  • PM E-DRIVE e-buses: 14,000 allocated; buses are procured through CESL tenders.
  • e-3W (L5) incentive: ended 26 December 2025; e-2W: ended 31 July 2026.
  • PLI-Auto: ₹25,938 crore; needs at least 50% domestic value addition.
  • Battery recycling: Battery Waste Management Rules, 2022 (EPR-based).
💡 Prelims Trap: FAME-II ended on 31 March 2024; the current demand scheme is PM E-DRIVE. PM E-DRIVE incentives go to manufacturers (OEMs), who pass them to buyers as a lower price. There is no notified national battery swapping policy, only NITI Aayog's 2022 draft.

🎯 EV Policy: Multi-Dimensional Analysis

🔹 Why EVs

  • Energy security: India imports most of its crude oil, and road transport is a large consumer of petrol and diesel.
  • Air quality: EVs have no tailpipe emissions, which matters in polluted cities.
  • Climate: Lifecycle benefits grow as the power grid adds more renewable energy, which links EVs to the 500 GW non-fossil target.

🔹 Policy Design: How Subsidies Have Changed

  • Tapering: Per-kWh incentives for e-2W and e-3W were halved from FY 2025-26, and the e-2W and e-3W (L5) segments have closed. Support now shifts to e-buses, e-trucks, e-ambulances and charging.
  • Localisation: Incentives are tied to the Phased Manufacturing Programme, so imported kits do not qualify.
  • Supply side: PLI-Auto has drawn investment, but the ACC battery PLI is behind schedule, so cells remain largely imported.

🔹 Critical Challenges & Way Forward

  • Battery cells: Firms cite limited technology access, skill gaps, imported equipment and missing upstream components. Cells are the costliest part of an EV.
  • Critical minerals: Lithium, nickel and cobalt supply chains are concentrated abroad; see the lithium notes.
  • Charging: 6,562 chargers had been approved under PM E-DRIVE by July 2026; highway and smaller-town coverage is still thin.
  • Grid and DISCOMs: Charging demand needs time-of-day tariffs and distribution upgrades.
  • Way forward: Deliver the ACC capacity, scale battery recycling, roll out charging along highways and in Tier-II and Tier-III cities, and electrify buses and trucks.

🔹 Mains Answer Framework

  1. Contextualize: Oil imports, air pollution, net zero by 2070.
  2. Explain the toolkit: Demand (FAME → PM E-DRIVE), supply (PLI-Auto, ACC PLI), investment (SPMEPCI), recycling (Battery Waste Management Rules, 2022).
  3. Give evidence: EV share 0.7% → 8.2%; 23 lakh EVs incentivised under PM E-DRIVE.
  4. Critically evaluate: Cell imports, charging gaps, subsidy dependence.

📌 Case 1: E-Buses for Cities under PM E-DRIVE

  • Plan: 14,000 e-buses allocated; quantities confirmed with bidders for 13,800.
  • Example: Surat and Hyderabad signed concession agreements for 600 and 915 e-buses respectively.
  • Model: Buses are run by operators who are paid per kilometre, so cities avoid the large upfront purchase cost.
  • UPSC Link: Urban transport + Air quality + Public finance + Cooperative federalism.

📌 Case 2: The Battery Gap – ACC PLI

  • Target: 50 GWh of domestic cell capacity; 40 GWh awarded to four firms.
  • Reality (July 2026): No incentive claimed yet; ₹5,180 crore invested and 1,277 direct jobs reported by the firms.
  • Lesson: Assembly can be localised quickly, but cell chemistry, equipment and upstream materials take far longer.
  • UPSC Link: Industrial policy + Critical minerals + Strategic autonomy.

Q1. With reference to India's electric vehicle schemes, consider the following statements:
1. FAME-II ended on 31 March 2024 with an outlay of ₹11,500 crore.
2. PM E-DRIVE has been extended up to 31 March 2028.
3. A national battery swapping policy was notified by the Ministry of Heavy Industries in 2022.

Which of the statements given above are correct?

✅ Answer: (a) 1 and 2 only

💡 Explanation: Statement 3 is incorrect: NITI Aayog released only a draft battery swapping policy in 2022; no national policy has been notified.

Q2. FAME in the FAME India Scheme stands for:

✅ Answer: (b) Faster Adoption and Manufacturing of (Hybrid &) Electric Vehicles

💡 Explanation: FAME-I ran from 2015 and FAME-II from 2019 to 2024; PM E-DRIVE succeeded them.

Q3. Consider the following pairs:
Scheme | Outlay
1. PM E-DRIVE | ₹10,900 crore
2. PLI for Automobile and Auto Components | ₹25,938 crore
3. PLI for ACC Battery Storage | ₹18,100 crore

How many pairs are correctly matched?

✅ Answer: (c) All three

💡 Explanation: All three outlays match Ministry of Heavy Industries figures.

Q4. Which of the following vehicle categories was newly brought under central EV demand incentives by PM E-DRIVE?

✅ Answer: (b) Electric trucks and electric ambulances

💡 Explanation: e-2W, e-3W and e-buses were also supported under FAME-II; PM E-DRIVE added incentives for e-trucks and e-ambulances.

Q5. According to the Ministry of Heavy Industries (July 2026), the share of EVs in vehicle sales in FY 2025-26 was about:

✅ Answer: (c) 8.2%

💡 Explanation: EV penetration rose from 0.7% in FY 2019-20 to 8.2% in FY 2025-26, helped by FAME-II, PM E-DRIVE and other measures.

🔁 EV Policy in 10 Seconds

  • FAME-II: 2019–24; ₹11,500 Cr; 16.7 lakh EVs supported
  • EMPS 2024: Apr–Sep 2024 bridge; merged into PM E-DRIVE
  • PM E-DRIVE: ₹10,900 Cr; to 31 Mar 2028; e-2W, e-3W, e-trucks, e-ambulances, 14,000 e-buses, ₹2,000 Cr for charging
  • PLI-Auto: ₹25,938 Cr; ACC PLI: ₹18,100 Cr for 50 GWh
  • SPMEPCI: 15% import duty for carmakers investing ≥ ₹4,150 Cr
  • EV share: 0.7% (FY20) → 8.2% (FY26)
  • Weak link: battery cells still largely imported

🧠 Mnemonic: "D-S-I-R" toolkit

D → Demand: FAME → EMPS → PM E-DRIVE

S → Supply: PLI-Auto + ACC battery PLI

I → Investment: SPMEPCI for electric cars

R → Recycling: Battery Waste Management Rules, 2022

📌 Prelims Traps to Avoid

  • ✘ FAME-II is over (31 March 2024); PM E-DRIVE is the current scheme
  • ✘ FAME-II's final outlay was ₹11,500 crore, not ₹10,000 crore
  • ✘ No notified national battery swapping policy, only NITI Aayog's 2022 draft
  • ✘ Nodal ministry is Heavy Industries, not Road Transport
  • ✘ Battery Waste Management Rules are under MoEFCC

🎯 Mains One-Liners

  • "India's EV policy has moved from subsidising buyers to building buses, trucks, chargers and cells"
  • "Without domestic cells, EV manufacturing is assembly; the ACC PLI is the real test"
  • "An EV is only as clean as the grid that charges it"
  • "Way Forward: deliver cell capacity, scale recycling, and take charging to highways and smaller cities"
