🚄 PM Gati Shakti

National Master Plan • Multimodal Connectivity • GIS-Based Planning • Infrastructure Transformation

High Yield GS-3 + Economy ⚡ Infrastructure + Federalism 📅 Last updated: Oct 2026
  • What is PM GatiShakti? → The National Master Plan (NMP) for multimodal connectivity, launched on 13 October 2021. It is a GIS-based platform where ministries and states plan infrastructure together, instead of each department working alone.
  • Origin: Announced in the Prime Minister's Independence Day speech of 15 August 2021, which described it as a plan of more than ₹100 lakh crore.
  • Scope then and now: 16 ministries at launch; by October 2026 it covered 58 Central Ministries and Departments and all 36 States and UTs.
  • Technology: Spatial planning tools using ISRO imagery, developed by BISAG-N (Bhaskaracharya National Institute for Space Applications and Geo-informatics).
  • Link with logistics: The National Logistics Policy 2022 covers the process and services side of logistics; GatiShakti covers integrated planning of the physical networks.
  • UPSC Angle: Infrastructure planning, cooperative federalism, digital governance and logistics costs.

📌 Seven Engines of GatiShakti

  • Roads: National highways, including Bharatmala Pariyojana.
  • Railways: Freight corridors, new lines and rail cargo terminals.
  • Ports: Port capacity and port connectivity, including Sagarmala.
  • Waterways: National Waterways and inland water transport.
  • Airports: Airports and air cargo, including regional connectivity (UDAN).
  • Mass Transport: Metro and other urban transit.
  • Logistics Infrastructure: Multimodal logistics parks, warehouses and terminals.

📌 Three-Tier Institutional Framework

  • Empowered Group of Secretaries (EGoS): Chaired by the Cabinet Secretary. It oversees implementation and reviews, considers and approves changes in the Master Plan.
  • Network Planning Group (NPG): Heads of the network planning divisions of the infrastructure ministries and departments. It examines infrastructure proposals against GatiShakti principles.
  • Technical Support Unit (TSU): Domain and subject-matter experts who support the evaluation. It assists the Logistics Division (DPIIT, Ministry of Commerce and Industry), which acts as the PM GatiShakti Secretariat.
  • States: States set up their own EGoS, NPG and TSU on the same pattern.

📌 The Digital Platform

  • Data layers: Over 23,493 data layers as of 12 October 2026, covering infrastructure, land, forests and other assets.
  • Public access: The PM GatiShakti Public platform gives access to 230 approved datasets, for site suitability, connectivity mapping, alignment planning and compliance checks.
  • Private sector: Opened to private users in October 2025 through a Query-Based Analytics Mechanism developed by BISAG-N.

📌 What It Has Done So Far (PIB, October 2026)

  • As of 11 August 2026, the NPG had evaluated 396 infrastructure projects worth about ₹18.66 lakh crore.
  • Of these, 256 had been sanctioned and 198 were under implementation.
  • Gati Shakti Vishwavidyalaya (set up in 2022) had trained more than 2.4 lakh officers in GatiShakti courses by July 2026.

✅ Quick Facts

  • Platform developer: BISAG-N, using ISRO imagery.
  • Coverage: 58 Central Ministries and Departments and all 36 States and UTs (October 2026).
  • Data layers: over 23,493 (12 October 2026); the public platform has 230 approved datasets.
  • ULIP: The Unified Logistics Interface Platform linked 46 government systems across 12 Central Ministries through 142 APIs (July 2026).

✅ Related Initiatives

  • National Infrastructure Pipeline (NIP): The investment pipeline for 2019-20 to 2024-25; GatiShakti is a planning tool, not a funding source.
  • National Logistics Policy 2022: Its Services Improvement Group works under the GatiShakti EGoS.
  • PM GatiShakti Cargo Terminals: The Railways' policy for building more rail cargo terminals, including with private investment.
  • Gati Shakti Vishwavidyalaya: A university for transport and logistics education, training and research.
💡 Prelims Trap: PM GatiShakti is a planning platform and coordination framework, not a scheme with its own budget. Projects are still funded by the ministries' own schemes, such as Bharatmala and Sagarmala. The Cabinet Secretary chairs the EGoS, not the NPG.

🎯 PM GatiShakti: Multi-Dimensional Analysis

🔹 Governance Innovation: Breaking Silos

  • The problem: Ministries planned roads, railways, pipelines and power lines separately. Projects clashed, were dug up again for other utilities, or waited years for clearances. The 2021 Red Fort speech said Gati Shakti "will break the silos".
  • The fix: Every ministry's assets sit on one GIS map, so a new project can be aligned around forests, settlements and existing networks before work starts.
  • Institutional check: The NPG examines proposals against GatiShakti principles, which brings a whole-of-government view into project appraisal.

🔹 Economic Significance

  • Logistics costs: Better multimodal links lower transport time and cost, which matters for exports and manufacturing.
  • Economic nodes: Plans connect industrial clusters, food parks and fishing harbours to highways, railways and ports.
  • Private planning: Opening the platform to private users in 2025 lets firms use the same data for site selection.

🔹 Federal Dimension

  • All 36 States and UTs are on the platform, and states have built their own EGoS, NPG and TSU structures.
  • Why this matters: Land, many roads and local utilities are state subjects, so central corridors only work if state plans align with them.

🔹 Critical Challenges & Way Forward

  • Data quality: The platform is only as good as its layers; states and ministries must keep data current and in common formats.
  • Capacity: District and state officials need GIS and planning skills, which is why Gati Shakti Vishwavidyalaya's training matters.
  • Land and clearances: Better alignment reduces, but does not remove, land acquisition disputes and forest and wildlife clearances.
  • Outcomes: Evaluation of projects is not completion. Of 396 projects evaluated by August 2026, 198 were under implementation.

🔹 Mains Answer Framework

  1. Contextualize: Fragmented infrastructure planning and high logistics costs.
  2. Explain the mechanism: GIS platform, seven engines, the EGoS–NPG–TSU structure.
  3. Give evidence: Ministries and states onboarded, projects evaluated, an example such as the Pune–Bengaluru Expressway.
  4. Critically evaluate: Data quality, capacity, land issues and the gap between planning and delivery.

📌 Case 1: Pune–Bengaluru Expressway

  • Project: A 699 km expressway across Maharashtra and Karnataka, expected to be completed in 2027-28.
  • GatiShakti use: The alignment was revised on the NMP platform to cut forest land overlap by 19%.
  • Connectivity: It links 15 National Highways, 25 State Highways and 6 railway stations.
  • Expected benefits: 7–8 hours less travel time; about 16 crore litres of fuel saved each year.
  • UPSC Link: Environmental governance + Multimodal planning + Regional development.

📌 Case 2: Dhubri–Phulbari Bridge over the Brahmaputra

  • Project: A four-lane bridge, under construction, between Dhubri (Assam) and Phulbari (Meghalaya).
  • Impact: Planned to cut the travel distance from 205 km to 20 km.
  • UPSC Link: North-East connectivity + Act East + Riverine geography.

📌 Case 3: Green Energy Corridor, Ladakh–Haryana

  • Project: Recommended by the NPG; evacuates 13 GW of renewable energy from Ladakh.
  • Scope: A 480 km HVDC line from Pang (Ladakh) to Kaithal (Haryana), with 5 GW HVDC terminals at both ends; target FY 2029-30.
  • Lesson: GatiShakti covers energy networks as well as transport. See the Green Energy Corridor notes.

Q1. With reference to the PM GatiShakti National Master Plan, consider the following statements:
1. It was launched in October 2021 and initially brought 16 ministries together for integrated planning.
2. Its GIS-based spatial planning tools were developed by BISAG-N.
3. It is a new funding scheme with its own separate budget allocation.

Which of the statements given above are correct?

✅ Answer: (a) 1 and 2 only

💡 Explanation: Statement 3 is incorrect. GatiShakti is a planning and coordination platform; projects are still funded through the ministries' own schemes.

Q2. Under PM GatiShakti, the Empowered Group of Secretaries (EGoS) is chaired by:

✅ Answer: (b) The Cabinet Secretary

💡 Explanation: The EGoS, chaired by the Cabinet Secretary, oversees implementation and approves changes in the Master Plan. NITI Aayog has no nodal role; the Logistics Division of DPIIT is the Secretariat.

Q3. Consider the following pairs about the PM GatiShakti institutional framework:
Body | Role
1. Network Planning Group | Heads of network planning divisions of infrastructure ministries; examines project proposals
2. Technical Support Unit | Domain experts supporting the evaluation process
3. Logistics Division, DPIIT | PM GatiShakti Secretariat

How many pairs are correctly matched?

✅ Answer: (c) All three

💡 Explanation: All three pairs match PIB's description of the three-tier framework (EGoS, NPG, TSU), with the TSU assisting the Logistics Division as Secretariat.

Q4. Which of the following is NOT one of the seven engines of PM GatiShakti?

✅ Answer: (c) Digital Infrastructure

💡 Explanation: The seven engines are Roads, Railways, Ports, Waterways, Airports, Mass Transport and Logistics Infrastructure.

Q5. PM GatiShakti is primarily designed to address which of the following challenges?

✅ Answer: (b) Fragmented planning leading to cost overruns and delays

💡 Explanation: GatiShakti brings every ministry's networks onto one GIS platform so projects are planned together, reducing duplication and delays.

🔁 PM GatiShakti in 10 Seconds

  • Launched: 13 October 2021 (announced 15 August 2021 as a plan of more than ₹100 lakh crore)
  • Type: GIS-based planning platform + coordination framework (no separate budget)
  • Coverage: 16 ministries at launch → 58 Ministries/Departments + all 36 States/UTs (Oct 2026)
  • Seven Engines: Roads, Railways, Ports, Waterways, Airports, Mass Transport, Logistics Infrastructure
  • Institutions: EGoS (Cabinet Secretary) → NPG → TSU; Secretariat: Logistics Division, DPIIT

  • Progress: NPG evaluated 396 projects (~₹18.66 lakh crore) by Aug 2026

N → NPG: network planning heads of ministries; examines proposals

T → TSU: domain experts; assists the Logistics Division (Secretariat)
