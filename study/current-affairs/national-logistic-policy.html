🚚 National Logistics Policy 2022

Reducing Logistics Cost • ULIP • Gati Shakti Integration • Export Competitiveness

High Yield GS-3 + Economy ⚡ Infrastructure + Governance 📅 Last updated: Oct 2026

Quick answers

What does the National Logistics Policy aim to do?
It has three targets: reduce the cost of logistics in India to be comparable to global benchmarks by 2030; endeavour to be among the top 25 countries in the Logistics Performance Index by 2030; and create a data-driven decision support mechanism for an efficient logistics ecosystem.

When was it launched?
The Prime Minister launched it on 17 September 2022.

Official source: PIB, 17 Sep 2022.

  • What is NLP 2022? → India's first comprehensive National Logistics Policy, launched on September 17, 2022, by the Prime Minister.
  • Core Objective: Reduce logistics cost (estimated at 13-14% of GDP) to be comparable to global benchmarks by 2030, and be among the top 25 in the LPI by 2030.
  • Why Needed? High logistics cost reduces export competitiveness, increases inflation, and hampers Ease of Doing Business.
  • Integration: Works in synergy with PM Gati Shakti National Master Plan for multimodal connectivity.
  • UPSC Angle: Tests understanding of infrastructure economics, supply chain management, federal coordination, and export-led growth strategies.

📌 Comprehensive Logistics Action Plan (CLAP): Eight Action Areas

  • Integrated Digital Logistics Systems
  • Standardisation of physical assets and benchmarking of service quality standards
  • Logistics Human Resources Development and Capacity Building
  • State Engagement
  • EXIM (Export-Import) Logistics
  • Service Improvement Framework
  • Sectoral Plan for Efficient Logistics
  • Facilitation of Development of Logistics Parks

📌 Key Initiatives Under NLP

  • ULIP (Unified Logistics Interface Platform): Integrated portal giving real-time information on the location of goods; links government digital systems through APIs, and private players can access its data after signing a non-disclosure agreement.
  • E-LogS (Ease of Logistics Services): Platform where industry raises logistics issues, which the concerned authorities take up on priority.
  • SIG (Services Improvement Group): Set up by the Empowered Group of Secretaries (EGoS) under PM GatiShakti, on the pattern of the Network Planning Group, to monitor process, regulatory and digital improvements in logistics.
  • LEADS (Logistics Ease Across Different States): India's annual ranking of states on logistics performance, modelled on the World Bank's Logistics Performance Index (LPI).

📌 Integration with PM Gati Shakti

  • Gati Shakti: ₹100 lakh crore infrastructure plan with GIS-based digital platform for multimodal connectivity planning.
  • Synergy: NLP focuses on soft infrastructure (processes, regulations, digital systems); Gati Shakti focuses on hard infrastructure (roads, rails, ports).
  • Outcome: Reduced transit time, lower inventory costs, improved reliability for exporters and manufacturers.

📌 Focus on MSMEs & Startups

  • Logistics Parks: Development of integrated logistics parks with warehousing, cold chains, and value-added services.
  • Technology Adoption: Incentives for IoT, AI, blockchain in supply chain management; support for logistics startups via DPIIT.
  • Green Logistics: Promotion of electric vehicles, renewable energy in warehouses, and sustainable packaging.

✅ Quick Facts

  • Nodal Ministry: Ministry of Commerce & Industry (Department for Promotion of Industry and Internal Trade - DPIIT).
  • Monitoring: Uses the existing PM GatiShakti institutional framework, the Empowered Group of Secretaries (EGoS).
  • LPI: The World Bank's country ranking, based on Customs, Infrastructure, International Shipments, Logistics Quality, Tracking and Timeliness.
  • Implemented through: The Comprehensive Logistics Action Plan (CLAP), with eight action areas.

✅ Global Benchmarking

  • India's LPI Rank (2023): 38th out of 139 countries (World Bank) — improved from 44th in 2018.
  • Target: Among the top 25 countries in the LPI by 2030.
💡 Prelims Trap: NLP is a policy framework, not a statutory body. Implementation depends on state cooperation, regulatory reforms, and digital infrastructure rollout.

🎯 NLP 2022: Multi-Dimensional Analysis

🔹 Economic Impact: Competitiveness & Growth

  • Export Competitiveness: Lower logistics cost = lower final price = higher global market share for Indian goods (textiles, pharma, engineering).
  • Inflation Control: Efficient supply chains reduce food wastage (30% post-harvest loss) and input costs for manufacturers.
  • FDI Attraction: Predictable logistics ecosystem makes India more attractive for global supply chain diversification ("China+1").

🔹 Governance & Federal Coordination

  • Concurrent List Challenge: Transport, trade, and commerce entries require Centre-State consensus; state rankings under LEADS encourage reforms.
  • Regulatory Fragmentation: Multiple permits, varying state taxes, and checkpoint delays addressed through E-LogS and the SIG.
  • Digital Federalism: ULIP enables data sharing while respecting state autonomy; API-based architecture allows incremental adoption.

🔹 Sustainability & Inclusion

  • Green Logistics: Modal shift from road to rail/waterways reduces carbon footprint; EV adoption in last-mile delivery.
  • MSME Integration: Simplified compliance, access to warehousing, and digital tools help small businesses compete with large corporations.
  • Employment Quality: Skill certification, formalization of informal logistics workforce, and social security linkages.

🔹 Critical Challenges & Way Forward

  • Infrastructure Gaps: Last-mile connectivity, port congestion, and rail freight imbalance require sustained Gati Shakti investments.
  • Digital Divide: Rural MSMEs may lack access to ULIP/E-LogS; need for offline-on-ramps and capacity building.
  • Data Privacy & Security: ULIP's data sharing must comply with DPDP Act 2023; robust cybersecurity frameworks essential.
  • Monitoring & Accountability: SIG must have teeth to enforce reforms; independent evaluation of the LEADS methodology needed.

🔹 Mains Answer Framework

  1. Contextualize: Link NLP to Make in India, Export Preparedness Index, and SDG-9 (Industry, Innovation, Infrastructure).
  2. Analyze the Plan: The eight CLAP action areas, from integrated digital systems (ULIP) to logistics parks and human resources.
  3. Critically Evaluate: Implementation gaps (federalism, digital access), trade-offs (speed vs. inclusion), global benchmarking.
  4. Way Forward: Strengthen SIG with statutory backing, expand ULIP to include private logistics players, link LEADS performance to incentives for states.

📌 Case 1: ULIP in Action – Reducing Documentation Time

  • Context: Logistics data sat in separate systems run by different ministries, so tracking cargo meant checking each one.
  • ULIP Intervention: One portal linking these systems through APIs, launched with the policy in September 2022.
  • Outcome: Real-time information on the location of goods; companies can build their own apps on ULIP data.
  • UPSC Link: Digital governance + Ease of Doing Business + Export promotion strategies.

📌 Case 2: LEADS Rankings – Competitive Federalism

  • Context: Logistics performance varies widely between states.
  • LEADS Framework: An annual survey-based ranking of states, modelled on the World Bank's LPI.
  • Outcome: Identifies areas of improvement and supports states in infrastructure, services and regulatory reforms. By the policy launch, 14 states had State Logistics Policies, with 13 more in draft.
  • UPSC Link: Cooperative federalism + Performance-based governance + Infrastructure planning.

Q1. With reference to the National Logistics Policy 2022, consider the following statements:
1. It aims to reduce logistics cost to be comparable to global benchmarks by 2030.
2. ULIP is a digital platform that integrates data from multiple government ministries.
3. The policy is implemented solely by the Ministry of Road Transport and Highways.

Which of the statements given above are correct?

✅ Answer: (a) 1 and 2 only

💡 Explanation: Statement 3 is incorrect. NLP is coordinated by DPIIT (Ministry of Commerce & Industry), not MoRTH. Statements 1 & 2 are correct.

Q2. The 'Unified Logistics Interface Platform' (ULIP) under NLP 2022 primarily aims to:

✅ Answer: (b) Provide real-time data integration across 30+ government digital systems

💡 Explanation: ULIP is the digital backbone of NLP, enabling seamless data exchange between ministries (Railways, Road Transport, Commerce, etc.) to improve visibility and efficiency.

Q3. Consider the following pairs:
Initiative | Purpose under NLP 2022
1. E-LogS | Platform for industry to raise logistics issues
2. SIG | Services Improvement Group monitoring process and regulatory improvements
3. LEADS | Annual ranking of states on logistics performance

How many pairs are correctly matched?

✅ Answer: (c) All three

💡 Explanation: All three pairs are correctly matched. E-LogS takes up industry issues, the SIG monitors improvements, and LEADS ranks states. Do not confuse LEADS with the LPI, which is the World Bank's ranking of countries.

Q4. India's current logistics cost as a percentage of GDP is approximately:

✅ Answer: (c) 13-14%

💡 Explanation: India's logistics cost is estimated at 13-14% of GDP, which the policy aims to bring down to global benchmarks by 2030.

Q5. Which of the following is NOT one of the eight action areas of the Comprehensive Logistics Action Plan under the National Logistics Policy 2022?

✅ Answer: (c) Privatization of all port operations

💡 Explanation: The eight action areas are Integrated Digital Logistics Systems; Standardisation of physical assets and service quality; Logistics HR Development; State Engagement; EXIM Logistics; Service Improvement Framework; Sectoral Plans; and Logistics Parks. Port privatisation is not one of them.
