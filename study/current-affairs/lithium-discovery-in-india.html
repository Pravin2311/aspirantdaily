⚡ Lithium Discoveries in India

Strategic Minerals • Energy Security • Critical Minerals Mission • Domestic Manufacturing

High Yield GS-3 + GS-1 + Essay ⚡ Critical Minerals + Economy 📅 Last updated: Oct 2026
  • What is Lithium? → The lightest metal (symbol Li, atomic number 3), essential for lithium-ion batteries in EVs, phones and grid storage.
  • The Reasi find: On 9 February 2023, the Ministry of Mines said the Geological Survey of India (GSI) had, for the first time, established lithium inferred resources (G3 stage) of 5.9 million tonnes in the Salal-Haimana area of Reasi district, Jammu & Kashmir.
  • "Resource", not "reserve": G3 is the earliest (reconnaissance) stage. The figure is the estimated ore, not pure lithium, and its economic viability has not been proven.
  • Other finds: The Atomic Minerals Directorate (AMD) established 1,600 tonnes of lithium resources at Marlagalla, Mandya district, Karnataka; preliminary survey work is under way at Degana, Rajasthan.
  • Policy response: The 2023 amendment to the mining law (MMDR Act) let the Centre auction critical minerals, and the National Critical Mineral Mission (2025) funds exploration and overseas assets.
  • UPSC Angle: Resource security, critical minerals diplomacy, Centre-State powers over minerals, and the EV value chain.

📌 Where Lithium Has Been Found in India

  • Salal-Haimana, Reasi (J&K): 5.9 million tonnes inferred resources (G3), established by GSI. The report was handed to the J&K Government at the 62nd Central Geological Programming Board meeting (9 February 2023).
  • Marlagalla, Mandya (Karnataka): 1,600 tonnes of lithium resources in pegmatites, established by AMD (Department of Atomic Energy); subsurface exploration continues.
  • Degana (Rajasthan): Preliminary survey activities under way as of March 2026.
  • Brine search: Reconnaissance surveys along the Saraswati river palaeochannel in Jodhpur and Barmer districts of Rajasthan.

📌 Resource Classification (UNFC stages)

  • G4: Reconnaissance survey
  • G3: Preliminary exploration, which gives an "inferred" resource (Reasi is here)
  • G2: General exploration
  • G1: Detailed exploration
  • Only after feasibility studies can part of a resource be called a reserve.

📌 Types of Lithium Deposits

  • Hard rock (pegmatite): Lithium minerals such as spodumene in pegmatite veins; Australia is the main producer. India's Karnataka find is pegmatite-hosted.
  • Brine: Lithium dissolved in salt-flat groundwater; the "Lithium Triangle" of Chile, Argentina and Bolivia.
  • Clay: Lithium held in clay minerals; extraction is still at an early stage globally.

📌 Legal and Policy Framework

  • Critical minerals list (2023): The Ministry of Mines identified 30 critical minerals, including lithium, cobalt, nickel, graphite and rare earth elements.
  • MMDR Amendment Act, 2023: Removed lithium (and five others) from the list of atomic minerals, opening it to private mining, and gave the Centre exclusive power to auction 24 critical minerals.
  • Auctions: The first tranche of critical mineral auctions was launched on 29 November 2023, and included the Salal-Haimana lithium block. By March 2026 the Centre had auctioned 46 critical and strategic mineral blocks in all.
  • National Critical Mineral Mission (NCMM): Approved on 29 January 2025; ₹16,300 crore of government spending plus about ₹18,000 crore expected from PSUs (₹34,300 crore in all), over 2024-25 to 2030-31.

📌 Overseas Acquisition

  • KABIL (Khanij Bidesh India Ltd): A joint venture of NALCO, Hindustan Copper and MECL, set up to acquire critical mineral assets abroad.
  • Argentina: KABIL signed an Exploration and Development Agreement with CAMYEN, a state-owned company of Catamarca province, for five lithium brine blocks covering about 15,703 hectares.
  • Minerals Security Partnership: India joined this US-led grouping in 2023 to diversify critical mineral supply chains.

✅ Quick Facts

  • Element: Li, atomic number 3, density about 0.53 g/cm³; so reactive it is stored under oil.
  • Constitution: Minerals are a State subject (State List Entry 23), subject to Union List Entry 54, which lets Parliament regulate mines and mineral development by law; the MMDR Act, 1957 is that law.
  • Atomic minerals: Before 2023, lithium was an atomic mineral, so only public bodies could mine it.
  • KABIL partners: NALCO, Hindustan Copper Ltd and Mineral Exploration & Consultancy Ltd.
  • Production: India does not yet mine lithium commercially; cells and lithium compounds are imported.
💡 Prelims Trap: Reasi's 5.9 million tonnes is an inferred resource at G3 stage, not a proven reserve, and it was announced in February 2023. The Karnataka (Mandya) find was made by AMD, not GSI.

🎯 Lithium: Multi-Dimensional Analysis

🔹 Why Lithium Matters

  • EVs and storage: Battery cells are the costliest part of an EV, and grid batteries are needed to integrate solar and wind power.
  • Concentrated supply: Mining is concentrated in a few countries (Australia, Chile and others), and refining is concentrated even more heavily, mainly in China.
  • Industrial policy link: The ACC battery PLI aims for domestic cells; domestic or secure overseas lithium supports that goal.

🔹 From Discovery to Mine: The Long Road

  • Exploration stages: A G3 resource needs G2 and G1 drilling before anyone can judge whether mining pays.
  • Investor interest: Bidders need confidence about grade and recovery. Early-stage blocks such as Salal-Haimana have drawn limited interest so far.
  • Processing gap: Even with ore, India needs plants to turn concentrate into battery-grade lithium carbonate or hydroxide.

🔹 Federal and Local Dimensions

  • Centre-State: The 2023 amendment shifted auction powers for critical minerals to the Centre, but auction revenue (royalties and premiums) still goes to the State.
  • Local concerns: Mining in the Himalayan foothills raises questions of land, water, slope stability and benefit-sharing through District Mineral Foundations.

🔹 Critical Challenges & Way Forward

  • Geological uncertainty: Grades and recoverability at Reasi are not yet established.
  • Technology: India lacks commercial lithium refining; the NCMM funds research and processing parks.
  • Diversification: Overseas assets (Argentina), partnerships (Minerals Security Partnership) and recycling under the Battery Waste Management Rules, 2022 reduce dependence on one supplier.
  • Alternatives: Sodium-ion and other chemistries could lower lithium demand.

🔹 Mains Answer Framework

  1. Contextualize: EV and storage demand, concentrated global supply.
  2. Explain the finds: Reasi (G3, 5.9 Mt), Mandya (AMD, 1,600 t), Degana (survey).
  3. Policy response: MMDR Amendment 2023, critical minerals auctions, NCMM, KABIL.
  4. Critically evaluate: Exploration stage, processing gap, investor interest, local impacts.

📌 Case 1: KABIL in Argentina's Lithium Triangle

  • Deal: An Exploration and Development Agreement with CAMYEN (Catamarca province) for five lithium brine blocks, about 15,703 hectares.
  • Why Argentina: It is part of the Lithium Triangle, with large brine deposits.
  • Lesson: Overseas assets take years to explore and develop, and depend on stable host-country rules.
  • UPSC Link: Resource diplomacy + India–Latin America ties + Supply chain resilience.

📌 Case 2: Salal-Haimana, Reasi – From Headline to Auction

  • February 2023: GSI established a G3 inferred resource of 5.9 Mt.
  • November 2023: The block was included in the first tranche of critical mineral auctions as a composite licence (exploration followed by mining).
  • Lesson: Early-stage blocks carry high geological risk, so they need more exploration data to attract bidders.
  • UPSC Link: Mineral governance + Centre-State relations + Investment climate.

Q1. With reference to lithium in India, consider the following statements:
1. GSI established lithium inferred resources of 5.9 million tonnes in Reasi district of Jammu & Kashmir.
2. The Reasi estimate is a proven reserve at the G1 stage of exploration.
3. The Atomic Minerals Directorate established lithium resources in Mandya district of Karnataka.

Which of the statements given above are correct?

✅ Answer: (c) 1 and 3 only

💡 Explanation: Statement 2 is incorrect: Reasi's figure is an inferred resource at the G3 (preliminary exploration) stage, not a proven reserve.

Q2. Spodumene, an important source of lithium, is found mainly in:

✅ Answer: (b) Hard rock pegmatites

💡 Explanation: Spodumene is a lithium-aluminium silicate mineral found in pegmatites; Australia's hard-rock mines are the main example.

Q3. Consider the following pairs:
Initiative | Description
1. KABIL | Joint venture of NALCO, HCL and MECL for overseas mineral assets
2. National Critical Mineral Mission | Approved in January 2025 with a total outlay of ₹34,300 crore
3. MMDR Amendment Act, 2023 | Added lithium to the list of atomic minerals

How many pairs are correctly matched?

✅ Answer: (b) Only two

💡 Explanation: Pair 3 is wrong: the 2023 amendment removed lithium from the atomic minerals list, so private firms can now mine it.

Q4. The list of 30 critical minerals of India was identified by the:

✅ Answer: (c) Ministry of Mines

💡 Explanation: The Ministry of Mines released the list of 30 critical minerals in 2023; it includes lithium, cobalt, nickel, graphite and rare earths.

Q5. Under the Constitution, regulation of mines and mineral development under Union control is provided for in:

✅ Answer: (b) Entry 54 of the Union List

💡 Explanation: Entry 54 (List I) covers regulation of mines and mineral development to the extent Parliament declares by law; Entry 23 of the State List is subject to it. Article 297 deals with resources of territorial waters and the EEZ; Article 246A is about GST.
