🗳️ One Nation One Election Debate

Simultaneous Elections • High-Level Committee Report • Constitutional Amendments • Federalism Concerns • Implementation Roadmap

High Yield Polity + Governance + Federalism ⚡ Ongoing Reform Debate 📅 Last updated: Oct 2026
  • What is ONOE? → Holding elections to the Lok Sabha and all State Legislative Assemblies together, followed by municipal and panchayat elections within a fixed window, instead of staggered polls every year.
  • History: The first four general elections (1951-52, 1957, 1962, 1967) were held together with Assembly polls. Premature dissolutions of several Assemblies in 1968-69 and of the Fourth Lok Sabha in December 1970 broke the cycle.
  • Kovind Committee: A High-Level Committee chaired by former President Ram Nath Kovind was set up on 2 September 2023 and submitted its report on 14 March 2024. The Union Cabinet accepted its recommendations on 18 September 2024.
  • Bills: The Constitution (129th Amendment) Bill, 2024 and the Union Territories Laws (Amendment) Bill, 2024 were introduced in the Lok Sabha on 17 December 2024 and referred to a Joint Parliamentary Committee (JPC) chaired by P.P. Chaudhary. In July 2026 the Lok Sabha extended the JPC's term to the Winter Session 2026; the Bills have not been passed.
  • UPSC Angle: GS-2 (Articles 83, 172, 327, 368; federalism; electoral reforms) and essay/ethics themes on democracy versus efficiency.

📌 Constitutional Framework Today

  • Article 83(2): Lok Sabha continues for five years from its first meeting, unless dissolved sooner.
  • Article 85(2)(b): The President may dissolve the Lok Sabha (acting on ministerial advice under Article 74).
  • Article 172(1): State Legislative Assembly continues for five years, unless dissolved sooner.
  • Article 174(2)(b): The Governor may dissolve the Assembly.
  • Article 324: Election Commission of India: superintendence, direction and control of elections.
  • Article 327: Parliament may make laws on elections to Parliament and State Legislatures.
  • Articles 243E and 243U: Five-year terms for panchayats and municipalities; elections are run by State Election Commissions (243K, 243ZA).

📌 Kovind Committee: Key Recommendations

  • Two steps: First, simultaneous elections to the Lok Sabha and State Assemblies. Second, municipal and panchayat elections synchronised so that they are held within 100 days of the Lok Sabha and Assembly polls.
  • Appointed date: The President would notify an "appointed date" on the first sitting of a newly elected Lok Sabha; Assemblies elected after that date would end with that Lok Sabha's term, so that the cycles align once.
  • Mid-term breakdown: If a House is dissolved early (hung House, no-confidence), fresh elections are held only for the unexpired remainder of the five-year term.
  • Single electoral roll and EPIC: One voter list and photo identity card for all three tiers, prepared by the ECI in consultation with State Election Commissions.
  • State ratification: Not needed for step one; needed (at least half the States, under Article 368(2)) for step two and the common roll, because they touch State subjects.
  • Implementation group: To oversee execution of the recommendations.

📌 The Constitution (129th Amendment) Bill, 2024

  • New Article 82A: Provides for the appointed date and simultaneous elections to the Lok Sabha and all Assemblies.
  • Amends Articles 83, 172 and 327: Sets "full term" and "unexpired term" rules, and adds simultaneous elections to Parliament's law-making power.
  • Deferral clause: If the ECI considers that an Assembly election cannot be held with the Lok Sabha poll, it may recommend to the President that it be held later; that Assembly's term would still end with the Lok Sabha's.
  • Scope: Covers only the Lok Sabha and Assemblies (step one); local bodies are not included. The Union Territories Laws (Amendment) Bill, 2024 aligns Delhi, Puducherry and Jammu & Kashmir.

📌 Arguments in Favour

  • Governance: Less frequent imposition of the Model Code of Conduct, which pauses new schemes and announcements.
  • Administration: Security forces, teachers and officials are pulled away from regular duties less often.
  • Cost: Shared polling infrastructure and staff can reduce the public cost of conducting elections.
  • Political finance: Fewer campaigns may reduce constant fund-raising pressure on parties.

📌 Arguments Against

  • Federalism: State election calendars would be tied to the Lok Sabha's, and Assembly terms could be cut short to align them.
  • Accountability: Elections for an "unexpired term" could produce short-lived Houses and more frequent polls in some States.
  • National issues dominating: Critics fear national campaigns would overshadow State and local issues and favour larger parties.
  • Logistics: Simultaneous polls would need far more EVMs, VVPATs, security personnel and polling staff at one time.

✅ Quick Facts

  • Earlier studies: Law Commission's 170th Report (1999); Parliamentary Standing Committee's 79th Report (2015); NITI Aayog discussion paper (2017); Law Commission draft report (2018).
  • Constructive vote of no-confidence: Suggested by the 2018 Law Commission draft (a motion must name an alternative government); it is not part of the 129th Amendment Bill.
  • Local bodies: Panchayat and municipal elections are conducted by State Election Commissions, not the ECI.
  • Model Code of Conduct: Not a statute; a code agreed with political parties and enforced by the ECI from the announcement of the poll schedule.
  • Other countries: South Africa holds national and provincial elections together; Sweden holds national, regional and municipal elections on the same day.
💡 Prelims Trap: The 129th Amendment Bill covers only the Lok Sabha and State Assemblies and was introduced without State ratification under Article 368(2). Ratification by half the States is needed only for the second step (local bodies) and the single electoral roll.

🎯 One Nation One Election: Multi-Dimensional Analysis

🔹 Constitutional Questions

  • Basic structure: Federalism and democracy are basic features (Kesavananda Bharati, S.R. Bommai). Critics argue that curtailing Assembly terms by central law affects them; supporters say the Bill leaves the five-year term and voting rights intact.
  • Ratification: Whether changing Assembly terms needs State ratification under Article 368(2) is debated; the government's view is that step one does not.
  • Deferral power: Allowing an Assembly poll to be deferred on the ECI's recommendation raises questions about how often, and on what grounds, it could be used.

🔹 Governance and Administration

  • Model Code of Conduct: With some State poll almost every year, the code repeatedly delays new schemes in those States.
  • Resources: Simultaneous polls concentrate demand for EVMs, VVPATs, central forces and staff into one period, needing a one-time investment and long preparation.
  • Local bodies: Many State Election Commissions delay local polls; a 100-day window could strengthen the third tier if States agree.

🔹 Democracy and Accountability

  • Voter choice: Frequent elections act as a check on governments between general elections; ONOE removes that mid-term signal.
  • Unexpired terms: A House elected for, say, one year may be less stable and less effective.
  • Party system: Regional parties fear national campaigns would crowd out State issues.

🔹 Way Forward

  • Consensus: Wide consultation with States and parties through the JPC process.
  • Safeguards: Clear limits on deferral and on unexpired-term elections.
  • Capacity: Advance procurement of EVMs/VVPATs and planning for security forces.
  • Local bodies: Pair synchronisation with stronger State Election Commissions.

🔹 Mains Answer Framework

  1. Contextualize: Simultaneous polls until 1967; Kovind Committee (2024); 129th Amendment Bill.
  2. Benefits: Governance continuity, administrative relief, cost.
  3. Concerns: Federalism, accountability, basic structure, logistics.
  4. Balanced conclusion: Consensus, safeguards and capacity before rollout.

📌 Case 1: How the 1967 Cycle Broke

  • 1967: The last time the Lok Sabha and Assemblies were elected together.
  • 1968-69: Several Assemblies were dissolved early amid unstable coalitions and defections.
  • December 1970: The Fourth Lok Sabha was dissolved a year early, and elections were held in 1971.
  • Lesson: Synchronisation fails without rules for what happens when a House is dissolved early.

📌 Case 2: States That Already Vote With the Lok Sabha

  • Example: Andhra Pradesh and Odisha held Assembly polls along with the 2024 Lok Sabha election.
  • Lesson: Combined polls are administratively possible at State scale; the challenge is doing it for every State at once.
  • UPSC Link: Electoral administration + federalism.

📌 Case 3: From Committee to JPC

  • 2023-24: Kovind Committee consulted political parties, experts and the public before reporting.
  • Dec 2024: Bills introduced and sent to a Joint Parliamentary Committee.
  • 2026: The JPC's term was extended twice (March and July 2026) as it held consultations, including with States.
  • UPSC Link: Parliamentary committees + legislative process.

Q1. With reference to the High-Level Committee on Simultaneous Elections, consider the following statements:
1. It was chaired by former President Ram Nath Kovind.
2. It recommended that municipal and panchayat elections be held within 100 days of the Lok Sabha and Assembly elections.
3. It recommended that State ratification is required for the first step of simultaneous Lok Sabha and Assembly elections.

Which of the statements given above are correct?

✅ Answer: (a) 1 and 2 only

💡 Explanation: The committee said the first step needs no State ratification; ratification is needed for the second step (local bodies) and the single electoral roll.

Q2. The Constitution (129th Amendment) Bill, 2024 proposes to insert which new Article?

✅ Answer: (a) Article 82A

💡 Explanation: Article 82A provides for the appointed date and simultaneous elections; the Bill also amends Articles 83, 172 and 327. Article 324A was proposed by the Kovind Committee for local bodies but is not in this Bill.

Q3. Under the Kovind Committee's proposal, if a State Assembly is dissolved before completing its term, the newly elected Assembly will:

✅ Answer: (b) Serve only the unexpired remainder of the term

💡 Explanation: This keeps the State in step with the national cycle. The constructive vote of no-confidence was a 2018 Law Commission idea, not part of the Bill.

Q4. Elections to panchayats and municipalities in India are conducted by:

✅ Answer: (b) State Election Commissions

💡 Explanation: Articles 243K (panchayats) and 243ZA (municipalities) vest these elections in State Election Commissions.

Q5. India last held simultaneous elections to the Lok Sabha and all State Legislative Assemblies in:

✅ Answer: (b) 1967

💡 Explanation: Early dissolutions of several Assemblies (1968-69) and of the Lok Sabha (December 1970) broke the cycle.

🔁 One Nation One Election in 10 Seconds

  • Until 1967: simultaneous polls; broken by 1968-69 and 1970 dissolutions
  • Kovind Committee: set up Sep 2023, report 14 Mar 2024, accepted by Cabinet 18 Sep 2024
  • Two steps: Lok Sabha + Assemblies; then local bodies within 100 days
  • Bill: 129th Amendment, 2024: new Article 82A; amends 83, 172, 327
  • Status: with JPC (chair P.P. Chaudhary); term extended to Winter Session 2026
  • Ratification: not for step one; half the States for step two and single roll

🧠 Mnemonic: "A-U-D" for the Bill

A → Appointed date (first sitting of new Lok Sabha)

U → Unexpired term after early dissolution

D → Deferral of an Assembly poll on ECI recommendation
