🔬 Semiconductor Mission India

₹76,000 Cr Semicon India • 12 Approved Units • Dholera Fab • Semicon 2.0 (₹1,27,500 Cr)

High Yield Economy + S&T + Strategic ⚡ Make in India Chips 📅 Last updated: Oct 2026
  • What is it? → The Semicon India Programme was approved in December 2021 with ₹76,000 crore to build chip design and manufacturing in India. The India Semiconductor Mission (ISM), under MeitY, runs it.
  • Support: Up to 50% fiscal support for fabs, assembly and packaging units (ATMP/OSAT), plus a Design Linked Incentive (DLI) for chip design.
  • Progress of ISM 1.0 (July 2026): 12 manufacturing units approved, with cumulative investment over ₹1.64 lakh crore: one silicon fab, one silicon carbide fab, one gallium nitride micro-LED display fab and nine packaging units. Micron, Kaynes and CG Semi have started commercial production.
  • Semicon 2.0: Approved by the Cabinet on 15 July 2026 with ₹1,27,500 crore, built on six pillars: design, machines and materials, more fabs, ATMP/OSAT, R&D, and talent.
  • UPSC Angle: Industrial policy, supply chain security, strategic autonomy and technology diplomacy.

📌 The Semiconductor Value Chain

  • Design: Chip architecture and circuit design, India's traditional strength.
  • Fabrication (fab): Making chips on silicon wafers; the most capital- and technology-intensive step.
  • ATMP/OSAT: Assembly, Testing, Marking and Packaging (Outsourced Semiconductor Assembly and Test); less capital-intensive, and India's entry point.
  • Equipment, materials, chemicals and gases: Highly concentrated globally; the target of Semicon 2.0's second pillar.

📌 Semicon India Programme (ISM 1.0)

  • Outlay: ₹76,000 crore, approved December 2021.
  • Schemes: semiconductor fabs; display fabs; compound semiconductor, silicon photonics and sensor fabs; ATMP/OSAT units; and the Design Linked Incentive (DLI).
  • Fiscal support: Up to 50% for fabrication, ATMP/OSAT and chip design.
  • DLI: 24 design projects by startups and MSMEs approved for support; 105 startups and MSMEs given access to industry-standard Electronic Design Automation (EDA) tools.
  • Talent: 315 universities training students in chip design with EDA tools; about 68,000 students trained.

📌 Major Approved Projects

  • Tata Electronics fab, Dholera (Gujarat): About ₹91,000 crore, with technology partner PSMC (Taiwan). India's first commercial silicon fab, due to be commissioned in 2028. Its Dholera SEZ was notified in April 2026.
  • Micron, Sanand (Gujarat): ATMP unit, investment ₹22,516 crore; in commercial production.
  • Tata Semiconductor Assembly and Test, Morigaon (Assam): ATMP unit, ₹27,000 crore.
  • CG Power (with Renesas, Japan and Stars Microelectronics, Thailand), Sanand: OSAT unit, ₹7,600 crore; CG Semi is in commercial production.
  • Kaynes Semicon, Sanand: OSAT unit, about ₹3,300 crore; in commercial production.
  • May 2026 approvals: Crystal Matrix (GaN micro-LED display fab, Dholera) and Suchi Semicon (OSAT, Surat), about ₹3,936 crore together.

📌 Semicon 2.0 – Six Pillars

  • 1. Design: Chip IPs and designs for strategic and commercial products.
  • 2. Machines and materials: Incentives for equipment makers and suppliers of chemicals and gases.
  • 3. More fabs: Silicon, compound semiconductor, discrete component and display fabs.
  • 4. ATMP/OSAT: Bring advanced packaging technologies to India.
  • 5. R&D: Move beyond the 28–110 nm nodes India has started with, towards more advanced nodes.
  • 6. Talent: Deepen chip design training, plus clean-room and fab construction skills.

✅ Quick Facts

  • First silicon fab: Tata Electronics, Dholera, with PSMC; commissioning scheduled for 2028.
  • In commercial production: Micron (Sanand), Kaynes (Sanand) and CG Semi (Sanand).
  • North-East: Tata's ATMP unit at Morigaon, Assam.
  • Nodes: India's journey has started at 28–110 nm, which suits automotive, power and industrial chips.
  • Earlier attempt: The Modified Special Incentive Package Scheme (M-SIPS, 2012) did not lead to a fab.
💡 Prelims Trap: Most approved units are packaging (ATMP/OSAT) plants, not fabs; only Tata's Dholera plant is a silicon fab. Micron's Sanand unit is an ATMP, not a fab. Semicon 2.0 (2026) is a new ₹1,27,500 crore programme, not a top-up of the ₹76,000 crore scheme.

🎯 Semiconductor Mission: Multi-Dimensional Analysis

🔹 Why Chips Matter

  • Supply shocks: The 2020–22 chip shortage stalled car and electronics production worldwide.
  • Concentration: Leading-edge manufacturing and key equipment are concentrated in a few countries, which exposes buyers to export controls and conflict risk.
  • Strategic use: Defence, space, telecom and power grids need trusted chips.

🔹 India's Strategy: Enter Where You Can, Build Up

  • Packaging first: Nine of 12 approved units are ATMP/OSAT, which are faster to build and create jobs and supplier networks.
  • Mature nodes: 28–110 nm chips serve cars, appliances, power electronics and telecom, which together are large markets.
  • Compound semiconductors: Silicon carbide and gallium nitride matter for EVs, power systems and displays.
  • Phase two: Semicon 2.0 targets the missing pieces: equipment, materials, more fabs and R&D.

🔹 Critical Challenges & Way Forward

  • Capital intensity: Fabs cost tens of thousands of crores and take years to reach output; the first fab is due only in 2028.
  • Inputs: Fabs need ultra-pure water, uninterrupted power and specialty gases and chemicals.
  • Technology access: Advanced lithography tools come from a handful of suppliers and are subject to export controls.
  • Talent: Design talent is strong, but fab operations need process engineers and technicians at scale.
  • Fiscal risk: 50% support is generous; outcomes must be tracked against investment actually made.

🔹 Mains Answer Framework

  1. Contextualize: Chip shortages, geopolitics and strategic autonomy.
  2. Explain the policy: ISM 1.0 (₹76,000 crore, 50% support) → Semicon 2.0 (₹1,27,500 crore, six pillars).
  3. Give evidence: 12 approved units, ₹1.64 lakh crore, 3 in production, first fab in 2028.
  4. Critically evaluate: Capital, inputs, technology access, talent and fiscal cost.

📌 Case 1: Tata–PSMC Fab at Dholera

  • Project: About ₹91,000 crore; Taiwan's PSMC is technology partner.
  • Milestones: Approved February 2024; Dholera SEZ notified April 2026; commissioning scheduled for 2028.
  • Significance: India's first commercial silicon fab and the anchor of a Gujarat cluster (Dholera and Sanand).
  • UPSC Link: Industrial policy + India–Taiwan technology ties + Cluster development.

📌 Case 2: Micron's Sanand ATMP – Packaging as the Entry Point

  • Project: ₹22,516 crore ATMP unit for memory chips, approved in 2023.
  • Status: Among the first ISM units in commercial production.
  • Lesson: Packaging builds suppliers, skills and credibility before fabs arrive.
  • UPSC Link: India–US technology partnership + Phased industrialisation.

📌 Case 3: Chip Design by Startups

  • Support: 24 design projects approved under the DLI; 105 startups and MSMEs given EDA tool access.
  • Applications: Satellite communications, drones, surveillance cameras, IoT devices, AI systems, telecom equipment and smart meters.
  • UPSC Link: Innovation policy + Startup ecosystem + Intellectual property.

Q1. With reference to the Semicon India Programme, consider the following statements:
1. It was approved in December 2021 with an outlay of ₹76,000 crore.
2. It offers fiscal support of up to 50% for fabs and ATMP/OSAT units.
3. It supports only fabrication, not chip design.

Which of the statements given above are correct?

✅ Answer: (a) 1 and 2 only

💡 Explanation: Statement 3 is incorrect: the Design Linked Incentive supports chip design by startups and MSMEs.

Q2. India's first commercial silicon semiconductor fab, approved under the India Semiconductor Mission, is being set up at:

✅ Answer: (b) Dholera, Gujarat

💡 Explanation: Tata Electronics is building the fab at Dholera with PSMC (Taiwan). Sanand hosts packaging units (Micron, CG Power, Kaynes) and Morigaon a Tata ATMP unit; no fab has been approved in Mysuru.

Q3. Semicon 2.0, approved by the Union Cabinet in July 2026, has a total outlay of:

✅ Answer: (c) ₹1,27,500 crore

💡 Explanation: ₹76,000 crore was the first programme; ₹1.64 lakh crore is the cumulative investment in the 12 units approved under ISM 1.0.

Q4. Consider the following pairs:
Unit | Type
1. Micron, Sanand | ATMP
2. Tata Electronics, Dholera | Silicon fab
3. Tata, Morigaon | Silicon fab

How many pairs are correctly matched?

✅ Answer: (b) Only two

💡 Explanation: Tata's Morigaon (Assam) unit is an assembly and test (ATMP) plant, not a fab.

Q5. Which of the following is NOT one of the six pillars of Semicon 2.0?

✅ Answer: (c) Import duty exemption for finished chips

💡 Explanation: The six pillars are design, machines and materials, more fabs, ATMP/OSAT, R&D, and talent development.
