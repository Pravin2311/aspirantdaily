🛕 South Indian Dynasties

Sangam Age • Satavahanas • Pallavas • Chalukyas • Rashtrakutas • Cholas • Hoysalas • Kakatiyas

High Yield History 📅 Last updated: Oct 2026
  • South Indian history is a favourite of UPSC and State PSC papers because each dynasty is tied to a capital, a famous ruler, a temple and a piece of literature — ideal material for matching questions.
  • The story runs from the Sangam age (roughly 3rd century BCE to 3rd century CE) through the Satavahanas of the Deccan, the long Pallava–Chalukya rivalry, the Rashtrakutas, and the peak of the Imperial Cholas in the 11th century.
  • The later Hoysalas and Kakatiyas were eventually overrun by the Delhi Sultanate in the early 14th century, setting the stage for Vijayanagara (covered in our Sultanate & Mughals notes).

The Sangam Age

"Sangam" refers to assemblies of Tamil poets said to have met at Madurai under Pandya patronage. Three kingdoms ruled the far south:

Pallavas vs Chalukyas

  • Mahendravarman I (Pallava) was a poet-king who wrote the Sanskrit farce Mattavilasa Prahasana and began rock-cut temples.
  • Pulakeshin II (Chalukya) defeated Harsha on the banks of the Narmada. His victories are recorded in the Aihole inscription, composed by his court poet Ravikirti. The Chinese pilgrim Xuanzang visited his kingdom.
  • Narasimhavarman I ("Mamalla") defeated Pulakeshin II and captured Vatapi in about 642 CE, taking the title Vatapikonda. The port of Mamallapuram is named after him, and its monolithic rathas date from his reign.
  • Narasimhavarman II (Rajasimha) built the Shore Temple at Mamallapuram and the Kailasanatha temple at Kanchipuram.
  • The Chalukyas built early temples at Aihole (the "cradle of Indian temple architecture"), Badami and Pattadakal, where the Virupaksha temple was built by Queen Lokamahadevi to celebrate a victory over the Pallavas.

The Rashtrakutas

Founded by Dantidurga, who overthrew the Chalukyas in the mid-8th century. Krishna I built the monolithic Kailasa (Kailasanatha) temple — Cave 16 at Ellora, carved top-down out of a single rock. Amoghavarsha I founded Manyakheta and wrote Kavirajamarga, the earliest surviving work on Kannada poetics. The Arab traveller Sulaiman counted him among the four great kings of the world.

The Imperial Cholas

  • Vijayalaya revived Chola power around 850 CE by capturing Thanjavur.
  • Rajaraja I (985–1014) conquered Sri Lanka's north and the Maldives and built the Brihadeeswara (Rajarajeswaram) temple at Thanjavur, completed in about 1010.
  • Rajendra I (1014–1044) marched to the Ganga, took the title Gangaikonda and built a new capital, Gangaikonda Cholapuram. His navy raided the Srivijaya kingdom in South-East Asia (about 1025).
  • The Great Living Chola Temples (UNESCO) are Brihadeeswara at Thanjavur, Gangaikonda Cholapuram and the Airavatesvara temple at Darasuram.
  • Village self-government: the Uttaramerur inscriptions of Parantaka I describe the sabha (Brahmin village assembly), with members chosen by lot through the kudavolai (pot-and-ticket) system. Non-Brahmin villages had an assembly called the ur.
  • Chola bronzes — above all the Nataraja — were made by the lost-wax method.

Hoysalas and Kakatiyas

The Hoysala temples at Belur (Chennakeshava), Halebidu (Hoysaleswara) and Somanathapura were inscribed on the UNESCO list in 2023 as the Sacred Ensembles of the Hoysalas. They stand on star-shaped platforms and are carved in soft soapstone. The Kakatiya Ramappa (Rudreswara) temple in Telangana, built with "floating bricks", became a World Heritage Site in 2021. Rudrama Devi was one of India's few ruling queens; Marco Polo visited her kingdom. Both kingdoms fell to Alauddin Khalji's general Malik Kafur in the early 1300s.

  1. Match the temple to the dynasty: Kailasa at Ellora (Rashtrakuta), Kailasanatha at Kanchi (Pallava), Brihadeeswara (Chola), Virupaksha at Pattadakal (Chalukya). The similar names are deliberate traps.
  2. Inscriptions: Aihole (Pulakeshin II, by Ravikirti) and Uttaramerur (Chola village assemblies).
  3. Sangam emblems and capitals — bow, tiger, fish; Vanji, Uraiyur, Madurai.

Q1. The Kailasa temple at Ellora was built by the:

✅ Answer: (b) Rashtrakutas

💡 Explanation: Krishna I of the Rashtrakuta dynasty had it carved from a single rock in the 8th century.

Q2. The Aihole inscription describes the achievements of:

✅ Answer: (a) Pulakeshin II

💡 Explanation: It was composed by Ravikirti, court poet of the Chalukya king Pulakeshin II.

Q3. The emblem of the Pandya kingdom was the:

✅ Answer: (d) Fish

💡 Explanation: Cheras — bow; Cholas — tiger; Pandyas — fish.

Q4. The Uttaramerur inscriptions are an important source on:

✅ Answer: (c) Chola village assemblies

💡 Explanation: They describe how members of the village sabha were chosen by the kudavolai (pot-ticket) system.

Q5. Which Chola ruler sent a naval expedition against the Srivijaya kingdom?

✅ Answer: (d) Rajendra I

💡 Explanation: Rajendra I raided Srivijaya in about 1025 and also took the title Gangaikonda.

🔁 Revise in 60 Seconds

  • Sangam: Cheras (bow, Vanji), Cholas (tiger, Uraiyur), Pandyas (fish, Madurai); Karikala — Kallanai.
  • Pulakeshin II beat Harsha (Aihole, Ravikirti); Narasimhavarman I took Vatapi (642).
  • Rashtrakutas: Krishna I — Kailasa, Ellora; Amoghavarsha — Kavirajamarga.
  • Cholas: Rajaraja I — Brihadeeswara; Rajendra I — Gangaikonda, Srivijaya; Uttaramerur — kudavolai.

📌 Prelims Traps to Avoid

  • ✘ Kailasa (Ellora, Rashtrakuta) and Kailasanatha (Kanchi, Pallava) are different temples.
  • ✘ Silappadikaram and Manimekalai are usually dated to the post-Sangam period.
  • ✘ Gangaikonda Cholapuram was built by Rajendra I, not Rajaraja I.

Who built the Brihadeeswara temple?

The Chola king Rajaraja I built the Brihadeeswara (Rajarajeswaram) temple at Thanjavur, completed in about 1010 CE. It is part of the UNESCO site "Great Living Chola Temples".

What was the kudavolai system?

A Chola-era method of choosing members of the village sabha. Names of eligible candidates were written on palm-leaf tickets, put in a pot, and drawn by a young boy. It is described in the Uttaramerur inscriptions.
