🏯 Indian Architecture & Sculpture

Stupas • Rock-cut Caves • Nagara, Dravida & Vesara • Sculpture Schools • Indo-Islamic • Mughal • Colonial

High Yield History 📅 Last updated: Oct 2026
🔍 Quick Search: Nagara vs Dravida temple style, Ajanta Ellora Elephanta difference, Gandhara Mathura Amaravati schools, Qutub Minar built by, first true arch India Alai Darwaza, Humayun tomb charbagh
  • Art and culture is one of the most unpredictable parts of UPSC Prelims, but architecture is its most reliable core: questions repeatedly ask about temple styles, cave sites, schools of sculpture and who built which monument.
  • The sequence to remember is Harappan → Mauryan → post-Mauryan (stupas, caves, sculpture schools) → Gupta (early temples) → regional temple styles → Indo-Islamic → Mughal → colonial.
  • Many monuments in these notes are UNESCO World Heritage Sites, which makes them doubly likely to appear in exams.

Mauryan and Post-Mauryan Art

  • Ashokan pillars are monolithic, made of Chunar sandstone with a mirror-like polish. The Lion Capital of Sarnath — four lions standing on an abacus with the Dharma Chakra — is India's national emblem (adopted January 26, 1950). The motto Satyameva Jayate comes from the Mundaka Upanishad.
  • The Didarganj Yakshi (Patna) is a celebrated polished-stone figure.
  • The Barabar caves (Bihar) are the oldest rock-cut caves, gifted to the Ajivika sect; the Lomas Rishi cave has a carved entrance imitating wood.

The core of the Sanchi stupa was built under Ashoka; it was enlarged under the Shungas and Satavahanas. Other major stupas: Bharhut, Amaravati and Nagarjunakonda.

Temple Architecture

Gupta-period temples were simple: a flat-roofed sanctum (garbhagriha) with a porch. The Dashavatara temple at Deogarh (UP) shows one of the earliest shikharas, and the Bhitargaon temple is an early brick temple.

The Konark Sun Temple, built by Narasimhadeva I of the Eastern Ganga dynasty in the 13th century, is shaped like the Sun god's chariot with 24 carved wheels; sailors called it the "Black Pagoda". The Odisha school has its own vocabulary: deul (sanctum tower) and jagamohana (assembly hall).

Indo-Islamic Architecture

  • New features: the true arch and dome, minarets, lime mortar, calligraphy and arabesque decoration instead of human figures.
  • Qutub Minar — begun by Qutbuddin Aibak, completed by Iltutmish, and repaired by Firoz Shah Tughlaq.
  • Alai Darwaza (1311), built by Alauddin Khalji, is regarded as the first building in India with a true arch and dome built on scientific principles.
  • The Tughlaqs used sloping (battered) walls, as at Tughlaqabad; the Lodis introduced the double dome.
  • Provincial styles: Gol Gumbaz at Bijapur (Mohammed Adil Shah, with a famous whispering gallery) and Charminar at Hyderabad (Muhammad Quli Qutb Shah, 1591).

Colonial Architecture

Victorian Gothic: Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj Terminus, Mumbai (F.W. Stevens; UNESCO 2004). Indo-Saracenic (Indian and Islamic motifs on European structures): Madras High Court and the Gateway of India (George Wittet). New Delhi was planned by Edwin Lutyens and Herbert Baker; Rashtrapati Bhavan is Lutyens's work.

  1. Feature-based questions: "Which feature belongs to the Dravida style?" — vimana, gopuram, enclosure wall, tank.
  2. Cave sites by religion: Ajanta (Buddhist only), Ellora (three faiths), Elephanta (Shaiva).
  3. Sculpture schools by material and influence — schist and Greek (Gandhara), red sandstone (Mathura), white marble (Amaravati).

Q1. The gopuram is a characteristic feature of which temple style?

✅ Answer: (c) Dravida

💡 Explanation: Large gateway towers called gopurams are typical of the Dravida (South Indian) style.

Q2. Which cave complex has Buddhist, Hindu and Jain caves?

✅ Answer: (a) Ellora

💡 Explanation: Ellora's 34 caves belong to all three faiths; Ajanta is entirely Buddhist.

Q3. Spotted red sandstone is associated with which school of sculpture?

✅ Answer: (d) Mathura

💡 Explanation: The Mathura school used locally available spotted red sandstone.

Q4. The Konark Sun Temple was built by:

✅ Answer: (b) Narasimhadeva I

💡 Explanation: Narasimhadeva I of the Eastern Ganga dynasty built it in the 13th century.

Q5. The double dome was introduced in India under the:

✅ Answer: (c) Lodis

💡 Explanation: The double dome appeared in Lodi-period tombs and was later perfected by the Mughals.

🔁 Revise in 60 Seconds

  • Stupa: anda, harmika, chhatra, medhi, torana; Sanchi (Ashoka). Barabar caves — Ajivikas.
  • Gandhara (schist, Greek), Mathura (red sandstone, indigenous), Amaravati (white marble).
  • Nagara — shikhara; Dravida — vimana + gopuram + wall; Vesara — Chalukya/Hoysala.
  • Qutub Minar (Aibak → Iltutmish); Alai Darwaza true arch; Lodi double dome; Humayun's Tomb charbagh.

📌 Prelims Traps to Avoid

  • ✘ Ajanta is only Buddhist; Ellora has three religions.
  • ✘ The Qutub Minar was completed by Iltutmish, not Aibak.
  • ✘ Humayun's Tomb was built by his widow, not by Akbar himself.

What is the difference between the Nagara and Dravida temple styles?

Nagara (North Indian) temples have a curvilinear shikhara over the sanctum and usually no boundary wall or large gateways. Dravida (South Indian) temples have a pyramidal vimana, are enclosed by a compound wall and have large gateway towers called gopurams.

Which is the first building in India with a true arch?

The Alai Darwaza at the Qutub complex in Delhi, built by Alauddin Khalji in 1311, is considered the first building in India with a true arch and dome built on scientific principles.
