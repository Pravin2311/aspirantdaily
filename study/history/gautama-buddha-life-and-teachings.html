Gautama Buddha: Life Events, Core Teachings, the Sangha, the Tipitaka and Mahayana vs Hinayana

Lumbini: birth • Bodh Gaya: enlightenment • Sarnath: first sermon • Kusinagara: nibbana • Tipitaka: Vinaya, Sutta, Abhidhamma

UPSC SSC Railway State PSC 📅 Last updated: 2026-11-01

NCERT reconstructs the Buddha's life from hagiographies, many written down at least a century after his time. The main events, in order:

  1. Birth: he was born Siddhartha, son of a chief of the Sakya clan, at Lumbini. He grew up sheltered in the palace.
  2. The four sights: on a trip into the city with his charioteer he saw an old man, a sick man and a corpse, and realised that the body's decay was inevitable. He then saw a homeless mendicant who seemed at peace with old age, disease and death.
  3. Renunciation: he left the palace to search for his own truth.
  4. Enlightenment: he tried bodily mortification until he was near death, gave it up, meditated for several days and attained enlightenment at Bodh Gaya. From then on he was the Buddha, the Enlightened One.
  5. Teaching: he gave his first sermon at Sarnath and taught dhamma, the path of righteous living, for the rest of his life.
  6. Nibbana: he attained nibbana at Kusinagara. Tradition records his last words as "Be lamps unto yourselves as all of you must work out your own liberation."

About 200 years after the Buddha, Asoka set up a pillar at Lumbini to record his visit there.

His teachings come mainly from stories in the Sutta Pitaka. Many of them show him persuading people by reason rather than by miracles. When a grieving mother asked him to bring her dead child back, he gently helped her accept that death is inevitable instead. He taught in the language ordinary people spoke.

  • Anicca: the world is transient and always changing.
  • Anatta: the world is soulless, because nothing in it is permanent or eternal.
  • Dukkha: sorrow is intrinsic to human existence.
  • The middle path: moderation between severe penance and self-indulgence lets people rise above these troubles.
  • God: in the earliest Buddhism, whether god existed was irrelevant.
  • Society: the social world was made by humans, not by divine order, so kings and gahapatis (householders) were told to be humane and ethical.
  • Nibbana: individual effort and right action end the cycle of rebirth. Nibbana literally means the extinguishing of the ego and desire.

Two values explain much of Buddhism's appeal: metta (fellow feeling) and karuna (compassion), especially towards the younger and weaker. Its stress on conduct rather than birth drew many people unsettled by the rapid social changes of the time.

The Buddha founded a sangha, an order of monks who also taught dhamma. They owned only essentials such as a bowl for their one daily meal from lay people. Because they lived on alms they were called bhikkhus.

  • Women: at first only men could join. Ananda, one of the Buddha's dearest disciples, persuaded him to admit women. The Buddha's foster mother Mahapajapati Gotami was the first bhikkhuni. Women who attained liberation were called theris, and their verses are collected in the Therigatha, part of the Sutta Pitaka.
  • Equality: members came from every group, from kings and wealthy gahapatis to workers, slaves and craftspeople. Inside the sangha they were all equal.
  • Decisions: the sangha followed the traditions of the ganas and sanghas. It tried for consensus through discussion and voted if that failed.

None of the Buddha's speeches was written down while he lived. After his death (c. fifth-fourth century BCE) his disciples compiled them at a council of elders at Vesali, the Pali name for Vaishali in Bihar. The result was the Tipitaka, the "three baskets":

As Buddhism reached Sri Lanka, chronicles such as the Dipavamsa and Mahavamsa recorded regional histories. The oldest texts are in Pali and later ones in Sanskrit. Chinese pilgrims such as Fa Xian and Xuan Zang travelled to India to collect texts and take them home.

Stupas were mounds over relics of the Buddha, such as his bodily remains or things he used. A stupa began as a semi-circular mound of earth, the anda. Above it sat the harmika, a balcony-like structure for the abode of the gods, and stone railings and gateways surrounded it. The Ashokavadana says Asoka shared out the relics and ordered stupas built over them. By the second century BCE there were stupas at Bharhut, Sanchi and Sarnath.

By the first century CE Buddhist ideas were changing. Early Buddhism stressed self-effort and saw the Buddha as a human who reached nibbana by his own efforts. The newer tradition saw him as a saviour and revered Bodhisattas, compassionate beings who used their merit to help others rather than leave the world. Image worship grew with it.

✅ Answer: (d) Sarnath

Q2. Which Pitaka contains the rules for those who joined the sangha? easy

✅ Answer: (a) Vinaya Pitaka

💡 Explanation: The Vinaya Pitaka holds monastic rules, the Sutta Pitaka the Buddha's teachings and the Abhidhamma Pitaka philosophical matters.

Q3. Who was the first woman ordained as a bhikkhuni? medium

✅ Answer: (b) Mahapajapati Gotami

💡 Explanation: Buddhist texts say Ananda persuaded the Buddha to admit women, and his foster mother Mahapajapati Gotami was ordained first.

Q4. In early Buddhist sculpture, the wheel stands for: medium

✅ Answer: (c) The first sermon

💡 Explanation: NCERT says the wheel stood for the first sermon at Sarnath, the empty seat for meditation and the stupa for the mahaparinibbana.

Q5. The term 'anatta' in Buddhist philosophy means the world is: hard

✅ Answer: (d) Soulless

💡 Explanation: NCERT explains anicca as transient, anatta as soulless because nothing in it is permanent, and dukkha as the sorrow intrinsic to existence.

Where was the first Buddhist council held?

NCERT says the Buddha's teachings were compiled after his death at a council of elders at Vesali, the Pali name for Vaishali in present-day Bihar. The compilation became the Tipitaka.

What is the difference between Mahayana and Hinayana?

Mahayana, the great vehicle, saw the Buddha as a saviour, revered compassionate Bodhisattas and worshipped images. Its followers called the older self-effort tradition Hinayana, whose followers called themselves theravadins.

What did the Buddha mean by the middle path?

NCERT describes it as the path of moderation between severe penance and self-indulgence. The Buddha had himself tried bodily mortification, nearly died and abandoned it before his enlightenment.
