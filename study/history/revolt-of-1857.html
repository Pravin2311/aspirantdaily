Revolt of 1857: Causes, How It Began at Meerut, Centres and Leaders, How It Was Suppressed, and the Changes of 1858

Rulers, peasants and sepoys • Greased cartridges • Mangal Pandey, 8 April • Meerut, 10 May • Bahadur Shah Zafar proclaimed • Kanpur, Lucknow, Jhansi, Bihar • Delhi retaken September 1857 • Crown rule from 1858

UPSC SSC Railway State PSC 📅 Last updated: 2026-11-04

NCERT traces the revolt to grievances that had built up over a century of Company rule. Different groups had different reasons.

  • Rulers: nawabs and rajas had lost power, armies and territory. Rani Lakshmibai of Jhansi asked the Company to recognise her adopted son as heir; Nana Saheb, adopted son of Peshwa Baji Rao II, asked for his father's pension. Both were refused. Awadh, under a subsidiary alliance since 1801, was annexed in 1856 for alleged misgovernment. The Company even planned to end the Mughal dynasty: in 1856 Governor-General Canning decided Bahadur Shah Zafar would be the last Mughal king.
  • Peasants: they resented high taxes and rigid collection, and many lost their land to moneylenders.
  • Sepoys: they were unhappy with pay and conditions. Sepoys who refused to go to Burma by sea in 1824 were punished, and an 1856 law made every new recruit agree to serve overseas. Many sepoys were peasants, so rural anger spread into the army.
  • Religion and custom: laws against sati and for widow remarriage, freedom for missionaries after 1830, and an 1850 law letting converts to Christianity inherit ancestral property made many feel their religion and way of life were under attack.

The spark was the new rifle cartridge, rumoured to be greased with the fat of cows and pigs, which would defile both Hindu and Muslim soldiers.

On 8 April 1857 a young soldier, Mangal Pandey, was hanged for attacking his officers at Barrackpore. Days later, sepoys at Meerut refused to drill with the new cartridges. On 9 May, 85 of them were dismissed and sentenced to ten years in jail.

On 10 May the other soldiers at Meerut marched to the jail, freed them, killed British officers, seized guns and set fire to British property. They rode through the night to Delhi, where the regiments also rose. The soldiers forced their way into the Red Fort and proclaimed the ageing emperor Bahadur Shah Zafar their leader. He wrote to chiefs across the country to form a confederacy against the British. That step turned a mutiny into a widespread revolt, because many rulers hoped to regain their lands under Mughal authority.

After about a week, regiment after regiment mutinied, and townspeople and villagers rallied around local leaders. The table lists the revolt of 1857 centres and leaders as NCERT gives them.

Awadh saw an especially wide popular uprising. A British officer, Lieutenant Colonel Tytler, wrote in August 1857 that every village was held against them and the zamindars had risen.

The Company brought troops from England, passed laws to convict rebels easily, and moved into the main centres.

  • September 1857: Delhi recaptured. Bahadur Shah Zafar was tried and sentenced to life imprisonment, sent to Rangoon with Begum Zinat Mahal in October 1858, and died there in November 1862.
  • March 1858: Lucknow taken.
  • June 1858: Rani Lakshmibai defeated and killed. Rani Avantibai, surrounded after an early victory, chose death.
  • April 1859: Tantia Tope, who had fought a guerrilla war in central India, captured, tried and killed.

The British offered rewards to loyal landholders and safety to rebels who submitted, but hundreds of sepoys, nawabs and rajas were hanged. Control was regained by the end of 1859.

  1. Crown rule: an Act of 1858 transferred the Company's powers to the British Crown. A Secretary of State for India, advised by the India Council, took charge, and the Governor-General became the Viceroy.
  2. No more annexation: rulers could pass kingdoms to heirs, including adopted sons, but had to accept the British Queen as Sovereign Paramount. This ended the Doctrine of Lapse.
  3. Army: fewer Indian and more European soldiers, and more recruits from Gurkhas, Sikhs and Pathans instead of Awadh, Bihar, central and south India.
  4. Muslims: their land and property were confiscated on a large scale, as the British blamed them heavily for the revolt.
  5. Customs: the British decided to respect customary religious and social practices.
  6. Landlords: policies protected zamindars and secured their rights over land.

For the land revenue grievances behind peasant anger, see Permanent Settlement, ryotwari and mahalwari. For the full sweep, see modern India and the freedom struggle.

Q1. Who led the revolt at Kanpur and proclaimed himself Peshwa? easy

✅ Answer: (c) Nana Saheb

💡 Explanation: Nana Saheb, the adopted son of Peshwa Baji Rao II, expelled the British garrison from Kanpur and proclaimed himself Peshwa under Bahadur Shah Zafar.

Q2. Begum Hazrat Mahal was associated with the revolt at: easy

✅ Answer: (a) Lucknow

💡 Explanation: In Lucknow her son Birjis Qadr was proclaimed Nawab, and Begum Hazrat Mahal took an active part in organising the uprising.

Q3. On which date did the soldiers at Meerut free the jailed sepoys and turn on their officers? medium

✅ Answer: (c) 10 May 1857

💡 Explanation: The 85 sepoys were jailed on 9 May; on 10 May the soldiers released them, killed British officers and rode to Delhi.

Q4. Which old zamindar led the revolt in Bihar? easy

✅ Answer: (b) Kunwar Singh

💡 Explanation: NCERT says Kunwar Singh, an old zamindar in Bihar, joined the rebel sepoys and fought the British for many months.

Q5. Which of these was a result of the Act of 1858? medium

✅ Answer: (d) The Governor-General was given the title of Viceroy

💡 Explanation: The 1858 Act moved power from the Company to the Crown, created a Secretary of State for India and made the Governor-General the Viceroy.

Where did the Revolt of 1857 begin?

Mangal Pandey's attack at Barrackpore came first, on 8 April 1857, but the uprising proper began at Meerut on 10 May 1857, when soldiers freed their jailed comrades and marched to Delhi.

Why was Bahadur Shah Zafar chosen as the leader?

The sepoys wanted the Mughal emperor to rule in place of the British. Many smaller rulers held land on behalf of the Mughals and hoped to regain their territories under Mughal authority, so his blessing turned a mutiny into a wide revolt.
