Ashoka's Edicts and Dhamma: Languages, Scripts, Decipherment, Key Edicts and Sites

Rock and pillar inscriptions • Prakrit, Aramaic, Greek • Brahmi and Kharosthi • Devanampiya Piyadassi • Dhamma and dhamma mahamatta • Kalinga edict • Five political centres

UPSC SSC State PSC 📅 Last updated: 2026-11-06

Asoka (c. 272/268-231 BCE), grandson of Chandragupta Maurya, was the first ruler to inscribe his messages to subjects and officials on stone, on natural rocks as well as polished pillars. He used these inscriptions to proclaim what he understood to be dhamma. NCERT calls them among the most valuable sources for the Mauryan Empire.

  • Most Asokan inscriptions were in Prakrit, a name for languages used by ordinary people. Those in the northwest of the subcontinent were in Aramaic and Greek.
  • Most Prakrit inscriptions were in the Brahmi script. Some in the northwest were in Kharosthi.
  • The Aramaic and Greek scripts were used for inscriptions in Afghanistan.
  • Most scripts used to write modern Indian languages are derived from Brahmi.

Exam trap: early scholars sometimes assumed the inscriptions were in Sanskrit. NCERT stresses that the earliest inscriptions were in fact in Prakrit.

How the scripts were read

In the 1830s James Prinsep deciphered Brahmi and Kharosthi. He found that most inscriptions named a king called Piyadassi, and a few called him Asoka, a ruler famous from Buddhist texts. Prinsep deciphered Asokan Brahmi in 1838, after decades of work by several epigraphists.

Kharosthi was read with the help of coins of Indo-Greek kings (c. second-first centuries BCE), which carry the kings' names in both Greek and Kharosthi. Scholars who could read Greek compared the letters, as in the name Apollodotus.

Alexander Cunningham published a set of Asokan inscriptions in 1877.

NCERT lists the principles of dhamma as Asoka proclaimed them:

  • respect towards elders;
  • generosity towards Brahmanas and those who renounced worldly life;
  • treating slaves and servants kindly; and
  • respect for religions and traditions other than one's own.

Asoka believed dhamma, being simple and virtually universally applicable, would ensure people's well-being in this world and the next. He also used it to hold his empire together. Special officers called dhamma mahamatta were appointed to spread the message.

NCERT's chapter on Buddhism invites readers to compare Asoka's advice to his subjects with the Buddha's advice to Sigala.

The orders of the king (on reporters)

Asoka says that in the past there were no arrangements for receiving regular reports. He orders that pativedakas (reporters) should report the affairs of the people to him at all times and anywhere, whether he is eating, in the bedroom or in the garden.

The anguish of the king (Kalinga)

Eight years into his reign, Asoka conquered Kalinga (present-day coastal Odisha). The edict says one hundred and fifty thousand people were deported, a hundred thousand killed and many more died. Afterwards, Devanampiya turned to an intense study and love of dhamma and expressed repentance for the conquest.

NCERT adds a twist: Asokan inscriptions have been found in present-day Odisha, but the one describing his anguish is missing there. In other words, it has not been found in the conquered region itself.

NCERT's map of Asokan inscriptions shows sites from Kandahar, Shahbazgarhi and Mansehra in the northwest to Brahmagiri and Siddapur in the south. Other sites on the map include Kalsi, Topra, Meerut, Bairat, Rummindei, Lauriya Nandangarh, Rampurwa, Sarnath, Sahasram, Sanchi, Girnar, Sopara, Jaugada, Shishupalgarh, Sannati and Maski.

Virtually the same message appears everywhere, from the North West Frontier Province of present-day Pakistan to Andhra Pradesh, Odisha and Uttarakhand.

Asoka and Buddhist sites

About 200 years after the Buddha, Asoka erected a pillar at Lumbini to mark his visit. According to the Buddhist text Ashokavadana, he distributed portions of the Buddha's relics to every important town and ordered stupas built over them.

NCERT places the inscriptions alongside other sources on the Mauryas:

  • archaeological finds, especially sculpture;
  • the account of Megasthenes, a Greek ambassador to the court of Chandragupta Maurya, which survives in fragments;
  • the Arthashastra, parts of which were probably composed by Kautilya or Chanakya; and
  • later Buddhist, Jaina and Puranic literature.

It also warns that inscriptions must be read critically. Most people were probably not literate, so passers-by may not have read orders carved on rocks near cities and routes. Historians also have to judge claims such as Asoka's statement that earlier rulers had no arrangements to receive reports.

Q1. Most of Asoka's inscriptions were written in which language? easy

✅ Answer: (a) Prakrit

💡 Explanation: NCERT says most Asokan inscriptions were in Prakrit; early scholars wrongly assumed Sanskrit.

Q2. Who deciphered Asokan Brahmi in 1838? easy

✅ Answer: (b) James Prinsep

💡 Explanation: Prinsep, an officer in the East India Company's mint, deciphered Asokan Brahmi in 1838.

Q3. Asokan inscriptions in Afghanistan used which scripts? medium

✅ Answer: (c) Aramaic and Greek

💡 Explanation: NCERT says the Aramaic and Greek scripts were used for inscriptions in Afghanistan.

Q4. The title Devanampiya means: easy

✅ Answer: (d) Beloved of the gods

💡 Explanation: Devanampiya is translated as beloved of the gods; Piyadassi means pleasant to behold.

Q5. Officers appointed to spread dhamma were called: medium

✅ Answer: (a) Dhamma mahamatta

💡 Explanation: NCERT says special officers known as dhamma mahamatta spread the message of dhamma.

Q6. Asoka conquered Kalinga after ruling for how many years? medium

✅ Answer: (b) Eight

💡 Explanation: The Kalinga edict says the conquest came when he had been ruling for eight years.

Q7. Kharosthi was deciphered with the help of: hard

✅ Answer: (c) Bilingual coins of Indo-Greek kings

💡 Explanation: Indo-Greek coins carried kings' names in Greek and Kharosthi, allowing letter-by-letter comparison.

Q8. Which was NOT one of the five Mauryan political centres named by NCERT? medium

✅ Answer: (d) Kanauj

💡 Explanation: The five were Pataliputra, Taxila, Ujjayini, Tosali and Suvarnagiri; Kanauj rose much later.

What were the main principles of Ashoka's dhamma?

NCERT lists respect towards elders, generosity towards Brahmanas and renouncers, kind treatment of slaves and servants, and respect for religions and traditions other than one's own.

Why is the Kalinga anguish edict puzzling?

Asokan inscriptions have been found in present-day Odisha, but the one describing his anguish over Kalinga is missing there, so it has not been found in the conquered region.
