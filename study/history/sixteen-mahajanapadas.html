16 Mahajanapadas: Capitals, Locations, Ganas and the Rise of Magadha

Capitals table • Kingdoms and ganas • Vajji sangha • Why Magadha rose • Rajagaha to Pataliputra • Timeline c. 600-321 BCE

UPSC SSC Railways State PSC 📅 Last updated: 2026-11-06

NCERT's Class 12 history text (Theme Two, Kings, Farmers and Towns) calls the sixth century BCE a major turning point. It is linked with early states, cities, the growing use of iron, coinage, and new systems of thought such as Buddhism and Jainism. Early Buddhist and Jaina texts mention sixteen states known as mahajanapadas.

The lists vary from text to text. NCERT says some names occur frequently: Vajji, Magadha, Koshala, Kuru, Panchala, Gandhara and Avanti. These were clearly among the most important.

The word itself: a janapada is the land where a jana (a people, clan or tribe) sets its foot or settles. The word is used in both Prakrit and Sanskrit.

The table follows the commonly cited list of sixteen. Capitals are those marked on NCERT's Map 1, "Early states and their capitals". A dash means the map shows no capital for that state.

NCERT's map also marks Vanga in the east. Ashmaka is the only one of the sixteen located south of the Vindhyas.

Most mahajanapadas were ruled by kings. Some, known as ganas or sanghas, were oligarchies. In these, power was shared by a number of men, often collectively called rajas. Both Mahavira and the Buddha belonged to such ganas. In the Vajji sangha the rajas probably controlled resources such as land collectively.

Each mahajanapada had a capital city, which was often fortified. Brahmanas began composing the Dharmasutras from about the sixth century BCE. These texts laid down norms for rulers, who were ideally expected to be Kshatriyas. Rulers were advised to collect taxes and tribute from cultivators, traders and artisans. Raids on neighbouring states were recognised as a legitimate way to acquire wealth.

Between the sixth and fourth centuries BCE, Magadha (in present-day Bihar) became the most powerful mahajanapada. NCERT gives two kinds of explanation:

  • Modern historians point to geography and resources: productive agriculture, accessible iron mines in present-day Jharkhand, elephants in the forests for the army, and the Ganga and its tributaries for cheap communication.
  • Early Buddhist and Jaina writers credited individuals: ambitious kings such as Bimbisara, Ajatasattu and Mahapadma Nanda, and their ministers.

Rajagaha, the Prakrit name for Rajgir, was the first capital. The name means "house of the king". It was a fortified settlement among hills. In the fourth century BCE the capital moved to Pataliputra (Patna), which commanded routes along the Ganga. Magadha's growth culminated in the Mauryan Empire, founded by Chandragupta Maurya around 321 BCE.

  • c. 600–500 BCE: paddy transplantation, urbanisation in the Ganga valley, the mahajanapadas, and punch-marked coins (silver and copper).
  • c. 500–400 BCE: the rulers of Magadha consolidate power.
  • Trap: Vaishali was the capital of the Vajji sangha, not Magadha. Kaushambi belongs to Vatsa, not Kashi.
  • Trap: the list of sixteen comes from Buddhist and Jaina texts, and the lists do not match exactly.

Q1. Which mahajanapada had Vaishali as its capital? easy

✅ Answer: (a) Vajji

💡 Explanation: NCERT's map places Vaishali in Vajji (Vrijji), the best-known gana or sangha.

Q2. Shravasti was the capital of: easy

✅ Answer: (b) Koshala

💡 Explanation: Shravasti is marked as the capital of Koshala; Kashi's was Varanasi and Vatsa's Kaushambi.

Q3. The first capital of Magadha was: easy

✅ Answer: (c) Rajagaha (Rajgir)

💡 Explanation: Rajagaha came first; the capital moved to Pataliputra in the fourth century BCE.

Q4. Ujjayini was the capital of which mahajanapada? easy

✅ Answer: (d) Avanti

💡 Explanation: Ujjayini, in Malwa, was the capital of Avanti and lay on long-distance land routes.

✅ Answer: (a) Oligarchies where power was shared

💡 Explanation: NCERT says ganas or sanghas were oligarchies where power was shared among men called rajas.

Which were the most important mahajanapadas?

NCERT says the lists vary, but Vajji, Magadha, Koshala, Kuru, Panchala, Gandhara and Avanti occur frequently, so these were clearly among the most important.

Why did Magadha become the most powerful mahajanapada?

Historians cite productive agriculture, iron mines, elephants and the Ganga for communication. Early Buddhist and Jaina writers credited ambitious kings such as Bimbisara and their ministers.
