Foreign Travellers to Medieval India: Al-Biruni, Ibn Battuta, Bernier and the Visitors to Vijayanagara

Al-Biruni and Mahmud of Ghazni • Ibn Battuta and Muhammad bin Tughlaq • Bernier at the Mughal court • Conti, Abdur Razzaq, Nikitin, Barbosa, Paes and Nuniz at Vijayanagara

UPSC SSC State PSC 📅 Last updated: 2026-11-06

NCERT's Class 12 history (Themes in Indian History, Part II) builds its chapter on travellers around three men: Al-Biruni (11th century), Ibn Battuta (14th century) and François Bernier (17th century). Its chapter on Vijayanagara adds the visitors to that city. The table below pairs each traveller with the ruler of his time.

  • Born in 973 in Khwarizm, an important centre of learning. He knew Syriac, Arabic, Persian, Hebrew and Sanskrit.
  • When Sultan Mahmud invaded Khwarizm in 1017, he took several scholars to his capital, Ghazni. Al-Biruni arrived as a hostage but stayed there for the rest of his life.
  • He spent years with Brahmana priests and scholars learning Sanskrit, and translated Patanjali's work on grammar into Arabic. He also translated Euclid into Sanskrit.
  • His Kitab-ul-Hind covers religion, festivals, astronomy, alchemy, social life, weights and measures, laws and metrology.
  • He accepted the Brahmanical account of caste but rejected the notion of pollution as contrary to the laws of nature.
  • Born in Tangier into a respectable, educated family. He valued travel experience over books.
  • Travelling overland through Central Asia, he reached Sind in 1333 and went to Delhi through Multan and Uch.
  • Muhammad bin Tughlaq appointed him qazi (judge) of Delhi. Later he fell out of favour and was imprisoned. After he was restored to service, he was sent in 1342 as the Sultan's envoy to the Mongol ruler of China.
  • He served as qazi in the Maldives for eighteen months.
  • He returned home in 1354, about 30 years after setting out. Ibn Juzayy wrote down the account he dictated.
  • He described unfamiliar things such as the coconut and paan for his readers.
  • A French doctor, political philosopher and historian. He was in India for twelve years, from 1656 to 1668.
  • He was physician to Prince Dara Shukoh, eldest son of Shah Jahan, and later worked with Danishmand Khan, an Armenian noble.
  • He constantly compared India with Europe, and in almost every instance described India's situation as bleak. NCERT notes that this assessment was not always accurate.
  • His works came out in France in 1670-71 and were translated into English, Dutch, German and Italian within five years.
  • Abdur Razzaq was a Persian ambassador sent to Calicut, not a Portuguese traveller.
  • Ibn Battuta wrote in Arabic (Rihla). Accounts by Bernier and other Europeans were printed in Europe, while Arabic and Persian accounts circulated as manuscripts until after 1800.
  • Barbosa, Paes and Nuniz were all Portuguese, and all came in the sixteenth century.

Q1. Ibn Battuta was appointed qazi of Delhi by: easy

✅ Answer: (a) Muhammad bin Tughlaq

💡 Explanation: NCERT says the Sultan, impressed by his scholarship, appointed him qazi or judge of Delhi.

Q2. Al-Biruni's Kitab-ul-Hind was written in: easy

✅ Answer: (b) Arabic

💡 Explanation: NCERT describes the Kitab-ul-Hind as written in Arabic, simple and lucid, in 80 chapters.

Q3. Al-Biruni came to Ghazni with which ruler? medium

✅ Answer: (c) Sultan Mahmud

💡 Explanation: When Sultan Mahmud invaded Khwarizm in 1017, he took Al-Biruni to Ghazni as a hostage.

Q4. François Bernier served as physician to: medium

✅ Answer: (d) Dara Shukoh

💡 Explanation: Bernier was physician to Prince Dara Shukoh, the eldest son of Emperor Shah Jahan.

Q5. Abdur Razzaq was an ambassador sent by the ruler of: medium

✅ Answer: (a) Persia

💡 Explanation: NCERT calls Abdur Razzaq an ambassador sent by the ruler of Persia to Calicut.

Q6. Which of these travellers was Russian? medium

✅ Answer: (b) Afanasii Nikitin

💡 Explanation: Nikitin was a Russian merchant; Conti was Italian, and Paes and Barbosa were Portuguese.

Q7. Ibn Battuta's birthplace was: easy

✅ Answer: (c) Tangier

💡 Explanation: NCERT says this Moroccan traveller was born in Tangier into a respectable, educated family.

Q8. Bernier stayed in India from: easy

✅ Answer: (d) 1656 to 1668

💡 Explanation: Bernier was in India for twelve years, from 1656 to 1668, close to the Mughal court.

Which travellers visited Vijayanagara?

NCERT names Nicolo de Conti of Italy, Abdur Razzaq of Persia and Afanasii Nikitin of Russia in the fifteenth century, and Barbosa, Paes and Nuniz of Portugal in the sixteenth.

Why was Ibn Battuta imprisoned?

NCERT says he fell out of favour with Muhammad bin Tughlaq after serving as qazi of Delhi. Once cleared, he was restored and sent as envoy to China in 1342.
