Battle of Plassey (1757) and Battle of Buxar (1764): Why They Were Fought, Who Won, and How They Led to the Diwani of 1765

Company trade in Bengal • Quarrels with the nawabs • Plassey 1757: Clive, Sirajuddaulah, Mir Jafar • Puppet nawabs • Buxar 1764: Mir Qasim • Diwani 1765 • Residents

UPSC SSC Railway State PSC 📅 Last updated: 2026-11-04

The English East India Company came to India to trade, not to rule. In 1600 it received a charter from Queen Elizabeth I giving it the sole right among English merchants to trade with the East. In Bengal it set up its first factory on the banks of the Hugli in 1651, began building a fort around the settlement by 1696, and soon got zamindari rights over three villages, one of which, Kalikata, grew into Calcutta.

The charter could not keep out other Europeans. The Portuguese were already based in Goa, after Vasco da Gama found the sea route to India in 1498, and the Dutch and French soon followed. All of them wanted the same cotton, silk and spices, so the companies fought one another and fortified their trading posts.

The Company also persuaded the Mughal emperor Aurangzeb to issue a farman letting it trade duty free. The trouble began when Company officials doing private trade on the side refused to pay duty too. Bengal lost a great deal of revenue, and the nawabs protested.

After Aurangzeb's death the Bengal nawabs, Murshid Quli Khan, then Alivardi Khan, then Sirajuddaulah, asserted their own power. They refused the Company new concessions, demanded large tributes, denied it the right to mint coins and stopped it from extending its forts. The Company, for its part, insisted that trade could flourish only if duties were removed and it was allowed to expand its settlements. This clash led to Plassey.

Sirajuddaulah became nawab in 1756. The Company, worried by his power, wanted a puppet ruler and tried unsuccessfully to help one of his rivals. The angry Nawab told it to stop meddling, stop fortifying and pay its revenues. When talks failed he marched with 30,000 soldiers to the English factory at Kassimbazar, seized it, and then took Calcutta.

Company officials in Madras sent forces under Robert Clive. After long negotiations, Clive led the Company's army against Sirajuddaulah at Plassey in 1757. A main reason for the Nawab's defeat was that Mir Jafar, one of his commanders, never fought: Clive had won him over with a promise to make him nawab.

Plassey became famous because it was the first major victory the Company won in India. The name itself is an anglicised form of Palashi, after the palash tree whose red flowers give gulal for Holi.

After Plassey, Sirajuddaulah was assassinated and Mir Jafar was made nawab. The Company still did not want to govern directly; it wanted rulers who would grant it privileges. But even puppet nawabs had to keep up an appearance of authority in front of their subjects.

When Mir Jafar protested, the Company deposed him and installed Mir Qasim. When Mir Qasim complained in turn, he was defeated at Buxar in 1764, driven out of Bengal, and Mir Jafar was put back. The Nawab now had to pay Rs. 500,000 a month, yet the Company wanted still more money and territory. By Mir Jafar's death in 1765, Clive had concluded: "We must indeed become nawabs ourselves."

On 12 August 1765 the Mughal emperor appointed the Company as the Diwan of Bengal. As Diwan, the Company became the chief financial administrator of the territory and could use Bengal's huge revenue. Before this it had bought Indian goods with gold and silver brought from Britain, because Britain had little to sell in India. That outflow slowed after Plassey and stopped completely after the Diwani. Bengal's revenue now paid for Company purchases, troops and forts.

After Buxar the Company also placed Residents in Indian states. They were political or commercial agents whose job was to further the Company's interests, and through them Company officials interfered in successions and appointments. NCERT notes that the treaties after Buxar forced Nawab Shujauddaulah of Awadh to give up much of his authority.

The Residents prepared the way for later methods of expansion such as the subsidiary alliance and the Doctrine of Lapse. The Diwani forced the Company to organise land revenue, which led to the Permanent Settlement, ryotwari and mahalwari systems. For the wider story, see modern India and the freedom struggle.

Q1. Whose forces never fought at Plassey, which NCERT gives as a main reason for Sirajuddaulah's defeat? easy

✅ Answer: (b) Mir Jafar

💡 Explanation: Mir Jafar, one of Sirajuddaulah's commanders, kept his forces out of the battle after Clive promised to make him nawab.

Q2. Which nawab of Bengal was defeated at the Battle of Buxar in 1764? easy

✅ Answer: (a) Mir Qasim

💡 Explanation: Mir Qasim, whom the Company had installed in place of Mir Jafar, was defeated at Buxar and driven out of Bengal.

Which battle was more important, Plassey or Buxar?

Plassey was the Company's first major victory and gave it a puppet nawab. Buxar was decisive in a different way: it removed Mir Qasim's resistance, led to the Diwani of 1765 and the posting of Residents. Many textbooks therefore treat Buxar as the battle that really established Company power.

What was the Diwani?

The right to collect revenue. As Diwan of Bengal from 1765, the Company became the chief financial administrator of the territory and could pay for its trade and troops from Bengal's revenue.
