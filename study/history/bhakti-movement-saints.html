Bhakti Movement Saints: Region, Language, Works and Ideas in One Table

Alvars (Vishnu) and Nayanars (Shiva): Tamil, c. 6th century • Basavanna: Virashaivas, Kannada vachanas • Kabir: Bijak • Guru Nanak: shabad • Mirabai: Krishna • Shankaradeva: Assam

UPSC SSC Railway State PSC 📅 Last updated: 2026-11-01

NCERT's Class 12 chapter on Bhakti-Sufi traditions traces devotional movements from Tamil Nadu in about the sixth century to north India from the fourteenth. Poet-saints led communities of devotees, often welcomed women and so-called "lower castes", and composed in the languages people spoke.

  • Alvars and Nayanars: the names mean those "immersed" in devotion to Vishnu, and leaders devoted to Shiva. Their members ranged from Brahmanas to artisans, cultivators and even castes considered "untouchable", so some historians see a protest against caste. The Chola kings (ninth to thirteenth centuries) supported them, and around 945 Parantaka I consecrated images of Appar, Sambandar and Sundarar in a Shiva temple.
  • Basavanna and the Lingayats: Basavanna was a Brahmana and a minister at a Kalachuri court. Lingayats worship Shiva as a linga, which men wear in a silver case. They bury their dead instead of cremating them, and they supported post-puberty marriage and widow remarriage, both disapproved by the Dharmashastras.
  • Kabir: he used Islamic, Vedantic and yogic terms for the Ultimate Reality. Vaishnava hagiographies say he was born a Hindu, raised by a family of Muslim weavers (julahas) and taught by Ramananda. Historians doubt the two were contemporaries.
  • Guru Nanak: he was born at Nankana Sahib near the Ravi, trained as an accountant and studied Persian. Mardana played the rabab while he sang. The fifth Guru, Guru Arjan, compiled the Adi Granth Sahib with hymns of Baba Farid, Ravidas and Kabir. The tenth, Guru Gobind Singh, added Guru Tegh Bahadur's hymns to form the Guru Granth Sahib and founded the Khalsa Panth.
  • Mirabai: a Rajput princess from Merta in Marwar, married against her wishes into the Sisodia clan of Mewar. She took Krishna as her lover and left the palace as a wandering saint. Her songs are still sung, especially by poor and "low caste" people in Gujarat and Rajasthan.
  • Shankaradeva: his teaching was called Bhagavati dharma because it rested on the Bhagavad Gita and the Bhagavata Purana. It stressed complete surrender to Vishnu.

Q1. The Nalayira Divyaprabandham, called the 'Tamil Veda', is an anthology of the: easy

✅ Answer: (c) Alvars

💡 Explanation: NCERT says this major anthology of the Alvars, devotees of Vishnu, was often described as the Tamil Veda.

Q2. Basavanna's followers, the Lingayats, expressed their ideas in vachanas composed in: easy

✅ Answer: (a) Kannada

💡 Explanation: The Virashaiva tradition is known from vachanas, or sayings, composed in Kannada by the women and men who joined the movement.

Q3. Which text is preserved by the Kabirpanth in Varanasi? medium

✅ Answer: (d) Kabir Bijak

💡 Explanation: The Kabir Bijak belongs to the Kabirpanth in Uttar Pradesh. The Kabir Granthavali belongs to the Dadupanth in Rajasthan.

Q4. Shankaradeva promoted Vaishnava bhakti in: easy

✅ Answer: (b) Assam

💡 Explanation: NCERT says Shankaradeva emerged in the late fifteenth century as a leading proponent of Vaishnavism in Assam.

Q5. Who compiled the Adi Granth Sahib? medium

✅ Answer: (d) Guru Arjan

💡 Explanation: The fifth Guru, Guru Arjan, compiled it. Guru Gobind Singh later added Guru Tegh Bahadur's hymns to form the Guru Granth Sahib.

Who were the Alvars and Nayanars?

Tamil poet-saints of some of the earliest bhakti movements, from about the sixth century. The Alvars were devoted to Vishnu and the Nayanars to Shiva, and both travelled singing hymns in Tamil.

Was Kabir a disciple of Ramananda?

Later Vaishnava hagiographies say so, but Kabir's verses name no specific guru. NCERT says historians find it very difficult to show the two were even contemporaries.

What did Guru Nanak teach?

A form of nirguna bhakti: God has no gender or form, and one connects to the Divine by remembering and repeating the Divine Name. He rejected sacrifices, ritual baths, image worship and austerities.
