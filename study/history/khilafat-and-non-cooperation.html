Khilafat and Non-Cooperation Movement: Causes, Programme, Spread and Why Chauri Chaura Ended It

War hardship • Rowlatt Act and Jallianwala Bagh, 1919 • Khilafat Committee, March 1919 • Calcutta, September 1920 • Nagpur, December 1920 • Launched January 1921 • Chauri Chaura, February 1922 • Swaraj Party

UPSC SSC Railway State PSC 📅 Last updated: 2026-11-04

NCERT traces the Non-Cooperation Movement to the hardship and anger left by the First World War and the events of 1919.

  • The war: defence spending was paid for with war loans and higher taxes; customs duties rose and income tax was introduced. Prices doubled between 1913 and 1918. Forced recruitment of soldiers angered the villages. Crops failed in 1918-19 and 1920-21, and an influenza epidemic followed. The 1921 census counted 12 to 13 million deaths from famine and the epidemic.
  • The Rowlatt Act, 1919: hurriedly passed despite the united opposition of the Indian members of the Imperial Legislative Council, it gave the government sweeping powers and allowed detention without trial for two years. Gandhi called for a nationwide satyagraha starting with a hartal on 6 April 1919.
  • Jallianwalla Bagh, 13 April 1919: after firing on a procession in Amritsar on 10 April, martial law was imposed under General Dyer. On Baisakhi day a crowd gathered in the enclosed ground; Dyer blocked the exits and opened fire, killing hundreds. Brutal repression followed, and Gandhi called off the Rowlatt satyagraha.

The Rowlatt satyagraha had stayed mostly in cities and towns. Gandhi now wanted a broad-based movement, and he was sure it needed Hindus and Muslims to act together.

The war ended with the defeat of Ottoman Turkey, and there were rumours of a harsh treaty on the Ottoman emperor, the Khalifa, spiritual head of the Islamic world. To defend his temporal powers, a Khilafat Committee was formed in Bombay in March 1919. Young Muslim leaders, the brothers Muhammad Ali and Shaukat Ali, discussed united mass action with Gandhi, who saw a chance to bring Muslims into a unified national movement.

NCERT's Class XII textbook lists the Khilafat demands: the Khalifa must keep control over the Muslim sacred places of the old Ottoman empire; the jazirat-ul-Arab (Arabia, Syria, Iraq, Palestine) must stay under Muslim sovereignty; and the Khalifa must keep enough territory to defend the Islamic faith.

At the Calcutta session in September 1920, Gandhi convinced other leaders to start non-cooperation in support of Khilafat as well as for swaraj.

The idea came from Gandhi's Hind Swaraj (1909): British rule was established and survived only through Indian cooperation. If Indians refused to cooperate, British rule would collapse within a year, and swaraj would come.

  1. Surrender of titles awarded by the government.
  2. Boycott of the civil services, army, police, courts and legislative councils, schools, and foreign goods.
  3. If the government used repression, a full civil disobedience campaign.

Many in the Congress were reluctant to boycott the council elections due in November 1920 and feared popular violence. After months of tussle, a compromise at the Nagpur session in December 1920 adopted the programme. The Non-Cooperation-Khilafat Movement began in January 1921.

In the towns the movement slowed down. Khadi was often dearer than mill cloth, and alternative Indian institutions were slow to come up, so students drifted back to government schools and lawyers to the courts. NCERT's Class XII book still judges that the Raj was shaken to its foundations for the first time since the Revolt of 1857.

In February 1922, a group of peasants attacked and torched a police station at Chauri Chaura in the United Provinces (Gorakhpur), and several constables died. Gandhi called off the movement. In NCERT's words, he felt the movement was turning violent in many places and satyagrahis needed proper training before they were ready for mass struggle. Gandhi was arrested in March 1922 and charged with sedition.

The ending divided the Congress. C. R. Das and Motilal Nehru formed the Swaraj Party to return to council politics, while younger leaders such as Jawaharlal Nehru and Subhas Chandra Bose pressed for radical mass agitation and full independence.

Summing up the non cooperation movement causes and end: wartime hardship, the Rowlatt Act, Jallianwalla Bagh and the Khilafat issue started it; violence at Chauri Chaura stopped it. The next mass campaign came in 1930: see Civil Disobedience and the Dandi March. For the earlier phase, see Moderates vs Extremists.

Q1. At which Congress session was the Non-Cooperation programme finally adopted? medium

✅ Answer: (b) Nagpur, December 1920

💡 Explanation: Gandhi won the leaders over at Calcutta in September 1920, but the programme was adopted as a compromise at the Nagpur session in December 1920.

Q2. In which province were the 1920 council elections not boycotted, because the Justice Party contested? medium

✅ Answer: (c) Madras

💡 Explanation: NCERT says the elections were boycotted in most provinces except Madras, where the non-Brahman Justice Party saw the council as a way to gain power.

Q3. Who led the peasant movement in Awadh that joined the Non-Cooperation wave? easy

✅ Answer: (d) Baba Ramchandra

💡 Explanation: Baba Ramchandra, a sanyasi who had been an indentured labourer in Fiji, led the Awadh peasants against talukdars and landlords.

Q4. What happened at Chauri Chaura in February 1922? easy

✅ Answer: (a) Peasants torched a police station and several constables died

💡 Explanation: A group of peasants attacked and set fire to a police station at Chauri Chaura in the United Provinces, and Gandhi called off the movement.

Q5. By how much did foreign cloth imports fall between 1921 and 1922? hard

✅ Answer: (b) They halved, from Rs 102 crore to Rs 57 crore

💡 Explanation: NCERT says the import of foreign cloth halved, its value dropping from Rs 102 crore to Rs 57 crore.

Why did Gandhi join non-cooperation with the Khilafat issue?

He felt no broad movement could succeed without bringing Hindus and Muslims closer. Taking up the Khalifa's cause was a way to bring Muslims under the umbrella of a unified national movement.

Why did Gandhi withdraw the Non-Cooperation Movement?

After the violence at Chauri Chaura in February 1922, he felt the movement was turning violent in many places and that satyagrahis needed proper training before they were ready for mass struggle.

What happened after the movement ended?

Some leaders, led by C. R. Das and Motilal Nehru, formed the Swaraj Party to contest the councils set up under the Government of India Act of 1919. Younger leaders such as Jawaharlal Nehru and Subhas Chandra Bose pressed for more radical agitation.
