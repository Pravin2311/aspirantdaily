Harappan Sites and Their Findings: Site, Location, Excavator and Key Find in One Table

Harappa: first discovered, 1921 • Mohenjodaro: Great Bath • Kalibangan: ploughed field • Lothal: carnelian, burnt-brick drains • Dholavira: reservoirs, signboard • Rakhigarhi: biggest city

UPSC SSC Railway State PSC 📅 Last updated: 2026-11-01

NCERT's Class 12 history book dates the Harappan or Indus Valley Civilisation from about 6000 BCE to 1300 BCE, in three phases:

PhaseDatesCharacter
Early Harappan6000-2600 BCEFormative phase
Mature Harappan2600-1900 BCEUrban phase, the most prosperous
Late Harappan1900-1300 BCEDecline

More than 2,000 Harappan sites have been found. Most lie between the Indus and Saraswati basins, and nearly two-thirds are in the Saraswati basin. NCERT identifies five major cities: Rakhigarhi, Mohenjodaro, Harappa, Dholavira and Ganweriwala. The other sites are regional centres, agricultural villages, ports and manufacturing centres.

The discovery came in stages. Cunningham was given a Harappan seal in the nineteenth century but did not realise how old it was. Daya Ram Sahni found seals at Harappa in older layers, Rakhal Das Banerji found similar seals at Mohenjodaro, and in 1924 John Marshall, Director-General of the ASI, announced a new civilisation in the Indus valley. After Partition the major known sites went to Pakistan, which pushed Indian archaeologists to search in Kutch, Punjab and Haryana. That search found Kalibangan, Lothal, Rakhigarhi and Dholavira.

  • Town plan: most settlements had a small, high western part (the Citadel) and a larger, lower eastern part (the Lower Town). At Dholavira and Lothal the whole settlement was walled instead.
  • Drains: roads and streets were laid out roughly on a grid, and every house was connected to street drains. Even small Lothal had burnt-brick drains.
  • Standard bricks and weights: bricks kept the same ratio from Jammu to Gujarat. Chert weights were binary in the lower values (1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32) and decimal in the higher ones (160, 200, 320, 640).
  • Crops and animals: wheat, barley, lentil, chickpea and sesame; millets from Gujarat sites; rice rare. Cattle, sheep, goat, buffalo and pig were domesticated.
  • Script: undeciphered and not alphabetical. Most inscriptions are short; the longest has about 26 signs.
  • End: by about 1800 BCE most Mature Harappan sites in regions such as Cholistan were abandoned, while new settlements grew in Gujarat, Haryana and western Uttar Pradesh.

Q1. Evidence of a ploughed field with furrows at right angles comes from: easy

✅ Answer: (b) Kalibangan

💡 Explanation: NCERT places the ploughed field at Kalibangan in Rajasthan, in Early Harappan levels. The two sets of furrows suggest two crops grown together.

Q2. Which site does NCERT call the biggest Harappan city? easy

✅ Answer: (d) Rakhigarhi

💡 Explanation: Rakhigarhi in Hisar district of Haryana covers about 550 hectares, which NCERT says makes it the biggest Harappan city.

Q3. Shortughai in Afghanistan lay near the best source of: medium

✅ Answer: (a) Lapis lazuli

💡 Explanation: NCERT says Shortughai was near the best source of lapis lazuli, a highly valued blue stone. It also has traces of canals.

Q4. S.R. Rao is associated with excavations at: medium

✅ Answer: (c) Lothal

💡 Explanation: NCERT's timeline lists S.R. Rao beginning excavations at Lothal. Dholavira was excavated by R.S. Bisht and Kalibangan by B.B. Lal and B.K. Thapar.

Q5. Which tiny settlement was almost wholly devoted to craft production such as bead-making? medium

✅ Answer: (b) Chanhudaro

💡 Explanation: Chanhudaro covered under 7 hectares and was almost exclusively a craft centre for beads, shell, metal, seals and weights.

Which was the first Harappan site to be discovered?

Harappa. Daya Ram Sahni found seals there in the early twentieth century, and the civilisation takes its name from the site, though brick robbers had already damaged much of it.

What are the five major Harappan cities?

NCERT names Rakhigarhi, Mohenjodaro, Harappa, Dholavira and Ganweriwala. The other sites are regional centres, agricultural villages, ports and manufacturing centres.

Where was the Great Bath found?

On the Citadel at Mohenjodaro. It was a rectangular tank made watertight with bricks set on edge and gypsum mortar, with steps leading down on the north and south.
