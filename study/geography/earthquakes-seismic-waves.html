Earthquakes and Seismic Waves: P Waves vs S Waves vs Surface Waves, Shadow Zones, Types of Earthquakes and How They Are Measured

P waves: fastest, through solids, liquids, gases • S waves: solids only • Surface waves: most destructive • Shadow zone 105°-145° • Richter: energy (0-10) • Mercalli: damage (1-12)

UPSC SSC Railway State PSC 📅 Last updated: 2026-11-01

The earth's radius is about 6,378 km, yet no one can go far below the surface. Gold mines in South Africa reach only 3–4 km, and the deepest drill, at Kola in the Arctic Ocean, has reached 12 km. Most of what we know is inferred.

  • Direct sources: surface and mine rocks, deep drilling projects such as the Deep Ocean Drilling Project and the Integrated Ocean Drilling Project, and magma from volcanoes.
  • Indirect sources: rising temperature, pressure and density with depth; meteors, which are made of material similar to the earth's; gravity, which is greater at the poles than at the equator, with its local differences called gravity anomalies; magnetic surveys; and seismic waves, which NCERT calls one of the most important sources.

Rocks on either side of a fault, a sharp break in the crust, try to move in opposite directions. Friction locks them, until the strain overcomes it and they slip suddenly. The energy released travels outward in all directions as waves.

  • Focus (hypocentre): the point inside the earth where the energy is released.
  • Epicentre: the point on the surface directly above the focus. It is the first place to feel the waves.
  • Where they happen: all natural earthquakes occur in the lithosphere, the top 200 km.
  • Seismograph: the instrument that records the waves.

The energy first travels as body waves through the earth. When body waves reach surface rocks, they create surface waves. All waves travel faster through denser material, and they reflect or refract where density changes.

Some places receive no waves from a given earthquake. These are its shadow zones, and every earthquake has its own.

  • Within 105° of the epicentre, both P and S waves are recorded.
  • Beyond 145°, P waves arrive but S waves do not.
  • The band from 105° to 145°, which is 40° wide, is the shadow zone for both wave types.
  • No S waves reach anywhere beyond 105°. So the S-wave shadow zone is much larger, a little over 40 per cent of the earth's surface.

The S-wave pattern is the key clue to the interior. S waves cannot pass through liquid, and the earth's outer core is liquid.

Effects: NCERT lists ground shaking, differential ground settlement, land and mud slides, soil liquefaction, ground lurching, avalanches, ground displacement, floods from dam and levee failures, fires, structural collapse, falling objects and tsunamis. The first six shape landforms; the rest are immediate threats to life and property. A quake lasts only seconds but can be devastating above magnitude 5.

Tsunami: it occurs only if the epicentre is under the ocean and the magnitude is high enough. A tsunami is the wave set off by the tremor, not an earthquake itself.

How often: quakes of magnitude 8 or more happen about once every 1–2 years, while tiny ones happen almost every minute.

LayerKey facts
CrustOutermost solid, brittle layer. Oceanic crust averages 5 km thick, continental about 30 km, and up to 70 km under the Himalaya.
MantleFrom the Moho discontinuity to 2,900 km. Its upper part, the asthenosphere ("astheno" means weak), extends to about 400 km and is the main source of magma. The lower mantle is solid.
LithosphereThe crust plus the uppermost mantle, 10–200 km thick
CoreThe core-mantle boundary is at 2,900 km. The outer core is liquid and the inner core solid, mostly nickel and iron, the "nife" layer.
  • Focus vs epicentre: the focus is the point inside the earth where energy is released. The epicentre is the surface point directly above it.
  • P vs S waves: P waves are faster, arrive first and pass through liquids. S waves are slower and pass only through solids.
  • Body vs surface waves: body waves travel through the earth. Surface waves form at the surface, arrive last and do the most damage.
  • Richter vs Mercalli: Richter measures the energy released (magnitude, 0–10). Mercalli measures the visible damage (intensity, 1–12).
  • Outer vs inner core: the outer core is liquid and the inner core is solid.

Q1. Which seismic waves cannot travel through liquids? easy

✅ Answer: (b) S waves

💡 Explanation: NCERT stresses that S waves can travel only through solid materials. P waves travel through solids, liquids and gases.

Q2. The point on the earth's surface directly above the focus is the: easy

✅ Answer: (c) Epicentre

💡 Explanation: The focus, or hypocentre, is where energy is released. The epicentre is the nearest surface point, directly above it, and feels the waves first.

Q3. The shadow zone for both P and S waves lies between: medium

✅ Answer: (d) 105° and 145° from the epicentre

💡 Explanation: Both waves arrive within 105°. Beyond 145° only P waves arrive. The band from 105° to 145° receives neither.

Q4. The Mercalli scale measures: easy

✅ Answer: (a) Intensity based on visible damage

💡 Explanation: The Mercalli intensity scale, from 1 to 12, is based on visible damage. The Richter scale measures magnitude, the energy released.

Q5. Tremors near large dams are called: easy

✅ Answer: (c) Reservoir-induced earthquakes

💡 Explanation: NCERT calls earthquakes in areas of large reservoirs reservoir-induced earthquakes.

What is the p waves and s waves difference in one line?

P waves are the fastest primary waves that push and pull material along their path and pass through solids, liquids and gases. S waves are slower secondary waves that shake material sideways and pass only through solids.

How do S waves tell us the outer core is liquid?

S waves cannot cross liquids. Beyond 105° from the epicentre no S waves arrive at all, which fits a liquid outer core that blocks them, and NCERT confirms the outer core is liquid.

Which earthquake waves cause the most damage?

Surface waves. They are generated when body waves reach the surface rocks, arrive last on the seismograph, displace rocks and cause buildings to collapse.
