Tiger Reserves in India: State-wise List of All 58 With Area, the First Nine of 1973, the Latest Additions and NTCA

The National Tiger Conservation Authority (NTCA) lists 58 tiger reserves in 18 States. Together they cover about 84,488 sq km: 46,701 sq km of core or critical tiger habitat plus buffer areas. NTCA's FAQ puts this at 2.56% of India's geographical area.

  • Began with: Project Tiger, launched in 1973 with nine reserves covering over 18,000 sq km.
  • Most reserves: Madhya Pradesh, with 9, followed by Maharashtra with 6.
  • Latest: Madhav (Madhya Pradesh), notified in 2025, the 58th.
  • Largest: Nagarjunsagar Srisailam (Andhra Pradesh), 3,296.31 sq km in total.

NTCA's list totals 46,701.29 sq km of core and 37,786.54 sq km of buffer, or 84,487.83 sq km in all.

NTCA's list marks nine reserves with the year 1973-74: Bandipur, Corbett, Kanha, Manas, Melghat, Palamau, Ranthambore, Similipal and Sundarban. The FAQ names the nine States of 1973 as Assam, Bihar, Karnataka, Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra, Odisha, Rajasthan, Uttar Pradesh and West Bengal.

The State names have since changed for two of them. Palamau, then in Bihar, is now in Jharkhand. Corbett, then in Uttar Pradesh, is now in Uttarakhand.

  • Largest by total area: Nagarjunsagar Srisailam, Andhra Pradesh (3,296.31 sq km), which also has the largest core (2,595.72 sq km).
  • Smallest by total area: Nameri, Assam (464.00 sq km).
  • Smallest core: Orang, Assam (79.28 sq km).
  • Only one reserve: Andhra Pradesh, Bihar, Jharkhand and Mizoram.
  • Project Tiger is a Centrally Sponsored Scheme of MoEFCC that funds tiger States for in-situ conservation in tiger reserves.
  • Funding: the Centre gives 60% for non-recurring items and 50% for recurring items. For North Eastern and Himalayan States it gives 90% for both.
  • NTCA is a statutory body constituted under section 38L of the Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972, as amended in 2006. The Environment Minister chairs it.
  • Powers: among others, NTCA approves the Tiger Conservation Plan prepared by each State Government.
  • Why tigers: the tiger is an umbrella species. Protecting it keeps prey, co-predators and the forest viable too.
  • Tiger numbers: the fifth all-India estimation, completed in 2022, put the population in a range of 3,167 to 3,925, up from 2,967 in 2018. NTCA's FAQ adds that about one-third of India's tigers live outside tiger reserves.
  • 58 reserves in 18 States. The total grows with each notification, so check the date of any figure you learn.
  • Most reserves: Madhya Pradesh (9), not Maharashtra (6) or Karnataka (5).
  • Largest reserve: Nagarjunsagar Srisailam, not Sundarban or Corbett.
  • Two Pench reserves exist, one in Madhya Pradesh and one in Maharashtra.
  • Palamau is in Jharkhand, though the 1973 list put it in Bihar.
