🌊 Rivers & Drainage System of India

Indus • Ganga • Brahmaputra • Peninsular Rivers • Panch Prayag • Lakes

High Yield Geography 📅 Last updated: Oct 2026
  • Indian rivers form two groups: the Himalayan rivers (perennial, fed by snow and rain) and the Peninsular rivers (mostly seasonal, rain-fed).
  • The Ganga (2,525 km) is the national river, declared in 2008. The Godavari is the longest peninsular river and is called the "Dakshin Ganga".
  • About 77% of drainage flows to the Bay of Bengal and about 23% to the Arabian Sea (NCERT).

The Indus System

  • Rises near Lake Mansarovar in Tibet and enters India in Ladakh.
  • Main tributaries (west to east): Jhelum (rises at Verinag), Chenab (the largest tributary), Ravi, Beas and Satluj (rises near Rakas Tal).
  • Indus Waters Treaty (1960), brokered by the World Bank: the eastern rivers (Ravi, Beas, Satluj) went to India, the western rivers (Indus, Jhelum, Chenab) mainly to Pakistan. India placed the treaty in abeyance on April 23, 2025 after the Pahalgam attack.

The Ganga System

  • The Bhagirathi (from Gaumukh, Gangotri glacier) and the Alaknanda meet at Devprayag to form the Ganga.
  • Panch Prayag on the Alaknanda, upstream to downstream: Vishnuprayag (Dhauliganga), Nandprayag (Nandakini), Karnaprayag (Pindar), Rudraprayag (Mandakini) and Devprayag (Bhagirathi).
  • Left-bank tributaries: Ramganga, Gomti, Ghaghara, Gandak, Kosi ("Sorrow of Bihar"). Right-bank: Yamuna (joins at Prayagraj; itself fed by the Chambal, Betwa and Ken) and Son.
  • The Damodar was called the "Sorrow of Bengal" before the Damodar Valley Corporation (1948).
  • In Bangladesh the Ganga is the Padma; it joins the Brahmaputra and then the Meghna before forming the Sundarbans delta with it.

The Brahmaputra System

Rises as the Tsangpo near Mansarovar, enters India as the Siang/Dihang in Arunachal Pradesh, and is joined by the Dibang and Lohit. It carries one of the largest silt loads in the world and forms Majuli, India's largest river island. In Bangladesh it is called the Jamuna.

Q1. The Bhagirathi and the Alaknanda meet at:

✅ Answer: (b) Devprayag

💡 Explanation: Their confluence at Devprayag forms the Ganga.

Q2. Which is the largest tributary of the Indus?

✅ Answer: (a) Chenab

💡 Explanation: The Chenab is the largest tributary of the Indus.

Q3. Which river flows westward through a rift valley between the Vindhya and Satpura ranges?

✅ Answer: (d) Narmada

💡 Explanation: The Narmada rises at Amarkantak and flows west into the Gulf of Khambhat.

Q4. India's only floating national park, Keibul Lamjao, is on which lake?

✅ Answer: (c) Loktak

💡 Explanation: Keibul Lamjao lies on the phumdis of Loktak Lake in Manipur.

Q5. The Godavari rises at:

✅ Answer: (a) Trimbakeshwar

💡 Explanation: The Godavari rises at Trimbakeshwar near Nashik, Maharashtra.

🔁 Revise in 60 Seconds

  • Indus tributaries W→E: Jhelum, Chenab, Ravi, Beas, Satluj.
  • Panch Prayag: Vishnu → Nand → Karna → Rudra → Dev.
  • East-flowing: Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna, Kaveri (deltas). West-flowing: Narmada, Tapi (estuaries).
  • Wular (freshwater), Chilika (brackish), Sambhar (salt), Vembanad (longest).

📌 Prelims Traps to Avoid

  • ✘ The Kosi is the "Sorrow of Bihar"; the Damodar was the "Sorrow of Bengal".
  • ✘ The Yamuna joins the Ganga at Prayagraj, not at Devprayag.
  • ✘ The Kaveri is fed by both the south-west and north-east monsoons.

What is the difference between Himalayan and Peninsular rivers?

Himalayan rivers are perennial, young and form large deltas; many are antecedent. Peninsular rivers are mostly seasonal, older, flow in fixed shallow valleys and, on the west coast, form estuaries.

Which is the longest river of peninsular India?

The Godavari, about 1,465 km long, which rises at Trimbakeshwar near Nashik and drains into the Bay of Bengal. It is also called the Dakshin Ganga.
