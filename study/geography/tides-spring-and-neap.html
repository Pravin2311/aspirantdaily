Tides Explained: Why There Are Two Bulges, Spring vs Neap Tides, Perigee and Perihelion, Tide Types and Uses

Spring tide: sun, moon, earth in a line (full and new moon) • Neap tide: sun and moon at right angles, about 7 days later • Perigee and perihelion: bigger range • Bay of Fundy: 15-16 m

UPSC SSC 📅 Last updated: 2026-11-01

Ocean water moves in two ways. Horizontal motion is ocean currents and waves. Vertical motion is the tides, the rise and fall of the sea. Upwelling of cold water from below, and sinking of surface water, are also vertical movements.

Waves carry energy, not water. Wind supplies the energy, and water particles only move in small circles as a wave passes. A wave slows as it nears the shore, because of friction with the sea floor. It breaks when the water is shallower than half its wavelength. Steep waves are young ones made by local wind; slow, steady swells may come from another hemisphere.

A tide is the regular rise and fall of sea level, once or twice a day, caused mainly by the attraction of the moon and the sun. Water movements caused by wind and air-pressure changes are surges, which are not regular.

  • The moon is the main cause. NCERT notes its attraction is more than twice as strong as the sun's.
  • The sun contributes to a lesser extent.
  • Centrifugal force counterbalances gravity.

Why two bulges? On the side of the earth facing the moon, the moon's pull is stronger than the centrifugal force, so water bulges towards the moon. On the opposite side the moon's pull is weaker, so centrifugal force wins and water bulges away from the moon. The tide-generating force is the difference between the two. Horizontal tide-generating forces matter more than vertical ones in raising the bulges.

Coastline effects: bulges are higher on wide continental shelves and lower at mid-ocean islands. Funnel-shaped bays and estuaries magnify tides. Tides channelled between islands or into bays are tidal currents.

So the cycle runs: spring tide at new moon, neap tide about a week later, spring tide again at full moon, and another neap tide a week after that.

Ebb and flood: the falling water between high and low tide is the ebb. The rising water between low and high tide is the flow or flood.

  • Semi-diurnal: the most common pattern, with two high and two low tides each day of about the same height.
  • Diurnal: one high and one low tide each day.
  • Mixed: tides of varying height, common on the west coast of North America and many Pacific islands.

The Bay of Fundy example: the world's highest tides occur in the Bay of Fundy, Nova Scotia, Canada, where the bulge is 15–16 m. With 4 tides in roughly 24 hours, each change takes about 24 ÷ 4 = 6 hours. So water rises about 1,440 cm ÷ 6 = 240 cm an hour, and NCERT warns that walkers on its beaches must watch the tide.

  • Prediction: the positions of the earth, moon and sun are known exactly, so tides can be forecast well ahead for navigators and fishermen.
  • Navigation: tidal height decides when ships can cross shallow bars at the mouths of harbours and estuaries.
  • Cleaning estuaries: tides desilt sediment and flush out polluted water.
  • Power: tides generate electricity in Canada, France, Russia and China. NCERT mentions a 3 MW tidal power project under way at Durgaduani in the Sunderbans, West Bengal.

Quick recall: the moon's pull is more than 2 times the sun's. Spring tides come with full and new moon. Neap tides come about a week later, when the sun and moon are at right angles.

  • Tide vs surge: tides are regular and caused by the moon and sun. Surges are irregular and caused by wind and air pressure.
  • Spring vs neap: spring tides come with sun, moon and earth in a line; neap tides with the sun and moon at right angles.
  • Perigee vs perihelion: perigee is the moon closest to the earth. Perihelion is the earth closest to the sun, around 3 January.
  • Ebb vs flood: the ebb is falling water; the flood, or flow, is rising water.
  • Diurnal vs semi-diurnal: one high tide a day vs two.

Q1. Spring tides occur when: easy

✅ Answer: (b) The sun, moon and earth are in a straight line

💡 Explanation: NCERT: when the sun, moon and earth are in a line, the tide is higher. These spring tides come twice a month, at full moon and new moon.

Q2. Neap tides occur roughly how many days after spring tides? easy

✅ Answer: (a) Seven

💡 Explanation: NCERT says there is normally a seven-day interval between spring tides and neap tides.

Q3. The second tidal bulge on the side of the earth away from the moon is caused mainly by: medium

✅ Answer: (c) Centrifugal force

💡 Explanation: On the far side the moon's pull is weaker, so centrifugal force dominates and creates a bulge away from the moon.

Q4. The highest tides in the world occur in the: easy

✅ Answer: (d) Bay of Fundy

💡 Explanation: NCERT names the Bay of Fundy in Nova Scotia, Canada, with a tidal bulge of 15-16 m.

Q5. The falling of water between high tide and low tide is called the: easy

✅ Answer: (c) Ebb

💡 Explanation: Falling water is the ebb; rising water between low and high tide is the flow or flood.

What is the difference between spring tide and neap tide?

Spring tides are the highest high tides, when the sun, moon and earth line up at full and new moon. Neap tides are weaker tides about a week later, when the sun and moon pull at right angles and partly cancel.

Why are there two high tides a day?

The moon pulls water into a bulge on the side facing it, while centrifugal force raises a second bulge on the opposite side. As the earth rotates, a coast passes through both bulges.

Are spring tides related to the spring season?

No. Spring tides happen twice every month, at full moon and new moon. The name refers to the water springing higher, not to the season.
