🏭 Agriculture, Minerals, Industries & Transport

Kharif, Rabi & Zaid • Crop Conditions • Minerals • Energy • Industries • Roads, Rail, Waterways & Ports

High Yield Geography 📅 Last updated: Oct 2026
  • Economic geography connects maps with the economy: where crops grow, minerals lie and industries cluster, and why. It is a regular source of "match the following" questions in UPSC, SSC and State PSC papers.
  • Indian agriculture follows the monsoon calendar through three cropping seasons — kharif, rabi and zaid.
  • Most of India's minerals lie in the old rocks of the Peninsular Plateau, especially the Chota Nagpur region — the "mineral heartland" spanning Jharkhand, Odisha and Chhattisgarh.

Oil and Energy

  • Digboi (Assam) had India's first oil discovery (1889) and is its oldest working refinery (1901). Mumbai High (offshore, discovered 1974) is the largest producing field. Other fields: Ankleshwar (Gujarat) and Barmer (Rajasthan).
  • Coal-based thermal power still produces most electricity. India's first nuclear plant was Tarapur (1969); Kudankulam (Tamil Nadu, built with Russia) is the largest.
  • Renewables: Bhadla Solar Park (Rajasthan) is one of the world's largest; the Khavda renewable energy park in Gujarat's Rann of Kutch is planned at about 30 GW.

Industries

  • Cotton textiles: the first successful mill opened in Bombay in 1854. Mumbai was called "Cottonopolis" thanks to black-soil cotton, a humid climate and the port.
  • Jute: the first mill opened at Rishra, near Kolkata, in 1855; the industry is concentrated along the Hooghly.
  • Iron and steel: TISCO was founded at Jamshedpur in 1907, close to iron ore and coal. Public-sector plants were built with foreign help:

Transport

  • Roads: the Golden Quadrilateral links Delhi, Mumbai, Chennai and Kolkata; the North–South corridor runs from Srinagar to Kanyakumari and the East–West corridor from Silchar to Porbandar.
  • Railways: the first passenger train ran from Bori Bunder (Bombay) to Thane, 34 km, on April 16, 1853. Dedicated freight corridors now run on the eastern (Ludhiana–Sonnagar) and western (Dadri–JNPT) routes.
  • Waterways: the National Waterways Act, 2016 declared 111 national waterways. NW-1 (Ganga, Haldia–Prayagraj) is the longest; NW-2 is the Brahmaputra (Dhubri–Sadiya); NW-3 is the West Coast Canal in Kerala.
  • Ports: India has 12 major ports under the Centre, and Vadhavan (Maharashtra) is being developed as the next. Deendayal (Kandla) is a tidal port in the Gulf of Kutch; Mumbai is the largest natural harbour; Jawaharlal Nehru Port is the busiest container port among major ports; Kolkata (Syama Prasad Mookerjee Port) is a riverine port. Among non-major ports, Mundra in Gujarat handles the most cargo.

Q1. Which of the following is a rabi crop?

✅ Answer: (c) Mustard

💡 Explanation: Mustard is sown in winter and harvested in spring; the others are kharif crops.

Q2. The Rourkela steel plant was built with the help of:

✅ Answer: (a) West Germany

💡 Explanation: Rourkela — West Germany; Bhilai and Bokaro — USSR; Durgapur — UK.

Q3. Jaduguda in Jharkhand is known for mining:

✅ Answer: (d) Uranium

💡 Explanation: Jaduguda has India's oldest uranium mine.

Q4. National Waterway 1 runs along the:

✅ Answer: (b) Ganga

💡 Explanation: NW-1 is the Ganga–Bhagirathi–Hooghly system from Haldia to Prayagraj.

Q5. Which state produces about 70% of India's coffee?

✅ Answer: (c) Karnataka

💡 Explanation: Karnataka, especially the Kodagu and Chikkamagaluru districts, dominates coffee output.

  • ✘ Most of India's coal is Gondwana coal; tertiary coal is found mainly in the north-east.

What is the difference between kharif, rabi and zaid crops?

Kharif crops such as rice and cotton are sown with the monsoon in June–July and harvested in autumn. Rabi crops such as wheat and mustard are sown in October–December and harvested in March–April. Zaid crops such as watermelon and cucumber grow in the short summer season between them.

Which steel plants in India were built with foreign collaboration?

Bhilai (Chhattisgarh) and Bokaro (Jharkhand) were built with Soviet help, Rourkela (Odisha) with West German help, and Durgapur (West Bengal) with British help.
