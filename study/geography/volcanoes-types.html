Types of Volcanoes: Shield, Composite, Caldera, Flood Basalt and Mid-Ocean Ridge, with Intrusive Landforms

Magma vs lava • Five NCERT volcano types • Deccan Traps • Batholith, laccolith, lapolith, phacolith, sill, dyke • Where volcanoes occur • Barren Island

UPSC SSC State PSC 📅 Last updated: 2026-11-01

NCERT defines a volcano as a place where gases, ashes and/or molten rock material (lava) escape to the ground. A volcano is called active if this material is being released, or has been released in the recent past.

The molten rock comes from below the crust. The mantle, which is denser than the crust, contains a weaker zone called the asthenosphere, and it is from here that molten rock finds its way up. The same material has two names depending on where it is:

  • Magma: molten material in the upper mantle.
  • Lava: the same material once it starts moving towards the crust or reaches the surface.

What reaches the ground is more than lava. NCERT lists lava flows, pyroclastic debris, volcanic bombs, ash and dust, and gases such as nitrogen compounds and sulphur compounds, with minor amounts of chlorine, hydrogen and argon.

NCERT classifies volcanoes on two bases at once: the nature of the eruption (gentle or explosive) and the form developed at the surface (a broad shield, a layered cone, a collapsed pit, a lava plain or a ridge). Five types follow.

Shield volcanoes are, apart from basalt flows, the largest volcanoes on earth, and the Hawaiian volcanoes are the most famous examples. They are made mostly of basalt, which is very fluid when it erupts, so lava spreads out and the volcano is not steep. They are usually of low explosivity, but become explosive if water gets into the vent. NCERT adds that lava rising as a fountain can throw out a cone at the top of the vent, which develops into a cinder cone.

Composite volcanoes erupt lava that is cooler and more viscous than basalt, and the eruptions are often explosive. Large amounts of pyroclastic material and ash come out with the lava and pile up near the vent in layers. Those alternating layers are what make the mountain "composite".

Calderas are the most explosive volcanoes. They erupt so violently that they collapse on themselves instead of building a tall structure, and the collapsed depression is the caldera. NCERT reads this explosiveness as a sign that the magma chamber is both huge and close to the surface.

The common thread is viscosity. Fluid basalt lets gas escape and flows away, giving gentle slopes and mild eruptions. Viscous lava traps gas, so pressure builds until it bursts, giving steep layered cones or, in the extreme, a collapse.

Flood basalt provinces pour out highly fluid lava that travels long distances. Some parts of the world are covered by thousands of square kilometres of thick basalt flows, sometimes a series of flows each more than 50 m thick, and individual flows may run for hundreds of kilometres. India's Deccan Traps, which now cover most of the Maharashtra plateau, are a much larger flood basalt province, and NCERT notes the traps probably once covered a much larger area.

Mid-ocean ridge volcanoes occur under the oceans. A system of mid-ocean ridges more than 70,000 km long runs through all the ocean basins, and its central portion erupts frequently. The next NCERT chapter calls the rift at the ridge crest the zone of intense volcanic activity, which is the starting point for sea-floor spreading.

Lava cools into igneous rock. If it cools at the surface it forms volcanic rock; if it cools inside the crust it forms plutonic rock, and the shapes it takes there are called intrusive forms.

A quick way to separate the look-alikes: lapolith is a plate or saucer (concave up), laccolith is a lens bulging up with a flat floor, phacolith follows the folds, a sill lies flat between layers, and a dyke stands upright and cuts across them.

For more on the rock types and landforms, see geomorphology, earth's interior and landforms.

NCERT's map of earthquakes and volcanoes shows them in a few belts. One runs through the central Atlantic, roughly parallel to the coasts, and continues into the Indian Ocean, following the mid-oceanic ridges. Another follows the Alpine-Himalayan system, and a third circles the rim of the Pacific Ocean.

The rim of the Pacific is called the rim of fire, better known as the Ring of Fire, because of the active volcanoes there. Earthquakes along the mid-oceanic ridges are shallow, while those along the Alpine-Himalayan belt and the Pacific rim are deep-seated, and volcanoes show a similar pattern.

In India, NCERT notes that while the Andaman and Nicobar Islands are thought to be an elevated part of submarine mountains, some smaller islands are volcanic in origin. Barren Island, the only active volcano in India, is in this group. See physiography of India for the island groups.

  • Deccan Traps = flood basalt, not shield. NCERT's own exercise asks this, with shield, flood, composite and caldera as options.
  • Largest vs most explosive: shield volcanoes are the largest (barring basalt flows); calderas are the most explosive.
  • Magma vs lava is about location, not composition.
  • Sill vs sheet is about thickness: thick is a sill, thin is a sheet.
  • Batholiths are granite and are the cooled magma chamber; they appear at the surface only after erosion removes the cover.

Q1. Which type of volcanic eruption formed the Deccan Traps? easy

✅ Answer: (b) Flood basalt

💡 Explanation: NCERT describes the Deccan Traps, covering most of the Maharashtra plateau, as a flood basalt province: highly fluid lava that spread over long distances.

Q2. Which volcanoes does NCERT call the most explosive on earth? easy

✅ Answer: (c) Calderas

💡 Explanation: Calderas are so explosive that they collapse on themselves instead of building a tall cone; the collapsed depression is the caldera.

Q3. Why are shield volcanoes not steep? medium

✅ Answer: (b) Their basalt lava is very fluid

💡 Explanation: Shield volcanoes are mostly basalt, which is very fluid when erupted, so it spreads out into gentle slopes.

Q4. A saucer-shaped intrusive body, concave to the sky, is called a: medium

✅ Answer: (c) Lapolith

💡 Explanation: A lapolith is saucer-shaped. A laccolith is a dome with a level base, a phacolith is a wavy mass in folds, and a batholith is a large deep granitic body.

Q5. Which intrusive form is most common in western Maharashtra? medium

✅ Answer: (b) Dykes

💡 Explanation: NCERT says dykes, wall-like intrusions almost perpendicular to the ground, are the commonest intrusive form there and fed the Deccan Traps eruptions.

What is the difference between magma and lava?

It is the same molten rock in different places. In the upper mantle it is called magma; once it moves towards the crust or reaches the surface, it is called lava.

What is the Ring of Fire?

The belt of active volcanoes around the Pacific Ocean. NCERT calls the rim of the Pacific the 'rim of fire' because of the active volcanoes there.

Which is the only active volcano in India?

Barren Island, in the Andaman and Nicobar Islands. NCERT calls it the only active volcano in India; some other smaller islands in the group are also volcanic in origin.
