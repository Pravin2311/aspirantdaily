Types of Rocks: Igneous, Sedimentary and Metamorphic Rocks, Examples and the Rock Cycle, From NCERT

Rock and mineral defined • Three types table • Extrusive vs intrusive • Fossils • Rock cycle • Uses and monuments • Exam traps

SSC Railways State PSC UPSC 📅 Last updated: 2026-11-07

NCERT's Class 7 geography chapter "Inside Our Earth" defines a rock as any natural mass of mineral matter that makes up the earth's crust. Rocks differ in colour, size and texture.

A mineral is a naturally occurring substance with certain physical properties and a definite chemical composition. Rocks are made up of different minerals.

NCERT names three major rock groups: igneous, sedimentary and metamorphic.

TypeWord originHow it formsExamples (NCERT)
Igneous (primary)Latin ignis, fireMolten magma cools and becomes solidBasalt (extrusive), granite (intrusive)
SedimentaryLatin sedimentum, settle downSediments are transported and deposited by wind or water, then compressed and hardened into layersSandstone, made from grains of sand
MetamorphicGreek metamorphose, change of formIgneous or sedimentary rocks change under great heat and pressureClay to slate, limestone to marble

NCERT also calls igneous rocks primary rocks, and divides them into two types.

The Class 11 chapter "Interior of the Earth" uses the same split. It classifies igneous rocks as volcanic rocks if they cool at the surface and plutonic rocks if they cool in the crust. Lava that cools inside the crust takes shapes called intrusive forms, such as batholiths, lacoliths, sills and dykes. Dykes are the most common intrusive form in western Maharashtra. See types of volcanoes and volcanic landforms for these forms.

Rocks roll down, crack and hit each other, breaking into small fragments called sediments. Wind and water carry and deposit these sediments. The loose layers are then compressed and hardened into rock.

Sedimentary rocks may contain fossils. NCERT defines fossils as the remains of dead plants and animals trapped in layers of rock. A standard exam question asks which rocks contain fossils, and the answer is sedimentary rocks.

NCERT calls the transformation of one rock type into another, in a cyclic manner, the rock cycle. It has four steps:

  1. Molten magma cools and solidifies into igneous rock.
  2. Igneous rocks break down into small particles, which are transported and deposited to form sedimentary rock.
  3. Igneous and sedimentary rocks, under heat and pressure, change into metamorphic rock.
  4. Metamorphic rocks still under great heat and pressure melt into molten magma, which can cool into igneous rock again.
  • Hard rocks are used to make roads, houses and buildings.
  • NCERT's monument examples are the Red Fort, built of red sandstone (sedimentary), and the Taj Mahal, built of white marble (metamorphic).
  • Minerals are used as fuels (coal, natural gas, petroleum) and in industry (iron, aluminium, gold, uranium). They are also used in medicine and in fertilisers.
  • "Primary rocks" means igneous rocks.
  • Basalt is extrusive and fine grained, while granite is intrusive and coarse grained. Slow cooling gives large grains.
  • Marble comes from limestone and slate comes from clay. Both are metamorphic.
  • Fossils are found in sedimentary rocks, not igneous rocks.

Q1. Rocks which contain fossils are: easy

✅ Answer: (a) Sedimentary rocks

💡 Explanation: NCERT says sedimentary rocks may contain fossils of plants, animals and micro-organisms that once lived on them.

Q2. Which rocks are also called primary rocks? easy

✅ Answer: (b) Igneous rocks

💡 Explanation: NCERT says rocks formed when molten magma cools are igneous rocks, also called primary rocks.

Q3. The Deccan plateau is made up mainly of: easy

✅ Answer: (c) Basalt

💡 Explanation: NCERT gives basalt as an extrusive igneous rock and says the Deccan plateau is made up of basalt.

Q4. Granite has large grains because it: medium

✅ Answer: (d) Cools slowly deep in the crust

💡 Explanation: NCERT says intrusive igneous rocks cool slowly inside the crust and so form large grains, as in granite.

Q5. Limestone changes into which metamorphic rock? easy

✅ Answer: (a) Marble

💡 Explanation: NCERT's example is that clay changes into slate and limestone into marble under heat and pressure.

Q6. Igneous rocks that cool within the crust are called in the Class 11 NCERT text: medium

✅ Answer: (b) Plutonic rocks

💡 Explanation: The Interior of the Earth chapter calls rocks cooling in the crust plutonic and those cooling at the surface volcanic.

Q7. The word 'sedimentary' comes from a Latin word meaning: medium

✅ Answer: (c) To settle down

💡 Explanation: NCERT's Word Origin box says sedimentary comes from the Latin sedimentum, meaning settle down.

Q8. The Taj Mahal is built of white marble, which is a: medium

✅ Answer: (d) Metamorphic rock

💡 Explanation: NCERT pairs the Taj Mahal with white marble, and marble forms from limestone under heat and pressure.

What are the three main kinds of rock?

Igneous rocks form when magma cools, sedimentary rocks form from compressed sediments, and metamorphic rocks form when igneous or sedimentary rocks change under heat and pressure.

What is the rock cycle?

It is the cyclic change of one rock type into another: magma forms igneous rock, which breaks into sediments, then metamorphic rock, which can melt back into magma.
