Forest Types of India by Rainfall: Evergreen, Deciduous, Thorn, Montane and Mangrove Forests with Regions and Trees

Evergreen: over 200 cm • Moist deciduous: 100-200 cm • Dry deciduous: 70-100 cm • Thorn: under 50 cm • Montane changes with altitude • Mangroves 4,992 sq km

UPSC SSC State PSC 📅 Last updated: 2026-11-02

NCERT groups India's forests into five types by their main vegetation and climate. Four are tied mainly to rainfall and temperature; montane forests change with altitude; and littoral and swamp forests depend on coasts and wetlands.

Key features: evergreen forests are layered, with trees up to 60 m and no fixed season of leaf fall, so they stay green all year. Deciduous (monsoon) forests are the most widespread in India; they shed all their leaves in the dry season. Thorn forests stay leafless most of the year, with tussocky grass up to 2 m tall as undergrowth.

South-facing Himalayan slopes carry thicker vegetation than the drier north-facing slopes because they get more rain. In the southern mountains (Western Ghats, Vindhyas, Nilgiris), which rise only about 1,500 m, the higher areas are temperate and the lower areas subtropical. The temperate forests of the Nilgiri, Anaimalai and Palani hills are called Sholas; magnolia, laurel, cinchona and wattle grow there, and similar forests are found in the Satpura and Maikal ranges.

India's wetlands cover 3.9 million hectares, about 70 per cent of it under paddy. NCERT names Chilika Lake (Odisha) and Keoladeo National Park (Bharatpur) as water-fowl habitats protected under the Ramsar Convention.

Mangroves grow in coastal salt marshes, tidal creeks, mud flats and estuaries. India has 4,992 sq km, about 7 per cent of the world's mangrove forests, best developed in the Andaman and Nicobar Islands and the Sunderbans, and significant in the Mahanadi, Godavari and Krishna deltas. Crisscrossed by creeks and tides, they shelter many birds but are being encroached upon.

  • Colonial impact: the British replaced oak forests in Garhwal and Kumaon with chir pine for railway lines, and cleared forests for tea, rubber and coffee.
  • Forest policy: adopted in 1952 and modified in 1988, it aims to bring 33 per cent of the geographical area under forest cover, conserve biodiversity, check soil erosion and floods, and involve people, especially women, in tree planting.
  • Social forestry (National Commission on Agriculture, 1976) has three parts: urban forestry (parks, green belts, roadside avenues), rural forestry (agro-forestry and community forestry on village commons, canal banks and school land) and farm forestry (farmers growing trees on their own land, often with free seedlings).
  • Wildlife Act, 1972: the main legal framework, amended in 1991 with stiffer penalties and protection for specified plants. NCERT gives 107 national parks and 573 wildlife sanctuaries.
  • Biosphere reserves: 18 in India under UNESCO's Man and Biosphere Programme, the first being Nilgiri (1986); 12 are on UNESCO's world network.
  • Project Tiger (1973): began with 9 reserves (16,339 sq km) and now has 58 reserves (84,487 sq km of core habitat in 18 states). Tigers rose from 1,411 in 2006 to 3,682 in 2023, more than 75 per cent of the world's tigers.
  • Project Elephant (1992) runs in 18 states; other efforts include crocodile breeding, Project Hangul and Himalayan musk deer conservation.

For in-situ and ex-situ conservation methods, see in-situ vs ex-situ conservation.

Q2. Tropical thorn forests grow where annual rainfall is: easy

✅ Answer: (d) Less than 50 cm

💡 Explanation: NCERT places thorn forests in areas with under 50 cm of rain, such as semi-arid Rajasthan, Gujarat and south-west Punjab, with babool, ber and khejri.

Q3. Shola forests are found in: easy

✅ Answer: (a) The Nilgiri, Anaimalai and Palani hills

💡 Explanation: NCERT calls the temperate forests of the Nilgiri, Anaimalai and Palani hills Sholas. They grow in the higher parts of the southern mountains.

Q4. Which forests are the most widespread in India? easy

✅ Answer: (c) Tropical deciduous

💡 Explanation: NCERT says tropical deciduous or monsoon forests are the most widespread, covering areas with 70-200 cm of rain.

How are India's forests classified by rainfall?

Tropical evergreen forests need over 200 cm, moist deciduous 100-200 cm, dry deciduous 70-100 cm and thorn forests under 50 cm. Montane forests depend on altitude, and littoral and swamp forests on coastal and wetland conditions.

Where are mangroves found in India?

Mangroves cover about 4,992 sq km, roughly 7 per cent of the world's. They are best developed in the Andaman and Nicobar Islands and the Sunderbans, with other important stands in the Mahanadi, Godavari and Krishna deltas.

Why are the British linked to pine forests in the Himalaya?

NCERT says the British replaced oak forests in Garhwal and Kumaon with chir pine, which was needed to lay railway lines, and cleared forests for tea, rubber and coffee plantations.
