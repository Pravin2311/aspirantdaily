Types of Rainfall: Convectional, Orographic (Relief) and Cyclonic (Frontal), with Indian Examples

Convectional: heated air rises, cumulus clouds, short heavy showers • Orographic: windward wet, leeward rain shadow • Cyclonic: fronts lift warm air • Western Ghats get 250-400 cm

SSC Railway State PSC 📅 Last updated: 2026-11-02

Rain falls when moist air rises, cools and condenses until the droplets grow too heavy for the air to hold. NCERT classifies rainfall by what makes the air rise, which gives three main types:

When the ground heats up, the air above it becomes light and rises in convection currents. As it rises it expands and loses heat, the moisture condenses, and tall cumulus clouds form. The result is heavy rain with thunder and lightning that does not last long.

NCERT says this rain is common in summer or in the hotter part of the day, and very common in equatorial regions and the interiors of continents, especially in the northern hemisphere.

In India: the hot-weather season brings violent local storms where dry and moist air meet. NCERT lists mango showers, pre-monsoon rain in Kerala and coastal Karnataka that helps mangoes ripen; blossom showers, which make coffee flowers bloom in Kerala; and Nor Westers, dreaded evening thunderstorms in Bengal and Assam, called Kalbaisakhi and in Assam Bardoisila, useful for tea, jute and rice.

When a saturated air mass meets a mountain, it is forced to climb. It expands, its temperature falls and the moisture condenses. The windward slope, facing the wind, gets heavy rain. Once over the crest, the air descends and warms, so it can hold more moisture and gives little rain. The dry leeward side is the rain-shadow area. This is also called relief rain.

  • Western Ghats: the Arabian Sea branch of the southwest monsoon climbs the Sahyadris. The windward side and the western coastal plain get 250-400 cm. East of the Ghats the descending, warmed winds give little rain: a rain-shadow area. NCERT asks students to compare Kozhikode and Mangalore with Pune and Bengaluru.
  • Khasi and Garo hills: a sub-branch of the Bay of Bengal branch strikes these hills. Mawsynram, on the crest of the Khasi hills, gets the highest average annual rainfall in the world. Cherrapunji and Mawsynram get over 1,080 cm a year, while Jaisalmer rarely gets 9 cm.
  • Tamil Nadu coast: stays dry in the southwest monsoon because it runs parallel to the Bay of Bengal branch and lies in the rain shadow of the Arabian Sea branch.

Worldwide, NCERT notes that wherever mountains run parallel to the coast, rain is greater on the coastal plain on the windward side and decreases towards the leeward side.

NCERT's rainfall section points to the chapter on weather systems for cyclonic rain. A front is the boundary where two different air masses meet. Fronts bring abrupt changes in temperature and force air to rise, forming clouds and precipitation. There are four types: cold, warm, stationary and occluded.

In a mid-latitude extra-tropical cyclone, a wedge of warm air sits between cold air ahead and behind. Ahead of the warm front, warm air glides up over the cold air, and a sequence of clouds brings precipitation. Along the cold front, the cold air pushes the warm air up from behind and cumulus clouds develop. The faster cold front eventually overtakes the warm front, lifting all the warm air (an occluded front), and the cyclone dies out.

  • Winter rain in north-west India: weak temperate cyclones from the Mediterranean, moistened over the Caspian Sea and Persian Gulf, bring winter rain to Punjab, Haryana, Delhi and western Uttar Pradesh, and snow to the lower Himalayas. The amount is small but highly beneficial for rabi crops.
  • Coromandel coast: in October and November, cyclonic depressions from the Andaman Sea cross the east coast. Most of the Coromandel coast's rain comes from them, and the Godavari, Krishna and Kaveri deltas are frequent targets.

Air holds more water vapour when warm. Relative humidity compares the moisture present with the air's full capacity; air at full capacity is saturated, and the temperature at which this happens is the dew point. Condensation needs cooling and tiny hygroscopic nuclei such as dust, smoke and sea salt.

Clouds: cirrus are highest (8,000-12,000 m), feathery and always white; cumulus look like cotton wool with flat bases (4,000-7,000 m); stratus are layered sheets; nimbus are dark, dense, low rain clouds.

World pattern: rainfall falls from the equator towards the poles and is higher on coasts and oceans than inland. Over 200 cm a year falls in the equatorial belt, on windward western coasts in the cool temperate zone and on monsoon coasts. Interior rain-shadow areas and high latitudes get under 50 cm. For the winds behind these patterns, see pressure belts and planetary winds.

Q1. Rainfall on the windward slopes of the Western Ghats is an example of: easy

✅ Answer: (c) Orographic rain

💡 Explanation: Moist monsoon winds are forced up the Ghats, cool and give heavy rain on the windward side: orographic or relief rain. The leeward side east of the Ghats is a rain-shadow area.

Q2. Which clouds are associated with convectional rain? easy

✅ Answer: (a) Cumulus

💡 Explanation: NCERT says rising heated air expands and cools, condensation takes place and cumulus clouds form, giving heavy but short rain with thunder and lightning.

Q3. Winter rainfall in Punjab and Haryana is mainly caused by: medium

✅ Answer: (d) Temperate cyclones from the Mediterranean

💡 Explanation: NCERT says weak temperate cyclones from the Mediterranean bring winter rain to Punjab, Haryana, Delhi and western Uttar Pradesh, which helps rabi crops.

Q4. Why is the Tamil Nadu coast dry during the southwest monsoon? medium

✅ Answer: (b) It lies parallel to the Bay of Bengal branch and in the rain shadow of the Arabian Sea branch

💡 Explanation: NCERT gives two reasons: the coast runs parallel to the Bay of Bengal branch, and it lies in the rain shadow of the Arabian Sea branch.

Q5. Which is the highest of the four basic cloud types? easy

✅ Answer: (d) Cirrus

💡 Explanation: Cirrus clouds form at 8,000-12,000 m, the highest of the four basic types, and are thin, feathery and always white. This is an NCERT exercise question.

What are the three types of rainfall?

Convectional rain, when heated air rises and cools; orographic or relief rain, when moist air is forced up a mountain; and cyclonic or frontal rain, when warm air is lifted along fronts in a cyclone.

What is a rain-shadow area?

The leeward side of a mountain. Air that has already rained on the windward slope descends, warms and can hold more moisture, so little rain falls. The area east of the Western Ghats is India's best-known example.

Which type of rainfall gives most of India's rain?

The southwest monsoon's heavy rains on the Western Ghats and the northeastern hills are orographic. NCERT does not give a national share for each type, so treat any exact percentage with care.
