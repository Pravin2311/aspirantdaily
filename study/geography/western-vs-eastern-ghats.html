Western Ghats vs Eastern Ghats: Continuity, Height, Peaks, Local Names and Rivers Compared

Western Ghats: continuous, about 1,500 m, Anaimudi 2,695 m, source of most Peninsular rivers • Eastern Ghats: discontinuous, low, cut by east-flowing rivers • Meet at the Nilgiris

UPSC SSC Railway State PSC 📅 Last updated: 2026-11-01

The Deccan plateau is framed by the Western Ghats on the west, the Eastern Ghats on the east, and the Satpura, Maikal range and Mahadeo hills on the north. The two Ghats meet at the Nilgiri hills.

  • Height and direction of slope: the Peninsular plateau is highest in the west and slopes east. The flow of its rivers confirms this. The Western Ghats are the raised western edge.
  • Erosion by rivers: because the big rivers rise in the west and flow east, they cut through the Eastern Ghats. That is why the Eastern Ghats are broken into separate hills, while the Western Ghats stay a continuous wall.
  • Water divide: the Western Ghats run close to the west coast. They separate the major rivers draining to the Bay of Bengal from the small rivulets that run down to the Arabian Sea.
  • Coasts: the western coastal plain is a submerged coast, narrow in the middle and broader to the north and south. Its rivers form no delta, but it has good natural harbours. The eastern coastal plain is an emergent coast. It is broader, with the deltas of the Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna and Kaveri, and has fewer ports.

The Peninsular plateau is an irregular triangle rising from about 150 m above the river plains to 600-900 m. Its outer limits are the Delhi ridge (an extension of the Aravalis) in the north-west, the Rajmahal hills in the east, the Gir range in the west and the Cardamom hills in the south. The Shillong and Karbi-Anglong plateaus in the north-east are extensions of it.

The Satpura range, a series of scarped plateaus at 600-900 m, forms the northernmost boundary of the Deccan plateau. NCERT calls it a classic example of relict mountains, highly denuded and discontinuous.

Q1. The highest peak of the Peninsular plateau is: easy

✅ Answer: (c) Anaimudi

💡 Explanation: NCERT says Anaimudi (2,695 m) on the Anaimalai hills of the Western Ghats is the highest, followed by Dodabetta (2,637 m) on the Nilgiris.

Q2. Where do the Eastern and Western Ghats meet? easy

✅ Answer: (a) Nilgiri hills

💡 Explanation: NCERT states that the Eastern and Western Ghats meet each other at the Nilgiri hills.

Q3. Which range is part of the Eastern Ghats? medium

✅ Answer: (d) Nallamala hills

💡 Explanation: NCERT lists the Javadi, Palconda, Nallamala and Mahendragiri ranges in the Eastern Ghats. The other three are names of parts of the Western Ghats.

Q4. Why are the Eastern Ghats discontinuous? medium

✅ Answer: (b) Rivers such as the Godavari and Krishna have eroded them

💡 Explanation: NCERT says the Eastern Ghats are low, discontinuous hills highly eroded by east-flowing rivers like the Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna and Kaveri.

Q5. Sahyadri is the local name of the Western Ghats in: easy

✅ Answer: (d) Maharashtra

💡 Explanation: NCERT says the Western Ghats are called the Sahyadri in Maharashtra, the Nilgiri hills in Karnataka and Tamil Nadu, and the Anaimalai and Cardamom hills in Kerala.

What is the main difference between the Western and Eastern Ghats?

The Western Ghats are higher (about 1,500 m on average) and continuous, and most Peninsular rivers rise in them. The Eastern Ghats are low, discontinuous hills broken up by the rivers that flow east.

What is the Western Ghats called in Maharashtra?

The Sahyadri. NCERT gives the local names as Sahyadri in Maharashtra, Nilgiri hills in Karnataka and Tamil Nadu, and Anaimalai and Cardamom hills in Kerala.

Which is the second highest peak of the Peninsula?

Dodabetta, at 2,637 m on the Nilgiri hills, after Anaimudi at 2,695 m on the Anaimalai hills. Both are in the Western Ghats, which rise in height from north to south.
