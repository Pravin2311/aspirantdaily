East-Flowing vs West-Flowing Peninsular Rivers: Source, Length, Tributaries and Why the Narmada and Tapi Form Estuaries

East: Mahanadi, Godavari (Dakshin Ganga), Krishna, Kaveri → deltas • West: Narmada, Tapi → rift valleys, estuaries • Peninsular drainage is older than Himalayan

UPSC SSC Railway State PSC 📅 Last updated: 2026-11-01

The Western Ghats run close to India's west coast and act as the main water divide of the Peninsula. Most major Peninsular rivers rise in or near them and flow east to the Bay of Bengal. Only small streams, plus the Narmada and the Tapi, which run through rift valleys, flow west to the Arabian Sea.

Nationally, about 77 per cent of India's drainage area, including the Ganga, Brahmaputra, Mahanadi and Krishna, drains to the Bay of Bengal. The other 23 per cent, including the Indus, Narmada, Tapi, Mahi and Periyar, drains to the Arabian Sea. The two are separated by the Delhi ridge, the Aravalis and the Sahyadris.

Peninsular drainage is older than Himalayan drainage. Its broad, shallow, graded valleys show maturity. NCERT names three events that shaped it:

  1. The western flank of the Peninsula subsided below the sea in the early Tertiary.
  2. When the Himalayas rose, the northern flank subsided and trough faults formed, which the Narmada and Tapi now occupy.
  3. The block tilted slightly from north-west to south-east, turning the drainage towards the Bay of Bengal.

Why no delta? The Narmada and Tapi flow through trough faults (rift valleys) and fill the cracks with their own debris, so NCERT says they lack alluvial and deltaic deposits. The narrow western coastal plain is also a submerged coast, and NCERT says the rivers crossing it form no delta.

NCERT also names drainage patterns. Rivers radiating from a hill form a radial pattern, as from the Amarkantak range. Parallel main tributaries joined at right angles by secondary ones form a trellis pattern.

Q1. Which river is called the Dakshin Ganga? easy

✅ Answer: (b) Godavari

💡 Explanation: NCERT says the Godavari is the largest Peninsular river system and is also called the Dakshin Ganga.

Q2. Why do the Narmada and Tapi form estuaries and not deltas? medium

✅ Answer: (b) They flow through trough faults (rift valleys)

💡 Explanation: They flow in trough faults filled with their own detritus, so NCERT says they lack alluvial and deltaic deposits. The Narmada forms a 27 km estuary.

Q3. Which Peninsular river carries water through the year with less fluctuation because it gets both monsoons? medium

✅ Answer: (a) Kaveri

💡 Explanation: The Kaveri's upper catchment gets the south-west monsoon and its lower part the north-east monsoon.

Q4. The Tapi originates at: medium

✅ Answer: (d) Multai

💡 Explanation: The Tapi rises at Multai in Betul district, Madhya Pradesh. The Narmada rises at Amarkantak, the Krishna near Mahabaleshwar and the Mahanadi near Sihawa.

Q5. Which is NOT a tributary of the Krishna? hard

✅ Answer: (c) Indravati

💡 Explanation: NCERT lists the Koyna, Tungabhadra and Bhima as the Krishna's major tributaries. The Indravati is a tributary of the Godavari.

Which are the west flowing rivers of India?

Among major Peninsular rivers, the Narmada and the Tapi flow west through rift valleys into the Arabian Sea. NCERT also lists the Mahi and Periyar among the Arabian Sea systems, along with short rivulets from the Western Ghats.

Why do most Peninsular rivers flow east?

The Western Ghats near the west coast form the water divide, and a slight north-west to south-east tilt of the Peninsular block turned the whole drainage towards the Bay of Bengal.

How are Peninsular rivers different from Himalayan rivers?

Peninsular rivers are older, with fixed courses, few meanders and seasonal flow from rain. Himalayan rivers are fed by snow and rain, so they are perennial, carve deep gorges and shift their courses on the plains.
