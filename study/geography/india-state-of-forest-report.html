India State of Forest Report (ISFR) 2023: Forest and Tree Cover, State Changes, Mangroves, Carbon Stock and Exam Facts

At a glance • What counts as forest cover • States that gained and lost • Mangroves • Fires • Growing stock, bamboo, agroforestry • Carbon and NDC • Ten-year change • Exam traps

The India State of Forest Report (ISFR) is a biennial report by the Forest Survey of India (FSI), which works under the Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change. The first report came out in 1987, and ISFR 2023 is the 18th in the series.

Measure (ISFR 2023)AreaShare of India's geographical area
Forest cover7,15,342.61 sq km21.76%
Tree cover1,12,014.34 sq km3.41%
Forest and tree cover8,27,356.95 sq km25.17%
Mangrove cover4,991.68 sq km0.15%

Compared with ISFR 2021, forest cover rose by 156.41 sq km, and forest and tree cover together rose by 1,445.81 sq km.

FSI defines forest cover as all lands of more than one hectare with a tree canopy density of 10% or more, whatever the ownership, legal status or land use. Such land need not be a recorded forest area, and the definition also includes orchards, bamboo and palm.

ClassCanopy density
Very Dense Forest (VDF)70% and above
Moderately Dense Forest (MDF)40% to below 70%
Open Forest (OF)10% to below 40%

Forest cover was mapped from ISRO's LISS-III sensor on the IRS Resourcesat series, at a spatial resolution of 23.5 m and a scale of 1:50,000. The images were mainly from October to December 2021.

The second input is the National Forest Inventory, started in 2002. Since 2016 it has used a grid of 5 km x 5 km cells, with about 6,000 plots in forest areas and 10,000 plots in trees outside forests every year.

Largest increaseChhattisgarh (683.62), Uttar Pradesh (559.19), Odisha (558.57), Rajasthan (394.46)
Largest decreaseMadhya Pradesh (612.41), Karnataka (459.36), Ladakh (159.26), Nagaland (125.22)
  • Tree cover: Maharashtra has the most (14,524.88 sq km), followed by Rajasthan and Uttar Pradesh. Twenty-one States and UTs showed rising tree cover, which FSI reads as agroforestry being promoted.
  • North East: forest and tree cover is 1,74,394.70 sq km, or 67% of the region's area, but forest cover fell by 327.30 sq km.
  • Hill districts: forest cover is 2,83,713.20 sq km, 40% of their area, and rose by 234.14 sq km.
  • Trees outside forests: 30.70 million hectares, which is 37.11% of all forest and tree cover.
  • Total mangrove cover is 4,991.68 sq km, a net decrease of 7.43 sq km from 2021.
  • Split by density: very dense 29.33%, moderately dense 30.07%, open 40.60%.
  • Gujarat showed a notable decrease of 36.39 sq km. The main increases were in Andhra Pradesh (13.01 sq km) and Maharashtra (12.39 sq km).

The SNPP-VIIRS satellite sensor detected 2,03,544 fire hotspots in the 2023-24 fire season. That was fewer than the 2,23,333 of 2021-22 and the 2,12,249 of 2022-23. The three States with the most fire incidents in 2023-24 were Uttarakhand, Odisha and Chhattisgarh.

  • Growing stock of wood: 6,429.64 million cubic metres in all, of which 4,478.89 is inside forests and 1,950.75 outside recorded forests. It rose 4.25% over ISFR 2021.
  • Most growing stock in forests: Arunachal Pradesh (457.83 million cubic metres), then Uttarakhand.
  • Top species by volume inside forests: Shorea robusta (sal), 11.43%, then Tectona grandis (teak). Outside forests the mango, Mangifera indica, leads with 13.25%.
  • Bamboo bearing area: 1,54,670 sq km, up 5,227 sq km. Madhya Pradesh has the most (20,421 sq km), followed by Arunachal Pradesh.
  • Agroforestry: tree green cover of 1,27,590.05 sq km, up 20.02% over ISFR 2013.
  • Forest carbon stock is 7,285.5 million tonnes, up 81.5 million tonnes on the previous assessment.
  • Soil organic carbon is the largest pool at 55.06%, followed by above-ground biomass at 32.69%.
  • Arunachal Pradesh has the largest carbon stock (1,021 million tonnes). Per hectare, Jammu & Kashmir leads with 174.10 tonnes.
  • For India's NDC target, the report puts the carbon sink at 30.43 billion tonnes of CO₂ equivalent, which is 2.29 billion tonnes more than in the base year of 2005.

FSI calls the decade's changes positive and encouraging:

  • forest cover up 16,630.25 sq km, almost all of it (16,189.78 sq km) outside recorded forest areas;
  • tree cover up 20,747.34 sq km;
  • mangrove cover up 296.33 sq km.
  • ISFR is published every two years by the Forest Survey of India, not by the Ministry directly. ISFR 2023 is the 18th report.
  • 25.17% is forest and tree cover. Forest cover alone is 21.76%.
  • Forest cover needs more than 1 hectare and at least 10% canopy density. It includes orchards, bamboo and palm, and need not be recorded forest.
  • Mangrove cover fell slightly (by 7.43 sq km) since 2021, though it rose over the decade.
  • Most bamboo area: Madhya Pradesh. Most carbon stock: Arunachal Pradesh. Most tree cover: Maharashtra.

Q1. As per ISFR 2023, India's total forest and tree cover is what share of its geographical area? easy

✅ Answer: (a) 25.17%

💡 Explanation: FSI puts forest and tree cover at 8,27,356.95 sq km, 25.17% of the geographical area; forest cover alone is 21.76%.

Q2. The India State of Forest Report is published by: easy

✅ Answer: (b) Forest Survey of India

💡 Explanation: ISFR is a biennial publication of the Forest Survey of India, under MoEFCC.

Q3. Which State showed the largest increase in forest and tree cover in ISFR 2023 compared with 2021? medium

✅ Answer: (c) Chhattisgarh

💡 Explanation: Chhattisgarh gained 683.62 sq km, followed by Uttar Pradesh, Odisha and Rajasthan; Madhya Pradesh showed the largest decrease.

Q4. Under FSI's classification, a 'Moderately Dense Forest' has a canopy density of: medium

✅ Answer: (d) 40% to below 70%

💡 Explanation: VDF is 70% and above, MDF 40% to below 70%, and Open Forest 10% to below 40%.

Q5. According to ISFR 2023, how did India's mangrove cover change compared with 2021? hard

✅ Answer: (a) It fell by 7.43 sq km

💡 Explanation: The 2023 report shows a net decrease of 7.43 sq km since 2021; the 296.33 sq km rise is the 2013-2023 decadal change.

Q6. Which State has the maximum bamboo bearing area as per ISFR 2023? medium

✅ Answer: (b) Madhya Pradesh

💡 Explanation: Madhya Pradesh has 20,421 sq km of bamboo bearing area, followed by Arunachal Pradesh.

Q7. The largest pool of forest carbon in India, per ISFR 2023, is: medium

✅ Answer: (c) Soil organic carbon

💡 Explanation: Soil organic carbon accounts for 55.06%, followed by above-ground biomass at 32.69%.

Q8. Which satellite sensor did FSI use for forest cover mapping in ISFR 2023? hard

✅ Answer: (d) LISS-III on IRS Resourcesat

💡 Explanation: FSI used ISRO's indigenous LISS-III sensor (23.5 m resolution) on the IRS Resourcesat series at 1:50,000 scale.
