El Niño and La Niña: ENSO, the Southern Oscillation and the Indian Monsoon

Warm vs cold phase of ENSO • NOAA's 0.5 °C threshold • Southern Oscillation Index • NCERT on El Niño and the monsoon • The 2026 El Niño Advisory

UPSC SSC State PSC 📅 Last updated: 2026-11-06

El Niño is the periodic warming of sea-surface temperatures across the central and east-central equatorial Pacific. La Niña is the periodic cooling of the same waters. They are the warm and cold phases of one ocean-atmosphere cycle, the El Niño/Southern Oscillation (ENSO). Because they shift tropical rainfall patterns, they affect weather far away, including the Indian monsoon.

Where things stand: NOAA's Climate Prediction Center (CPC) kept an El Niño Advisory in force on 8 October 2026. It said a strong-to-very strong El Niño is likely through January-March 2027, with a greater than 83% chance.

Periods with neither event are called ENSO-neutral. Ocean temperatures, tropical rainfall and winds over the equatorial Pacific are then near their long-term average.

NOAA describes both as naturally occurring phenomena that result from interactions between the ocean surface and the atmosphere over the tropical Pacific. The process feeds on itself:

  1. Changes in ocean surface temperature alter tropical rainfall and atmospheric winds over the Pacific.
  2. Those winds in turn change ocean temperatures and currents.
  3. The resulting shift in tropical rainfall, which stretches from Indonesia to the west coast of South America (about half the way around the world), alters weather patterns across the globe.

This is why a warm patch of the Pacific Ocean, thousands of kilometres away, matters to farmers and policymakers in India.

NOAA's CPC declares the onset of an El Niño episode when the 3-month average sea-surface temperature departure exceeds 0.5 °C in the east-central equatorial Pacific, between 5°N and 5°S and 170°W to 120°W. This box is the Niño-3.4 region.

In a typical El Niño, sea-surface temperatures there run 1.5 °C to 2.5 °C above average. The ocean thermocline is also deeper than usual, typically 150 to 175 m.

The Southern Oscillation

The ocean changes come with even larger swings in air pressure between the western and eastern tropical Pacific, called the Southern Oscillation. The Southern Oscillation Index (SOI) uses departures from normal in the surface pressure difference between Tahiti (French Polynesia) and Darwin (Australia). These two stations are used because their records are long. NCERT also notes that the strength of the southern oscillation can be measured from the Tahiti-Darwin pressure difference.

NCERT's Class 11 chapter on India's climate describes El Niño as a complex weather system that appears once every three to seven years, bringing drought, floods and other weather extremes to different parts of the world. It notes three results of the warm current off Peru:

  1. distortion of equatorial atmospheric circulation;
  2. irregularities in the evaporation of sea water; and
  3. fewer plankton, and so fewer fish in the sea.

The name means "Child Christ", because the current appears around Christmas, which is summer in Peru. NCERT adds that El Niño is used in India for forecasting long-range monsoon rainfall. In 1990-91, an El Niño event delayed the onset of the southwest monsoon by five to twelve days over most of the country.

NCERT also states that the IMD can forecast monsoon behaviour using 16 indicators.

  • Strength: by 8 October 2026, sea-surface temperature anomalies exceeded +4.0 °C in parts of the eastern equatorial Pacific. Niño-3.4 reached +2.1 °C, Niño-3 +3.0 °C and Niño-1+2 +3.9 °C.
  • Atmosphere: rainfall stayed suppressed over Indonesia, and the Southern Oscillation indices were significantly negative.
  • Historic event? CPC gave a 54% chance for September-November, 83% for October-December and 70% for November-January of exceeding every El Niño since 1950, defined as +2.5 °C or more on the 3-month RONI.
  • Next update: CPC's next ENSO discussion is scheduled for 12 November 2026.

Effect in India

RBI's Monetary Policy Committee, in its 7 October 2026 resolution, said a deficient south-west monsoon and strong El Niño conditions pose risks to the agriculture sector's outlook and rural demand. It also listed the deficient monsoon and ongoing El Niño conditions among the supply-side pressures on inflation.

  • El Niño = warm phase of ENSO; La Niña = cold phase; ENSO-neutral = neither.
  • NOAA threshold: 3-month SST anomaly above +0.5 °C in the Niño-3.4 region.
  • SOI = Tahiti minus Darwin pressure: strongly negative in El Niño, positive in La Niña.
  • NCERT: El Niño recurs every three to seven years and is used in long-range monsoon forecasts.
  • 2026: strong-to-very strong El Niño, with an El Niño Advisory in force.

Q1. El Niño is the ___ phase of the ENSO cycle. easy

✅ Answer: (a) Warm

💡 Explanation: NOAA calls El Niño the warm phase of ENSO, or a Pacific warm episode.

Q2. NOAA declares the onset of El Niño when the 3-month SST anomaly in the east-central Pacific exceeds: medium

✅ Answer: (b) +0.5 °C

💡 Explanation: The CPC rule is a 3-month average sea-surface temperature departure above 0.5 °C.

Q3. The Southern Oscillation Index is based on the pressure difference between: easy

✅ Answer: (c) Tahiti and Darwin

💡 Explanation: Both NOAA and NCERT use the Tahiti-Darwin surface pressure difference for the Southern Oscillation.

Q4. During El Niño, the Southern Oscillation Index is usually: medium

✅ Answer: (d) Strongly negative

💡 Explanation: Lower pressure at Tahiti and higher pressure at Darwin give a large negative SOI.

Q5. According to NCERT, El Niño appears roughly once every: easy

✅ Answer: (a) Three to seven years

💡 Explanation: NCERT describes El Niño as a system that appears once every three to seven years.

Q6. The name El Niño means: easy

💡 Explanation: NCERT says it means Child Christ, since the current appears around Christmas in December.

Q7. During El Niño, rainfall over Indonesia is typically: medium

💡 Explanation: NOAA lists suppressed convective rainfall over Indonesia as a typical El Niño condition.

How does El Niño affect the Indian monsoon?

El Niño shifts tropical rainfall patterns and is used in India to forecast long-range monsoon rainfall. In 2026 RBI linked a deficient monsoon and strong El Niño to risks for farm output.

What is the difference between El Niño and La Niña?

El Niño is the warming of the central and east-central equatorial Pacific, the warm phase of ENSO. La Niña is the cooling of the same waters, the cold phase, with largely opposite effects.

Is there an El Niño in 2026?

Yes. NOAA's discussion of 8 October 2026 kept an El Niño Advisory and said a strong-to-very strong El Niño is likely through January-March 2027.
