How Cyclones Are Named in the North Indian Ocean: WMO/ESCAP Panel, RSMC New Delhi, the 2020 Name List and the Rules

Why names • How naming began • Five regional bodies • North Indian Ocean timeline • 13 member countries • India's names • Selection rules • IMD categories • Exam traps

UPSC State PSC SSC 📅 Last updated: 2026-11-10

Tropical cyclones over the Bay of Bengal and the Arabian Sea get their names from a list agreed by the WMO/ESCAP Panel on Tropical Cyclones (PTC). The India Meteorological Department (IMD), working as the Regional Specialised Meteorological Centre (RSMC) for tropical cyclones, New Delhi, picks the next name from that list and assigns it.

  • Who names: RSMC New Delhi, for both the Bay of Bengal and the Arabian Sea.
  • When: once a system is analysed with maximum wind speeds of 34 knots or more, which is cyclonic storm strength.
  • Current list: adopted in April 2020, with 13 names from each of 13 member countries.
  • No repeats: a name used once is never used again.

IMD's naming document gives the purpose plainly: names are easier to remember than numbers and technical terms. Its list of benefits includes:

  • it helps identify each individual cyclone;
  • it does not confuse the public when more than one cyclone is in the same area;
  • warnings reach a much wider audience very rapidly if a name is attached.

The WMO fact sheet makes the same point. Naming is the fastest way to communicate warnings and raise public awareness, and it avoids confusion among meteorologists, media, emergency agencies and the public. Both bodies stress that cyclones are not named after individuals.

  • Early storms were named haphazardly. WMO gives the example of an Atlantic storm that ripped the mast off a boat called Antje and became known as Antje's hurricane.
  • Female names came into use in the mid-20th century. North Atlantic forecasters then moved to alphabetical lists, so the first storm of a year started with A.
  • From 1953 the US National Hurricane Center provided the Atlantic lists. WMO says male names were introduced in 1979, alternating with female names.
  • IMD's FAQ notes that the first use of a proper name for a tropical cyclone was by an Australian forecaster early in the 20th century.

Names are not set globally. Each ocean basin has a tropical cyclone regional body that fixes its list at an annual or biennial session. WMO and IMD name the same five:

  1. ESCAP/WMO Typhoon Committee
  2. WMO/ESCAP Panel on Tropical Cyclones (covers the north Indian Ocean)
  3. RA I Tropical Cyclone Committee
  4. RA IV Hurricane Committee
  5. RA V Tropical Cyclone Committee

The rules differ by region. WMO notes that the Atlantic and Southern Hemisphere name storms alphabetically, alternating men's and women's names, while other regions follow the alphabetical order of countries. IMD adds that the north Atlantic uses six lists in rotation. The north Indian Ocean list, by contrast, is used sequentially and is not rotated.

YearStep
2000The PTC's 27th session, at Muscat (Oman), agrees in principle to name cyclones over the Bay of Bengal and Arabian Sea.
2004Naming begins, using a list of 64 names from 8 countries.
2018The 45th PTC session at Muscat proposes a new list, as only 6 of the 64 names remain unused.
2020A new list of 169 names is adopted in April, to be used after the name Amphan from the old list.

The new list was needed for another reason too. Five new countries joined the Panel during 2016-18, so the list had to include all 13 members.

Each country gave 13 names. They are listed alphabetically by country:

Bangladesh, India, Iran, Maldives, Myanmar, Oman, Pakistan, Qatar, Saudi Arabia, Sri Lanka, Thailand, United Arab Emirates and Yemen.

Names are used column-wise. Column 1 runs from Bangladesh's name down to Yemen's, then Column 2 begins, and so on to the last row of Column 13. The 2020 list therefore opens with Nisarga (Bangladesh) followed by Gati (India).

  • Column 1: Nisarga, Gati, Nivar, Burevi, Tauktae, Yaas, Gulab, Shaheen, Jawad, Asani, Sitrang, Mandous, Mocha.
  • Column 2: Biparjoy, Tej, Hamoon, Midhili, Michaung, Remal, Asna, Dana, Fengal, Shakhti, Montha, Senyar, Ditwah.

The PTC's criteria for a proposed name include:

  • it must be neutral to politics and political figures, religious beliefs, cultures and gender;
  • it must not hurt the sentiments of any group of people anywhere;
  • it should be short, easy to pronounce and not offensive to any member;
  • it can be at most eight letters long;
  • it must come with its pronunciation, meaning and a voice-over;
  • it must be new, and must not appear in the list of any RSMC in the world.

The Panel can reject any name that fails these tests. One more implementation rule matters for exams: a cyclone from the South China Sea that crosses Thailand and enters the Bay of Bengal as a tropical cyclone keeps its original name.

RSMC New Delhi names a system only when it reaches cyclonic storm strength, 34 knots. IMD's terminology sets the categories:

CategoryMaximum sustained windNamed?
Depression17-27 knotsNo
Deep depression28-33 knotsNo
Cyclonic storm34-47 knots (62-88 kmph)Yes
Severe cyclonic storm48-63 knots (89-117 kmph)Yes
Very severe cyclonic storm64-89 knots (118-166 kmph)Yes

IMD's glossary defines a knot as one nautical mile per hour.

  • IMD's RSMC New Delhi assigns the name, but the list belongs to the WMO/ESCAP Panel on Tropical Cyclones.
  • The threshold is 34 knots. A depression or deep depression is not named.
  • North Indian Ocean names are used once and never repeated. The list is not rotated like the Atlantic lists.
  • 2004 list: 8 countries, 64 names. 2020 list: 13 countries, 169 names.
  • Maximum length of a name: eight letters.
  • "Hurricane" (Atlantic) and "typhoon" (Pacific) are regional names for a strong tropical cyclone.

Q1. Which body assigns names to tropical cyclones over the Bay of Bengal and the Arabian Sea? easy

✅ Answer: (a) RSMC New Delhi (IMD)

💡 Explanation: IMD's naming document says the RSMC for tropical cyclones, New Delhi, gives each cyclone a name from the Panel's list, covering both seas.

Q2. A system over the north Indian Ocean is named once its maximum wind speed reaches at least: medium

✅ Answer: (b) 34 knots

💡 Explanation: RSMC New Delhi names cyclones analysed with maximum wind speeds of 34 knots or more, the cyclonic storm threshold.

Q3. How many countries contributed names to the north Indian Ocean list adopted in April 2020? medium

✅ Answer: (c) 13

💡 Explanation: Five countries joined the Panel during 2016-18, so the new list includes all 13 members, each giving 13 names.

Q4. What is the maximum length allowed for a proposed cyclone name in the north Indian Ocean? medium

✅ Answer: (d) Eight letters

💡 Explanation: The PTC's selection criteria cap a name at eight letters.

Q5. Which of these was a name contributed by India to the April 2020 list? medium

✅ Answer: (a) Gati

💡 Explanation: India's 2020 names begin with Gati, Tej and Murasu. Nisarga and Biparjoy are Bangladesh's; Hamoon is Iran's.

Q6. The decision in principle to name north Indian Ocean cyclones was taken by the WMO/ESCAP Panel in 2000 at: hard

✅ Answer: (b) Muscat

💡 Explanation: IMD records that the Panel's 27th session, held in 2000 in Muscat, Oman, agreed in principle to name the cyclones.

Q7. A cyclone that forms in the South China Sea, crosses Thailand and enters the Bay of Bengal will: hard

✅ Answer: (c) Keep its original name

💡 Explanation: The PTC's implementation rules say such a cyclone's name will not be changed.

Q8. Which statement about north Indian Ocean cyclone names is correct? easy

✅ Answer: (d) A name, once used, is never used again

💡 Explanation: IMD says the lists are used sequentially and not rotated, and a name once used ceases to be used again.

Who names cyclones in India?

IMD, as RSMC New Delhi, gives Bay of Bengal and Arabian Sea cyclones the next name on the list fixed by the WMO/ESCAP Panel on Tropical Cyclones, once winds reach 34 knots.

Which names did India give to the 2020 cyclone list?

India gave 13 names: Gati, Tej, Murasu, Aag, Vyom, Jhar, Probaho, Neer, Prabhanjan, Ghurni, Ambud, Jaladhi and Vega, used in column order.
