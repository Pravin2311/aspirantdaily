Ramsar Sites in India: State-wise List of All 103 Wetlands, First and Largest Sites, Latest Additions and the Nine Criteria

A Ramsar site is a wetland designated as a Wetland of International Importance under the Ramsar Convention. According to the Press Information Bureau's backgrounder of 25 October 2026, India had 103 Ramsar sites as of 15 October 2026. Together they cover more than 13.87 lakh hectares across 28 States and Union Territories.

  • Growth: up from 26 sites in 2014, a nearly fourfold rise.
  • Rank: first in Asia and third in the world by number of sites.
  • Top States: Tamil Nadu has the most, with 20, followed by Uttar Pradesh with 14.
  • Latest additions: Sukhna Wetland (Chandigarh) and Sauj Jheel (Uttar Pradesh), on 15 October 2026.

About the latest two:

  • Sukhna: a human-made reservoir created in 1958 at the foothills of the Shivaliks.
  • Sauj Jheel: a shallow floodplain lake in Mainpuri district that regularly hosts more than 30,000 waterbirds.
  • Loktak Lake (Manipur): floating phumdis, the habitat of the Sangai deer.
  • Lonar Lake (Maharashtra): a meteorite impact crater lake with a saline-alkaline ecosystem.
  • Vembanad-Kol (Kerala): the largest brackish wetland on the southwest coast.
  • Beas Conservation Reserve (Punjab): India's only known population of the endangered Indus River dolphin.
  • Khichan (Rajasthan): more than 22,000 wintering demoiselle cranes on just 54 hectares.
  • East Kolkata Wetlands (West Bengal): treat the city's wastewater naturally through fish ponds.
  • The Convention: adopted at Ramsar, Iran, on 2 February 1971 and in force from 1975. It has 172 Contracting Parties and over 2,536 Ramsar sites worldwide. India became a Party on 1 February 1982.
  • Three pillars: wise use of all wetlands, designation and management of Ramsar sites, and international cooperation.
  • Process in India: the State Government or UT Administration identifies a wetland, and MoEFCC coordinates approval and forwards the proposal to the Ramsar Secretariat.
  • Criteria: a wetland must meet at least one of nine criteria, for example regularly supporting 20,000 or more waterbirds.
  • After designation: the site is deemed notified under the Wetlands (Conservation and Management) Rules, 2017, and its Ramsar Information Sheet must be updated every six years.
  • Montreux Record: a register of Ramsar sites where the ecological character has changed or is likely to change. Chilika was removed from it in 2002 after restoration.
  • The count changes with every designation. This page is dated to 15 October 2026, when the total was 103.
  • The first sites (1981) predate India becoming a Party (1 February 1982). PIB gives both dates.
  • Largest is Sundarban, not Chilika. The first sites are Chilika and Keoladeo.
  • Bihar's Kanwar Jheel is also called Kabartal. Surha Tal is the Jai Prakash Narayan Bird Sanctuary.
