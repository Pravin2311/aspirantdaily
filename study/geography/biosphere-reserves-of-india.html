Biosphere Reserves of India: All 18 With Year and State, the 13 in UNESCO's World Network, Largest, Smallest and Key Facts

What a biosphere reserve is • Full list of 18 • The 13 UNESCO sites • Five not yet listed • Records • Programme history • Funding • Exam traps

UPSC State PSC SSC 📅 Last updated: 2026-11-07

NCERT's Class 11 text India: Physical Environment defines a biosphere reserve as a unique and representative ecosystem of terrestrial and coastal areas, internationally recognised within the framework of UNESCO's Man and Biosphere (MAB) Programme. The Press Information Bureau's November 2025 backgrounder adds that reserves are nominated by national governments and remain under the sovereign jurisdiction of the States where they lie. UNESCO's role is recognition, not ownership.

The Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change (MoEFCC) lists the objectives:

  • in-situ conservation, at every level from genes to ecosystems;
  • wider understanding of ecosystems through research and monitoring;
  • integrated development that improves the quality of life of local communities.

Its scheme focuses on supplementary and alternate livelihood support for people in the buffer and transition zones. The aim is to reduce pressure on the core zone.

NCERT's Table 5.1 lists all 18 reserves with the year each was designated. The UNESCO column follows the MoEFCC list, which marks 12 sites with an asterisk, plus Cold Desert, which joined in September 2025.

PIB puts the total area of the 18 reserves at about 91,425 sq km.

Of the 18, five are not yet in the World Network of Biosphere Reserves:

  1. Manas (Assam);
  2. Dibru-Saikhowa (Assam);
  3. Dehang-Dibang (Arunachal Pradesh);
  4. Great Rann of Kutch (Gujarat);
  5. Seshachalam (Andhra Pradesh).

Older books say "12 of 18". The MoEFCC note and NCERT's current reprint still say 12, because they predate Cold Desert's inclusion in September 2025. The current figure is 13.

The two three-reserve States are a common question:

  • Madhya Pradesh: Pachmarhi, Achanakmar-Amarkantak and Panna.
  • Tamil Nadu: Nilgiri, Gulf of Mannar and Agasthyamalai.
  • 1979: the Indian MAB Committee identified potential sites.
  • 1983: the Committee of Secretaries approved biosphere reserves for critical ecosystems.
  • 1986: the Indian National Biosphere Reserve Programme began, broadly following UNESCO guidelines. Nilgiri was the first reserve.

Funding runs through a Centrally Sponsored Scheme. The Centre and States share costs 60:40, or 90:10 for North Eastern and Himalayan States. At the global level, UNESCO's MAB International Coordinating Council (MAB-ICC) is the governing body that designates sites.

  • 18 reserves, 13 in UNESCO's network (from September 2025). "12" is the older figure.
  • Cold Desert was designated in 2009, but it joined the UNESCO network only in 2025.
  • Largest is the Great Rann of Kutch and smallest is Nokrek. Do not confuse largest with the oldest, Nilgiri.
  • Five reserves, two of them in Assam (Manas and Dibru-Saikhowa), are still outside the WNBR.
  • Biosphere reserves are designated by India and recognised by UNESCO, not owned by it.

Q1. Which was India's first biosphere reserve, designated in 1986? easy

✅ Answer: (a) Nilgiri

💡 Explanation: NCERT's table and the PIB backgrounder both give Nilgiri (Tamil Nadu, Kerala and Karnataka), designated in 1986, as the oldest.

Q2. Which is the largest biosphere reserve in India? easy

✅ Answer: (b) Great Rann of Kutch

💡 Explanation: PIB lists the Great Rann of Kutch, Gujarat, at 12,454 sq km as the largest.

Q3. Which biosphere reserve joined UNESCO's World Network in September 2025, making India's count 13? medium

✅ Answer: (c) Cold Desert

💡 Explanation: PIB says the Cold Desert Biosphere Reserve in Himachal Pradesh was included in the WNBR in September 2025.

Q4. Which is the smallest biosphere reserve in India? medium

✅ Answer: (d) Nokrek

💡 Explanation: PIB lists Nokrek, Meghalaya, at 820 sq km as the smallest.

✅ Answer: (a) Manas

Q6. Which two States have three biosphere reserves each? medium

✅ Answer: (b) Madhya Pradesh and Tamil Nadu

💡 Explanation: PIB names Madhya Pradesh (Pachmarhi, Achanakmar-Amarkantak, Panna) and Tamil Nadu (Nilgiri, Gulf of Mannar, Agasthyamalai).

Q7. What is the Centre-State funding ratio for biosphere reserves in North Eastern and Himalayan States? medium

✅ Answer: (c) 90:10

💡 Explanation: PIB says the Centrally Sponsored Scheme uses 60:40, and 90:10 for North Eastern and Himalayan States.

Q8. The Achanakmar-Amarkantak biosphere reserve lies in: medium

✅ Answer: (d) Madhya Pradesh and Chhattisgarh

💡 Explanation: NCERT's Table 5.1 lists Achanakmar-Amarkantak (2005) in Madhya Pradesh and Chhattisgarh.

How many biosphere reserves does India have?

18, covering about 91,425 sq km. Of these, 13 are in UNESCO's World Network of Biosphere Reserves, after the Cold Desert reserve joined in September 2025.

Which biosphere reserves are not in the UNESCO network?

Five: Manas, Dibru-Saikhowa, Dehang-Dibang, Great Rann of Kutch and Seshachalam, based on the MoEFCC list and the 2025 Cold Desert inclusion.
