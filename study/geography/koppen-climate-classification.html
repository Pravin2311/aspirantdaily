Koeppen's Climate Classification: Groups A to E, Letter Codes Decoded, and Where India Fits

A tropical (coldest month 18°C+) • B dry • C warm temperate • D cold snow forest • E polar (all months below 10°C) • f, m, w, s = rain season • India: Am and Cwa

UPSC State PSC 📅 Last updated: 2026-11-02

NCERT describes three broad ways to classify climate. Empirical schemes use observed data, mainly temperature and precipitation. Genetic schemes group climates by their causes. Applied schemes serve a specific purpose. The most widely used is the empirical scheme of V. Koeppen (also spelled Köppen or Koppen).

Koeppen noticed a close link between climate and the distribution of vegetation. He chose threshold values of mean annual and mean monthly temperature and precipitation that matched vegetation boundaries, and used them to classify climates. First developed in 1918 and modified since, the scheme is still popular. It uses capital letters for climate groups and small letters for types within them.

Koeppen recognised five major groups. Four (A, C, D, E) are based on temperature and are humid; one (B) is based on precipitation and is dry.

GroupNameDefining condition (NCERT Table 11.1)
ATropicalColdest month averages 18°C or higher
BDryPotential evaporation exceeds precipitation
CWarm temperate (mid-latitude)Coldest month above minus 3°C but below 18°C
DCold snow forestColdest month minus 3°C or below
ECold (polar)Every month averages below 10°C

Reading the letters:

  • f = no dry season; m = monsoon; w = winter dry season; s = summer dry season.
  • a, b, c, d = how severe the temperature is (for example, a hot summer).
  • In group B, S = steppe or semi-arid and W = desert; h and k separate low-latitude (hot) from mid-latitude dry climates.
  • In group E, T = tundra and F = perennial ice.

So Cwa reads as warm temperate (C), dry winter (w), hot summer (a), and BWh as dry (B), desert (W), low latitude (h).

NCERT's Table 11.2 lists fourteen codes (the B rows hold four). Its text adds Cwa as a fourth warm temperate type, which is why it appears here too.

NCERT's world-climate chapter places India in two Koeppen types:

  • Tropical monsoon (Am) over the Indian sub-continent: heavy rain mostly in summer and a dry winter. NCERT says the detailed account of this type is in its book India: Physical Environment.
  • Humid subtropical with dry winter (Cwa) mainly in the North Indian plains: similar to tropical wet and dry, but with warm winters.

Exam questions sometimes ask about India's other Koeppen regions, such as the Thar desert or the Himalayan heights. An older NCERT edition carried a detailed India map, but the current edition does not. Use the group definitions above to reason it out: a hot desert where evaporation exceeds rainfall is B, and high mountains with every month below 10°C are E.

For the rainfall processes behind these climates, see types of rainfall, and for India's monsoon seasons see climate and monsoon of India.

  • Dry climates spread over 15°-60° of latitude. At 15°-30° they lie under the subtropical high, where sinking air and temperature inversion stop rain. On west coasts beside cold currents, especially in South America, they reach towards the equator.
  • Fog is common in coastal deserts beside cold currents, and desert rain comes in short, intense showers that do little for soil moisture.
  • Record heat: the highest shade temperature, 58°C, was recorded at Al Aziziyah, Libya, on 13 September 1922.
  • Mediterranean lands get summer under the subtropical high and winter rain from the westerlies.
  • Marine west coast winters are warmer than their latitude would suggest because of the sea's influence.
  • Dw in north-eastern Asia sees a monsoon-like reversal of winds as a strong winter anticyclone weakens in summer.
  • Ice caps build up until the pressure deforms them and they break off as icebergs; Plateau Station in Antarctica (79°S) typifies EF.

Q1. In Koeppen's scheme, which letter marks a climate with no dry season? easy

✅ Answer: (a) f

💡 Explanation: NCERT: f means no dry season, m monsoon, w winter dry season and s summer dry season. So Af is tropical wet with rain in every month.

Q2. Koeppen's climate over the North Indian plains is: medium

✅ Answer: (c) Cwa

💡 Explanation: NCERT places the humid subtropical climate with dry winter (Cwa) mainly in the North Indian plains and the interior plains of South China.

Q3. Which Koeppen group is defined by precipitation rather than temperature? medium

✅ Answer: (d) B

💡 Explanation: Four groups rest on temperature; only B, the dry climates, is defined by moisture: potential evaporation exceeds precipitation.

Q4. Hot, dry summers and mild, rainy winters describe which climate? easy

✅ Answer: (b) Mediterranean (Cs)

💡 Explanation: The s in Cs means a dry summer. NCERT explains the subtropical high dominates in summer and westerlies bring rain in winter.

Q5. For Koeppen's group A, the average temperature of the coldest month must be: medium

✅ Answer: (d) 18°C or higher

💡 Explanation: NCERT's Table 11.1 defines tropical (A) climates by a coldest month of 18°C or more. Between minus 3°C and 18°C is group C.

What does Am mean in Koeppen's classification?

A is the tropical group, where the coldest month averages at least 18°C, and m means a monsoon climate with a short dry season. NCERT places Am over the Indian sub-continent, north-eastern South America and northern Australia.

Which Koeppen climates does NCERT place in India?

Tropical monsoon (Am) over the Indian sub-continent and humid subtropical with dry winter (Cwa) over the North Indian plains. NCERT's current chapter does not map India's other climate types under Koeppen.

Why is Koeppen's scheme called empirical?

Because it is based on observed data, mean annual and monthly temperature and precipitation, rather than on the causes of climate. Koeppen picked threshold values that matched the distribution of vegetation.
