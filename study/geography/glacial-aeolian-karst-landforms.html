Glacial, Wind (Aeolian) and Karst Landforms: Cirques to Drumlins, Pediments to Barchans, Sinkholes to Stalactites

Glacial: cirque, horn, arête, U-valley, fjord, moraine, esker, drumlin • Wind: pediment, inselberg, playa, mushroom rock, barchan, seif • Karst: sinkhole, uvala, lapies, stalactite, stalagmite

UPSC State PSC 📅 Last updated: 2026-11-01

Glaciers are masses of ice moving as sheets over land (continental or piedmont glaciers) or as linear flows down mountain valleys (valley glaciers). They move slowly, from a few centimetres to a few metres a day, mainly under gravity. India's glaciers are in the Himalayas. The Bhagirathi is fed by meltwater from the snout of the Gangotri glacier at Gaumukh.

Glacial erosion is tremendous because of the friction of the ice's sheer weight. Dragged rock fragments cause damage through abrasion and plucking, and can reduce high mountains to low hills and plains.

LandformWhat it is
CirqueThe most common glacial landform: a deep, wide basin with steep walls at the head of a glacial valley. A lake left in it is a cirque or tarn lake
HornA sharp, steep peak formed when three or more radiating glaciers cut back until their cirques meet. The Matterhorn and Everest are horns
ArêteA serrated, saw-toothed ridge left as divides between cirques narrow
Glacial valleyA U-shaped trough with a broad floor and steep sides
Hanging valleyA side valley left high above the main glacial valley; its spurs are often truncated into triangular facets
FjordA very deep glacial trough drowned by the sea, on high-latitude coasts

Melting glaciers drop two kinds of material. Glacial till is unsorted coarse and fine debris with angular fragments. Outwash deposits are laid by meltwater streams and are roughly stratified and sorted, with rounded fragments.

LandformWhat it is
Terminal moraineLong ridge of debris at the end (toe) of a glacier
Lateral moraineRidges along the valley sides; may join a terminal moraine in a horseshoe shape
Medial moraineIn the centre of the valley, flanked by lateral moraines
Ground moraineAn irregular sheet of till left on the valley floor by a fast-retreating glacier
EskerA sinuous ridge of boulders and debris left by a stream that flowed beneath the ice
Outwash plainBroad flat glacio-fluvial fans of gravel, silt, sand and clay beyond the ice
DrumlinA smooth oval ridge of till, up to about 1 km long and 30 m high. Its long axis lies parallel to ice flow. The blunt stoss end faces the glacier and the other end is the tail, so drumlins show which way the ice moved

Wind is one of the two dominant agents in hot deserts, the other being sheet floods. It works by deflation (lifting dust and fine particles), abrasion (sand acting as a tool) and impact (sand blasting against rock). Rain is scarce but torrential, so sheet floods also shift much of the weathered debris.

LandformWhat it is
PedimentA gently inclined rocky floor at the foot of a mountain, formed by lateral stream erosion and sheet flooding
InselbergThe remnant of a mountain worn back by parallel retreat of slopes
PediplainThe low featureless plain left when pediments spread and mountains are reduced
Playa / alkali flatA shallow basin lake that holds water briefly; when salt-covered, an alkali flat
Deflation hollow / blow-outShallow depressions blown out by persistent wind; some deepen into caves
Mushroom rockA resistant remnant with a slender stalk and broad cap, polished by wind; some are table-topped or pedestals

Sand dunes (wind is a good sorting agent):

  • Barchan: crescent-shaped, wings pointing downwind; forms with constant, moderate wind over a uniform surface.
  • Parabolic: a reversed barchan, on sand partly covered by vegetation.
  • Seif: like a barchan with only one wing, formed when the wind shifts; the wing can grow very long and high.
  • Longitudinal: long, low ridges where sand supply is poor and wind direction constant.
  • Transverse: perpendicular to the wind, where the sand source runs at right angles to it.

Groundwater makes landforms mainly in limestone or dolomite, rocks rich in calcium carbonate, through solution and precipitation. Mechanical removal by groundwater is insignificant. Such landscapes are called karst topography, after the Karst region of the Balkans beside the Adriatic Sea.

LandformTypeWhat it is
Swallow holeErosionalA shallow, roughly round solution depression on limestone
SinkholeErosionalCircular at the top, funnel-shaped below. Solution sinks are more common than collapse sinks (dolines)
Uvala (valley sink)ErosionalA long trench formed when sinkholes and dolines join
LapiesErosionalA maze of points, grooves and ridges on eaten-away limestone
Cave / tunnelErosionalGaps dissolved along bedding planes; a cave open at both ends is a tunnel
StalactiteDepositionalHangs from the cave roof like an icicle, broad at the base
StalagmiteDepositionalRises from the cave floor beneath dripping water

Calcium carbonate dissolves easily in carbonated rainwater. It is deposited again when that water evaporates or loses its carbon dioxide while trickling over rock, which builds the cave formations.

Q1. Which landform is formed by glacial deposition? easy

✅ Answer: (c) Esker

💡 Explanation: An esker is a sinuous ridge of coarse debris laid by a stream under a glacier. Cirques, horns and arêtes are erosional.

Q2. According to NCERT, Mount Everest is an example of a: medium

✅ Answer: (a) Horn

💡 Explanation: NCERT says the Matterhorn and Everest are horns formed by headward erosion of radiating cirques.

Q3. Crescent-shaped dunes with wings pointing downwind are: easy

✅ Answer: (d) Barchans

💡 Explanation: Barchans have wings pointing downwind under constant, moderate winds. Parabolic dunes are reversed barchans, and seifs have one wing.

Q4. Long trenches formed when sinkholes and dolines join are called: medium

✅ Answer: (b) Uvalas

💡 Explanation: NCERT calls them valley sinks or uvalas. Lapies are the ridges left on eaten-away limestone, and tunnels are caves open at both ends.

Q5. A playa covered with salts is called: medium

✅ Answer: (c) An alkali flat

💡 Explanation: NCERT says playas are shallow, short-lived desert lakes, and a playa plain covered by salts is an alkali flat.

What are erosional and depositional glacial landforms?

Erosional: cirques, horns, arêtes, U-shaped valleys, hanging valleys and fjords. Depositional: moraines (terminal, lateral, medial, ground), eskers, outwash plains and drumlins.

What is the difference between stalactites and stalagmites?

Stalactites hang from cave roofs like icicles, broad at the base and tapering down. Stalagmites rise from the cave floor, built by water dripping from the roof or a stalactite above.

Is wind the only agent shaping deserts?

No. NCERT says many desert features owe their formation to mass wasting and sheet floods. Rain is scarce but torrential, and it removes weathered debris; wind mainly moves fine material.
