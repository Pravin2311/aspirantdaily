Indus Waters Treaty 1960: Eastern and Western Rivers, India's Rights, the Permanent Indus Commission, Dispute Settlement and the 2025 Abeyance

Treaty in brief • Eastern vs Western rivers • India's permitted uses • Transition period • Permanent Indus Commission • Dispute tiers • Article XII • 2025 abeyance • Exam traps

UPSC State PSC SSC CDS 📅 Last updated: 2026-11-08

The Indus Waters Treaty divides the six rivers of the Indus system between India and Pakistan. It was signed on 19 September 1960 but takes effect from 1 April 1960. The World Bank, formally the International Bank for Reconstruction and Development, also signed it for specific purposes. The treaty text in the UN Treaty Series records that it was done in triplicate in English at Karachi. Jawaharlal Nehru signed for India and Field Marshal Mohammad Ayub Khan for Pakistan.

The core bargain is simple:

  • Eastern Rivers (Sutlej, Beas and Ravi) go to India for unrestricted use.
  • Western Rivers (Indus, Jhelum and Chenab) must be let flow to Pakistan. India keeps a short list of permitted uses on them.

Since April 2025 India has held the treaty in abeyance (see the last section).

Article I of the treaty defines the two groups:

GroupRiversWho gets the water
Eastern RiversSutlej, Beas, RaviIndia: "available for the unrestricted use of India" (Article II)
Western RiversIndus, Jhelum, ChenabPakistan receives the waters India must let flow (Article III)

The Jal Shakti ministry's brief adds that each group includes its tributaries.

Under Article III, India must let all the waters of the Western Rivers flow and must not interfere with them, except for listed uses. The Jal Shakti ministry brief summarises these permitted uses:

  1. domestic use;
  2. non-consumptive use;
  3. agricultural use, as specified in the treaty;
  4. generation of hydro-electric power, as specified in the treaty.

The brief adds specific limits:

  • Storage: India may build up to 3.6 MAF (million acre-feet) of storage on the Western Rivers. The brief says no storage had been developed.
  • Irrigation: India may irrigate an extra 7,01,000 acres beyond the irrigated cropped area as on 1 April 1960. Only 2,70,000 acres of this can be developed until storages are built.

These limits are the basis of Pakistan's objections to Indian hydro projects on the Western Rivers, two of which went to the treaty's dispute bodies (below).

India did not stop Eastern River supplies at once. Article II set a transition period from 1 April 1960 to 31 March 1970, extendable but ending no later than 31 March 1973. During it India released Eastern River water to Pakistan while Pakistan built replacement canals. After it, Pakistan had no claim to those releases.

Under Article V, India agreed to a fixed contribution of Pounds Sterling 62,060,000 towards the cost of Pakistan's replacement works. It was paid in ten equal annual instalments, beginning on 1 November 1960.

Article VIII sets up the treaty's working machinery:

  • India and Pakistan each create a permanent post of Commissioner for Indus Waters, ordinarily a high-ranking engineer in hydrology and water use.
  • Together the two Commissioners form the Permanent Indus Commission.
  • The Commission must meet at least once a year, alternately in India and Pakistan. The regular annual meeting is held in November or another agreed month.

The Jal Shakti brief notes that both sides must exchange river flow data within three months of observation. As a goodwill gesture, India shares flood data with Pakistan from 1 July to 10 October each year.

Article IX sets out a graded procedure:

TierBodyHandles
1Permanent Indus CommissionAny "question" is first examined by the Commission, which tries to resolve it by agreement
2Neutral ExpertA "difference" of the technical kind listed in Annexure F
3Court of ArbitrationA "dispute", set up under Annexure G

The Jal Shakti brief records two cases:

  • Baglihar: a Neutral Expert appointed by the World Bank at Pakistan's request gave an Expert Determination in 2007.
  • Kishenganga: a seven-member Court of Arbitration was set up in 2010 at Pakistan's request and gave its final award on 20 December 2013.

Article XII is the treaty's final clause:

  • It may be modified "from time to time" only by a duly ratified treaty between the two Governments.
  • It continues in force until terminated by a duly ratified treaty between the two Governments.

There is no clause for one side to withdraw alone. That is why India's 2025 step was framed as keeping the treaty "in abeyance" rather than ending it.

After the terrorist attack at Pahalgam on 22 April 2025, the Cabinet Committee on Security approved several measures against Pakistan. According to the Press Information Bureau, the first was:

  • keeping in abeyance the Indus Waters Treaty of 1960 with immediate effect, until Pakistan credibly and irrevocably abjures its support for cross-border terrorism.

The other measures in the same list included closing the Integrated Check Post at Attari, ending SAARC visa exemption travel for Pakistani nationals, and cutting High Commission strength to 30 from 55.

  • Signed 19 September 1960, at Karachi, but effective from 1 April 1960.
  • India gets the Eastern rivers (Sutlej, Beas, Ravi). The Chenab is a Western river, even though India builds projects on it.
  • India's storage right on the Western Rivers is 3.6 MAF.
  • Order of dispute bodies: Commission, then Neutral Expert, then Court of Arbitration.
  • Baglihar went to a Neutral Expert (2007). Kishenganga went to a Court of Arbitration (award 2013).
  • "Abeyance" is not termination. Article XII allows termination only by a new ratified treaty.

Q1. Under the Indus Waters Treaty, which rivers are allocated to India for unrestricted use? easy

✅ Answer: (a) Sutlej, Beas and Ravi

💡 Explanation: Article I(5) defines the Eastern Rivers as the Sutlej, Beas and Ravi, and Article II(1) makes them available for India's unrestricted use.

Q2. The Indus Waters Treaty was signed in 1960 at: medium

✅ Answer: (b) Karachi

💡 Explanation: The treaty text says it was done in triplicate in English at Karachi on 19 September 1960.

Q3. From which date does the Indus Waters Treaty take effect? medium

✅ Answer: (c) 1 April 1960

💡 Explanation: It was signed on 19 September 1960 but is effective from 1 April 1960, per the treaty and the Jal Shakti brief.

Q4. How much storage may India build on the Western Rivers under the treaty? medium

✅ Answer: (d) 3.6 MAF

💡 Explanation: The Jal Shakti brief says India is permitted storage of up to 3.6 MAF on the Western Rivers.

Q5. Which is the first body to examine any question under the Indus Waters Treaty? medium

✅ Answer: (a) Permanent Indus Commission

💡 Explanation: Article IX(1) says any question shall first be examined by the Commission, which will try to resolve it by agreement.

Q6. The Baglihar project dispute was decided in 2007 by: hard

✅ Answer: (b) A Neutral Expert appointed by the World Bank

💡 Explanation: The Jal Shakti brief says a World Bank-appointed Neutral Expert gave the Baglihar Expert Determination in 2007; Kishenganga went to a Court of Arbitration.

Q7. Under Article XII, the Indus Waters Treaty can be terminated only by: hard

✅ Answer: (c) A duly ratified treaty between the two Governments

💡 Explanation: Article XII(4) says it continues in force until terminated by a duly ratified treaty concluded for that purpose between the two Governments.

Q8. In April 2025, after the Pahalgam attack, India decided to: easy

✅ Answer: (d) Keep the Indus Waters Treaty in abeyance

💡 Explanation: PIB records the CCS decision to keep the treaty in abeyance with immediate effect until Pakistan credibly and irrevocably abjures support for cross-border terrorism.

Which rivers does India get under the Indus Waters Treaty?

The Eastern Rivers: the Sutlej, Beas and Ravi, for unrestricted use. The Western Rivers (Indus, Jhelum, Chenab) must be let flow to Pakistan, apart from listed Indian uses.

Is the Indus Waters Treaty suspended?

India has kept it in abeyance since April 2025, after the Pahalgam attack, until Pakistan credibly and irrevocably abjures support for cross-border terrorism, per PIB.
