Landforms Formed by Rivers: Erosional and Depositional Features from Gorge to Delta

Erosional: V-valley, gorge, canyon, pothole, plunge pool, incised meander, terrace • Depositional: alluvial fan, delta, floodplain, natural levee, point bar • Meander → oxbow lake

UPSC SSC State PSC 📅 Last updated: 2026-11-01

In humid regions with heavy rainfall, NCERT calls running water the most important geomorphic agent. It works in two ways: overland flow as a sheet over the land surface, and linear flow in streams and rivers.

  • Erosion first: most erosional landforms come from young, vigorous rivers on steep gradients.
  • Deposition later: as continued erosion makes the channel gentler, the river loses speed and drops its load. The gentler the gradient, the greater the deposition.
  • From sheet to valley: overland flow causes sheet erosion. Its paths become rills, rills grow into gullies, and gullies deepen, widen and join into a network of valleys.
  • End point: divides are lowered until only a lowland of faint relief remains, with a few resistant remnants called monadnocks. This "almost plain" is a peneplain.

LandformHow it forms / what it is
ValleysGrow from rills and gullies. Types include the V-shaped valley, the gorge and the canyon
GorgeDeep valley with very steep to straight sides, about equally wide at top and bottom; forms in hard rock
CanyonSteep, step-like sides, wider at the top than the bottom; a variant of the gorge, common in horizontal sedimentary beds
PotholesRoughly circular hollows on rocky stream beds. Pebbles caught in a hollow are spun by the water and grind it deeper
Plunge poolsLarge, deep holes at the foot of waterfalls, made by falling water and rotating boulders
Incised (entrenched) meandersVery deep, wide meanders cut into hard rock
River terracesOld valley-floor or floodplain levels left when the river cuts down into its own floodplain. If they match on both sides they are paired terraces

On steep gradients, erosion works mainly on the channel bottom (down-cutting). On gentle slopes, lateral erosion of the banks takes over, which is why rivers there begin to meander.

LandformHow it forms / what it is
Alluvial fanStreams leaving mountains onto low-gradient foot slopes dump their coarse load as a cone. Channels shift across it as distributaries. Low, gentle cones in humid areas; high, steep cones in arid ones
DeltaLike a fan but built where a river enters the sea. Deposits are well sorted and stratified: coarse first, silt and clay carried further out. Distributaries lengthen as the delta grows seaward
FloodplainThe major depositional landform. The river bed is the active floodplain and the land above the banks the inactive floodplain. In a delta it is a delta plain
Natural leveesLow, linear, parallel ridges of coarse deposits along the banks of large rivers
Point bars (meander bars)Sediments laid along the bank on the inside of meanders of large rivers. NCERT's wording on which bank is discussed below

A river builds its floodplain by deposition, just as it cuts valleys by erosion. When a channel breaks onto a gentle slope, large materials settle first. Slow water in gentler plains channels carries finer sand, silt and clay, and drops them on the bed and over the banks during floods.

  • Channel deposits: in the plains, channels shift sideways and change course. They leave cut-off courses that slowly fill with coarse material.
  • Flood deposits: water spilling over the banks carries finer silt and clay across the inactive floodplain.
  • Alluvial fan channels: streams on fans rarely stay in one channel for long. They shift across the fan as many distributaries, much as distributaries keep lengthening on a growing delta.

These processes explain why the Ganga and Brahmaputra plains, covered elsewhere on this site, show meanders, oxbow lakes, braided channels and riverine islands.

NCERT stresses that a meander is not a landform but a type of channel pattern. Meanders develop for three reasons:

  1. water on very gentle gradients works sideways on the banks;
  2. loose alluvial banks have many irregularities for the water to attack;
  3. the Coriolis force deflects flowing water, as it deflects wind.

A slight curve deepens because of deposition on the convex (inner) bank and undercutting on the concave (outer) bank. The concave bank becomes a steep scarp, the cut-off bank, and the convex bank has a long, gentle slope. As loops deepen, erosion at the inflection points can cut a loop off, leaving an oxbow lake.

Note: one NCERT paragraph places point bars on the "concave side" of meanders, while the meander paragraph places deposition on the convex bank. Most textbooks put point bars on the inner (convex) bank, where deposition happens.

Q1. How does a canyon differ from a gorge? medium

✅ Answer: (a) A canyon is wider at the top than at the bottom

💡 Explanation: NCERT says a gorge is about equally wide at top and bottom, while a canyon has step-like sides and is wider at the top. Canyons form in horizontal sedimentary beds.

Q2. An oxbow lake is formed when: easy

✅ Answer: (b) A meander loop is cut off

💡 Explanation: As meanders grow into deep loops, erosion at the inflection points can cut them off, leaving them as oxbow lakes.

Q3. Large deep holes at the base of waterfalls are called: easy

✅ Answer: (c) Plunge pools

💡 Explanation: NCERT calls them plunge pools, formed by the impact of falling water and rotating boulders. Potholes are smaller depressions on rocky stream beds.

Q4. Which statement about deltas is correct per NCERT? medium

✅ Answer: (d) Their deposits are well sorted and stratified

💡 Explanation: NCERT says delta deposits are very well sorted with clear stratification, unlike alluvial fans, which form where streams leave mountains.

Q5. Low resistant remnants standing on a peneplain are called: hard

✅ Answer: (a) Monadnocks

💡 Explanation: NCERT names the remnants on a stream-eroded peneplain monadnocks. Inselbergs are the desert equivalent left by retreating slopes.

What are erosional landforms of a river?

Landforms cut by running water, mostly in the young, steep stage: V-shaped valleys, gorges, canyons, potholes, plunge pools, incised or entrenched meanders and river terraces.

What are depositional landforms of a river?

Landforms built where the gradient becomes gentle and the river drops its load: alluvial fans, deltas, floodplains, natural levees and point bars. Oxbow lakes form from cut-off meanders.

Is a meander a landform?

No. NCERT says a meander is only a type of channel pattern. It forms because water on gentle gradients erodes laterally, the alluvial banks are loose, and the Coriolis force deflects the flow.
