Basic Proportionality Theorem and Similar Triangles: Side Ratios, Area Ratios, Shadows and the Altitude Rule

A line parallel to one side cuts the other two in the same ratio • The converse • AA, SSS and SAS similarity • Areas go as the square of sides • Shadows • Altitude on the hypotenuse

SSC 📅 Last updated: 2026-11-02

Draw triangle ABC and a line DE parallel to BC, with D on AB and E on AC. The basic proportionality theorem (also called Thales' theorem) says the line cuts the two sides in the same ratio:

If DE ∥ BC, then AD/DB = AE/EC    and also AD/AB = AE/AC = DE/BC

The second form compares the small triangle ADE with the whole triangle ABC. They are similar, because the parallel line creates equal corresponding angles. Watch which ratio a question uses: AD/DB compares the two parts of a side, while AD/AB compares a part with the whole side.

Worked example 1: finding a segment

DE ∥ BC, AD = 3 cm, DB = 2 cm and AE = 4.5 cm. Then 3/2 = 4.5/EC, so EC = 4.5 × 2/3 = 3 cm.

Worked example 2: the parallel side itself

AD = 4 cm, AB = 10 cm and BC = 15 cm. DE/BC = AD/AB, so DE = 15 × 4/10 = 6 cm. Using AD/DB here would give the wrong answer, because DE is compared with the whole of BC.

The theorem also works backwards: if a line divides two sides of a triangle in the same ratio, it is parallel to the third side.

Worked example 3: is DE parallel to BC?

AD = 6, DB = 4, AE = 9 and EC = 6. AD/DB = 3/2 and AE/EC = 3/2, so the ratios are equal (true) and DE ∥ BC.

The special case where D and E are midpoints is the midpoint theorem: the line joining the midpoints of two sides is parallel to the third side and half its length. The small triangle then has a quarter of the area.

Worked example 4: midpoints

Triangle ABC has an area of 48 cm². D and E are the midpoints of AB and AC. The side ratio is 1 : 2, so triangle ADE has 48 × (1/2)² = 12 cm².

Two triangles are similar when their corresponding angles are equal and their corresponding sides are in the same ratio. You do not need to check everything; any one of these tests is enough:

TestWhat you need
AATwo pairs of equal angles (the third pair then follows)
SSSAll three pairs of sides in the same ratio
SASTwo pairs of sides in the same ratio and the angle between them equal

In exam figures AA is the usual route: parallel lines give equal corresponding angles, and a shared angle or vertically opposite angles give the second pair. Always write the vertices in matching order, such as △ADE ~ △ABC, so the sides you compare actually correspond.

If two similar triangles have sides in the ratio k, every length (perimeter, altitude, median) is in the ratio k as well, but areas are in the ratio k².

Area ratio = (side ratio)²    and    side ratio = √(area ratio)

Worked example 5: sides 3 : 5

The areas are in the ratio 3² : 5² = 9 : 25.

Worked example 6: area from the side ratio

A triangle of area 75 cm² is similar to a smaller one with sides in the ratio 5 : 2. The smaller area is 75 × (2/5)² = 12 cm².

For the area of a trapezium cut off by a parallel line, subtract the small triangle from the whole: with AD : AB = 2 : 5, the small triangle is 4 parts out of 25, leaving 21 parts for the trapezium.

Objects standing upright at the same time of day form similar triangles with their shadows, because the sun's rays arrive at the same angle. So height ÷ shadow is the same for both. For angle-based versions, see heights and distances.

Worked example 7: a tower's height

A 6 m pole casts a 4 m shadow while a tower casts a 28 m shadow. The tower is 6 × 28/4 = 42 m tall.

In a right triangle, the altitude from the right angle to the hypotenuse splits it into two similar triangles. That gives a quick rule: the altitude squared equals the product of the two pieces of the hypotenuse.

Worked example 8: the altitude

The altitude divides the hypotenuse into 4 cm and 9 cm. Altitude = √(4 × 9) = 6 cm. For related triangle facts, see triangle centres.

  • Mixing AD/DB with AD/AB. Use part : part for the two sides, but part : whole for the parallel side.
  • Using the side ratio for areas. Square it.
  • Squaring perimeters. Perimeters follow the side ratio, not its square.
  • Mismatched vertices. Write the similarity in order so corresponding sides line up.
  • Forgetting the converse. Equal ratios on two sides prove the line is parallel.

Q1. In triangle ABC, DE ∥ BC with D on AB and E on AC. If AD = 4 cm, DB = 6 cm and AE = 6 cm, what is EC? easy

✅ Answer: (c) 9 cm

💡 Explanation: By BPT (Thales' theorem), AD/DB = AE/EC, so 4/6 = 6/EC and EC = 9 cm.

Q2. Two similar triangles have corresponding sides in the ratio 4 : 7. The larger has an area of 98 cm². What is the area of the smaller? easy

✅ Answer: (a) 32 cm²

💡 Explanation: Areas are in the ratio of the squares of the sides, 16 : 49. So the smaller area is 98 × 16/49 = 32 cm².

Q3. The areas of two similar triangles are in the ratio 16 : 81. The smaller has a perimeter of 36 cm. What is the larger one's perimeter? medium

✅ Answer: (d) 81 cm

💡 Explanation: The side ratio is √16 : √81 = 4 : 9, and perimeters follow the side ratio. So the larger perimeter is 36 × 9/4 = 81 cm.

Q4. A 1.5 m tall man casts a 2 m shadow. At the same time a tree casts a 24 m shadow. How tall is the tree? easy

✅ Answer: (b) 18 m

💡 Explanation: The sun's rays make similar triangles, so height/shadow is the same: 1.5/2 = h/24, giving h = 18 m.

Q5. In triangle ABC, DE ∥ BC with AD : DB = 2 : 3. What is the ratio of the area of triangle ADE to the area of trapezium DBCE? hard

✅ Answer: (c) 4 : 21

💡 Explanation: AD : AB = 2 : 5, so the areas of ADE and ABC are 4 : 25. The trapezium is the rest, 25 − 4 = 21, giving 4 : 21.

What does BPT say?

If a line is drawn parallel to one side of a triangle and meets the other two sides at distinct points, it divides those two sides in the same ratio. It is also called Thales' theorem.

Why do areas go as the square of the side ratio?

Area is half base times height. In similar triangles both the base and the height scale by the same factor k, so the area scales by k × k = k².

Do perimeters, medians and altitudes follow the side ratio?

Yes. Every length in similar triangles, including perimeters, medians, altitudes and angle bisectors, is in the same ratio as the sides. Only areas use the square.
