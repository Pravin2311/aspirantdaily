Bar Graph Data Interpretation: Read, Compare and Calculate

Read the scale • Percentage change • Average and total • Ratios • Double-bar comparisons

SSC Banking 📅 Last updated: 2026-10-24

A bar graph shows one value per category as the height of a bar. In bank prelims and mains and in SSC exams, a set of four or five questions usually hangs on one graph, so the time you spend reading it carefully once pays back across the whole set. The questions themselves use only a handful of operations: percentage change, average, total, ratio, and "what percentage of".

The graphs below show invented practice data: the two-wheeler sales, in thousand units, of two companies from 2021 to 2025. Every worked example on this page uses them.

Before touching any question, read three things: the unit (thousand units here, so 75 means 75,000), the scale on the vertical axis (gridlines every 20), and whether the bars are values or percentages. Most wrong answers in DI come from misreading one of these, not from bad arithmetic.

Percentage change always uses the earlier value as the base: change ÷ old value × 100. A rise from 60 to 75 is a 15-unit change on a base of 60, not of 75.

Worked example 1: growth in one year

Company A's sales rose from 60 thousand in 2024 to 75 thousand in 2025. Growth = (75 − 60) ÷ 60 × 100 = 25%.

For an average, add the bars and divide by the number of bars. A quick sense check: the answer must lie between the shortest and the tallest bar.

Worked example 2: five-year average

A's bars are 40, 50, 45, 60 and 75, which add to 270. The average is 270 ÷ 5 = 54 thousand units, which sits between 40 and 75 as it should.

A double (or clustered) bar graph puts two series side by side for each category. Questions then ask you to compare across series, across years, or both.

Worked example 3: ratio in one year

In 2023, A sold 45 and B sold 55 thousand units. The ratio A : B = 45 : 55 = 9 : 11 after dividing both by 5.

Worked example 4: "by what percent is one total more than the other"

A's total is 270 and B's is 250. A exceeds B by (270 − 250) ÷ 250 × 100 = 8%. The base is B, because the question compares A with B.

Writing the totals as an extra column, as in the table above, takes a few seconds and answers several questions at once.

"In which year was the percentage increase the highest?" is a favourite because the biggest jump in height is not always the biggest percentage jump. A small base makes a modest rise look large in percentage terms.

Worked example 5: Company B's best year

B's rises were 10 (2022, on 35), 10 (2023, on 45), a fall in 2024 and 15 (2025, on 50). The 2025 rise is 15 ÷ 50 × 100 = 30%.

The 2022 rise is 10 ÷ 35 × 100 = 28.57%, a little lower, so 2025 is the answer. Compare fractions such as 10/35 and 15/50 directly when you can: 15/50 = 0.30 is just above 10/35 ≈ 0.286.

Worked example 6: combined growth over the period

The two companies sold 40 + 35 = 75 thousand together in 2021 and 75 + 65 = 140 thousand in 2025. Sales in 2025 were 140 ÷ 75 × 100 = 186.67% of the 2021 level, an increase of 86.67%.

  • ✘ Using the later value as the base for percentage change.
  • ✘ Confusing "A is what percent of B" (A ÷ B) with "A is what percent more than B" ((A − B) ÷ B).
  • ✘ Reading a bar against the wrong gridline, or forgetting the unit when the answer needs actual numbers.
  • ✘ Assuming the tallest jump is the highest percentage growth.

Q1. Using the double bar graph, Company A's sales in 2022 were approximately what percentage of Company B's sales in 2022? easy

✅ Answer: (b) 111.11%

💡 Explanation: A sold 50 and B sold 45 thousand units in 2022, so 50 ÷ 45 × 100 = 111.11%. Dividing the other way, 45 ÷ 50, gives the trap answer of 90%.

Q2. By what percentage did Company B's sales increase from 2021 to 2025? medium

✅ Answer: (c) 85.7%

💡 Explanation: B rose from 35 to 65 thousand units, an increase of 30 on a base of 35: 30 ÷ 35 × 100 = 85.7%. Dividing by the final value, 65, gives 46.2%.

Q3. What were Company B's average annual sales over the five years? easy

✅ Answer: (a) 50 thousand

💡 Explanation: B's five bars add to 35 + 45 + 55 + 50 + 65 = 250 thousand units, and 250 ÷ 5 = 50 thousand. Company A's average is 54 thousand.

Q4. In how many of the five years did Company A sell more than Company B? easy

✅ Answer: (d) 4

💡 Explanation: A beat B in 2021 (40 vs 35), 2022 (50 vs 45), 2024 (60 vs 50) and 2025 (75 vs 65). Only in 2023 did B lead, 55 to 45, so the answer is 4 years.

Q5. By how many thousand units did Company A's five-year total exceed Company B's? easy

✅ Answer: (a) 20

💡 Explanation: A's total is 40 + 50 + 45 + 60 + 75 = 270 and B's is 250, so the difference is 20 thousand units.

Q6. What is the ratio of Company A's sales in 2021 to Company B's sales in 2025? medium

✅ Answer: (b) 8 : 13

💡 Explanation: A sold 40 thousand in 2021 and B sold 65 thousand in 2025. 40 : 65 simplifies by 5 to 8 : 13. Reversing the order gives the trap 13 : 8.

Should I calculate exact values in bar graph questions?

Only when the options are close together. Look at the options first. If they differ by more than a few percent, round the bar values to friendly numbers and estimate; if two options are within one or two percent, work exactly.

What if the bar's value is not printed on the graph?

Read it from the scale on the vertical axis. Check the unit and the gap between gridlines first, because a bar halfway between 40 and 60 on a scale marked in steps of 20 is 50, not 45.
