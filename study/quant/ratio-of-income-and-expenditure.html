Ratio of Income and Expenditure Problems: Two People, Equal Savings, One Known Income and a Shortcut Formula

Incomes ax and bx, expenditures cy and dy • Equal savings • The shortcut x = S(c − d)/(bc − ad) • One income known • Unequal savings • Savings as percentages

SSC Banking 📅 Last updated: 2026-11-02

A typical question reads: "The incomes of A and B are in the ratio 3 : 2 and their expenditures in the ratio 5 : 3. If each saves ₹2,000, find A's income." The key step is to use one multiplier for incomes and a different one for expenditures.

  • Incomes: 3x and 2x
  • Expenditures: 5y and 3y
  • Savings = income − expenditure: 3x − 5y and 2x − 3y

That gives two equations in two unknowns. Every ratio of income and expenditure problem is a version of this setup; the rest is solving the pair. For the basics of ratios, see ratio and proportion basics.

Worked example 1: each saves ₹2,000

3x − 5y = 2,000 and 2x − 3y = 2,000. Multiply the first by 3 and the second by 5: 9x − 15y = 6,000 and 10x − 15y = 10,000. Subtracting gives x = 4,000, so A's income is 3 × 4,000 = ₹12,000.

Worked example 2: the expenditures

From 2x − 3y = 2,000 with x = 4,000: 3y = 6,000, so y = 2,000. A spends 5 × 2,000 = ₹10,000 and B spends ₹6,000. Check: 12,000 − 10,000 = 2,000 and 8,000 − 6,000 = 2,000.

When incomes are a : b, expenditures c : d, and both people save the same amount S, eliminating y once and for all gives:

x = S(c − d) ÷ (bc − ad)    so the incomes are ax and bx.

To remember the denominator, cross-multiply the two ratios, a : b against c : d, and subtract: b × c minus a × d. If the result is negative, the numerator will be negative too, so x still comes out positive.

Worked example 3: example 1 in one line

a : b = 3 : 2, c : d = 5 : 3, S = 2,000. x = 2,000 × 2 ÷ (10 − 9) = 4,000, and A's income is ₹12,000, matching the long method.

Worked example 4: incomes 5 : 4, expenditures 3 : 2, each saves ₹1,600

x = 1,600 × (3 − 2) ÷ (4 × 3 − 5 × 2) = 800, so the incomes are ₹4,000 and ₹3,200. With y = 800 the expenditures are 2,400 and 1,600, and both savings come to 1,600.

If bc − ad is 0, the two ratios are proportional (for example 4 : 5 and 4 : 5, where 4 × 5 − 5 × 4 = 0). Then the incomes and expenditures scale together, so equal savings are only possible if both save nothing, and the question has no unique answer.

If one income is given, the income ratio fixes the other income immediately, and only y remains.

Worked example 5: A earns ₹20,000

Incomes are 4 : 3 and expenditures 3 : 2, with equal savings. B earns 15,000. Equal savings: 20,000 − 3y = 15,000 − 2y, so y = 5,000. A spends 15,000 and saves ₹5,000; B spends 10,000 and also saves 5,000.

A quick trick for this type: with equal savings, the difference in incomes equals the difference in expenditures. Here 20,000 − 15,000 = 5,000 = 3y − 2y, which gives y directly.

When the two savings figures differ, the shortcut no longer applies. Solve the pair of equations by elimination or cross-multiplication.

Worked example 6: savings of ₹2,500 and ₹2,000

Incomes 5 : 3, expenditures 9 : 5. Then 5x − 9y = 2,500 and 3x − 5y = 2,000. Cross-multiplying gives x = 2,750 and y = 1,250. A's income is 5 × 2,750 = ₹13,750, and A spends 11,250, which leaves 2,500 as stated.

Sometimes the savings are given as percentages of income instead of amounts. Then each expenditure is a known share of its income, and the expenditure ratio follows at once.

Worked example 7: savings of 20% and 30%

Incomes are 4 : 5. A saves 20%, so spends 80%; B saves 30%, so spends 70%. Expenditures are 4 × 80 : 5 × 70 = 320 : 350 = 32 : 35.

For questions where one person's income and spending change by percentages, see income, expenditure and savings problems.

  • Using one multiplier for both ratios. Incomes 3x and expenditures 5x would assume the same scale.
  • Using the shortcut with unequal savings. It only works when both save the same amount.
  • Getting the cross-product the wrong way round. The denominator is bc − ad and the numerator S(c − d); a sign slip in one must be matched in the other.
  • Stopping at x. The question asks for an income, ax, not the multiplier itself.
  • Skipping the check. Income minus expenditure must equal the stated savings for both people.

Q1. The incomes of A and B are in the ratio 4 : 3 and their expenditures in the ratio 3 : 2. If each saves ₹6,000, what is A's income? easy

✅ Answer: (d) ₹24,000

💡 Explanation: Shortcut: x = S(c − d)/(bc − ad) = 6,000 × 1/(9 − 8) = 6,000, so A's income is 4 × 6,000 = ₹24,000. Check: expenditure is 3 × 6,000 = 18,000 and 24,000 − 18,000 = 6,000.

Q2. The incomes of P and Q are in the ratio 7 : 5 and their expenditures in the ratio 3 : 2. Each saves ₹4,000. What is Q's income? medium

✅ Answer: (a) ₹20,000

💡 Explanation: x = 4,000 × (3 − 2)/(5 × 3 − 7 × 2) = 4,000, so Q's income is 5 × 4,000 = ₹20,000.

Q3. Incomes are in the ratio 3 : 4 and expenditures in the ratio 2 : 3. Each person saves ₹5,000. What is the first person's expenditure? medium

✅ Answer: (c) ₹10,000

💡 Explanation: x = 5,000 × (2 − 3)/(4 × 2 − 3 × 3) = 5,000, so the incomes are 15,000 and 20,000. The first spends 15,000 − 5,000 = ₹10,000.

Q4. The incomes of X and Y are in the ratio 5 : 4 and their expenditures 7 : 5. X earns ₹25,000 and both save the same amount. How much does each save? medium

✅ Answer: (b) ₹7,500

💡 Explanation: Y earns 20,000. Equal savings: 25,000 − 7y = 20,000 − 5y, so y = 2,500. X spends 17,500 and saves ₹7,500.

Q5. Incomes are in the ratio 2 : 3. The first person saves 25% of income and the second 40%. What is the ratio of their expenditures? easy

✅ Answer: (d) 5 : 6

💡 Explanation: They spend 75% and 60%: 2 × 75 : 3 × 60 = 150 : 180 = 5 : 6.

Why do we need two different multipliers, x and y?

The income ratio and the expenditure ratio are separate facts. Incomes are 3x and 2x for some x, and expenditures 5y and 3y for a different y. Using the same letter for both would wrongly assume the same scale.

Where does the shortcut x = S(c − d)/(bc − ad) come from?

With equal savings S, the equations are ax − cy = S and bx − dy = S. Eliminating y gives x(bc − ad) = S(c − d). It only applies when both people save the same amount.

What if the savings are not equal?

Write the two equations with their own savings figures and solve them as simultaneous equations, by elimination or by cross-multiplication. The shortcut does not apply.
