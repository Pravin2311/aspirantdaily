AP and GP Formulas: nth Term, Sum of n Terms, Number of Terms, Infinite GP and AM–GM

AP: a + (n − 1)d and n/2 × (first + last) • Counting terms • Multiples in a range • GP: arⁿ⁻¹ and a(rⁿ − 1)/(r − 1) • Infinite GP: a/(1 − r) • AM and GM

SSC Banking Railway 📅 Last updated: 2026-11-02

An arithmetic progression (AP) adds the same number, the common difference d, at every step: 5, 8, 11, 14 … has a = 5 and d = 3. Two formulas cover most AP questions:

nth term: Tₙ = a + (n − 1)d

Sum of n terms: Sₙ = n/2 × (first + last) = n/2 × [2a + (n − 1)d]

The (n − 1) appears because the first term has had no difference added yet. The sum formula pairs the first term with the last, the second with the second-last, and so on: every pair has the same total.

Worked example 1: the 20th term

For 5, 8, 11, …: T₂₀ = 5 + 19 × 3 = 62.

Worked example 2: the sum of 20 terms

S₂₀ = 20/2 × (5 + 62) = 10 × 67 = 670.

Rearranging the nth-term formula gives the number of terms directly:

n = (last − first)/d + 1

Worked example 3: 7, 11, …, 207

n = (207 − 7)/4 + 1 = 50 + 1 = 51 terms.

Questions about multiples in a range are APs in disguise. Find the first and last multiples inside the range, count the terms, then use the sum formula. The divisibility rules help find the end points.

Worked example 4: multiples of 7 up to 100

The multiples run 7, 14, …, 98: that is (98 − 7)/7 + 1 = 14 terms. Their sum is 14/2 × (7 + 98) = 735.

When two terms are given, their difference fixes d, because the gap between the mth and nth terms is (n − m)d.

Worked example 5: d from two terms

The 4th term is 17 and the 10th is 41. Six steps add 24, so d = 4.

Worked example 6: the first term

From the 4th term, go back 3 steps: a = 17 − 3 × 4 = 5. The AP is 5, 9, 13, 17, …

A geometric progression (GP) multiplies by the same number, the common ratio r, at every step: 3, 6, 12, 24 … has a = 3 and r = 2.

nth term: Tₙ = a × rⁿ⁻¹

Sum of n terms: Sₙ = a(rⁿ − 1)/(r − 1) for r ≠ 1

Worked example 7: the 8th term

For 3, 6, 12, …: T₈ = 3 × 2⁷ = 3 × 128 = 384.

Worked example 8: the sum of 8 terms

S₈ = 3 × (2⁸ − 1)/(2 − 1) = 3 × 255 = 765. Adding the eight terms one by one gives the same total.

When 0 < r < 1 the terms shrink, and adding infinitely many of them approaches a finite limit:

Infinite GP sum = a/(1 − r), valid only for −1 < r < 1

Worked example 9: 12 + 6 + 3 + …

a = 12 and r = 1/2, so the sum is 12 ÷ 1/2 = 24. Recurring decimals are infinite GPs too; see recurring decimals to fractions.

Between two positive numbers a and b, the arithmetic mean is (a + b)/2 and the geometric mean is √(ab). They are the middle terms of an AP and a GP through a and b. The AM is never smaller than the GM, and they are equal only when a = b.

Worked example 10: AM and GM of 4 and 16

AM = 10 and GM = √64 = 8. So 4, 10, 16 is an AP and 4, 8, 16 is a GP.

  • Using n instead of n − 1. The nth term adds (n − 1) differences.
  • Forgetting the + 1 when counting terms. (last − first)/d counts gaps.
  • Using the infinite formula when r ≥ 1. The terms grow and there is no finite sum.
  • Mixing up AP and GP. Check differences and ratios before choosing a formula.
  • Wrong end points for multiples. The first multiple of 6 above 100 is 102, not 100.

For sums of squares and cubes, see sum of natural numbers, squares and cubes.

Q1. What is the 15th term of the AP 3, 8, 13, …? easy

✅ Answer: (a) 73

💡 Explanation: a = 3 and d = 5, so the 15th term is 3 + 14 × 5 = 73. Using 15 × 5 instead of 14 × 5 gives the trap answer 78.

Q2. What is the sum of the first 25 odd numbers? easy

✅ Answer: (c) 625

💡 Explanation: The odd numbers form an AP with a = 1 and d = 2. Sum = 25/2 × (2 + 24 × 2) = 625, which is also 25².

Q3. What is the 6th term of the GP 2, 6, 18, …? easy

✅ Answer: (b) 486

💡 Explanation: a = 2 and r = 3, so the 6th term is 2 × 3⁵ = 2 × 243 = 486.

Q4. What is the sum of the infinite GP 9 + 3 + 1 + …? medium

✅ Answer: (d) 13.5

💡 Explanation: a = 9 and r = 1/3, so the sum is 9 ÷ (1 − 1/3) = 9 ÷ 2/3 = 13.5.

Q5. How many three-digit numbers are divisible by 6? medium

✅ Answer: (a) 150

💡 Explanation: The first is 102 and the last 996. Number of terms = (996 − 102)/6 + 1 = 149 + 1 = 150.

How do I tell an AP from a GP?

Subtract neighbouring terms: a constant difference means an AP. Divide neighbouring terms: a constant ratio means a GP. 2, 6, 18 has differences 4 and 12 but a constant ratio of 3, so it is a GP.

Why is there a + 1 when counting terms?

(last − first)/d counts the gaps between terms, not the terms. There is always one more term than there are gaps, just as 5 fence posts have 4 gaps between them.

When does an infinite GP have a sum?

Only when the common ratio is between −1 and 1. Then each term shrinks and the total approaches a/(1 − r). For r = 2 or r = −3, the terms grow and there is no finite sum.
