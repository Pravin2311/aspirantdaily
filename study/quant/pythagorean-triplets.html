Pythagorean Triplets List up to 65, How to Generate Them, and Right-Triangle Shortcuts

The primitive triplets to memorise • Multiples like 6-8-10 • Odd-number rule • Even-number rule • Euclid's formula • Ladders, diagonals and quick checks

A Pythagorean triplet is three whole numbers a, b, c with a² + b² = c², so they can be the sides of a right triangle with c as the hypotenuse. Knowing the common ones turns many geometry and mensuration questions into recognition rather than calculation.

Checked: six primitive triplets

5-12-13, 8-15-17, 7-24-25, 20-21-29, 12-35-37 and 9-40-41 all satisfy a² + b² = c²: 6 of 6.

Checked: the rest of the list

28-45-53, 11-60-61, 33-56-65, 16-63-65 and 3-4-5: 5 of 5. Note that 65 is the hypotenuse of two different primitive triplets.

If a question gives one side and the number is not in your list, build the triplet. These rules give one triplet for each starting number, usually the primitive one; a number can belong to several triplets, so if the result does not match the other given side, try dividing out a common factor first.

  • Odd number a: the triplet is a, (a² − 1)/2, (a² + 1)/2. The other two numbers are consecutive.
  • Even number a: the triplet is a, (a/2)² − 1, (a/2)² + 1. The other two differ by 2.
  • Euclid's formula: for whole numbers m > n, the triplet is m² − n², 2mn, m² + n².

Worked example 1: starting from 7

(49 − 1)/2 = 24 and (49 + 1)/2 = 25, giving 7-24-25.

Worked example 2: starting from 12

(12/2)² − 1 = 35 and 36 + 1 = 37, giving 12-35-37.

Worked example 3: Euclid with m = 5, n = 2

25 − 4 = 21, 2 × 5 × 2 = 20 and 25 + 4 = 29: the triplet 20-21-29, and 21² + 20² = 29² holds (true).

Before squaring and taking roots, divide the given sides by any common factor and look for a known triplet.

Worked example 4: legs 18 and 24

Divide by 6: 3 and 4. So the hypotenuse is 5 × 6 = 30.

Worked example 5: a ladder

A 25 m ladder reaches up a wall with its foot 7 m away. 7-24-25 gives the height straight away: 24 m.

Worked example 6: a rectangle's diagonal

A 16 m by 30 m field: halve to 8 and 15, part of 8-15-17, so the diagonal is 34 m.

The same shortcut works wherever a right angle hides inside another shape.

Worked example 7: a rhombus

A rhombus has diagonals 30 cm and 16 cm. The diagonals bisect each other at right angles, so each side is the hypotenuse of a triangle with legs 15 and 8: 8-15-17 gives a side of 17 cm.

Worked example 8: a cone's slant height

A cone of radius 7 cm and height 24 cm has slant height 25 cm, from 7-24-25.

Worked example 9: distance between two points

From (1, 2) to (6, 14), the horizontal and vertical gaps are 5 and 12, so the distance is 13.

Not every three numbers that look close form a triplet. Test quickly with squares, or use parity: in a primitive triplet exactly one leg is even and the hypotenuse is odd.

Worked example 10: is 6, 8, 11 a triplet?

36 + 64 = 100, but 11² = 121, so no (false). The right hypotenuse for 6 and 8 is 10.

  • Treating the longest side as a leg. The hypotenuse is always the largest number.
  • Forgetting to scale back. After dividing by a common factor, multiply the answer by it.
  • Assuming 65 has one triplet. Both 33-56-65 and 16-63-65 exist, plus multiples like 25-60-65 and 39-52-65.
  • Using triplets for non-right triangles. The shortcut applies only when there is a right angle.

Q1. The two shorter sides of a right triangle are 20 cm and 21 cm. What is the hypotenuse? easy

✅ Answer: (a) 29 cm

💡 Explanation: 20-21-29 is a primitive triplet: 400 + 441 = 841 = 29².

Q2. A right triangle has one leg 9 cm and hypotenuse 41 cm. What is the other leg? easy

✅ Answer: (b) 40 cm

💡 Explanation: 9 is odd, so (81 − 1)/2 = 40 and (81 + 1)/2 = 41. The triplet is 9-40-41.

Q3. The legs of a right triangle are 11 and 60. What is the hypotenuse? medium

✅ Answer: (c) 61

💡 Explanation: By the odd-number rule, 11 gives (121 − 1)/2 = 60 and (121 + 1)/2 = 61.

Q4. A rectangle measures 33 m by 56 m. How long is its diagonal? medium

✅ Answer: (d) 65 m

💡 Explanation: 33-56-65 is a primitive triplet: 1,089 + 3,136 = 4,225 = 65².

Q5. The legs of a right triangle are 15 cm and 36 cm. What is the hypotenuse? medium

✅ Answer: (b) 39 cm

💡 Explanation: Divide by 3 to get 5 and 12, part of 5-12-13. Multiply back: 13 × 3 = 39 cm.

What is a primitive Pythagorean triplet?

One whose three numbers share no common factor, such as 3-4-5 or 8-15-17. Every other triplet, such as 6-8-10 or 15-20-25, is a whole-number multiple of a primitive one.

Can every whole number be part of a triplet?

Every whole number from 3 upwards can. An odd number a gives (a, (a² − 1)/2, (a² + 1)/2), and an even number a gives (a, (a/2)² − 1, (a/2)² + 1).

Is one side of a primitive triplet always even?

Yes. In a primitive triplet, exactly one leg is even and the hypotenuse is always odd. This is a quick way to reject options.
