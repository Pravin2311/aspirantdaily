Average Speed: Why 2xy/(x + y) and Not (x + y)/2

Equal distances • Equal times • Three legs • Unequal distances • Required return speed

SSC Banking Railway 📅 Last updated: 2026-10-20

Average speed is defined as total distance ÷ total time. It is not the average of the speeds. The two agree only when each speed is held for the same length of time. In most exam questions the speeds are held over the same distance, which means the slower leg takes longer and drags the average below the simple mean.

So the first question to ask is: are the distances equal, the times equal, or neither? Each case has its own short formula, and all of them come from the definition.

Let a journey have two legs of the same length d, covered at speeds x and y. The times are d/x and d/y, so the total time is d(x + y)/xy. Dividing the total distance 2d by this time gives:

Average speed (equal distances) = 2xy ÷ (x + y)

The distance d cancels, which is why the question never needs to tell you how long the road is.

Worked example 1: 40 km/h out, 60 km/h back

Average = 2 × 40 × 60 ÷ 100 = 48 km/h, not 50. For a 120 km road, the trip out takes 3 hours and the trip back 2 hours, so 240 km in 5 hours is indeed 48 km/h.

With three equal legs at x, y and z, the same algebra gives 3xyz ÷ (xy + yz + zx).

Worked example 2: three equal stretches

Speeds 30, 40 and 60 km/h. Average = 3 × 30 × 40 × 60 ÷ (1,200 + 2,400 + 1,800) = 216,000 ÷ 5,400 = 40 km/h.

If each speed is held for the same time t, the distances are xt and yt, and the average is (xt + yt) ÷ 2t = (x + y) ÷ 2. Here, and only here, the simple mean is correct.

Worked example 3: equal times

A train runs for one hour at 40 km/h and one hour at 60 km/h. Average = (40 + 60) ÷ 2 = 50 km/h.

When neither the distances nor the times are equal, go back to the definition. Find each leg's time, add them, and divide the total distance by the total time.

Worked example 4: unequal distances

A car covers 120 km at 40 km/h (3 hours) and 180 km at 60 km/h (3 hours). Average = 300 ÷ 6 = 50 km/h. The legs happen to take equal times, so the simple mean also works; that is a coincidence, not a rule.

Questions often fix the outward speed and the target average, and ask for the return speed. Set 2xy ÷ (x + y) equal to the target A and solve for y: y = Ax ÷ (2x − A).

Worked example 5: the return speed

A driver goes out at 40 km/h and wants to average 48 km/h for the round trip. y = 48 × 40 ÷ (80 − 48) = 1,920 ÷ 32 = 60 km/h.

The formula also shows when a target cannot be reached. If the target average is twice the outward speed, the denominator 2x − A becomes zero. Going out at 40 km/h and hoping to average 80 km/h would require an infinite return speed, because all the allowed time has already been used on the way out. Some questions test exactly this.

Halts are part of the journey time even though no distance is covered. A bus that stops at stations has a lower average speed than its running speed, and questions sometimes ask how many minutes per hour it stops.

Worked example 6: a 30-minute halt

A bus runs 120 km at 60 km/h, which takes 2 hours, and also halts for 30 minutes. The total time is 2.5 hours, so the average speed is 120 ÷ 2.5 = 48 km/h.

Turned round, if a bus averages 48 km/h including halts but runs at 60 km/h, it loses 12 km of running in every hour. At 60 km/h, 12 km takes 12 minutes, so it stops for 12 minutes in each hour.

  • ✘ Taking the simple mean when the distances are equal.
  • ✘ Using 2xy/(x + y) when the distances are different.
  • ✘ Forgetting to include stoppage time when the question mentions halts.
  • ✘ Assuming any target average is possible. It must be less than twice the first speed.

Q1. A cyclist rides to a town at 30 km/h and returns along the same road at 20 km/h. What is the average speed for the round trip? easy

✅ Answer: (b) 24 km/h

💡 Explanation: Equal distances, so use 2xy/(x + y) = 2 × 30 × 20 ÷ 50 = 24 km/h. The simple mean, 25, ignores the extra time spent at 20 km/h.

Q2. A car covers 100 km at 50 km/h and then 150 km at 75 km/h. What is its average speed? medium

✅ Answer: (a) 62.5 km/h

💡 Explanation: Total distance = 250 km and total time = 2 + 2 = 4 hours, so the average is 250 ÷ 4 = 62.5 km/h. The two legs take equal times, so the simple mean also works here.

Q3. A runner covers three equal stretches at 10, 20 and 30 km/h. What is the average speed, to two decimal places? medium

✅ Answer: (c) 16.36 km/h

💡 Explanation: For three equal distances the average is 3xyz/(xy + yz + zx) = 18,000 ÷ 1,100 = 16.36 km/h.

Q4. A bus travels half of its journey time at 40 km/h and the other half at 80 km/h. What is its average speed? easy

✅ Answer: (d) 60 km/h

💡 Explanation: When the times are equal, the average speed is the simple mean: (40 + 80) ÷ 2 = 60 km/h. 53.33 km/h would be right only for equal distances.

Q5. A man drives to a city at 60 km/h. At what speed must he return along the same road to average 75 km/h for the round trip? hard

✅ Answer: (b) 100 km/h

💡 Explanation: Solve 2 × 60 × y ÷ (60 + y) = 75: 120y = 4,500 + 75y, so y = 100 km/h.

Q6. A car covers one-third of a distance at 20 km/h, one-third at 30 km/h and the rest at 60 km/h. What is its average speed? medium

✅ Answer: (a) 30 km/h

💡 Explanation: Three equal distances: 3 × 20 × 30 × 60 ÷ (600 + 1,800 + 1,200) = 108,000 ÷ 3,600 = 30 km/h.

Is average speed the harmonic mean of the speeds?

For equal distances, yes: 2xy/(x + y) is the harmonic mean of x and y. For equal times it is the arithmetic mean. For anything else, divide total distance by total time, which covers both special cases.

Can the formula 2xy/(x + y) be used when the distances differ?

No. The formula assumes both legs are the same length. With different distances, find the time for each leg separately, add the times, and divide the total distance by the total time.
