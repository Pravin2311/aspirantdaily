Interior Angle of Polygon Formula: Exterior Angles, Sides and Diagonals

Sum of interior angles (n − 2) × 180° • Exterior angles add to 360° • Sides from one angle • Interior : exterior ratios • Diagonals n(n − 3)/2

SSC Railway 📅 Last updated: 2026-10-23

A polygon is a closed figure with straight sides. Almost every polygon question in SSC and railway papers uses just two facts, both true for any convex polygon with n sides.

  • The interior angles add up to (n − 2) × 180°. Drawing diagonals from one vertex cuts the polygon into n − 2 triangles, and each triangle contributes 180°.
  • The exterior angles add up to 360°, whatever the number of sides. Walking once round the boundary turns you through exactly one full turn.

At each vertex the interior and exterior angles lie on a straight line, so they add to 180°. That links the two facts and lets you move between them.

Worked example 1: sum for a hexagon

A hexagon has 6 sides, so its interior angles add to (6 − 2) × 180° = 720°.

In a regular polygon all sides and all angles are equal, so each angle is the sum divided by n.

Worked example 2: each angle of an octagon

(8 − 2) × 180° ÷ 8 = 1080° ÷ 8 = 135°. Faster: the exterior angle is 360° ÷ 8 = 45°, so the interior is 180° − 45° = 135°.

The exterior angle is the fastest route to n, because n = 360° ÷ exterior angle. If the question gives the interior angle, subtract it from 180° first.

Worked example 3: from an exterior angle

Each exterior angle is 24°, so n = 360 ÷ 24 = 15.

Worked example 4: from an interior angle

Each interior angle is 160°, so each exterior angle is 20° and n = 360 ÷ 20 = 18.

Ratio and difference questions use the same 180° link. If the interior and exterior angles are in the ratio p : q, the exterior angle is q/(p + q) × 180°.

Worked example 5: a ratio of 7 : 2

The exterior angle is 2/9 × 180° = 40°, so n = 360 ÷ 40 = 9.

Worked example 6: a difference of 108°

Each interior angle exceeds each exterior angle by 108°. Since they add to 180°, the exterior angle is (180 − 108) ÷ 2 = 36°, and n = 10.

When the angles are not all equal, the interior-sum rule still holds, so a missing angle is the sum minus the angles you know. This type appears often with pentagons and hexagons, where the sums are 540° and 720°.

Worked example 7: a missing angle

A pentagon has angles of 100°, 110°, 95° and 120°. The five angles add to 540°, so the fifth is 540 − 425 = 115°.

Some questions give the angles in a pattern, such as consecutive terms of an arithmetic progression. Write them as a, a + d, a + 2d and so on, add them, and set the total equal to (n − 2) × 180°.

Worked example 8: angles in an arithmetic progression

The angles of a hexagon are in arithmetic progression and the smallest is 95°. Then 6 × 95 + 15d = 720, so 15d = 150 and d = 10°. The largest angle is 95 + 5 × 10 = 145°. Each interior angle of a convex polygon must stay below 180°, which 145° does.

Each vertex joins to every other vertex except itself and its two neighbours, giving n − 3 diagonals per vertex. Every diagonal is counted from both ends, so divide by 2.

Number of diagonals = n(n − 3) ÷ 2

Worked example 9: a decagon

10 × 7 ÷ 2 = 35 diagonals.

Worked example 10: working backwards

A polygon has 54 diagonals. Then n(n − 3) = 108. Trying nearby values, 12 × 9 = 108, so n = 12.

  • ✘ Using n × 180° for the interior sum. It is (n − 2) × 180°.
  • ✘ Giving the interior angle when the question asks for the exterior one.
  • ✘ Counting n(n − 1) ÷ 2 for diagonals. That includes the n sides.
  • ✘ Accepting a number of sides that is not a whole number.

Q1. What is the sum of the interior angles of a 12-sided polygon? easy

✅ Answer: (a) 1800°

💡 Explanation: Sum of interior angles = (n − 2) × 180° = 10 × 180° = 1800°.

Q2. What is each exterior angle of a regular pentagon? easy

✅ Answer: (c) 72°

💡 Explanation: The exterior angles of any polygon add to 360°. A regular pentagon has 5 equal ones, so each is 360° ÷ 5 = 72°. The interior angle, 108°, is the usual distractor.

Q3. Each interior angle of a regular polygon is 150°. How many sides does it have? medium

✅ Answer: (d) 12

💡 Explanation: Each exterior angle is 180° − 150° = 30°. The number of sides is 360° ÷ 30° = 12.

Q4. How many diagonals does a 20-sided polygon have? medium

✅ Answer: (b) 170

💡 Explanation: Number of diagonals = n(n − 3) ÷ 2 = 20 × 17 ÷ 2 = 170. The value 190 counts every pair of vertices, which includes the 20 sides.

Q5. In a regular polygon, the interior and exterior angles are in the ratio 4 : 1. How many sides does it have? medium

✅ Answer: (c) 10

💡 Explanation: Interior and exterior angles add to 180°, so the exterior angle is 1/5 × 180° = 36°. The number of sides is 360° ÷ 36° = 10.

Q6. The interior angles of a polygon add up to 1260°. How many sides does it have? easy

✅ Answer: (a) 9

💡 Explanation: (n − 2) × 180° = 1260°, so n − 2 = 7 and n = 9.

Do the exterior angles add to 360° for polygons that are not regular?

Yes, for any convex polygon. Walking once around the boundary turns you through one full turn, 360°, whatever the side lengths or angles. For regular polygons each exterior angle is simply 360° divided by the number of sides.

Can a regular polygon have an interior angle of 100°?

No. Its exterior angle would be 80°, and 360 ÷ 80 = 4.5 is not a whole number. A regular polygon exists only when 360 divided by the exterior angle gives a whole number of sides of at least 3.
