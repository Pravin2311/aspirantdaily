Ratio and Proportion: Combining Ratios and Mean, Third and Fourth Proportionals

Combining two ratios • Duplicate, triplicate and compounded ratios • Fourth, third and mean proportional • Sharing by fractions • Changing a ratio

SSC Banking Railway 📅 Last updated: 2026-10-23

A ratio compares two quantities of the same kind by division. A : B = 3 : 5 means that for every 3 units of A there are 5 units of B, so A is 3/5 of B. The actual amounts can be 3 and 5, 30 and 50, or 3k and 5k for any k. Writing the quantities as 3k and 5k is the starting point for almost every ratio problem.

A proportion is a statement that two ratios are equal, written a : b :: c : d. The outer terms a and d are the extremes and the inner terms b and c are the means. In any proportion, the product of the extremes equals the product of the means: ad = bc. Every proportional formula on this page comes from that one rule.

When you know A : B and B : C, make the common term B equal in both, then read off A : B : C. Multiply each ratio by whatever makes the two B values match, usually their LCM.

Worked example 1: A : B = 2 : 3 and B : C = 4 : 5

The B values are 3 and 4, with LCM 12. Multiply the first ratio by 4 and the second by 3: A : B = 8 : 12 and B : C = 12 : 15. So A : B : C = 8 : 12 : 15. If ₹7,000 is shared this way, A gets 8/35 × 7,000 = ₹1,600.

A quick method for three ratios is to cross-multiply in a pattern, but making the shared term equal is easier to remember and works for any number of ratios.

The duplicate ratio of 3 : 4 is 9 : 16, and its sub-duplicate ratio is √3 : 2. The compounded ratio multiplies the first terms together and the second terms together.

Worked example 2: compounding three ratios

The compounded ratio of 2 : 3, 4 : 5 and 5 : 8 is (2 × 4 × 5) : (3 × 5 × 8) = 40 : 120 = 1 : 3.

To share an amount in a ratio of fractions, clear the fractions first by multiplying every term by the LCM of the denominators.

Worked example 6: shares of 1/2 : 1/3 : 1/4

Multiply by 12 to get 6 : 4 : 3, a total of 13 parts. Out of ₹1,300, the first share is 6/13 × 1,300 = ₹600.

When the same number is added to both terms to change the ratio, write the new ratio as an equation and cross-multiply.

Worked example 7: adding to both terms

What must be added to each term of 3 : 5 to make it 5 : 6? (3 + x) ÷ (5 + x) = 5 ÷ 6, so 18 + 6x = 25 + 5x and x = 7. Check: 10 : 12 = 5 : 6.

Worked example 8: incomes and expenses

Two people's incomes are in the ratio 3 : 4 and their expenses in the ratio 5 : 7. Each saves ₹3,000. With incomes 3x and 4x and expenses 5y and 7y: 3x − 5y = 3,000 and 4x − 7y = 3,000. Solving gives y = 3,000 and x = 6,000, so the first income is ₹18,000.

Q1. If A : B = 3 : 4 and B : C = 6 : 7, what is A : C? easy

✅ Answer: (b) 9 : 14

💡 Explanation: Make B the same in both ratios. LCM of 4 and 6 is 12, so A : B = 9 : 12 and B : C = 12 : 14. Then A : C = 9 : 14.

Q2. What is the fourth proportional to 5, 8 and 15? easy

✅ Answer: (d) 24

💡 Explanation: If 5 : 8 :: 15 : d, the product of the extremes equals the product of the means: 5d = 8 × 15 = 120, so d = 24.

Q3. What is the mean proportional between 9 and 16? easy

✅ Answer: (a) 12

💡 Explanation: The mean proportional x satisfies 9 : x :: x : 16, so x² = 9 × 16 = 144 and x = 12.

Q4. What is the third proportional to 16 and 24? medium

✅ Answer: (c) 36

💡 Explanation: The third proportional c satisfies 16 : 24 :: 24 : c, so 16c = 24² = 576 and c = 36.

Q5. ₹1,080 is divided in the ratio 1/3 : 1/4 : 1/6. What is the largest share? medium

✅ Answer: (a) ₹480

💡 Explanation: Multiply each fraction by the LCM 12 to get 4 : 3 : 2, which is 9 parts. The largest share is 4/9 × 1,080 = ₹480.

Q6. What number must be subtracted from each of 23, 30, 57 and 78 so that the results are in proportion? hard

✅ Answer: (d) 6

💡 Explanation: Set (23 − x)(78 − x) = (30 − x)(57 − x). The x² terms cancel, leaving 1,794 − 101x = 1,710 − 87x, so 14x = 84 and x = 6. Check: 17 : 24 :: 51 : 72.

What is the difference between the third and the fourth proportional?

The fourth proportional to a, b and c is the d in a : b :: c : d, so d = bc ÷ a. The third proportional to a and b is the c in a : b :: b : c, so the middle term repeats and c = b² ÷ a.

Why do we multiply fractional ratios by the LCM?

Multiplying every term of a ratio by the same number does not change the ratio. Using the LCM of the denominators turns the fractions into whole numbers, which are much easier to add and divide.
