Maximum Value of a sin x + b cos x Is √(a² + b²): Max and Min of Common Trigonometric Expressions

a sin x + b cos x: ±√(a² + b²) • Adding a constant • a sin²x + b cos²x: between a and b • a tan²x + b cot²x: at least 2√(ab) • sin x cos x • Reciprocals

SSC 📅 Last updated: 2026-11-02

For any numbers a and b, the expression a sin x + b cos x can be rewritten as a single sine wave, √(a² + b²) × sin(x + α), for some fixed angle α. A sine wave runs between −1 and +1, so:

Maximum of a sin x + b cos x = √(a² + b²)    Minimum = −√(a² + b²)

The angle α only decides where the maximum occurs, not how large it is, so exam questions almost never need it. You can answer with the amplitude alone.

The sum is never a + b, because sin x and cos x never both equal 1 at once. Pythagorean triplets make these questions quick: 3 and 4 give 5, 5 and 12 give 13, 8 and 15 give 17. See Pythagorean triplets.

Worked example 1: 3 sin x + 4 cos x

Maximum = √(9 + 16) = 5, minimum −5.

Is the maximum really reached?

At the angle where tan x = 3/4 (about 36.87°), sin x = 3/5 and cos x = 4/5, so 3 sin x + 4 cos x = 9/5 + 16/5 = 5. The maximum is attained, not just approached.

A constant c shifts the whole range: c + a sin x + b cos x runs from c − √(a² + b²) to c + √(a² + b²).

Worked example 2: 7 + 5 sin x − 12 cos x

√(25 + 144) = 13, so the maximum is 7 + 13 = 20.

Worked example 3: the minimum of the same expression

7 − 13 = −6.

For a reciprocal such as 1 ÷ (expression), the largest value of the fraction comes from the smallest positive value of the denominator.

Worked example 4: 1 ÷ (3 sin x + 4 cos x + 10)

The denominator runs from 5 to 15, so the fraction's maximum is 1/5 = 0.2 and its minimum is 1/15.

Replace sin²x by 1 − cos²x (or the other way round). a sin²x + b cos²x = a + (b − a)cos²x, and cos²x runs from 0 to 1, so the expression lies between a and b.

a sin²x + b cos²x lies between a and b (whichever is smaller is the minimum)

Worked example 5: 4 sin²x + 9 cos²x at x = 0°

sin 0° = 0 and cos 0° = 1, giving the maximum 9.

Worked example 6: the same at x = 90°

sin 90° = 1 and cos 90° = 0, giving the minimum 4.

When two positive terms multiply to a constant, their sum is smallest when they are equal (AM ≥ GM). Since tan²x × cot²x = 1:

a tan²x + b cot²x ≥ 2√(ab) for positive a and b. There is no maximum.

Worked example 7: 9 tan²x + 4 cot²x

Minimum = 2√36 = 12, reached when 9 tan²x = 4 cot²x, that is tan²x = 2/3.

The same rule gives sin²x + cosec²x ≥ 2 and sec²x + cos²x ≥ 2, each reaching 2 only when both terms equal 1. For products, sin x cos x = (1/2) sin 2x, so it runs from −1/2 to 1/2.

Worked example 8: sin x cos x

At x = 45°, sin x cos x = (1/√2)(1/√2) = 0.5, its maximum.

  • Answering a + b. The maximum of a sin x + b cos x is √(a² + b²), which is always smaller unless a or b is 0.
  • Letting a minus sign change the range. 6 sin x − 8 cos x still ranges over ±10.
  • Using √(a² + b²) for squared forms. a sin²x + b cos²x simply lies between a and b.
  • Looking for a maximum of tan²x + cot²x. It grows without limit; only the minimum exists.
  • Forgetting the constant. Add c to both ends of the range.

For identities used in these rewrites, see trigonometric identities.

Q1. What is the maximum value of 8 sin x + 15 cos x? easy

✅ Answer: (b) 17

💡 Explanation: The maximum of a sin x + b cos x is √(a² + b²) = √(64 + 225) = √289 = 17. 23 would need sin x and cos x to equal 1 together, which never happens.

Q2. What is the minimum value of 6 sin x − 8 cos x? easy

✅ Answer: (a) −10

💡 Explanation: The range is −√(36 + 64) to +√(36 + 64), so the minimum is −10. The minus sign on b does not change √(a² + b²).

Q3. What is the maximum value of 2 sin²x + 5 cos²x? medium

✅ Answer: (d) 5

💡 Explanation: Write it as 2 + 3cos²x. Since cos²x runs from 0 to 1, the expression runs from 2 to 5. The maximum is 5.

Q4. What is the minimum value of 16 tan²x + 9 cot²x? medium

✅ Answer: (c) 24

💡 Explanation: By AM ≥ GM, 16 tan²x + 9 cot²x ≥ 2√(16 × 9) = 2 × 12 = 24, because tan²x × cot²x = 1.

Q5. What is the maximum value of 7 + 3 sin x − 4 cos x? easy

✅ Answer: (b) 12

💡 Explanation: 3 sin x − 4 cos x ranges from −5 to 5, so adding 7 gives a range of 2 to 12. The maximum is 12.

Why is the maximum √(a² + b²) and not a + b?

sin x and cos x cannot both equal 1 at the same angle. Writing a sin x + b cos x as √(a² + b²) × sin(x + α) for a suitable angle α shows the expression is a single sine wave with amplitude √(a² + b²).

Does the sign of a or b matter?

Not for the size of the range. a sin x − b cos x still runs from −√(a² + b²) to +√(a² + b²); the sign only shifts where the maximum occurs.

When does the AM ≥ GM rule apply?

When the two terms are positive and their product is constant, as with a tan²x and b cot²x (product ab). Then their sum is at least 2√(ab), with equality when the two terms are equal.
