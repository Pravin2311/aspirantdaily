Circle Theorems for SSC: Chord, Tangent, Angle and Common Tangent Properties with Worked Examples

Chord length 2√(r² − d²) • Tangent ⟂ radius • Equal tangents • Angle at centre = 2 × angle at circumference • Cyclic quadrilateral • PA × PB = PC × PD • Common tangents

SSC 📅 Last updated: 2026-11-02

A chord joins two points on a circle; the diameter is the longest chord. Three facts solve almost every chord question:

  • The perpendicular from the centre to a chord bisects the chord, and the line from the centre to the midpoint of a chord is perpendicular to it.
  • Equal chords are equally far from the centre, and chords equally far from the centre are equal. A longer chord is closer to the centre.
  • Equal chords subtend equal angles at the centre.

The first fact gives a right triangle with the radius r as hypotenuse, the distance d from the centre as one side and half the chord as the other:

Chord length = 2√(r² − d²)

Worked example 1: chord from radius and distance

Radius 13 cm, chord 5 cm from the centre. Half-chord = √(169 − 25) = 12, so the chord is 24 cm.

  • The angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference standing on the same arc.
  • Angles in the same segment are equal.
  • The angle in a semicircle is 90°.
  • In a cyclic quadrilateral (all four vertices on the circle), opposite angles add up to 180°, and an exterior angle equals the interior opposite angle.

Worked example 2: centre and circumference

An arc subtends 35° at a point on the circle. At the centre it subtends 2 × 35° = 70°.

Worked example 3: cyclic quadrilateral

In cyclic quadrilateral PQRS, ∠P = 75°. Then ∠R = 180° − 75° = 105°, and the exterior angle at R also equals 75°.

  • A tangent touches the circle at exactly one point and is perpendicular to the radius there.
  • The two tangents from an external point are equal in length and make equal angles with the line joining the point to the centre.
  • The angle between the two tangents and the angle between the two radii at the points of contact add up to 180°.
  • Alternate segment theorem: the angle between a tangent and a chord equals the angle in the alternate segment.
  • If a quadrilateral has a circle touching all four sides, its opposite sides add up to the same total (AB + CD = AD + BC), because equal tangents pair up.

The right angle at the point of contact gives:

Tangent length = √(d² − r²), where d is the distance from the external point to the centre.

Worked example 4: tangent length

A point is 17 cm from the centre of a circle of radius 8 cm. Tangent = √(289 − 64) = √225 = 15 cm.

Worked example 5: angle between tangents

Tangents PA and PB touch a circle with centre O, and ∠AOB = 110°. In quadrilateral PAOB the angles at A and B are 90° each, so ∠APB = 180° − 110° = 70°.

When two chords AB and CD cross at P inside a circle, the products of their pieces are equal. When P is outside, the same rule holds for secants, and a tangent PT counts as a secant whose two points have merged:

PA × PB = PC × PD    and    PT² = PA × PB

Worked example 6: crossing chords

AP = 4, PB = 9 and CP = 6. Then 36 = 6 × PD, so PD = 6.

Worked example 7: tangent and secant

From an external point P, a secant meets the circle at A and B with PA = 4 cm and PB = 16 cm. The tangent from P is √(4 × 16) = 8 cm.

For two circles with radii r₁ and r₂ and centres d apart:

TangentCrosses the line of centres?Length
Direct (external)No√(d² − (r₁ − r₂)²)
Transverse (internal)Yes, between the circles√(d² − (r₁ + r₂)²)

Worked example 8: both tangents

Radii 8 cm and 3 cm, centres 13 cm apart. Direct tangent = √(169 − 25) = 12 cm. Transverse tangent = √(169 − 121) = √48 ≈ 6.93 cm.

Common traps: using r₁ + r₂ for the direct tangent (it is the difference), forgetting that the perpendicular from the centre bisects only the chord it meets, and treating any quadrilateral as cyclic. For angle formulas at the centres of a triangle, see triangle centres.

Q1. A chord of length 16 cm is drawn in a circle of radius 10 cm. How far is the chord from the centre? easy

✅ Answer: (a) 6 cm

💡 Explanation: The perpendicular from the centre bisects the chord, giving a right triangle with hypotenuse 10 and one side 8. Distance = √(100 − 64) = 6 cm.

Q2. A point is 25 cm from the centre of a circle of radius 7 cm. What is the length of the tangent from the point? easy

✅ Answer: (c) 24 cm

💡 Explanation: The tangent is perpendicular to the radius at the point of contact, so tangent = √(25² − 7²) = √576 = 24 cm.

Q3. Chords AB and CD intersect inside a circle at P. If AP = 3 cm, PB = 8 cm and CP = 4 cm, what is PD? medium

✅ Answer: (d) 6 cm

💡 Explanation: Intersecting chords give AP × PB = CP × PD, so 24 = 4 × PD and PD = 6 cm.

Q4. ABCD is a cyclic quadrilateral with ∠B = 110°. What is ∠D? easy

✅ Answer: (b) 70°

💡 Explanation: Opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral add up to 180°, so ∠D = 180° − 110° = 70°.

Q5. Two circles of radii 9 cm and 4 cm have centres 13 cm apart. What is the length of their direct common tangent? hard

✅ Answer: (a) 12 cm

💡 Explanation: Direct common tangent = √(d² − (r₁ − r₂)²) = √(169 − 25) = √144 = 12 cm.

What is the alternate segment theorem?

The angle between a tangent and a chord through the point of contact equals the angle that chord makes at any point in the opposite (alternate) segment of the circle. It turns a tangent angle into an inscribed angle you can work with.

How many common tangents can two circles have?

Four if the circles are separate (two direct, two transverse), three if they touch externally, two if they intersect, one if they touch internally, and none if one lies inside the other without touching.

Why does a transverse common tangent need d > r₁ + r₂?

The formula √(d² − (r₁ + r₂)²) only gives a real length when the centres are farther apart than the sum of the radii, which is exactly when the circles do not touch or overlap.
