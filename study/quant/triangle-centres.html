Centroid, Incentre, Circumcentre and Orthocentre: Properties and Angle Formulas for SSC

Medians, bisectors, perpendicular bisectors, altitudes • ∠BIC = 90° + A/2 • ∠BOC = 2A • ∠BHC = 180° − A • R and r • Equilateral and right triangles

SSC 📅 Last updated: 2026-11-02

Each centre is where three special lines of a triangle meet. Learn the line first, and most properties follow.

CentreLines that meetKey factAngle formula
Centroid (G)Medians (vertex to midpoint of opposite side)Divides each median 2 : 1 from the vertexNo angle formula
Incentre (I)Angle bisectorsEquidistant from the three sides; centre of the inscribed circle∠BIC = 90° + A/2
Circumcentre (O)Perpendicular bisectors of the sidesEquidistant from the three vertices; centre of the circumcircle∠BOC = 2A (A acute)
Orthocentre (H)Altitudes (vertex perpendicular to opposite side)Lies on the right-angle vertex of a right triangle∠BHC = 180° − A

A memory hook: I for inscribed circle and bisectors; O for the circle outside; H for heights (altitudes); G for the centre of gravity, where a cardboard triangle balances.

These are the most tested results in SSC geometry. Each takes one line to apply.

Worked example 1: incentre

In triangle ABC, ∠A = 70°. Then ∠BIC = 90° + 35° = 125°. Why: ∠IBC + ∠ICB = (B + C)/2 = (180° − A)/2, so the angle at I is 180° minus that, which simplifies to 90° + A/2.

Worked example 2: circumcentre

∠A = 50° in an acute triangle. ∠BOC = 2 × 50° = 100°, because the angle at the centre of a circle is twice the angle at the circumference on the same arc.

Worked example 3: orthocentre

∠A = 65°. ∠BHC = 180° − 65° = 115°. The altitudes from B and C, with the two sides at A, form a quadrilateral with two right angles, so the angle at H is 180° − A.

A quick way to keep them apart: for A = 60°, the incentre gives 120°, the circumcentre 120° and the orthocentre 120°. The three formulas agree only at 60°, which makes a handy check in an equilateral triangle, where all four centres are the same point.

The centroid cuts each median so that the part next to the vertex is twice the part next to the side. The three medians also divide the triangle into six smaller triangles of equal area.

Worked example 4: length along a median

Median AD is 9 cm long. AG = 2/3 × 9 = 6 cm and GD = 3 cm.

In coordinate geometry the centroid is simply the average of the three vertices: G = ((x₁ + x₂ + x₃)/3, (y₁ + y₂ + y₃)/3).

Worked example 5: centroid from coordinates

Vertices (2, 3), (4, 7) and (6, −1). G = (12/3, 9/3) = (4, 3).

Two formulas cover any triangle, where Δ is the area and s the semi-perimeter:

R = abc ÷ 4Δ    and    r = Δ ÷ s

Worked example 6: the 13-14-15 triangle

s = 21, and Heron's formula gives Δ = √(21 × 8 × 7 × 6) = 84. R = 13 × 14 × 15 ÷ 336 = 8.125 and r = 84 ÷ 21 = 4.

Right triangles are simpler. The circumcentre is the midpoint of the hypotenuse, so R = hypotenuse ÷ 2, and the orthocentre sits at the right-angle vertex.

Worked example 7: 6-8-10 triangle

The hypotenuse is 10, so R = 5. Area = 24 and s = 12, so r = 2. For a right triangle, r also equals (sum of legs − hypotenuse)/2 = (14 − 10)/2 = 2.

Equilateral triangles have all four centres at one point, with R = a/√3, r = a/(2√3) and R = 2r.

Worked example 8: equilateral, side 12

R = 12/√3 = 4√3 ≈ 6.93 and r = 2√3 ≈ 3.46, exactly half.

  • Swapping 90° + A/2 and 180° − A. Incentre adds half of A to 90°; orthocentre subtracts A from 180°.
  • Using 2 : 1 from the wrong end. The longer part of a median is next to the vertex.
  • Assuming every centre is inside. Only the centroid and incentre always are.
  • Using ∠BOC = 2A for an obtuse angle. Then the non-reflex angle is 360° − 2A.
  • Confusing r and R in equilateral triangles. The circumradius is the larger one, twice the inradius.

For angle sums of polygons, see polygon angles.

Q1. I is the incentre of triangle ABC and ∠A = 80°. What is ∠BIC? easy

✅ Answer: (a) 130°

💡 Explanation: At the incentre, ∠BIC = 90° + A/2 = 90° + 40° = 130°. 160° would be the circumcentre's 2A.

Q2. O is the circumcentre of an acute triangle ABC and ∠BOC = 140°. What is ∠A? easy

✅ Answer: (c) 70°

💡 Explanation: The angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference: ∠BOC = 2∠A, so ∠A = 70°.

Q3. A right triangle has legs 9 cm and 12 cm. What is its circumradius? medium

✅ Answer: (b) 7.5 cm

💡 Explanation: The hypotenuse is 15 cm, and in a right triangle the circumcentre is the midpoint of the hypotenuse, so R = 15 ÷ 2 = 7.5 cm.

Q4. H is the orthocentre of triangle ABC and ∠BHC = 100°. What is ∠A? medium

✅ Answer: (d) 80°

💡 Explanation: At the orthocentre, ∠BHC = 180° − A, so ∠A = 180° − 100° = 80°.

Q5. What is the inradius of an equilateral triangle of side 6√3 cm? medium

✅ Answer: (c) 3 cm

💡 Explanation: For an equilateral triangle r = a ÷ (2√3) = 6√3 ÷ 2√3 = 3 cm. The circumradius is twice that, 6 cm.

Which triangle centre always lies inside the triangle?

The centroid and the incentre are always inside. The circumcentre and orthocentre are inside only for acute triangles; for a right triangle they lie on it (midpoint of the hypotenuse and the right-angle vertex), and for an obtuse triangle they lie outside.

What is the Euler line?

In any triangle that is not equilateral, the orthocentre H, centroid G and circumcentre O lie on one straight line, with HG = 2 × GO. The incentre is not on this line except in an isosceles triangle.

Does ∠BOC = 2A work for an obtuse angle A?

If A is obtuse, O lies outside the triangle on the far side of BC, and the reflex angle at O is 2A. The non-reflex angle BOC is then 360° − 2A. Exams usually stick to acute triangles for this formula.
