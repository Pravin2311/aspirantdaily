Remainder Problems: Negative Remainders, Cycles and Large Powers

Remainder of a product • Negative remainders • Powers of (divisor ± 1) • Cycles of remainders • Prime divisors

SSC Banking 📅 Last updated: 2026-10-20

Remainder questions look frightening because the numbers are huge: 2¹⁰⁰, or a product of five three-digit numbers. You never need to calculate those numbers. One rule does almost all the work: the remainder of a product equals the product of the remainders, reduced again by the divisor. The same holds for sums.

So you replace every number with its remainder first, multiply those small remainders, and divide once more at the end. Large powers are just repeated products, so the same rule applies to them, helped by two shortcuts covered below: negative remainders and cycles.

Worked example 1: a product

17 × 23 × 29 divided by 6. The remainders are 5, 5 and 5. Their product is 125, and 125 ÷ 6 leaves remainder 5.

When a number is just below the divisor, write its remainder as a small negative number. 47 divided by 49 leaves 47, but it is easier to think of 47 as 2 short of 49, a remainder of −2. Negative remainders keep the multiplication small.

Worked example 2: two numbers just below the divisor

47 × 48 divided by 49. Remainders −2 and −1 multiply to 2, so the remainder is 2.

If the final result is negative, add the divisor to make it a proper remainder between 0 and the divisor.

Sums follow the same rule: replace each term by its remainder, add, and reduce once more. With 9 as the divisor this is especially fast, because a number leaves the same remainder as its digit sum.

Worked example 3: a sum divided by 9

1,234 + 5,678 + 9,012 divided by 9. The digit sums give remainders 1, 8 and 3. Their total is 12, and 12 ÷ 9 leaves remainder 3. There is no need to add the three numbers first.

When the base is one more than a multiple of the divisor, its remainder is 1, and every power of it also leaves 1. When the base is one less, its remainder is −1, and the power leaves 1 for an even exponent and −1 for an odd one.

Worked example 4: 2¹⁰⁰ ÷ 3

2 is one less than 3, so its remainder is −1. With an even exponent, (−1)¹⁰⁰ = 1, so the remainder is 1.

Worked example 5: 7⁵⁰ ÷ 8

7 leaves −1 on division by 8, and 50 is even, so the remainder is 1.

With an odd exponent the sign stays negative: 3³³ ÷ 4 gives (−1)³³ = −1, which is the remainder 3.

Worked example 6: 17²³ ÷ 18

17 leaves −1 on division by 18, and 23 is odd, so the result is −1, that is, a remainder of 18 − 1 = 17.

When neither shortcut applies, list the remainders of the first few powers. They always start repeating, and once you see a remainder of 1 the cycle restarts. Divide the exponent by the cycle length and use the position, exactly as with unit digits.

Worked example 7: 2¹⁰⁰ ÷ 7

2¹ leaves 2, 2² leaves 4, 2³ = 8 leaves 1. The cycle is 2, 4, 1, of length 3. 100 ÷ 3 leaves remainder 1, so take the first term: 2.

Worked example 8: 2³¹ ÷ 5

Powers of 2 leave 2, 4, 3, 1 on division by 5, a cycle of 4. 31 ÷ 4 leaves 3, so the answer is the third term, 3.

For a prime divisor p, a base not divisible by p always has a cycle whose length divides p − 1. This is Fermat's little theorem. For example, 3⁴⁰ ÷ 41 leaves 1, because 41 is prime and the exponent is 40 = 41 − 1.

  • ✘ Leaving a negative remainder as the final answer. Add the divisor.
  • ✘ Multiplying the original numbers instead of their remainders. Reduce first.
  • ✘ Taking the first term of the cycle when the exponent is an exact multiple of the cycle length. Take the last term.
  • ✘ Applying the prime shortcut to a divisor that is not prime.

The cycle idea is the same one used for unit digits of powers, which are simply remainders on division by 10. Quick divisibility tests help when the divisor is 9 or 11, and the key results are collected in the Quant cheat code.

Q1. What is the remainder when 23 × 37 × 41 is divided by 9? easy

✅ Answer: (b) 7

💡 Explanation: The remainders on division by 9 are 5, 1 and 5. Their product is 25, and 25 ÷ 9 leaves 7.

Q2. What is the remainder when 99 × 98 is divided by 100? easy

✅ Answer: (c) 2

💡 Explanation: 99 is 1 less than 100 and 98 is 2 less, so their remainders can be written as −1 and −2. The product (−1) × (−2) = 2 is the remainder.

Q3. What is the remainder when 5⁷⁰ is divided by 6? medium

✅ Answer: (d) 1

💡 Explanation: 5 leaves remainder −1 on division by 6. (−1)⁷⁰ = 1 because 70 is even, so the remainder is 1.

Q4. What is the remainder when 2⁷⁵ is divided by 7? medium

✅ Answer: (a) 1

💡 Explanation: Powers of 2 leave remainders 2, 4, 1 on division by 7 and then repeat, a cycle of 3. 75 is a multiple of 3, so take the last remainder in the cycle, 1.

Q5. What is the remainder when 29⁴⁷ is divided by 30? medium

✅ Answer: (b) 29

💡 Explanation: 29 leaves remainder −1 on division by 30. An odd power of −1 is −1, and a remainder of −1 means 30 − 1 = 29.

Q6. What is the remainder when 3⁹⁹ is divided by 8? hard

✅ Answer: (d) 3

💡 Explanation: 3² = 9 leaves remainder 1 on division by 8. So 3⁹⁸ = (3²)⁴⁹ leaves 1, and 3⁹⁹ = 3⁹⁸ × 3 leaves 1 × 3 = 3.

Is a negative remainder a valid final answer?

No. A negative remainder is only a working step. To report the answer, add the divisor: a remainder of −3 on division by 10 means the actual remainder is 7.

How do I find the cycle of remainders quickly?

Write out the remainders of the first few powers until you get a remainder of 1. The cycle restarts from there, because multiplying by the base again repeats the earlier remainders in the same order.
