Volume of Cone, Cylinder and Sphere: Formulas, Surface Areas and Ratios

Cylinder πr²h • Cone ⅓πr²h and slant height • Sphere ⁴⁄₃πr³ • Hemisphere • Cone : hemisphere : cylinder = 1 : 2 : 3 • Melting and recasting

SSC Railway 📅 Last updated: 2026-10-23

These solids share one measurement, the radius r of the circular base or of the sphere, and the cylinder and cone also have a height h. Most questions use π = 22/7 with a radius that is a multiple of 7, so the 7 cancels and the arithmetic stays whole.

Cylinderπr²h2πrh2πr(r + h)
Cone⅓πr²hπrlπr(l + r)
Sphere⁴⁄₃πr³4πr²4πr²
Hemisphere⅔πr³2πr²3πr²

For a cone, l is the slant height, the distance from the tip to the edge of the base. It is the hypotenuse of a right triangle with sides r and h, so l = √(r² + h²).

Worked example 1: volume

A cylinder has r = 7 cm and h = 10 cm. V = 22/7 × 49 × 10 = 1,540 cm³.

Its curved surface area is 2 × 22/7 × 7 × 10 = 440 cm². This is the area of the label on a can, which unrolls into a rectangle of length 2πr and height h.

Most cone questions need the slant height first. Look for familiar right-triangle triples such as 3–4–5, 5–12–13, 7–24–25 and 20–21–29.

Worked example 2: slant height

A cone has r = 7 cm and h = 24 cm. Then l = √(49 + 576) = √625 = 25 cm.

Its curved surface area is πrl = 22/7 × 7 × 25 = 550 cm².

Its volume is ⅓ × 22/7 × 49 × 24 = 1,232 cm³.

Worked example 3: sphere of radius 21 cm

Surface area = 4 × 22/7 × 441 = 5,544 cm².

Its volume is ⁴⁄₃ × 22/7 × 9,261 = 38,808 cm³.

Worked example 4: a solid hemisphere

A solid hemisphere of radius 7 cm has total surface area 3 × 22/7 × 49 = 462 cm²: 308 cm² curved and 154 cm² flat.

When a cone, a hemisphere and a cylinder share the same radius r, and the cone and cylinder have height h = r, their volumes are ⅓πr³, ⅔πr³ and πr³.

Cone : hemisphere : cylinder = 1 : 2 : 3

Worked example 5: cone and hemisphere

With the same radius and h = r, the cone holds ⅓πr³ and the hemisphere ⅔πr³, a ratio of 1 : 2. Even without equal heights, any cone holds exactly one third of the cylinder with the same base and height.

Changing the radius scales the solids quickly. Areas grow with r² and volumes with r³, so doubling the radius of a sphere multiplies its surface area by 4.

Worked example 6: doubling the radius

Doubling a sphere's radius multiplies its volume by 2³ = 8.

When one solid is melted and recast into another, the volume does not change. Write both volumes, set them equal, and solve for the unknown measurement. π cancels from both sides, so you rarely need its value.

Worked example 7: sphere into cylinder

A sphere of radius 6 cm is melted into a cylinder of radius 4 cm. Then ⁴⁄₃π × 216 = π × 16 × h, so h = 288 ÷ 16 = 18 cm.

A pipe or a hollow cylinder has an outer radius R and an inner radius r. The metal in it is the outer cylinder minus the hole: π(R² − r²)h. Factorising R² − r² as (R + r)(R − r) often makes the arithmetic easier.

Worked example 8: metal in a pipe

A pipe 14 cm long has outer radius 8 cm and inner radius 6 cm. The metal volume is 22/7 × (64 − 36) × 14 = 22/7 × 28 × 14 = 1,232 cm³.

Combined solids, such as a cone on top of a hemisphere or a cylinder with hemispherical ends, are handled piece by piece. Add the volumes of the parts. For surface area, add only the faces that remain on the outside: the joined circles are hidden and must not be counted.

  • ✘ Using the height h instead of the slant height l in the cone's surface area.
  • ✘ Forgetting the flat face in a solid hemisphere's total surface area.
  • ✘ Taking the diameter as the radius. Halve it first.
  • ✘ Scaling volumes by r² instead of r³ when the radius changes.

Flat-faced solids are on cube and cuboid. The right-triangle triples used here also appear in square roots questions, and the formula list is in the Quant cheat code.

Q1. What is the volume of a cylinder with radius 14 cm and height 5 cm? (Use π = 22/7.) easy

✅ Answer: (c) 3,080 cm³

💡 Explanation: V = πr²h = 22/7 × 196 × 5 = 22 × 28 × 5 = 3,080 cm³.

Q2. A cone has radius 21 cm and height 20 cm. What is its curved surface area? (Use π = 22/7.) medium

✅ Answer: (a) 1,914 cm²

💡 Explanation: The slant height is √(21² + 20²) = √841 = 29 cm. CSA = πrl = 22/7 × 21 × 29 = 66 × 29 = 1,914 cm².

Q3. What is the surface area of a sphere of radius 7 cm? (Use π = 22/7.) easy

✅ Answer: (b) 616 cm²

💡 Explanation: Surface area = 4πr² = 4 × 22/7 × 49 = 4 × 154 = 616 cm². 154 cm² is the area of one great circle, πr².

Q4. What is the volume of a hemisphere of radius 21 cm? (Use π = 22/7.) medium

✅ Answer: (d) 19,404 cm³

💡 Explanation: V = ⅔πr³ = ⅔ × 22/7 × 9,261 = 19,404 cm³, exactly half the volume of the full sphere, 38,808 cm³.

Q5. How many solid spheres of radius 1 cm can be made by melting a solid sphere of radius 4 cm? easy

✅ Answer: (b) 64

💡 Explanation: Volume scales with the cube of the radius, so the large sphere holds (4 ÷ 1)³ = 64 times as much metal as each small one.

Q6. A cone and a cylinder have the same base and the same height. The cylinder's volume is 1,500 cm³. What is the cone's volume? easy

✅ Answer: (a) 500 cm³

💡 Explanation: A cone holds one third of the cylinder with the same base and height: 1,500 ÷ 3 = 500 cm³.

Should I use π = 22/7 or 3.14?

Use whatever the question states. Most SSC questions choose radii that are multiples of 7 so that 22/7 cancels neatly. If the question gives no value and the radius is a multiple of 7, 22/7 is usually the intended choice.

Why is the hemisphere's total surface area 3πr²?

The curved part is half of the sphere's 4πr², which is 2πr². A solid hemisphere also has a flat circular face of area πr². Together they give 3πr². A hollow bowl has only the curved 2πr².
