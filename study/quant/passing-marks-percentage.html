Passing Marks Percentage Problems: Fail-By and Pass-By Margins, Maximum Marks and Pass Percentages

The pass mark sits between two scores • Two students, one exam • One student and the pass percentage • Pass mark as a percentage • Passing both subjects

SSC Banking Railway 📅 Last updated: 2026-11-02

Every passing-marks question rests on one picture: a pass mark on a number line, with one score below it and one above it.

  • Fails by f marks: score + f = pass mark.
  • Passes by p marks: score − p = pass mark.

When two students are compared, the gap between their scores in marks equals the gap in percentages times the maximum marks. Their marks gap is the fail margin plus the pass margin, because the pass mark lies between them.

Maximum marks = (fail margin + pass margin) ÷ (difference in percentages)

Worked example 1: 30% fails, 40% passes

A student scoring 30% fails by 15 marks; another scoring 40% passes by 35. The 10% gap equals 15 + 35 = 50 marks, so the maximum is 500.

Worked example 2: the pass mark

From the same data: 30% of 500 = 150, plus the 15 marks short, gives a pass mark of 165. Check from the other side: 40% of 500 = 200, minus 35 = 165.

If both students are on the same side of the pass mark, the margins overlap, so subtract them instead of adding.

Worked example 3: both students fail

A student scoring 25% fails by 50 marks and another scoring 35% fails by 10. Their scores differ by 50 − 10 = 40 marks, which is the 10% gap, so the maximum is 400.

Worked example 4: confirming the pass mark

25% of 400 is 100, and 100 + 50 = 150. From the second student: 35% of 400 is 140, and 140 + 10 = 150. Both routes agree, which confirms the subtraction was right. Whenever you find the maximum, compute the pass mark from both students; if the two values differ, you added when you should have subtracted, or the other way round.

Some questions add grace marks: a student who fails by 12 and is given 12 grace marks reaches the pass mark exactly. Treat grace marks just like a fail margin.

When the pass percentage is given, one student is enough. Add the fail margin to the score to get the pass mark, then divide by the pass percentage.

Worked example 5: needs 36%

A student needs 36% to pass, scores 180 and fails by 36. Pass mark = 216, so maximum marks = 216 ÷ 0.36 = 600.

Worked example 6: score as a percentage

The pass mark is 40%. A student scores 25% and fails by 60. The 15% gap equals 60 marks, so the maximum is 400 and the pass mark is 160.

Worked example 7: finding the pass percentage

A student scores 210 out of 600 and fails by 30. Pass mark = 240, which is 240 ÷ 600 = 40%.

When the pass rule applies to an aggregate across papers, add up the maximum marks first.

Worked example 8: two papers of 100

A student must get 33% of the aggregate over two papers of 100 marks each and scores 25 in the first. The aggregate pass mark is 66, so the student needs 41 in the second paper.

Questions about students passing two subjects are two-set problems. Work with the failure percentages, because "failed both" is usually given.

Worked example 9: passing both subjects

70% passed English, 60% passed Maths and 15% failed both. Failed English = 30%, failed Maths = 40%, failed at least one = 30 + 40 − 15 = 55%. So 45% passed both. For more on overlapping groups, see Venn diagram reasoning.

  • Subtracting the margins when one student passes. One below and one above the pass mark means add.
  • Treating the pass mark as the score. A student who fails by 30 needs score + 30.
  • Using the student's percentage as the pass percentage. They are different numbers.
  • Forgetting aggregate rules. 33% of two papers combined is 66 out of 200, not 33 in each.
  • Adding pass percentages in two-subject questions. Work with failures and subtract the overlap once.

The same margin idea solves vote questions; see percentage election problems.

Q1. A student who scores 35% fails by 20 marks, while another who scores 42% passes by 22 marks. What are the maximum marks? easy

✅ Answer: (a) 600

💡 Explanation: The gap between the two scores is 20 + 22 = 42 marks, which equals 42% − 35% = 7% of the maximum. 42 ÷ 0.07 = 600.

Q2. A candidate needs 33% to pass. She scores 125 marks and fails by 40. What are the maximum marks? easy

✅ Answer: (b) 500

💡 Explanation: Pass mark = 125 + 40 = 165, which is 33% of the maximum: 165 ÷ 0.33 = 500.

Q3. The pass mark is 40%. A student scoring 30% fails by 30 marks. What is the pass mark? medium

✅ Answer: (d) 120

💡 Explanation: The 10% gap between 30% and 40% is 30 marks, so the maximum is 300 and the pass mark is 40% of 300 = 120.

Q4. In an exam, 80% of students passed Hindi, 70% passed English and 10% failed both. What percentage passed both? medium

✅ Answer: (c) 60%

💡 Explanation: Failed Hindi = 20%, failed English = 30%. Failed at least one = 20 + 30 − 10 = 40%, so 60% passed both.

Q5. A student scored 260 out of 650 and failed by 26 marks. What is the pass percentage? easy

✅ Answer: (b) 44%

💡 Explanation: Pass mark = 260 + 26 = 286. As a percentage of 650: 286 ÷ 650 × 100 = 44%.

What does 'fails by 20 marks' mean?

The student needed 20 more marks to reach the pass mark. So the pass mark equals the student's score plus 20. 'Passes by 20 marks' means the score is 20 above the pass mark.

Why do the fail and pass margins add up?

The pass mark sits between the two scores. One student is below it by the fail margin and the other above it by the pass margin, so the distance between their scores is the sum of the two margins.

How is passing both subjects related to Venn diagrams?

It is a two-set problem. Find the percentage who failed at least one subject (fail A + fail B − fail both), then subtract from 100% to get those who passed both.
