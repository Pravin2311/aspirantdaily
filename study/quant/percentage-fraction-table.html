Percentage to Fraction Chart: Fractions 1/2 to 1/20, Their Multiples and Percentage-Change Factors

Unit fractions 1/2 to 1/20 • 1/24 to 1/50 • Multiples such as 2/3, 3/8, 5/6 • Increase and decrease factors • Undoing a change • Worked examples

SSC Banking Railway 📅 Last updated: 2026-11-04

Most percentage questions in SSC, banking and railway papers are designed around a handful of fractions. If you see 12.5% and think "one-eighth" straight away, a question like "12.5% of 480" becomes 480 ÷ 8 = 60, with no long multiplication. A percentage is simply a fraction with denominator 100, which is how NCERT's Grade 8 textbook defines it; the word comes from the Latin per centum, "by the hundred". To turn a unit fraction into a percentage, divide 100 by the denominator. This is the same as finding the equivalent fraction with denominator 100: for 1/7 that is 100 ÷ 7 = 14 remainder 2, so 14 2/7%. The remainder over the denominator is the fraction part, which is why so many of these entries share a denominator with the fraction itself.

A useful pair: x/n and (n − x)/n add up to 100%. Knowing 3/8 = 37 1/2% gives 5/8 = 62 1/2% at once.

Fractions make percentage change one step. A 25% increase is "add a quarter", so the value is multiplied by 5/4. To undo a change, use the fraction based on the new value. After a 25% rise the new value is 5 parts out of the original 4, so taking away 1 of those 5 parts is a cut of 1/5, or 20%.

20% decrease× 4/5increase by 1/4 = 25%
25% decrease× 3/4increase by 1/3 = 33 1/3%
50% decrease× 1/2increase by 1/1 = 100%

The rule behind the last column: if a value goes up by 1/n, it must come down by 1/(n + 1) to return; if it goes down by 1/n, it must go up by 1/(n − 1). So a 20% (1/5) fall needs a 25% (1/4) rise. The same idea drives price and consumption problems and successive percentage change.

  1. 37.5% of 480. 37.5% = 3/8, so 480 × 3/8 = 180.
  2. 83 1/3% of 720. 83 1/3% = 5/6, so 720 × 5/6 = 600.
  3. 14 2/7% of 1,540. 14 2/7% = 1/7, so 1,540 ÷ 7 = 220.
  4. A price rises 25% to ₹8,000. What was it before? The new price is 5/4 of the old, so the old price is 8,000 × 4/5 = ₹6,400.
  5. After a 12.5% discount an item costs ₹336. What was the marked price? 12.5% = 1/8, so the price paid is 7/8 of the marked price: 336 ÷ 7/8 = ₹384.
  6. A rise of 25% followed by a fall of 20%. 5/4 × 4/5 = 1, so ₹2,400 ends at ₹2,400: no net change.

Q1. 9 1/11% written as a fraction is: easy

✅ Answer: (b) 1/11

💡 Explanation: 1/11 = 100/11 % = 9 1/11 %. Do not confuse it with 1/9, which is 11 1/9 %.

Q2. A price of ₹990 rises by 11 1/9%. What is the new price? easy

✅ Answer: (c) ₹1,100

💡 Explanation: 11 1/9% = 1/9, so the new price is 990 × 10/9 = ₹1,100.

Q3. A salary is raised by 25%. By what percentage must it be cut to bring it back to the original? medium

✅ Answer: (a) 20%

💡 Explanation: A 25% rise multiplies by 5/4. To undo it, multiply by 4/5, which is a cut of 1/5 = 20%.

Q4. 5/12 as a percentage is: medium

✅ Answer: (d) 41 2/3%

💡 Explanation: 1/12 = 8 1/3%, so 5/12 = 5 × 8 1/3 = 41 2/3% (about 41.67%).

Q5. 53 11/13% of a number is 273. What is the number? hard

✅ Answer: (a) 507

💡 Explanation: 1/13 = 7 9/13%, and 7 × 7 9/13 = 53 11/13%, so the fraction is 7/13. The number is 273 ÷ 7/13 = 273 × 13/7 = 507.

What is 1/7 as a percentage?

1/7 = 14 2/7%, about 14.29%. Multiples follow from it: 2/7 = 28 4/7%, 3/7 = 42 6/7%.

How do I convert any percentage to a fraction?

Write it over 100 and simplify: 35% = 35/100 = 7/20. For a mixed percentage such as 12 1/2%, first turn it into an improper fraction (25/2) and then divide by 100 to get 1/8.
