Number of Handshakes Formula n(n − 1)/2, Diagonals of a Polygon, League Matches, Lines and Triangles from Points

Handshakes = nC2 • Working back from the total • Diagonals n(n − 3)/2 • League, double league and knockout • Lines and triangles when some points are collinear

SSC Banking 📅 Last updated: 2026-11-02

When each of n people shakes hands exactly once with every other person, every handshake is just a pair of people. So the number of handshakes is the number of ways to choose 2 people from n:

Handshakes = nC2 = n(n − 1)/2

The reasoning behind it: each person shakes n − 1 hands, giving n(n − 1), but that counts every handshake twice, once from each side. For more on choosing, see permutations and combinations.

Worked example 1: ten people

10 people, all shaking hands once: 10 × 9 ÷ 2 = 45 handshakes.

Questions often give the total and ask for n. Double the total and look for two consecutive numbers with that product.

Worked example 2: working back from 66

66 handshakes means n(n − 1) = 132 = 12 × 11, so there were 12 people.

If handshakes happen only between two groups, such as 5 men each shaking hands with 4 women but not with each other, the count is simply 5 × 4.

Worked example 3: two groups

5 men each shake hands with each of 4 women, and nobody else: 20 handshakes.

Joining any two vertices of an n-sided polygon gives nC2 lines. n of those are the sides; the rest are diagonals:

Diagonals = nC2 − n = n(n − 3)/2

The n − 3 comes from each vertex: it cannot join itself or its two neighbours by a diagonal.

Worked example 4: an octagon

8C2 − 8 = 28 − 8 = 20 diagonals.

Worked example 5: which polygon has 35 diagonals?

n(n − 3)/2 = 35 gives n(n − 3) = 70 = 10 × 7, so the polygon has 10 sides.

For interior and exterior angles of polygons, see polygon angles.

  • Single league (round robin): every team plays every other once, so matches = nC2.
  • Double league (home and away): twice that, n(n − 1).
  • Knockout: each match eliminates one team, and all but the winner must be eliminated, so matches = n − 1.

Worked example 6: eight teams in a league

8C2 = 28 matches in a single round robin.

Worked example 7: home and away

The same 8 teams playing home and away: 2 × 28 = 56 matches.

Worked example 8: a knockout

16 teams in a knockout: 15 matches (8 + 4 + 2 + 1).

Handshakes are unordered: A shaking hands with B is the same event as B shaking hands with A. Some questions describe ordered pairs instead, and then you must not halve the count.

Worked example 9: exchanging gifts

Each of 10 friends gives a gift to every other friend. A gift from A to B is different from one from B to A, so the count is 10 × 9 = 90 gifts, twice the 45 handshakes. Greeting cards, letters and phone calls "to each other" usually follow the same rule; read whether each pair acts once or each person acts separately.

Choosing three vertices of a polygon gives a triangle, so an n-sided polygon has nC3 triangles with its vertices as corners.

Worked example 10: triangles in an octagon

8C3 = 56 triangles can be drawn using the vertices of an octagon.

Worked example 11: triangles sharing no side with the octagon

A harder variant asks for triangles none of whose sides is a side of the polygon. The count is n(n − 4)(n − 5)/6, which for n = 8 gives 8 × 4 × 3 ÷ 6 = 16.

With n points and no three on a line, there are nC2 straight lines and nC3 triangles. If m of the points lie on one line, adjust:

  • Lines: nC2 − mC2 + 1. The mC2 lines joining collinear points are really just one line.
  • Triangles: nC3 − mC3. Three collinear points do not make a triangle.

Worked example 12: lines

10 points, 4 of them collinear: 45 − 6 + 1 = 40 lines.

Worked example 13: triangles

The same points: 120 − 4 = 116 triangles.

Common traps: forgetting to halve the handshake count, counting sides as diagonals, using nC2 for a knockout, and forgetting the "+ 1" when collinear points share a line.

Q1. At a meeting, each of 15 people shakes hands once with every other person. How many handshakes are there? easy

✅ Answer: (a) 105

💡 Explanation: Handshakes = 15C2 = 15 × 14 ÷ 2 = 105. 210 counts each handshake twice.

Q2. How many diagonals does a 12-sided polygon have? easy

✅ Answer: (b) 54

💡 Explanation: n(n − 3)/2 = 12 × 9 ÷ 2 = 54. 66 is 12C2, which wrongly includes the 12 sides.

Q3. There were 28 handshakes at a party, everyone shaking hands with everyone else once. How many people were there? medium

✅ Answer: (d) 8

💡 Explanation: n(n − 1)/2 = 28 gives n(n − 1) = 56 = 8 × 7, so n = 8.

Q4. In a league of 10 teams, each team plays every other team twice (home and away). How many matches are played? easy

✅ Answer: (c) 90

💡 Explanation: Single round = 10C2 = 45 matches; playing twice doubles it to 90.

Q5. 12 points lie in a plane, and exactly 5 of them are on one straight line. How many triangles can be formed using these points as vertices? hard

✅ Answer: (b) 210

💡 Explanation: 12C3 = 220, but any 3 of the 5 collinear points form no triangle: 5C3 = 10. So 220 − 10 = 210.

Why divide by 2 in the handshake formula?

Each person shakes n − 1 hands, so n(n − 1) counts every handshake twice: once from each person's side. Dividing by 2 gives each handshake once.

How many matches does a knockout tournament need?

One fewer than the number of teams. Every match knocks out exactly one team, and all but the champion must be knocked out, so n teams need n − 1 matches.

What if people shake hands only with the other group?

Then it is not nC2. With m people in one group and k in the other, each of the m shakes k hands, giving m × k handshakes in all.
