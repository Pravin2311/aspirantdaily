Trigonometry Table: Values of sin, cos, tan, cosec, sec and cot for 0°, 30°, 45°, 60° and 90°

Standard values table • Decimal equivalents • The √n ÷ 2 memory trick • Reciprocal pairs • Complementary angles • Not-defined values

SSC Railway Banking Defence 📅 Last updated: 2026-11-01

Most trigonometry questions in SSC, Railway and Defence papers need only five angles: 0°, 30°, 45°, 60° and 90°. NCERT's Class X textbook gathers the six ratios for these angles in a single table, reproduced below with the same values. Because a ratio depends only on the angle and not on how big the triangle is, these numbers work in every right triangle.

Read the sin and cos rows together. As the angle grows from 0° to 90°, sin climbs from 0 to 1 while cos falls from 1 to 0, and the two meet at 45°.

Options are sometimes given as decimals, or a question asks which of two expressions is larger. These rounded values, to four places, settle such comparisons without a calculator.

Write the numbers 0, 1, 2, 3 and 4 under the five angles, take the square root of each and divide by 2. That gives √0/2, √1/2, √2/2, √3/2 and √4/2, which simplify to 0, 1/2, 1/√2, √3/2 and 1: the whole sin row. Write the same list in reverse order and you have the cos row.

For tan, divide sin by cos at each angle. At 30° that is (1/2) ÷ (√3/2) = 1/√3; at 60° it is (√3/2) ÷ (1/2) = √3. Then the bottom three rows are reciprocals: cosec pairs with sin, sec with cos, and cot with tan. A reciprocal of 0 is not defined, which is why the gaps fall where sin, cos or tan is zero.

As a final check on any value you recall, square the sin and cos of the same angle and add them. The total must be 1, because cos² A + sin² A = 1 holds for every angle from 0° to 90°.

Two angles are complementary when they add to 90°, like 20° and 70°. The ratios of one angle equal the co-ratios of the other:

sin (90° − θ) = cos θ • tan (90° − θ) = cot θ • sec (90° − θ) = cosec θ

The same rules work the other way round, so cos (90° − θ) = sin θ, and so on. They let you turn an unfamiliar angle into a familiar pair. A product such as tan 20° × tan 70° looks impossible without tables, but tan 70° = cot 20°, so the product is tan 20° × cot 20° = 1. Fittingly, the word for cosine began in exactly this idea: NCERT's history note says the cosine arose from computing the sine of the complementary angle, and Aryabhata called it kotijya.

  • ✘ Writing tan 90° = 0. It is not defined; it is cot 90° that equals 0.
  • ✘ Swapping sin 30° and sin 60°. sin 30° is the smaller one, 1/2.
  • ✘ Treating sin A as sin × A. The symbol is one word for the ratio, not a product.
  • ✘ Forgetting that 1/√2 and √2/2 are the same number.

Q1. What is the value of 2 sin 30° cos 60° + tan 45°? easy

✅ Answer: (b) 1.5

💡 Explanation: sin 30° = 1/2, cos 60° = 1/2 and tan 45° = 1. So 2 × 1/2 × 1/2 + 1 = 1/2 + 1 = 1.5.

Q2. Which of these is not defined? easy

✅ Answer: (c) tan 90°

💡 Explanation: tan 90° = sin 90° ÷ cos 90° = 1 ÷ 0, which is not defined. sin 90° = 1, cot 90° = 0 and cos 0° = 1.

Q3. What is tan 20° × tan 70°? medium

✅ Answer: (b) 1

💡 Explanation: 20° and 70° are complementary, so tan 70° = cot 20°. The product is tan 20° × cot 20° = 1.

Q4. sin 60° cos 30° + cos 60° sin 30° equals: medium

✅ Answer: (c) 1

💡 Explanation: (√3/2)(√3/2) + (1/2)(1/2) = 3/4 + 1/4 = 1. It is sin(60° + 30°) = sin 90°.

Q5. If sec θ = 2 and θ is between 0° and 90°, what is θ? easy

✅ Answer: (c) 60°

💡 Explanation: sec θ = 2 means cos θ = 1/2, and cos 60° = 1/2. So θ = 60°.

Do I need to learn the cosec, sec and cot rows separately?

No. Each is the reciprocal of a ratio you already know: cosec is 1 ÷ sin, sec is 1 ÷ cos and cot is 1 ÷ tan. Learn the sin row, read the cos row backwards, and divide to get tan; the other three rows follow by turning each value upside down.

Why is tan 90° not defined but cot 90° equal to 0?

tan 90° = sin 90° ÷ cos 90° = 1 ÷ 0, and division by zero has no value. cot 90° is the same fraction turned over, 0 ÷ 1, which is simply 0.
