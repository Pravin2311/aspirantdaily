Linear Equations Word Problems: Turning Sentences into Two Equations, Then Solving Fast

Name the unknowns • One sentence, one equation • Elimination and substitution • Sum and difference shortcut • Prices, fractions, digits, ages, fares • When there is no solution

SSC Banking Railway 📅 Last updated: 2026-11-02

Linear equations word problems test translation more than algebra. Once the two equations are on paper, solving them takes seconds. A reliable routine:

  1. Name the unknowns with letters that remind you what they mean, such as p for a pen's price.
  2. Turn each fact into one equation. "Costs ₹52 together" becomes "= 52"; "is 3 more than" becomes "= … + 3".
  3. Solve by elimination or substitution.
  4. Substitute back into both original sentences, not just the equations you wrote.

Step 4 matters because a mistake in translation gives a correct solution to the wrong equations. Checking against the words catches it.

If two numbers have sum S and difference D, the larger is (S + D)/2 and the smaller (S − D)/2. This shortcut appears inside many other problems.

Worked example 1: sum 50, difference 12

Larger = (50 + 12)/2 = 31, smaller = 19.

Price questions give two shopping baskets and ask for the price of one item. Multiply to match one coefficient, then subtract.

Worked example 2: pens and pencils

3 pens + 4 pencils = ₹52 and 5 pens + 2 pencils = ₹54. Double the second: 10 pens + 4 pencils = 108. Subtract the first: 7 pens = 56, so a pen costs ₹8. Then 4 pencils = 52 − 24 = 28, so a pencil costs ₹7. Check: 5 × 8 + 2 × 7 = 54.

When the two baskets are mirror images, as in "2 chairs + 3 tables" and "3 chairs + 2 tables", add and subtract the equations instead. Adding gives the price of one of each; subtracting gives the difference.

Worked example 3: a fraction

A fraction becomes 1/2 when 1 is added to the numerator, and 1/3 when 1 is subtracted from the denominator. With numerator x and denominator y: 2(x + 1) = y and 3x = y − 1. Substituting, 3x = 2x + 1, so x = 1 and y = 4. The fraction is 1/4.

For a two-digit number with tens digit a and units digit b, the value is 10a + b, not ab. Reversing the digits gives 10b + a, and the change is always 9(b − a).

Worked example 4: digits

The digits add up to 9, and reversing them increases the number by 27. So b − a = 3 and a + b = 9, giving a = 3 and b = 6. The number is 36, and 63 − 36 = 27.

Worked example 5: ages

A father is three times as old as his son. In 5 years he will be 2.5 times as old. With son s and father 3s: 3s + 5 = 2.5(s + 5), so 0.5s = 7.5 and s = 15. The father is 45. For more age patterns, see problems on ages.

Fare questions combine a fixed charge with a rate. Two journeys give two equations, and subtracting removes the fixed part.

Worked example 6: a taxi fare

A 10 km ride costs ₹105 and a 15 km ride costs ₹155. The extra 5 km cost ₹50, so the rate is ₹10 per km.

Worked example 7: the fixed charge

From the first ride: 105 − 10 × 10 = ₹5 fixed charge. Check: 5 + 15 × 10 = 155.

When a total count is given, one unknown can be written in terms of the other, which leaves a single equation.

Worked example 8: tickets

Adult tickets cost ₹100 and child tickets ₹60. 50 tickets bring in ₹4,200. If all were child tickets, the takings would be 3,000; each adult adds 40 more, so there are (4,200 − 3,000)/40 = 30 adults.

For a₁x + b₁y = c₁ and a₂x + b₂y = c₂, compare the ratios of the coefficients:

ConditionLinesSolutions
a₁/a₂ ≠ b₁/b₂IntersectingExactly one
a₁/a₂ = b₁/b₂ ≠ c₁/c₂ParallelNone
a₁/a₂ = b₁/b₂ = c₁/c₂CoincidentInfinitely many

Worked example 9: 2x + 3y = 5 and 4x + 6y = 12

2/4 = 3/6 = 1/2, but 5/12 is different. Both conditions for parallel lines hold (1 means true), so there is no solution. In a word problem this signals contradictory facts.

  • Writing a two-digit number as a + b or ab. It is 10a + b.
  • Applying "in 5 years" to only one person. Both ages increase by 5.
  • Checking only your equations. Substitute back into the original sentences.
  • Mixing units. Paise and rupees, or minutes and hours, must match before forming equations.
  • Stopping at one variable. Read which quantity the question actually asks for.

Q1. The sum of two numbers is 75 and their difference is 15. What is the larger number? easy

✅ Answer: (b) 45

💡 Explanation: Larger = (sum + difference)/2 = (75 + 15)/2 = 45. The smaller is 30.

Q2. 2 chairs and 3 tables cost ₹2,600, while 3 chairs and 2 tables cost ₹2,400. What is the price of one table? medium

✅ Answer: (d) ₹600

💡 Explanation: Adding: 5 chairs + 5 tables = 5,000, so chair + table = 1,000. Subtracting: table − chair = 200. So a table costs ₹600 and a chair ₹400.

Q3. The digits of a two-digit number add up to 11. Reversing the digits increases the number by 9. What is the number? medium

✅ Answer: (a) 56

💡 Explanation: Reversing changes the number by 9 × (difference of digits), so the units digit is 1 more than the tens digit. With a sum of 11, the digits are 5 and 6: the number is 56.

Q4. A bag has 40 coins, all ₹5 or ₹2, worth ₹134 in total. How many ₹5 coins are there? easy

✅ Answer: (c) 18

💡 Explanation: If all 40 were ₹2 coins, the total would be ₹80. Each ₹5 coin adds ₹3 more, and 134 − 80 = 54, so there are 54 ÷ 3 = 18 coins of ₹5.

Q5. For what value of k do the equations 3x + ky = 7 and 6x + 10y = 15 have no solution? medium

✅ Answer: (b) 5

💡 Explanation: No solution needs 3/6 = k/10 ≠ 7/15. So k = 5, and 5/10 = 1/2 is not equal to 7/15.

Should I use elimination or substitution?

Use elimination when the coefficients line up or can be matched by a small multiple. Use substitution when one equation already gives a variable directly, such as y = 3x.

How many equations do I need?

As many as there are unknowns. Two unknowns need two independent facts from the question. If you can only form one equation, look for a hidden fact such as a total count.

When does a pair of equations have no solution?

For a₁x + b₁y = c₁ and a₂x + b₂y = c₂, there is no solution when a₁/a₂ = b₁/b₂ but this ratio differs from c₁/c₂. The lines are parallel. If all three ratios are equal, there are infinitely many solutions.
