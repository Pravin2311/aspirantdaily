Simplification and BODMAS: The Order of Operations and Its Traps

Bar, ( ), { }, [ ] • 'Of' before division • ÷ and × left to right • + and − left to right • Identity shortcuts • Digit-sum check

SSC Banking Railway 📅 Last updated: 2026-10-23

Simplification questions give a long string of numbers and signs and ask for a single value. The arithmetic is easy; the trap is doing the steps in the wrong order. The order taught for exams is usually remembered as BODMAS, or VBODMAS when the bar is included:

  1. Vinculum (the bar drawn over an expression)
  2. Brackets, from the inside out: ( ), then { }, then [ ]
  3. Of
  4. Division and Multiplication, from left to right
  5. Addition and Subtraction, from left to right

The key detail is in steps 4 and 5. Division and multiplication share the same rank, so you work them in the order they appear, left to right. Addition and subtraction also share a rank. Reading BODMAS as "add before subtract" is the single most common error in this chapter.

Worked example 1: a mixed string

18 − 6 ÷ 3 × 2 + 4. First 6 ÷ 3 = 2, then 2 × 2 = 4. Now 18 − 4 + 4, left to right: 14 + 4 = 18.

"Of" means multiplication, but in exam simplification it is worked before ordinary division and multiplication. That makes "of" behave as if it had its own bracket.

Worked example 2: 48 ÷ 4 of 3

"4 of 3" is 12, so 48 ÷ 12 = 4.

Compare 48 ÷ 4 × 3, where there is no "of": left to right gives 12 × 3 = 36. One word changes the answer ninefold.

Worked example 3: fraction chains

3/4 of 2/3 of 120 is a chain of multiplications: 2/3 × 120 = 80, and 3/4 × 80 = 60. Cancelling first, 3/4 × 2/3 = 1/2, makes it faster still.

A bar, or vinculum, drawn over part of an expression groups it even more tightly than a bracket, so it is worked first. Then clear brackets from the innermost outwards. Write each step on a new line instead of keeping all of it in your head.

Worked example 4: four levels

20 − [8 − {6 − (5 − 3 − 1)}], with a bar drawn over 3 − 1. The bar gives 2. Then (5 − 2) = 3, {6 − 3} = 3, [8 − 3] = 5, and 20 − 5 = 15.

A minus sign in front of a bracket changes the sign of every term inside it when the bracket is removed. Most bracket mistakes come from forgetting this when expanding rather than evaluating.

Worked example 5: the classic trap

15 − 7 + 3. Left to right: 15 − 7 = 8, then 8 + 3 = 11. Adding first, 15 − 10 = 5, is wrong.

A safe habit is to think of each minus sign as belonging to the number after it: 15 + (−7) + (+3). Then the terms can be added in any order and the answer is always 11.

Worked example 6: decimals

0.5 × 0.5 + 0.5 ÷ 5. Multiplication and division first: 0.25 and 0.1. Then 0.25 + 0.1 = 0.35.

Simplification questions often hide an identity. When you see a difference of squares divided by a sum or difference, use a² − b² = (a + b)(a − b) instead of squaring.

Worked example 7: a difference of squares

(73² − 27²) ÷ (73 − 27) = 73 + 27 = 100.

To check a long multiplication without redoing it, compare digit sums. The digit sum of the answer must match the product of the digit sums, both reduced to a single digit.

Worked example 8: the digit-sum check

123 × 456 = 56,088. Check: 123 has digit sum 6 and 456 has 15, which reduces to 6. 6 × 6 = 36 reduces to 9. The answer's digits add to 27, which also reduces to 9, so the result passes.

The check catches most slips, though not a swap of two digits, which leaves the digit sum unchanged.

  • ✘ Adding before subtracting. Work + and − left to right.
  • ✘ Dividing before multiplying when multiplication comes first. Work ÷ and × left to right.
  • ✘ Treating "of" like ordinary ×. Work it before ÷ and ×.
  • ✘ Forgetting to change signs when removing a bracket after a minus.

Q1. Simplify: 36 ÷ 6 × 3 + 2 − 5 easy

✅ Answer: (a) 15

💡 Explanation: Division and multiplication go left to right: 36 ÷ 6 = 6, then 6 × 3 = 18. Then 18 + 2 − 5 = 15, again left to right.

Q2. Simplify: 64 ÷ 4 of 2 + 3 easy

✅ Answer: (c) 11

💡 Explanation: 'Of' is worked before division: 4 of 2 = 8. Then 64 ÷ 8 = 8, and 8 + 3 = 11.

Q3. Simplify: 30 − [12 − {8 − (6 − 2)}] medium

✅ Answer: (b) 22

💡 Explanation: Work from the innermost bracket out: (6 − 2) = 4, {8 − 4} = 4, [12 − 4] = 8, and 30 − 8 = 22.

Q4. What is 2/5 of 3/4 of 800? easy

✅ Answer: (d) 240

💡 Explanation: 'Of' means multiply: 3/4 × 800 = 600, and 2/5 × 600 = 240.

Q5. Simplify: (68² − 32²) ÷ (68 + 32) medium

✅ Answer: (c) 36

💡 Explanation: 68² − 32² = (68 + 32)(68 − 32). Dividing by (68 + 32) leaves 68 − 32 = 36, with no squaring needed.

Q6. Simplify: 50 − 20 + 10 easy

✅ Answer: (b) 40

💡 Explanation: Addition and subtraction have equal priority and go left to right: 50 − 20 = 30, then 30 + 10 = 40. Adding first would give the wrong 20.

Does BODMAS mean division always comes before multiplication?

No. Division and multiplication have equal priority and are worked from left to right, and the same is true for addition and subtraction. The letters only give a memory order; reading the D before the M literally can give wrong answers in other contexts.

What is the 'of' rule?

'Of' means multiplication, but in exam simplification it is worked before ordinary division and multiplication. So 48 ÷ 4 of 3 means 48 ÷ 12 = 4, while 48 ÷ 4 × 3 means 12 × 3 = 36.
