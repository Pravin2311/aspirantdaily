Equal Instalments Under Compound Interest and Simple Interest: Find the Instalment, the Loan or the Interest Paid

Present value of each instalment • Two and three equal instalments • Loan from a given instalment • Total interest paid • Simple-interest instalments for a debt due

SSC Banking 📅 Last updated: 2026-11-02

When a loan is repaid in instalments, the lender is not just collecting the original sum. Each payment arrives later, so its value today is less. Under compound interest at r% a year, a payment made after k years is worth payment ÷ (1 + r/100)ᵏ today. The loan equals the total of all these present values:

P = x/(1 + R) + x/(1 + R)² + … + x/(1 + R)ⁿ, where R = r/100 and x is each instalment.

This one equation answers three kinds of question: find x from P, find P from x, or find the interest paid (n × x − P). For the basic compound interest formula, see compound interest formulas.

Worked example 1: two instalments

₹2,100 is borrowed at 10% compound interest and repaid in two equal annual instalments. x(1/1.1 + 1/1.21) = 2,100. Using fractions, 10/11 + 100/121 = 210/121, so x = 2,100 × 121/210 = ₹1,210.

Worked example 2: three instalments

₹6,620 at 10% repaid in three equal annual instalments. The three factors are 10/11, 100/121 and 1,000/1,331, which add to 3,310/1,331. So x = 6,620 × 1,331/3,310 = ₹2,662.

Working in fractions with denominator 11ⁿ (for 10%) or 21ⁿ (for 5%, since 1.05 = 21/20) keeps the arithmetic exact.

If the instalment is given, simply discount each payment and add.

Worked example 3: loan from the instalment

Two annual instalments of ₹4,410 at 5% compound interest. Present values: 4,410 ÷ 1.05 = 4,200 and 4,410 ÷ 1.1025 = 4,000. The loan was ₹8,200.

Worked example 4: interest paid

In example 1, the borrower pays 2 × 1,210 = ₹2,420 in all, so the interest is 2,420 − 2,100 = ₹320.

Worked example 5: tracing the balance

A year-by-year check of example 1: after one year the loan grows to 2,310; paying 1,210 leaves 1,100. After the second year that becomes 1,210, which the second instalment clears exactly: ₹1,210. Tracing the balance is a reliable way to confirm any instalment answer.

When the instalments are not equal, the balance trace is the quickest method: grow the balance by one period's interest, subtract the payment, and repeat.

Worked example 6: the second payment clears the rest

₹5,000 is borrowed at 10% compound interest. ₹2,500 is paid after one year, and a second payment one year later clears the loan. After year 1 the balance is 5,500 − 2,500 = 3,000; after year 2 it is 3,300. The second payment is ₹3,300.

For half-yearly instalments, halve the annual rate and count half-years as periods. A loan at 10% a year compounded half-yearly behaves exactly like one at 5% per period.

Worked example 7: two half-yearly instalments

₹8,200 at 10% a year, compounded half-yearly and repaid in two equal half-yearly instalments, works at 5% per period: each instalment is ₹4,410, the same as two annual instalments at 5%.

Simple interest questions usually state the debt due at the end of n years. Each instalment paid before the due date earns simple interest until then. For instalments at the end of each year:

Debt due = n·x + x·R·[0 + 1 + 2 + … + (n − 1)], with R = r/100.

Worked example 8: three SI instalments

A debt of ₹1,092 is due in 3 years at 12% simple interest. The first instalment earns 2 years' interest, the second 1 year's, the third none: x(3 + 0.12 × 3) = 3.36x = 1,092, so x = ₹325.

Read the question carefully: "a sum borrowed" (present value) and "a debt due" (future value) lead to different equations.

  • Splitting the loan into equal parts. ₹2,100 ÷ 2 = ₹1,050 ignores interest altogether.
  • Multiplying instead of dividing. Future payments are discounted to today, not grown.
  • Mixing SI and CI methods. SI questions add simple interest to each early payment; CI questions discount by powers of (1 + R).
  • Using decimals too early. 1/1.1 is 10/11 exactly; rounding to 0.909 can shift the answer by a rupee.
  • Forgetting to check. A two-line balance trace confirms the answer in seconds.

For the gap between simple and compound interest, see CI and SI difference.

Q1. ₹8,200 is borrowed at 5% per annum compound interest and repaid in two equal annual instalments. What is each instalment? medium

✅ Answer: (a) ₹4,410

💡 Explanation: x/1.05 + x/1.05² = 8,200. The present values of ₹4,410 are ₹4,200 and ₹4,000, which add to ₹8,200.

Q2. A loan of ₹2,310 at 10% compound interest is repaid in two equal annual instalments. Find each instalment. medium

✅ Answer: (b) ₹1,331

💡 Explanation: ₹1,331 is worth 1,331/1.1 = ₹1,210 and 1,331/1.21 = ₹1,100 today, together ₹2,310.

Q3. A debt of ₹2,550 is due in 2 years. It is cleared by two equal annual instalments at 4% simple interest. What is each instalment? hard

✅ Answer: (d) ₹1,250

💡 Explanation: The first instalment earns interest for 1 year, the second for none: x(1.04) + x = 2,550, so 2.04x = 2,550 and x = ₹1,250.

Q4. Two equal annual instalments of ₹1,210 repay a loan at 10% compound interest. What was the loan? easy

✅ Answer: (c) ₹2,100

💡 Explanation: Discount each payment: 1,210/1.1 = 1,100 and 1,210/1.21 = 1,000. The loan was ₹2,100.

Q5. In the ₹2,310 loan above, repaid by two instalments of ₹1,331, how much interest is paid in total? easy

✅ Answer: (b) ₹352

💡 Explanation: Total paid = 2 × 1,331 = ₹2,662. Interest = 2,662 − 2,310 = ₹352.

Why divide by (1 + r) instead of multiplying?

Each instalment is paid in the future. Dividing by (1 + r/100) once for every year of waiting finds what it is worth today. The loan equals the total of these present values.

Is there a shortcut for two instalments?

With x as the instalment, P = x/(1 + R) + x/(1 + R)², where R = r/100. At 10% the factors are 10/11 and 100/121, so a loan of 2,100 needs x = 2,100 × 121/210 = 1,210.

How is the simple interest version different?

Simple interest questions usually state the debt due at the end. Each instalment paid early earns simple interest until that date, so x(1 + R(n − 1)) + … + x(1 + R) + x = debt due, with R = r/100.
