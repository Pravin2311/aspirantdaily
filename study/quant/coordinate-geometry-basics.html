Section Formula in Coordinate Geometry, Plus Distance, Midpoint, Centroid and Area of a Triangle

Distance formula • Section formula: internal and external • Midpoint • Centroid • Area of a triangle • Collinear points • Fourth vertex of a parallelogram

SSC 📅 Last updated: 2026-11-02

Every point in the plane is written as (x, y). The distance between A(x₁, y₁) and B(x₂, y₂) comes straight from Pythagoras: the horizontal and vertical gaps are the two legs of a right triangle and AB is the hypotenuse.

AB = √[(x₂ − x₁)² + (y₂ − y₁)²]

The distance from the origin to (x, y) is √(x² + y²). Spotting a Pythagorean triplet in the gaps often saves the square root.

Worked example 1: (1, 2) to (4, 6)

The gaps are 3 and 4, so AB = √(9 + 16) = 5.

The distance formula also tests shapes: four equal sides with equal diagonals make a square, and two pairs of equal opposite sides make a parallelogram.

The point P that divides the join of A(x₁, y₁) and B(x₂, y₂) internally in the ratio m : n is:

P = ((mx₂ + nx₁)/(m + n), (my₂ + ny₁)/(m + n))

Notice the cross-pairing: m goes with B's coordinates and n with A's. Mixing them up is the most common error with the section formula in coordinate geometry, so say "m to the far end" as you write it.

Worked example 2: 1 : 2 between (2, 3) and (8, 9)

x = (1 × 8 + 2 × 2)/3 = 4 and y = (1 × 9 + 2 × 3)/3 = 5, giving P = (4, 5). It sits one third of the way from A to B, as a 1 : 2 ratio should.

The midpoint is the special case m = n = 1: ((x₁ + x₂)/2, (y₁ + y₂)/2).

Worked example 3: midpoint of (−2, 4) and (6, −8)

((−2 + 6)/2, (4 − 8)/2) = (2, −2).

For external division, the point lies outside AB, and the plus signs become minus signs: ((mx₂ − nx₁)/(m − n), (my₂ − ny₁)/(m − n)).

Worked example 4: external division

Divide the join of (1, 2) and (4, 5) externally in the ratio 2 : 1. x = (8 − 1)/1 = 7 and y = (10 − 2)/1 = 8, so P = (7, 8), beyond B.

Some questions give the dividing point (or the line it lies on) and ask for the ratio. Write the ratio as k : 1, apply the formula to the coordinate you know, and solve for k.

Worked example 5: where the x-axis cuts a segment

In what ratio does the x-axis divide the join of (2, −3) and (5, 6)? On the x-axis y = 0, so (6k − 3)/(k + 1) = 0, giving k = 3/6 = 1/2. The ratio is 1 : 2.

Worked example 6: the crossing point

With the ratio 1 : 2, x = (1 × 5 + 2 × 2)/3 = 3, so the segment crosses the x-axis at (3, 0).

A quick shortcut: the x-axis divides the join of (x₁, y₁) and (x₂, y₂) in the ratio −y₁ : y₂, and the y-axis in the ratio −x₁ : x₂. A positive ratio means internal division.

The centroid G, where the three medians meet, is the average of the vertices: G = ((x₁ + x₂ + x₃)/3, (y₁ + y₂ + y₃)/3). It divides each median in the ratio 2 : 1 from the vertex. For the other centres, see triangle centres.

Worked example 7: centroid of (1, 2), (3, 4), (5, 9)

((1 + 3 + 5)/3, (2 + 4 + 9)/3) = (3, 5).

The area of a triangle from its vertices is:

Area = ½ |x₁(y₂ − y₃) + x₂(y₃ − y₁) + x₃(y₁ − y₂)|

Worked example 8: (1, 1), (4, 2), (2, 5)

½ |1(2 − 5) + 4(5 − 1) + 2(1 − 2)| = ½ |−3 + 16 − 2| = 5.5 square units.

If the area works out to zero, the three points are collinear. For (1, 2), (3, 6) and (5, 10), the bracket is 1(6 − 10) + 3(10 − 2) + 5(2 − 6) = −4 + 24 − 20 = 0, so the points lie on one line.

Worked example 9: the fourth vertex of a parallelogram

A(1, 2), B(4, 3) and C(6, 6) are three vertices of parallelogram ABCD. The diagonals bisect each other, so A + C = B + D, giving D = (1 + 6 − 4, 2 + 6 − 3) = (3, 5).

  • Pairing m with the wrong point. m multiplies the coordinates of the second point B.
  • Using + for external division. External division uses m − n in the denominator.
  • Forgetting the ½ or the absolute value in the area formula.
  • Squaring a negative gap wrongly. (−8)² is +64; the order of subtraction does not matter for distance.
  • Taking the vertex order of a parallelogram for granted. D = A + C − B only when the vertices are listed in order around the shape.

Q1. What is the distance between the points (−3, 2) and (5, 8)? easy

✅ Answer: (a) 10

💡 Explanation: The differences are 8 and 6, so the distance is √(64 + 36) = √100 = 10.

Q2. Which point divides the join of (−1, 7) and (4, −3) internally in the ratio 2 : 3? medium

✅ Answer: (b) (1, 3)

💡 Explanation: x = (2 × 4 + 3 × (−1))/5 = 1 and y = (2 × (−3) + 3 × 7)/5 = 3. The point is (1, 3).

Q3. What is the centroid of the triangle with vertices (2, 5), (−4, 3) and (8, −2)? easy

✅ Answer: (c) (2, 2)

💡 Explanation: Average the x-coordinates: (2 − 4 + 8)/3 = 2. Average the y-coordinates: (5 + 3 − 2)/3 = 2.

Q4. What is the area of the triangle with vertices (2, 3), (−1, 0) and (2, −4)? medium

✅ Answer: (d) 10.5

💡 Explanation: ½|2(0 + 4) + (−1)(−4 − 3) + 2(3 − 0)| = ½|8 + 7 + 6| = 10.5 square units.

Q5. For what value of k are the points (1, k), (2, 5) and (3, 7) collinear? medium

✅ Answer: (a) 3

💡 Explanation: The x-coordinates go up by 1 each time, so the y-coordinates must go up by the same amount: 5 − k = 7 − 5, giving k = 3. The area formula then gives 0.

What is the difference between internal and external division?

Internal division finds a point between A and B, so the ratio terms are added in the denominator. External division finds a point on the line beyond A or B, so the terms are subtracted: x = (mx₂ − nx₁)/(m − n).

How do I check that three points are collinear?

Put them into the triangle-area formula. If the area is zero, the points lie on one line. Equivalently, the slopes AB and BC are equal.

Why can the area formula give a negative number?

The sign depends on whether the vertices are listed clockwise or anticlockwise. Area is always the absolute value, so drop the minus sign.
