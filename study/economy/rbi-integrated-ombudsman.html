RBI Integrated Ombudsman Scheme 2026 (RB-IOS): Who Is Covered, How to Complain, Compensation Limits and Appeals

Scheme in brief • 2021 to 2026 • Key terms • Who is covered • 30-day and 90-day rules • Non-maintainable complaints • Settlement, Award, rejection • Appeals • Exam traps

The Reserve Bank - Integrated Ombudsman Scheme, 2026 (RB-IOS, 2026) is RBI's free complaint route for customers of banks and certain other regulated entities. RBI describes it as a cost-free, expeditious and non-adversarial alternate grievance redress mechanism for complaints about deficiency in service.

  • In force from: 1 July 2026.
  • Replaces: the Reserve Bank - Integrated Ombudsman Scheme, 2021.
  • Fee: none, for filing or for resolving a complaint.
  • Where to file: online at cms.rbi.org.in, by e-mail to crpc@rbi.org.in, or by post to the Centralised Receipt and Processing Centre (CRPC) in Chandigarh.
  • Compensation: up to Rs 30 lakh for consequential loss, plus up to Rs 3 lakh for lost time, expenses, harassment or mental anguish.

The changeover has a clear rule. Complaints received before 1 July 2026, appeals arising from decisions under RB-IOS 2021, and execution of awards issued under it, all stay under the 2021 scheme.

  • Deficiency in service: a shortcoming or inadequacy in any service that the regulated entity must provide, statutorily or otherwise. It may or may not cause the customer financial loss.
  • RBI Ombudsman: an officer appointed by RBI to examine and deal with complaints from customers of regulated entities about deficiency in service.
  • RBI Deputy Ombudsman: an officer appointed by RBI to assist. The Deputy Ombudsman can consider complaints and can close or reject them to the extent the scheme permits.
  • CRPC: the Centralised Receipt and Processing Centre, which receives e-mail and physical complaints. Online complaints are registered on the CMS portal.

RBI calls the wider set-up its Alternate Grievance Redress (AGR) Framework: the Offices of RBI Ombudsman, the CRPC, Consumer Education and Protection Cells (CEPCs) and the Consumer Education and Protection Department (CEPD). CEPD assists the Appellate Authority and processes appeals.

CategoryCovered under RB-IOS, 2026
BanksAll commercial banks, Regional Rural Banks, State and Central Co-operative Banks, scheduled urban co-operative banks, and non-scheduled urban co-operative banks with deposits of Rs 50 crore and above
NBFCsNBFCs registered with RBI that accept deposits or have customer interface with assets of Rs 100 crore and above, except listed types such as Housing Finance Companies, Core Investment Companies and Primary Dealers

A complaint against an RBI-regulated entity that is not in this list is not maintainable under the scheme. It may be handled under other RBI arrangements, such as the Consumer Education and Protection Cells.

  1. Go to the entity first. The customer must first complain to the bank or other regulated entity, and keep proof of it.
  2. Wait for a reply. The Ombudsman route opens if there is no reply within 30 days (or a longer time set by RBI, NPCI or card network rules), or if the customer is not satisfied with the reply.
  3. File within 90 days. The complaint to the Ombudsman must be made within 90 days of that timeline expiring or of the entity's last communication, whichever is later.

The original complaint to the entity must also have been made within the limitation period under the Limitation Act, 1963.

A complaint can be filed personally or through an authorised representative. The representative cannot be an advocate, unless the advocate is the aggrieved person.

RBI's FAQ lists the non-maintainable categories. The ones most often asked about are:

  • complaints where RBI is only marked in copy, not addressed directly;
  • matters of the entity's commercial judgment or decision;
  • disputes between a vendor and a regulated entity, or between regulated entities;
  • grievances against the management or executives of an entity;
  • employee-employer disputes;
  • a grievance already pending or decided on merits before a court, tribunal, arbitrator or other forum;
  • services outside RBI's regulatory purview;
  • complaints that are abusive, frivolous or vexatious.

A complaint is first checked for maintainability. If it can be taken up, it goes to the regulated entity for a response, and the Ombudsman's office examines both sides' documents and the applicable RBI instructions. It then ends in one of three ways:

OutcomeWhat it means
SettlementThe Ombudsman or Deputy Ombudsman helps the two sides reach a mutually acceptable resolution, through facilitation, advisory or conciliation.
AwardIf there is no settlement and the Ombudsman finds deficiency in service, an Award can direct the entity to act and/or pay compensation.
RejectionIf there is no deficiency in service, or a rejection ground applies, the complaint is rejected with reasons.

There is no limit on the amount in dispute. The caps apply only to compensation: Rs 30 lakh for consequential loss and Rs 3 lakh for time, expenses and mental anguish. Before an Award, both sides get a reasonable opportunity to be heard.

  • Complainant: must send the entity a letter accepting the Award in full and final settlement within 30 days of receiving it, unless they appeal.
  • Entity: must comply and inform the Ombudsman, or appeal where permitted, within 30 days of receiving that letter.
  • Appeal: lies only against an Award, to the Appellate Authority, within 30 days. The Appellate Authority may allow up to 30 more days for sufficient reason.
  • Appellate options: dismiss the appeal, set aside the Award, remand the case, modify the Award, or pass another suitable order.

An entity cannot appeal an Award passed because it failed to furnish the required information or documents.

  • The current scheme is RB-IOS, 2026, in force from 1 July 2026. The 2021 scheme still governs complaints received before that date.
  • Compensation caps: Rs 30 lakh (consequential loss) and Rs 3 lakh (time, expenses, mental anguish). There is no cap on the disputed amount itself.
  • First complain to the entity, wait 30 days, then file within 90 days.
  • The RBI Contact Centre's toll-free number 14448 gives guidance and status updates, but complaints cannot be filed through it.
  • Appeal lies only against an Award, not against a rejection.

Q1. From which date is the Reserve Bank - Integrated Ombudsman Scheme, 2026 in force? medium

✅ Answer: (a) 1 July 2026

💡 Explanation: RBI issued the scheme on 16 January 2026; its FAQ says it comes into force with effect from 1 July 2026.

Q2. Under RB-IOS 2026, the maximum compensation for consequential loss that the RBI Ombudsman can award is: easy

✅ Answer: (b) Rs 30 lakh

💡 Explanation: The Ombudsman can award up to Rs 30 lakh for consequential loss, plus up to Rs 3 lakh for time, expenses and mental anguish.

Q3. A customer complained to a bank and got no reply. After how many days can they approach the RBI Ombudsman (absent a longer RBI, NPCI or card network timeline)? medium

✅ Answer: (c) 30 days

💡 Explanation: The Ombudsman route opens if there is no reply within 30 days, or the higher time set by RBI, NPCI or card network guidelines.

Q4. Within how many days must a complaint reach the RBI Ombudsman after the entity's timeline expires or its last communication? medium

✅ Answer: (d) 90 days

💡 Explanation: RBI's FAQ sets 90 days from the expiry of the timeline or the entity's last communication, whichever is later.

Q5. Which of these is NOT covered under RB-IOS 2026? hard

✅ Answer: (a) Housing Finance Companies

💡 Explanation: Housing Finance Companies are in the list of NBFC types excluded from the scheme; the other three are covered.

Q6. Under RB-IOS 2026, an appeal to the Appellate Authority lies against: medium

✅ Answer: (b) An Award passed by the RBI Ombudsman

💡 Explanation: RBI's FAQ says an appeal is available only against an Award passed by the RBI Ombudsman.

Q7. Which complaint is non-maintainable under RB-IOS 2026? medium

✅ Answer: (c) A dispute about a bank's commercial judgment

💡 Explanation: Matters relating to the commercial judgment or decision of a regulated entity are listed as non-maintainable.

Q8. RBI's toll-free Contact Centre number for ombudsman guidance is: easy

✅ Answer: (d) 14448

💡 Explanation: The FAQ gives toll-free number 14448, with IVRS available 24x7; complaints cannot be filed through it.

What is the compensation limit under the RBI Ombudsman Scheme 2026?

Up to Rs 30 lakh for consequential loss, and up to Rs 3 lakh for loss of time, expenses and mental anguish. There is no limit on the amount in dispute.

When can I complain to the RBI Ombudsman?

After complaining to the bank or entity first, if it does not reply within 30 days or you are unhappy with its reply. File within 90 days after that.
