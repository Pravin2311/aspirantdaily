Repo, Reverse Repo, SDF and MSF Explained: The LAF Corridor and Current RBI Rates

Repo 5.50% • SDF 5.25% • MSF and Bank Rate 5.75% • Fixed reverse repo 3.35% • LAF corridor • October 2026 MPC hike

UPSC SSC Banking 📅 Last updated: 2026-11-06

The repo rate is the rate at which the RBI lends to banks overnight against government securities. The standing deposit facility (SDF) rate is the rate it pays when banks park surplus money with it. The marginal standing facility (MSF) rate is the penal rate for emergency overnight borrowing. Together they form the LAF corridor. RBI's current rates, as shown on rbi.org.in on 6 November 2026:

The SDF arrived in April 2022 and replaced the fixed reverse repo as the floor of the corridor. The key difference is collateral: under reverse repo RBI absorbs money against eligible government securities, while SDF deposits are uncollateralised. RBI also describes the SDF as a financial stability tool. Fixed-rate reverse repo operations are now at RBI's discretion, for purposes it specifies from time to time.

Exam trap: if a question asks for the floor of the LAF corridor, the answer is the SDF rate, not the reverse repo rate. Since April 2022 the floor has been the SDF, and the fixed reverse repo sits well below it at 3.35%.

Under the MSF, banks can borrow overnight from RBI by dipping into their statutory liquidity ratio (SLR) portfolio up to a set limit of 2 per cent. RBI calls it a safety valve against unexpected liquidity shocks. It is placed 25 bps above the repo rate, which makes it the most expensive overnight window in the corridor.

The Bank Rate is the rate at which RBI buys or rediscounts bills of exchange and other commercial papers. It is published under Section 49 of the RBI Act, 1934, and it is the penal rate for banks that fall short on CRR or SLR. It is now aligned with the MSF rate and moves automatically whenever the MSF rate does.

The liquidity adjustment facility (LAF) covers RBI's operations to inject or absorb liquidity: overnight and term repo and reverse repo (at fixed and variable rates), the SDF and the MSF. Outside the LAF, RBI also manages liquidity through outright open market operations (OMOs), forex swaps and the market stabilisation scheme.

The corridor is 50 basis points wide. RBI's operating target is the weighted average call rate (WACR): through liquidity management it aims to keep the WACR aligned with the repo rate, so that a repo change passes through the whole financial system and, in turn, influences aggregate demand.

For day-to-day fine-tuning, RBI's main tool is the 7-day variable rate repo or reverse repo auction (VRR or VRRR), with other variable-rate auctions from overnight up to 14 days.

Because SDF = repo − 0.25% and MSF = Bank Rate = repo + 0.25%, a single MPC decision moves the whole corridor. In October 2026, a 25 bps hike in repo from 5.25% to 5.50% took:

  • the SDF from 5.00% to 5.25%;
  • the MSF and Bank Rate from 5.50% to 5.75%.

The fixed reverse repo rate (3.35%) does not move with these decisions, which is why it now sits far below the floor.

  • Meeting: the MPC's 63rd meeting, 5 to 7 October 2026, chaired by Governor Sanjay Malhotra.
  • Vote: unanimous for a 25 bps hike to 5.50%.
  • Stance: changed to "calibrated tightening", meaning rate cuts are off the table in the near term. Two members, Nagesh Kumar and Ram Singh, wanted the stance kept at neutral.
  • Reasons cited: CPI inflation rose to 4.8% in August 2026 from 4.5% in July, with food, fuel and core prices all rising. The MPC also cited a deficient monsoon, El Niño and high energy prices.

What is the difference between repo and reverse repo?

In a repo, RBI lends money to banks against government and other approved securities, which injects liquidity. In a reverse repo, RBI borrows from banks against eligible government securities, which absorbs liquidity.

Why is the SDF rate higher than the reverse repo rate?

The SDF rate is tied to repo at 25 bps below it, so it moves with every MPC decision. The fixed reverse repo rate stays at 3.35% and is used only at RBI's discretion.
