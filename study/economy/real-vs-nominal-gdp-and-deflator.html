Real vs Nominal GDP and the GDP Deflator: Formula, Steps, Worked Examples and How It Differs from CPI

Nominal GDP = current prices • Real GDP = base-year prices • Deflator = Nominal ÷ Real × 100 • Real = Nominal ÷ Deflator × 100 • CPI uses a fixed basket

UPSC SSC Banking 📅 Last updated: 2026-11-01

Suppose a country's GDP doubles from one year to the next. It is tempting to say production doubled. NCERT warns that this may be wrong: perhaps every price doubled and output did not change at all. To compare GDP across years, or across countries, we need to separate the effect of prices from the effect of production.

Because nominal and real GDP measure the same output, any gap between them comes only from prices. NCERT calls their ratio the GDP deflator, a well-known price index.

  • GDP deflator = Nominal GDP ÷ Real GDP (as a ratio), or × 100 to express it in per cent
  • Real GDP = Nominal GDP ÷ Deflator × 100
  • Nominal GDP = Real GDP × Deflator ÷ 100

In the base year, nominal and real GDP are equal, so the deflator is 100. A deflator of 124 means prices are 24 per cent above the base year. A GNP deflator can be built in exactly the same way.

  1. Pick the base year. Its prices are the constant prices.
  2. Nominal GDP of the current year = Σ (current-year quantity × current-year price).
  3. Real GDP of the current year = Σ (current-year quantity × base-year price). Keep the quantities and change only the prices.
  4. Deflator = nominal ÷ real × 100.
  5. Real growth = (real GDP this year − real GDP last year) ÷ real GDP last year × 100.

A country produces only bread. In 2000 it makes 100 loaves at ₹10 each, so GDP is ₹1,000. In 2001 it makes 110 loaves at ₹15 each. Take 2000 as the base year.

  • Nominal GDP 2001 = 110 × 15 = ₹1650
  • Real GDP 2001 at 2000 prices = 110 × 10 = ₹1100
  • Deflator = 1,650 ÷ 1,100 × 100 = 150 per cent

The answer makes sense: the price of bread really did rise 1.5 times, from ₹10 to ₹15.

The Consumer Price Index (CPI) tracks the price of a fixed basket of goods bought by a representative consumer. You price the same basket in the base year and the current year, then express the current cost as a percentage of the base cost.

NCERT's example: a consumer buys 90 kg of rice and 5 pieces of cloth. In 2000, rice is ₹10 a kg and cloth ₹100 a piece, so the basket costs ₹900 + ₹500 = ₹1,400. In 2005, rice is ₹15 and cloth ₹120, so the same basket costs ₹1,350 + ₹600 = ₹1,950. CPI = 1,950 ÷ 1,400 × 100 ≈ 139.29.

Many goods have two prices: the retail price consumers pay and the wholesale price for bulk trade. They differ because of traders' margins. The index of wholesale prices is the Wholesale Price Index (WPI). Countries such as the USA call it the Producer Price Index (PPI).

Common traps: real GDP uses base-year prices but current-year quantities. Do not mix them up. Growth is always measured on the earlier year's value. In the base year, the deflator is 100.

Q1. Nominal GDP is 800 and the GDP deflator is 160. Real GDP is: easy

✅ Answer: (a) 500

💡 Explanation: Real GDP = nominal GDP ÷ deflator × 100 = 800 ÷ 160 × 100 = 500.

Q2. Nominal GDP is 660 and real GDP is 600. The GDP deflator (per cent) is: easy

✅ Answer: (b) 110

💡 Explanation: Deflator = nominal ÷ real × 100 = 660 ÷ 600 × 100 = 110, so prices are 10 per cent above the base year.

Q3. Real GDP rises from 400 to 500. The real growth rate is: medium

✅ Answer: (c) 25 per cent

💡 Explanation: Growth = (500 − 400) ÷ 400 × 100 = 25 per cent. The base is the earlier year's value, not the later one.

Q4. Which statement about CPI and the GDP deflator is correct? medium

✅ Answer: (d) CPI includes imported goods; the GDP deflator does not

💡 Explanation: NCERT says CPI includes prices of imported goods bought by consumers, while the GDP deflator does not include import prices.

Q5. If nominal GDP doubles between two years while real GDP is unchanged, then: medium

✅ Answer: (b) The price level has doubled

💡 Explanation: Real GDP holds prices fixed, so unchanged real GDP means output did not change. The whole rise came from prices, so the deflator doubled.

What is the gdp deflator formula?

GDP deflator = nominal GDP ÷ real GDP, often multiplied by 100 to show it in per cent. Nominal GDP uses current prices and real GDP uses base-year prices for the same output.

Why is real GDP used for growth rates?

Nominal GDP can rise just because prices rise. Real GDP keeps prices at base-year levels, so its change shows only the change in the volume of goods and services produced.

Is the GDP deflator the same as inflation?

It is a price index, not a rate. The inflation rate by the deflator is the percentage change in the deflator from one year to the next, just as CPI inflation is the change in CPI.
