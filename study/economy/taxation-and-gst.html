🧾 Taxation & GST

Direct vs Indirect • GST Structure • GST 2.0 (2025) • Income-tax Act 2025 • Tax Terms

High Yield Economy 📅 Last updated: Oct 2026
  • A direct tax is paid by the person on whom it is levied (income tax, corporate tax); an indirect tax is passed on to the consumer (GST, customs duty).
  • Article 265 says no tax can be levied or collected except by the authority of law.
  • Two big reforms reshaped the system recently: GST 2.0, with mainly two slabs, from September 22, 2025, and the Income-tax Act, 2025, which replaced the 1961 Act from April 1, 2026.

Key Tax Terms

  • Tax buoyancy — how tax revenue responds to GDP growth including the effect of rate changes. Tax elasticity excludes rate changes.
  • Tax–GDP ratio — total tax revenue as a share of GDP; a measure of a government's ability to raise resources.
  • Laffer curve — beyond a point, raising tax rates reduces revenue because people work, invest or comply less.
  • Cess and surcharge — levies on top of a tax; unlike the main tax, they are not shared with states through the divisible pool.
  • Pigouvian tax — a tax on activities that cause harm, such as pollution or tobacco use.

The GST System

  • Introduced by the 101st Amendment (2016) and launched on July 1, 2017; the idea was recommended by the Kelkar Task Force (2004). It is a destination-based tax on consumption.
  • CGST (Centre) and SGST (state) are charged on sales within a state; IGST is charged by the Centre on inter-state sales and imports, then shared.
  • It subsumed central taxes such as excise duty and service tax, and state taxes such as VAT, entry tax and entertainment tax.
  • Outside GST: petroleum crude, diesel, petrol, natural gas and aviation turbine fuel (their inclusion is left to the GST Council), alcohol for human consumption (excluded by the Constitution) and electricity.
  • The GST Council (Article 279A) is chaired by the Union Finance Minister. The Centre holds one-third of the voting weight and the states two-thirds; decisions need a three-fourths majority, so neither side can decide alone.
  • The GST Network (GSTN) runs the IT backbone; the GST Appellate Tribunal hears disputes.

Income Tax and Corporate Tax

  • The Income-tax Act, 2025, passed in August 2025, replaced the Income-tax Act, 1961 from April 1, 2026. It simplifies the language and cuts the number of sections, and introduces a single "tax year" instead of separate "previous year" and "assessment year". It does not change tax rates by itself.
  • Under the new tax regime (the default since 2023–24), the Budget for 2025–26 made income up to ₹12 lakh effectively tax-free through a rebate under section 87A — up to ₹12.75 lakh for salaried people, with the ₹75,000 standard deduction.
  • In September 2019, the base corporate tax rate was cut to 22% for companies giving up exemptions, and to 15% for new manufacturing companies.

Q1. Which of these is NOT subsumed under GST?

✅ Answer: (d) Alcohol for human consumption

💡 Explanation: Alcohol for human consumption is constitutionally outside GST; states still tax it.

Q2. Under GST 2.0, from September 22, 2025, the two main GST slabs are:

✅ Answer: (a) 5% and 18%

💡 Explanation: The 12% and 28% slabs were removed; a 40% rate applies to luxury and sin goods.

Q3. In the GST Council, the Centre's share of the total voting weight is:

✅ Answer: (c) One-third

💡 Explanation: The Centre has one-third and the states two-thirds; decisions need three-fourths.

Q4. The responsiveness of tax revenue to GDP growth, including the effect of changes in tax rates, is called:

✅ Answer: (b) Tax buoyancy

💡 Explanation: Buoyancy includes discretionary changes; elasticity excludes them.

Q5. Which tax is collected by the Centre on inter-state supplies under GST?

✅ Answer: (c) IGST

💡 Explanation: IGST applies to inter-state supplies and imports and is later shared with the destination state.

🔁 Revise in 60 Seconds

  • Direct (CBDT): income, corporate tax. Indirect (CBIC): GST, customs.
  • GST: 101st Amendment, July 1, 2017; CGST + SGST intra-state, IGST inter-state; Council: Centre 1/3, states 2/3, decisions 3/4.
  • GST 2.0 (Sept 22, 2025): 5% and 18%, plus 40%.
  • Income-tax Act 2025 from April 1, 2026 ("tax year"); ₹12 lakh tax-free via rebate under the new regime.

📌 Prelims Traps to Avoid

  • ✘ Petrol and diesel are outside GST for now, but they are not constitutionally excluded — alcohol is.
  • ✘ Cess and surcharge are not part of the divisible pool shared with states.
  • ✘ The ₹12 lakh relief is a rebate, not a raised exemption limit.

What are the new GST slabs after GST 2.0?

From September 22, 2025, most goods and services fall in two slabs — 5% (merit rate) and 18% (standard rate). A special 40% rate applies to luxury and sin goods such as tobacco products and luxury cars.

What is the Income-tax Act, 2025?

A new law, passed in August 2025, that replaced the Income-tax Act, 1961 from April 1, 2026. It simplifies the language, reduces the number of sections and introduces a single "tax year", without changing tax rates by itself.
