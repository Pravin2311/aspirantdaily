Priority Sector Lending: Categories, Targets by Bank Type, Weaker Sections, PSLCs and Shortfall Rules

8 categories • 40% of ANBC or CEOBSE • Targets for RRBs, SFBs, UCBs, foreign banks • Housing and education limits • 13 weaker sections • PSLCs • RIDF rates

Banking UPSC 📅 Last updated: 2026-11-06

Priority sector lending (PSL) is the RBI rule that makes banks direct a fixed share of their credit to sectors that matter for socio-economic development. The aim is to help segments whose credit needs stay underserved even though borrowers are creditworthy. The current rulebook is the Reserve Bank of India (Priority Sector Lending – Targets and Classification) Directions, 2025. These Directions came into effect on 1 April 2025 and replaced the 2020 Directions. This page follows the version updated as on 11 September 2026.

The Directions apply to every commercial bank, including Regional Rural Banks (RRBs), Small Finance Banks (SFBs) and Local Area Banks, and to Primary (Urban) Co-operative Banks (UCBs). Salary Earners' Banks are excluded. RBI issues them under Sections 21 and 35A, read with Section 56, of the Banking Regulation Act, 1949.

Para 5 of the Directions lists eight categories:

  1. Agriculture
  2. Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises (MSMEs)
  3. Export Credit
  4. Education
  5. Housing
  6. Social Infrastructure
  7. Renewable Energy
  8. Others

"Weaker sections" is not a separate category. It is an overlapping sub-target: a loan to a small farmer counts under agriculture and also under weaker sections.

All targets are a percentage of Adjusted Net Bank Credit (ANBC) or the Credit Equivalent of Off-Balance Sheet Exposures (CEOBSE), whichever is higher. Each is computed on the figure for the corresponding date of the preceding year.

Small and marginal farmers: marginal farmers hold up to 1 hectare and small farmers more than 1 and up to 2 hectares. Landless agricultural labourers, tenant farmers, oral lessees and share-croppers within those limits also count.

Housing loans to a bank's own employees are not priority sector lending.

Para 17 lists 13 kinds of borrower whose priority sector loans count as lending to weaker sections:

  • small and marginal farmers;
  • artisans and village and cottage industries with credit limits up to ₹2 lakh;
  • beneficiaries of government schemes such as NRLM and SRMS;
  • Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes;
  • beneficiaries of the Differential Rate of Interest (DRI) scheme;
  • SHGs and JLGs, and microfinance borrowers;
  • individual women up to ₹2 lakh per borrower;
  • distressed farmers, and distressed non-farm persons up to ₹1 lakh;
  • persons with disabilities;
  • transgender persons;
  • notified minority communities.

Overdrafts availed by PMJDY account holders can also be classified as loans to weaker sections. In Punjab, Meghalaya, Mizoram, Nagaland, Lakshadweep and Jammu and Kashmir, the minority item covers only the notified minorities other than the one in majority there.

District weights: from FY 2024-25, incremental priority sector credit in districts with low credit flow (per capita PSL below ₹9,000) gets a higher weight of 125%. RRBs, UCBs, LABs and foreign banks are exempt from these weight adjustments.

Priority Sector Lending Certificates (PSLCs): these are traded on RBI's e-Kuber portal. A bank with surplus PSL can sell certificates to a bank with a shortfall.

  • There are four types: Agriculture, SF/MF, Micro Enterprises and General.
  • A bank short on a sub-target must buy that specific PSLC.
  • The standard lot size is ₹25 lakh.
  • All PSLCs expire on 31 March.
  • The fee is market-determined. The buyer books it as an expense; the seller books it as miscellaneous income.

Shortfall: achievement is the simple average of all four quarters. Banks that fall short must deposit the shortfall in the Rural Infrastructure Development Fund (RIDF) and other funds with NABARD, NHB, SIDBI or MUDRA. These deposits pay below Bank Rate, by an amount set by the size of the shortfall:

Shortfall in overall targetDeposit rate
Less than 5 percentage pointsBank Rate minus 2 percentage points
5 to under 10 percentage pointsBank Rate minus 3 percentage points
10 percentage points and aboveBank Rate minus 4 percentage points

Q1. The overall PSL target for domestic scheduled commercial banks is: easy

✅ Answer: (a) 40% of ANBC or CEOBSE, whichever is higher

💡 Explanation: Para 7.1 sets 40 per cent of ANBC or CEOBSE, whichever is higher, for domestic banks.

Q2. Within the 18% agriculture target, the small and marginal farmers sub-target is: medium

✅ Answer: (b) 10%

💡 Explanation: Of 18 per cent, 14 per cent is for non-corporate farmers, of which 10 per cent is for SMFs.

Q3. The weaker sections target for Regional Rural Banks is: medium

✅ Answer: (c) 15%

💡 Explanation: RRBs have a 15 per cent weaker sections target; domestic banks, SFBs and UCBs have 12 per cent.

Q4. A marginal farmer, for PSL, holds land of: easy

✅ Answer: (d) Up to 1 hectare

💡 Explanation: Para 9.4 defines marginal farmers as up to 1 hectare and small farmers as 1 to 2 hectares.

Q5. The maximum education loan eligible as priority sector is: easy

✅ Answer: (a) ₹25 lakh

💡 Explanation: Loans to individuals for education, including vocational courses, up to ₹25 lakh qualify.

Q6. PSLCs are traded on which RBI platform? medium

✅ Answer: (b) e-Kuber

💡 Explanation: Annex IIIA says PSLCs are traded through RBI's CBS portal, e-Kuber.

Q7. Which is NOT one of the four types of PSLC? medium

✅ Answer: (c) PSLC Housing

💡 Explanation: The four PSLCs are Agriculture, SF/MF, Micro Enterprises and General; there is no housing PSLC.

Q8. A bank with a 7-point shortfall in its overall PSL target gets RIDF deposit interest of: hard

✅ Answer: (d) Bank Rate minus 3 percentage points

💡 Explanation: A shortfall of 5 to under 10 points earns Bank Rate minus 3 percentage points on RIDF deposits.

What is ANBC in priority sector lending?

Adjusted Net Bank Credit is net bank credit adjusted for items such as RIDF deposits, PSLCs and certain bonds and investments. PSL targets use ANBC or CEOBSE, whichever is higher.

Is a housing loan priority sector lending?

Yes, within limits: up to ₹50 lakh in centres of 50 lakh+ population, ₹45 lakh in 10-50 lakh centres and ₹35 lakh elsewhere, with dwelling cost caps.
