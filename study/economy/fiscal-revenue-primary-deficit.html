Fiscal Deficit Formula, with Revenue, Effective Revenue and Primary Deficit Worked Out from Budget 2026-27

Four definitions • Inputs from Budget at a Glance 2026-27 • FD ₹16,95,768 crore (4.3% of GDP) • RD, ERD and PD worked out • Three-year trend • Practice problem

UPSC SSC Banking State PSC 📅 Last updated: 2026-11-04

The Union Budget reports four deficits. Each answers a different question about the government's finances, and exam questions usually test whether you know which items go into which one. The definitions below follow the Ministry of Finance's Budget at a Glance 2026-27, which publishes all four every year.

DeficitFormulaWhat it tells you
Fiscal deficit (FD)Total expenditure − (revenue receipts + non-debt capital receipts)How much the government must borrow this year
Revenue deficit (RD)Revenue expenditure − revenue receiptsWhether day-to-day spending is covered by day-to-day income
Effective revenue deficit (ERD)Revenue deficit − grants-in-aid for creation of capital assetsThe revenue gap after setting aside grants that build assets (mostly in the States)
Primary deficit (PD)Fiscal deficit − interest paymentsBorrowing needed for this year's activities, excluding the cost of past debt

"Non-debt capital receipts" means capital receipts that do not create a liability: recovery of loans and other receipts such as disinvestment. Borrowings are left out because the fiscal deficit is what borrowings pay for. The Budget's own wording is that the fiscal deficit "is reflective of the total borrowing requirement of Government".

All figures are Budget Estimates for 2026-27 in ₹ crore, from the summary table on page 1 of Budget at a Glance. The row numbers are the ones the Budget uses, so you can match them to the document.

Two quick consistency checks: revenue expenditure plus capital expenditure gives total expenditure (41,25,494 + 12,21,821 = 53,47,315), and total receipts equal total expenditure because borrowings close the gap.

Step 1: Fiscal deficit

Add the receipts that are not borrowing: 35,33,150 + 38,397 + 80,000 = 36,51,547. Subtract from total expenditure: 53,47,315 − 36,51,547 = ₹16,95,768 crore. That is exactly row 7, borrowings and other liabilities, which is the point of the measure.

Step 2: Revenue deficit

Revenue expenditure minus revenue receipts: 41,25,494 − 35,33,150 = ₹5,92,344 crore. The government is borrowing to meet part of its running costs, such as interest, salaries, subsidies and transfers.

Step 3: Effective revenue deficit

Some revenue expenditure is grants to States and other bodies that are used to build assets. Taking those out: 5,92,344 − 4,92,702 = ₹99,642 crore. This much smaller figure is why the Budget reports ERD alongside RD.

Step 4: Primary deficit

Fiscal deficit minus interest: 16,95,768 − 14,03,972 = ₹2,91,796 crore. Interest on past debt accounts for most of this year's borrowing.

Step 5: As a share of GDP

The Budget takes nominal GDP for 2026-27 as ₹393,00,393 crore (₹3,93,00,393 crore in the usual Indian grouping). Divide each deficit by it and multiply by 100:

  • Fiscal deficit: 16,95,768 ÷ 3,93,00,393 × 100 ≈ 4.3%
  • Revenue deficit: 5,92,344 ÷ 3,93,00,393 × 100 ≈ 1.5%
  • Effective revenue deficit: 99,642 ÷ 3,93,00,393 × 100 ≈ 0.3%
  • Primary deficit: 2,91,796 ÷ 3,93,00,393 × 100 ≈ 0.7%

These match the percentages printed in brackets in the Budget.

The fiscal deficit rises in rupees in 2026-27 but falls as a share of GDP, because GDP is assumed to grow faster. Questions that ask whether the deficit "increased" usually turn on this difference, so check which measure is meant.

  • FD equals borrowing. If a question gives borrowings and other liabilities, that number is the fiscal deficit. You do not need the expenditure side at all.
  • FD minus RD. The gap between them equals capital expenditure minus non-debt capital receipts: 12,21,821 − 38,397 − 80,000 = 11,03,424, the same as 16,95,768 − 5,92,344.
  • Interest is revenue expenditure. It sits inside row 10, so it raises both the revenue and fiscal deficits. Only the primary deficit removes it.
  • Do not subtract borrowings twice. A common wrong method subtracts total receipts (including borrowings) from total expenditure, which always gives zero.
  • A negative primary deficit is a primary surplus: receipts cover all spending except interest.

The wider picture, including the FRBM Act and the Finance Commission, is on budget and fiscal policy. How the deficit interacts with RBI policy is covered in fiscal vs monetary policy.

A government's budget shows (₹ crore): revenue receipts 800, recovery of loans 20, disinvestment 30, revenue expenditure 1,000 (of which interest 250 and grants for capital assets 60), and capital expenditure 300.

  • Total expenditure = 1,000 + 300 = 1,300.
  • Fiscal deficit = 1,300 − (800 + 20 + 30) = 450.
  • Revenue deficit = 1,000 − 800 = 200.
  • Effective revenue deficit = 200 − 60 = 140.
  • Primary deficit = 450 − 250 = 200.

Check: FD − RD = 250, and capital expenditure minus non-debt capital receipts = 300 − 50 = 250.

Q1. Primary deficit is equal to: easy

✅ Answer: (b) Fiscal deficit minus interest payments

💡 Explanation: Budget at a Glance defines primary deficit as fiscal deficit less interest payments. It shows borrowing needed for current activities, excluding the cost of past debt.

Q2. Which deficit measures the government's total borrowing requirement? easy

✅ Answer: (d) Fiscal deficit

💡 Explanation: The Budget says the fiscal deficit 'is reflective of the total borrowing requirement of Government'. In the 2026-27 BE it equals row 7, borrowings and other liabilities (₹16,95,768 crore).

Q3. Effective revenue deficit is obtained by subtracting which item from the revenue deficit? medium

✅ Answer: (a) Grants-in-aid for creation of capital assets

💡 Explanation: ERD = revenue deficit − grants-in-aid for creation of capital assets. For 2026-27 BE: 5,92,344 − 4,92,702 = ₹99,642 crore.

Q4. Total expenditure is ₹600 crore, revenue receipts ₹450 crore and non-debt capital receipts ₹50 crore. Interest payments are ₹70 crore. What is the primary deficit? medium

✅ Answer: (c) ₹30 crore

💡 Explanation: Fiscal deficit = 600 − (450 + 50) = 100. Primary deficit = 100 − 70 = ₹30 crore.

Q5. In the 2026-27 Budget Estimates, the fiscal deficit as a percentage of GDP is: easy

✅ Answer: (c) 4.3%

💡 Explanation: Budget at a Glance 2026-27 puts the fiscal deficit at ₹16,95,768 crore, 4.3% of GDP. 4.4% is the 2025-26 BE and RE figure; 4.8% is the 2024-25 actual.

Is fiscal deficit the same as total borrowing?

Yes, in the Union Budget the fiscal deficit equals 'borrowings and other liabilities'. For 2026-27 BE both are ₹16,95,768 crore. The Budget notes that this row includes drawdown of cash balance.

Why is the effective revenue deficit so much smaller than the revenue deficit?

Because a large part of revenue expenditure is grants to States and other bodies for building assets: ₹4,92,702 crore in 2026-27 BE. They count as revenue spending in the Centre's accounts but create capital, so ERD removes them.

Can the primary deficit be negative?

Yes. A negative primary deficit is a primary surplus: receipts other than borrowing cover all spending except interest. India's Union Budget has shown a primary deficit in each of the years in the table above.
