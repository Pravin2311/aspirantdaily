MSME Definition and Classification: New Investment and Turnover Limits from 1 April 2025, Old vs Revised Criteria and Udyam Registration

Current limits • What changed in 2025 • The 2020 composite criterion • Exports and turnover • Udyam Registration • MSMEs in the economy • Exam traps

UPSC SSC Banking 📅 Last updated: 2026-11-10

An enterprise is classed as micro, small or medium by two tests applied together: its investment in plant and machinery or equipment and its annual turnover. The Ministry of MSME revised both limits by a notification dated 21 March 2025, effective from 1 April 2025, following the Union Budget 2025-26.

CategoryInvestment up toTurnover up to
Micro₹2.5 crore₹10 crore
Small₹25 crore₹100 crore
Medium₹125 crore₹500 crore

An enterprise must satisfy both conditions. A unit with ₹2 crore investment but ₹15 crore turnover is not micro; its turnover places it in the small category.

In her Budget speech on 1 February 2025, the Finance Minister announced that the limits "will be enhanced to 2.5 and 2 times respectively". Investment limits rose 2.5 times and turnover limits 2 times.

CategoryInvestment: old → new (₹ crore)Turnover: old → new (₹ crore)
Micro1 → 2.55 → 10
Small10 → 2550 → 100
Medium50 → 125250 → 500

The Ministry says the higher limits are meant to help MSMEs scale up, adopt technology and create jobs without losing their status. The same Budget also announced a higher credit guarantee cover for micro and small enterprises, from ₹5 crore to ₹10 crore, and customised credit cards with a ₹5 lakh limit for micro enterprises registered on the Udyam portal.

The 2025 change built on a larger reform in 2020. Until then, the definition came from the MSMED Act, 2006, which had different criteria for manufacturing and service units and very low rupee limits.

The definition notified in June 2020, effective 1 July 2020, changed three things:

  • One composite formula: there is no longer any difference between manufacturing and service enterprises.
  • Turnover added: classification now uses turnover as well as investment.
  • Exports excluded: exports are not counted in turnover for any micro, small or medium enterprise, so a unit can export more without losing MSME benefits.

The 2020 limits, announced in the Atmanirbhar Bharat package and then raised for medium units on 1 June 2020, were ₹1/5 crore (micro), ₹10/50 crore (small) and ₹50/250 crore (medium). Those are the "old" figures in the table above.

Apply both tests and take the higher category that either test points to. The enterprise is micro only if both figures are within the micro limits.

  • Investment ₹2 crore, turnover ₹8 crore: both are within ₹2.5 crore and ₹10 crore, so it is a micro enterprise.
  • Investment ₹20 crore, turnover ₹120 crore: investment fits small (up to ₹25 crore), but turnover exceeds ₹100 crore, so it is a medium enterprise.
  • Investment ₹130 crore, turnover ₹400 crore: investment exceeds the medium limit of ₹125 crore, so it is not an MSME at all, even though its turnover is within ₹500 crore.

Remember that the turnover figure leaves out exports, so a large exporter can still qualify on turnover.

Formal recognition as an MSME under the MSMED Act comes through the Udyam Registration Portal. According to the Ministry:

  • registration is free of cost and based on self-declaration;
  • Udyam replaced the earlier Udyog Aadhaar Memorandum (UAM);
  • the database is integrated with PAN and GST systems.

An amendment of 18 October 2022 protects growing firms. If an enterprise moves up a category because its investment or turnover rises, it keeps the non-tax benefits of its earlier category for three years from the date of the change.

The Udyam Assist Platform (UAP), launched with SIDBI on 11 January 2023, brings informal micro enterprises into the formal fold. Its certificate is treated at par with a Udyam certificate for priority sector lending.

Micro enterprises make up the vast majority of registrations, followed by small and then medium enterprises.

The Ministry of MSME's Annual Report 2025-26 says the sector contributes around 31.1% of India's GDP and over 48.5% of exports. It also says MSMEs generate employment at a comparatively low capital cost, next only to agriculture.

The Budget speech of February 2025 used different figures: over 1 crore registered MSMEs employing 7.5 crore people, generating 36% of manufacturing and 45% of exports. The two documents cover different years and bases. In an exam, go with the figure the question cites, and quote the latest Annual Report when in doubt.

  • ✘ Quoting the 2020 limits (₹1/5, ₹10/50, ₹50/250 crore) as current. From 1 April 2025 they are ₹2.5/10, ₹25/100 and ₹125/500 crore.
  • ✘ Mixing the multipliers. Investment rose 2.5 times and turnover 2 times, not the other way round.
  • ✘ Using separate manufacturing and service limits. That was the MSMED Act 2006 system, replaced in 2020.
  • ✘ Counting exports in turnover. They are excluded.
  • ✘ Treating either test alone as enough. Both investment and turnover must be within the limit.

What is the new MSME definition from 1 April 2025?

Micro: investment up to ₹2.5 crore and turnover up to ₹10 crore. Small: up to ₹25 crore and ₹100 crore. Medium: up to ₹125 crore and ₹500 crore. Both conditions must be met.

Are exports counted in MSME turnover?

No. Since the 2020 revision, exports are not counted in turnover for any micro, small or medium enterprise, so exporting more does not push a unit out of its category.

Is there a different limit for manufacturing and service MSMEs?

No. Since 1 July 2020 a single composite criterion of investment and turnover applies to both. The old MSMED Act 2006 criteria had separate limits for the two sectors.

Is Udyam Registration free?

Yes. The Ministry of MSME says Udyam Registration is free of cost and based on self-declaration, and the portal is linked with PAN and GST data.
