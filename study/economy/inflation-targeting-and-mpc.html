Inflation Targeting and the Monetary Policy Committee: The 4% Target, Failure Rule and MPC Voting

Section 45ZA target • 4% CPI, 2% to 6% band • Retained to 31 March 2031 • Failure rule • Six-member MPC • Quorum and casting vote • Minutes in 14 days

UPSC Banking 📅 Last updated: 2026-11-06

India follows flexible inflation targeting. The central government, in consultation with the RBI, sets a target for CPI inflation once every five years. The target is 4%, with an upper tolerance limit of 6% and a lower limit of 2%. A six-member Monetary Policy Committee (MPC) then sets the policy repo rate needed to reach it.

The target is measured on the Consumer Price Index (CPI), not the wholesale price index. Section 45ZA says it is fixed once in five years and notified in the Official Gazette.

The central government has notified that the target is missed if average inflation is:

  • above the upper tolerance level (6%) for any three consecutive quarters; or
  • below the lower tolerance level (2%) for any three consecutive quarters.

A single month, or even a single quarter, outside the band is not a failure. If RBI does fail, it must send the central government a report that sets out:

  1. the reasons for failing to achieve the target;
  2. the remedial actions it proposes; and
  3. an estimate of the time within which the target will be achieved once those actions are taken.

Why it matters in late 2026: the October 2026 resolution projects CPI inflation at 6.0% for Q3 of 2026-27 and 5.7% for Q4, and expects headline inflation to average almost 5.8% over the next three quarters. That is close to the upper limit, but below it on average.

The three external members were notified by the central government in the Official Gazette of 1 October 2024. They hold office for four years or until further orders, whichever is earlier.

At the October 2026 meeting, the chair was Governor Sanjay Malhotra. The other members who attended were Nagesh Kumar, Saugata Bhattacharya, Ram Singh, Poonam Gupta and Indranil Bhattacharyya.

  • The MPC must meet at least four times a year. In practice RBI's 2026-27 schedule has six meetings.
  • The quorum is four members.
  • Each member has one vote. If votes are equal, the Governor has a second or casting vote.
  • Each member writes a statement giving the reasons for voting for or against the resolution.
  • The resolution, including the repo rate decision, is published after every meeting.
  • The minutes come out on the 14th day after the meeting, at 5 pm, under Section 45ZL. They include each member's vote and statement.

Members are normally given at least 15 days' notice of a meeting. An emergency meeting can be called with 24 hours' notice.

The MPC's 63rd meeting, from 5 to 7 October 2026, shows the framework at work:

  • Data: CPI inflation rose to 4.8% in August 2026 from 4.5% in July. Core inflation rose to 4.2%.
  • Outlook: CPI inflation projected at 5.2% for 2026-27; real GDP growth at 7.1%.
  • Decision: a unanimous vote to raise the repo rate by 25 bps to 5.50%.
  • Stance: changed to calibrated tightening by majority. Two members, Nagesh Kumar and Ram Singh, wanted it kept at neutral.
  • Minutes: due on 21 October 2026, the 14th day after the decision.

The vote on the rate and the vote on the stance are separate, which is why a decision can be unanimous on one and split on the other.

Once every six months, RBI publishes a Monetary Policy Report. It covers:

  • inflation in the last six months and the near-term outlook;
  • projections of inflation and growth, and the balance of risks;
  • the state of the economy, including the real economy, financial markets, the fiscal situation and the external sector;
  • an updated review of the operating procedure of monetary policy; and
  • an assessment of how well past projections performed.

RBI's Monetary Policy Department assists the MPC. Separately, a Financial Market Committee meets daily to review liquidity conditions.

  • Section 45ZA: inflation target (government, with RBI, every five years). Section 45ZB: MPC.
  • Target: 4% CPI, band 2% to 6%, now retained to 31 March 2031.
  • Failure: average outside the band for three consecutive quarters.
  • MPC: six members, three from RBI and three external; quorum four; Governor's casting vote.
  • Minutes on the 14th day under Section 45ZL.

What is the inflation target of RBI?

The target is 4% CPI inflation with a tolerance band of 2% to 6%. The government retained it on 25 March 2026 for the period up to 31 March 2031.

Who appoints the external members of the MPC?

The central government notifies them in the Official Gazette. The current three external members were notified on 1 October 2024 and serve four years or until further orders.

Can the MPC vote be split?

Yes. In October 2026 the rate hike was unanimous, but two members, Nagesh Kumar and Ram Singh, voted to keep the stance at neutral instead of calibrated tightening.
