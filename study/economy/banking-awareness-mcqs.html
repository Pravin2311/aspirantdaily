Q1. The policy repo rate as shown on rbi.org.in on 6 November 2026 is: easy

✅ Answer: (b) 5.50%

💡 Explanation: RBI's home page listed the policy repo rate at 5.50% after the October 2026 hike.

Q2. The standing deposit facility rate is placed: easy

✅ Answer: (a) 25 bps below the repo rate

💡 Explanation: RBI's overview places the SDF rate 25 basis points below the policy repo rate.

Q3. The upper bound (ceiling) of the LAF corridor is the: easy

✅ Answer: (d) MSF rate

💡 Explanation: The corridor has MSF as ceiling, SDF as floor and the repo rate in the middle.

Q4. Deposits under the standing deposit facility are: medium

✅ Answer: (c) Uncollateralised

💡 Explanation: RBI accepts SDF deposits on an overnight basis without collateral from LAF participants.

Q5. The SDF replaced the fixed reverse repo as the corridor floor in: medium

✅ Answer: (b) April 2022

💡 Explanation: RBI's overview records the SDF's introduction in April 2022 as the new floor.

Q6. The MSF rate is described by RBI as a: medium

✅ Answer: (a) Penal rate for overnight borrowing

💡 Explanation: MSF is the penal rate at which banks borrow overnight by dipping into their SLR portfolio.

Q7. The Bank Rate currently changes automatically with the: easy

✅ Answer: (d) MSF rate

💡 Explanation: RBI aligned the Bank Rate with the MSF rate, so both move together with repo changes.

Q8. Which instrument is NOT part of the LAF? hard

✅ Answer: (c) Market stabilisation scheme

💡 Explanation: RBI lists the MSS with OMOs and forex swaps as tools apart from the LAF.

Q9. RBI's main tool for managing transient liquidity is the: hard

✅ Answer: (b) 7-day variable rate repo or reverse repo auction

💡 Explanation: RBI manages short-term liquidity primarily through 7-day variable rate repo and reverse repo auctions.

Q10. Outright purchase of government securities by RBI: medium

✅ Answer: (a) Injects durable liquidity

💡 Explanation: OMO purchases put money into the banking system; OMO sales take it out.

Q11. The CRR shown on rbi.org.in on 6 November 2026 is: easy

✅ Answer: (d) 3.00%

💡 Explanation: RBI's home page listed the CRR at 3.00% of NDTL on 6 November 2026.

Q12. The SLR shown on rbi.org.in on 6 November 2026 is: easy

✅ Answer: (c) 18.00%

💡 Explanation: RBI's home page listed the SLR at 18.00%; 40% is only its legal ceiling.

Q13. RBI pays what interest on CRR balances held by scheduled commercial banks? medium

✅ Answer: (b) No interest

💡 Explanation: The CRR and SLR Master Direction says RBI does not pay any interest on CRR balances.

Q14. NDTL stands for: easy

✅ Answer: (a) Net demand and time liabilities

💡 Explanation: Both CRR and SLR are worked out as a percentage of a bank's net demand and time liabilities.

Q15. Only which part of approved securities counts towards the SLR? medium

✅ Answer: (d) The unencumbered portion

💡 Explanation: For SLR purposes, only the unencumbered portion of investment in approved securities qualifies.

Q16. Under the Master Direction, SLR cannot exceed what share of demand and time liabilities? hard

✅ Answer: (c) 40 per cent

💡 Explanation: The Direction says the SLR percentage may not exceed forty per cent of demand and time liabilities.

Q17. A bank must maintain at least what share of its required CRR on every day? hard

✅ Answer: (b) 90 per cent

💡 Explanation: The daily minimum is ninety per cent, while the fortnightly average must meet the full requirement.

Q18. A daily CRR shortfall attracts penal interest of: hard

✅ Answer: (a) 3% a year above the Bank Rate

💡 Explanation: The Master Direction sets 3% above the Bank Rate, rising to 5% if the shortfall continues.

Q19. A bank has NDTL of ₹2,00,000 crore. At an SLR of 18%, its required SLR assets are: medium

✅ Answer: (d) ₹36,000 crore

💡 Explanation: Multiply NDTL by the ratio: 2,00,000 × 18 ÷ 100 gives ₹36,000 crore of SLR assets.

Q20. Under MSF, a bank can dip into its SLR portfolio up to: medium

✅ Answer: (c) 2 per cent

💡 Explanation: RBI sets the predefined limit for dipping into SLR holdings under MSF at 2 per cent.

Q21. India's inflation target is: easy

✅ Answer: (b) 4% CPI with a band of 2% to 6%

💡 Explanation: The government notified 4% CPI inflation with 6% upper and 2% lower tolerance limits.

Q22. The inflation target is fixed by the: medium

✅ Answer: (a) Central government in consultation with RBI

💡 Explanation: Under Section 45ZA, the central government determines the target in consultation with the RBI.

Q23. The inflation target is fixed once every: easy

✅ Answer: (d) Five years

💡 Explanation: Section 45ZA requires the target to be set once in five years and gazetted.

Q24. The first Monetary Policy Committee was constituted on: medium

✅ Answer: (c) 29 September 2016

💡 Explanation: RBI's overview says the first MPC was constituted on 29 September 2016.

Q25. How many members does the MPC have? easy

✅ Answer: (b) Six

💡 Explanation: Section 45ZB provides for a six-member MPC: three from RBI and three external members.

Q26. The MPC must meet at least how many times a year? medium

✅ Answer: (a) Four

💡 Explanation: The law needs at least four meetings a year; RBI's 2026-27 schedule has six.

Q27. The 63rd MPC meeting in October 2026 was chaired by: easy

✅ Answer: (d) Governor Sanjay Malhotra

💡 Explanation: The resolution says the meeting was held under the chairmanship of Governor Sanjay Malhotra.

Q28. In October 2026, the MPC changed its stance to: medium

✅ Answer: (c) Calibrated tightening

💡 Explanation: The MPC moved to calibrated tightening; two members preferred to keep the stance neutral.

Q29. CPI inflation for 2026-27 was projected in October 2026 at: medium

✅ Answer: (b) 5.2%

💡 Explanation: The resolution projects 5.2% CPI inflation; 7.1% is the projection for real GDP growth.

Q30. The next MPC meeting after October 2026 is scheduled for: easy

✅ Answer: (a) 2 to 4 December 2026

💡 Explanation: The October 2026 resolution schedules the next MPC meeting for 2 to 4 December 2026.
