GDP vs GNP vs NNP vs NDP: Domestic vs National, Gross vs Net, Market Price vs Factor Cost

GNP = GDP + NFIA • NDP = GDP − Depreciation • NNP = GNP − Depreciation • Factor cost = Market price − Net indirect taxes • NNP at factor cost = National Income

UPSC SSC Banking Railway State PSC 📅 Last updated: 2026-11-01

GDP, GNP, NDP and NNP look like four separate ideas, but each one is GDP with up to three switches flipped. Learn the switches and you can rebuild any formula in the exam hall.

SwitchQuestion it answersHow to flip it
Domestic → NationalWhere was it produced, or who earned it?Add net factor income from abroad (NFIA)
Gross → NetIs wear and tear of capital included?Subtract depreciation
Market price → Factor costAre taxes and subsidies in the price?Subtract indirect taxes, add subsidies

NCERT defines GDP as the aggregate production of final goods and services within the domestic economy during a year. "Domestic" is about geography: anything produced inside the country counts, whether a resident or a non-resident made it and whether a local or a foreign company owns it.

Not all of the income from GDP goes to the country's own citizens. NCERT gives two examples:

  • An Indian citizen working in Saudi Arabia earns a wage that is counted in Saudi Arabia's GDP. She is still an Indian, so India would want to add her earnings.
  • The profits of the Korean-owned Hyundai car factory are produced in India, so they are in India's GDP. They go to foreign owners, so they must be subtracted to get India's national product.

The measure that makes both adjustments is Gross National Product (GNP):

GNP = GDP + Net factor income from abroad

Here NFIA = factor income earned by domestic factors working in the rest of the world − factor income earned by foreign factors working in the domestic economy. "National" is about people: GNP covers the output of a country's normal residents, whether they work at home or abroad.

Because NFIA can be positive or negative, GNP can be larger or smaller than GDP. It is larger when residents earn more abroad than foreigners earn at home.

Machines, vehicles and buildings wear out as they are used. NCERT calls this wear and tear depreciation, also known as consumption of fixed capital. Depreciation is not anyone's income. It is the part of output that would be needed just to replace worn-out capital.

  • NDP = GDP − Depreciation: NCERT notes this tells policy-makers how much must be spent just to maintain current GDP. If lost capital is not replaced, GDP will fall.
  • NNP = GNP − Depreciation: a measure of how much a country can consume in a period.

You can also reach NNP from NDP: NNP = NDP + NFIA. The order of the switches does not matter.

Market price is what the buyer pays, so it includes indirect taxes and is lowered by subsidies. Factor cost is what the producer receives and pays out to the factors of production as wages, rent, interest and profit. It includes no tax.

Factor cost = Market price − (Indirect taxes − Subsidies) = Market price − Net indirect taxes

NCERT's Indian example: petrol is heavily taxed, which pushes its market price above factor cost, while cooking gas is subsidised, which pulls its market price below.

Basic prices lie in between. NCERT splits indirect taxes into two kinds:

KindHow chargedExamplesIn basic prices?
Production taxes (less production subsidies)Independent of the volume producedLand revenue, stamp and registration feesYes
Product taxes (less product subsidies)Per unit of productExcise, service tax, export and import dutiesNo

So GVA at factor cost + net production taxes = GVA at basic prices, and GVA at basic prices + net product taxes = GVA at market prices.

Why it matters for India: GDP at factor cost used to be India's headline measure. In its January 2015 revision, the CSO replaced it with GVA at basic prices, and GDP at market prices, now called simply GDP, became the most highlighted figure.

Suppose a country has GDP at market prices of ₹1,000 crore. Its NFIA is −₹20 crore, depreciation is ₹100 crore, indirect taxes are ₹90 crore and subsidies are ₹10 crore. These are made-up numbers for practice.

  1. GNPMP = 1,000 + (−20) = 980
  2. NDPMP = 1,000 − 100 = 900
  3. NNPMP = 980 − 100 = 880. Check by the other route: NDPMP + NFIA = 900 + (−20) = 880.
  4. Net indirect taxes = 90 − 10 = 80, so GDPFC = 1,000 − 80 = 920.
  5. National income (NNPFC) = 880 − 80 = 800.

Exam tip: when a question gives you several items, first decide which of the three switches it wants flipped. Then apply only those adjustments.

NCERT carries the chain further, towards what households actually receive.

  • Personal Income (PI) = NI − undistributed profits − net interest paid by households − corporate tax + transfer payments to households, such as pensions, scholarships and prizes.
  • Personal Disposable Income (PDI) = PI − personal tax payments, such as income tax − non-tax payments, such as fines. This is the income households can spend or save as they choose.
  • National Disposable Income = NNPMP + other current transfers from the rest of the world, such as gifts and aid. It shows the most goods and services the economy has at its disposal.
  • Domestic vs national: domestic is about where production happens. National is about whose residents earn the income. NFIA is the bridge between them.
  • Gross vs net: the only difference is depreciation. Taxes do not come into it.
  • Market price vs factor cost: the difference is net indirect taxes. Direct taxes such as income tax are not part of this switch; they appear only when moving from PI to PDI.
  • NNPMP vs NNPFC: only NNP at factor cost is called national income.
  • Subsidies: they are added, not subtracted, when moving from market price to factor cost.

Q1. GNP at market prices is obtained from GDP at market prices by: easy

✅ Answer: (b) Adding net factor income from abroad

💡 Explanation: NCERT gives GNP = GDP + net factor income from abroad. Subtracting depreciation gives a net measure, not a national one.

Q2. National income in NCERT's terms is: easy

✅ Answer: (d) NNP at factor cost

💡 Explanation: NCERT writes NNP at factor cost as national income: NNP at market prices minus net indirect taxes.

Q3. Profits of a foreign-owned car factory in India are: medium

✅ Answer: (a) Part of India's GDP but deducted when computing India's GNP

💡 Explanation: GDP counts all production inside India. GNP subtracts factor income paid to foreign-owned factors, as in NCERT's Hyundai example.

Q4. If NNP at market prices is 880, indirect taxes are 90 and subsidies are 10, NNP at factor cost is: medium

✅ Answer: (c) 800

💡 Explanation: Net indirect taxes are 90 minus 10, which is 80. So NNP at factor cost is 880 minus 80, which equals 800.

Q5. Since the January 2015 revision, the CSO's headline output measures are: medium

✅ Answer: (d) GVA at basic prices and GDP at market prices

💡 Explanation: NCERT says the CSO replaced GDP at factor cost with GVA at basic prices, and GDP at market prices became the headline measure.

What is the difference between gdp and gnp in one line?

GDP counts production inside a country's borders by anyone; GNP counts income of the country's normal residents wherever they earn it. GNP equals GDP plus net factor income from abroad.

Can GNP be smaller than GDP?

Yes. If foreign-owned factors earn more inside the country than the country's residents earn abroad, net factor income from abroad is negative, so GNP is smaller than GDP.

Why is depreciation subtracted to get NDP and NNP?

Depreciation is the wear and tear of machines and buildings used in production. It does not become anyone's income, so the net measures remove it to show what the economy can use without running down its capital.
