CRR vs SLR: Differences in Form, Interest, Legal Basis and Current Rates

CRR 3.00% • SLR 18.00% • Cash with RBI vs securities with the bank • No interest on CRR • Section 42 vs Section 24 • Penalties

UPSC SSC Banking 📅 Last updated: 2026-11-06

Both ratios are a share of a bank's net demand and time liabilities (NDTL), which for exam purposes means mainly its deposits. The difference is where the money sits and what form it takes:

  • Cash reserve ratio (CRR): a cash balance the bank must keep with the RBI. RBI pays no interest on it.
  • Statutory liquidity ratio (SLR): liquid assets the bank keeps itself, typically unencumbered government securities, cash and gold. Government securities earn their normal interest.

Current levels on rbi.org.in, as at 6 November 2026: CRR 3.00% and SLR 18.00%.

Exam point: the CRR blocks cash with the central bank, so a CRR change directly alters the money banks can lend. The SLR mostly changes how much of a bank's funds must sit in government securities.

RBI defines the CRR as the average daily balance that a bank must maintain with it as a per cent of its NDTL, as on the last Friday of the second preceding fortnight. "Average daily balance" means the average of the balances held at the close of business on each day of a fortnight.

The average alone is not enough. Every scheduled bank, small finance bank and payments bank must keep at least 90% of the required CRR on every day of the fortnight, while the average over the fortnight must still meet the full requirement.

RBI's Master Direction on CRR and SLR states plainly that RBI "does not pay any interest on the CRR balances" kept by scheduled commercial banks. That is why a CRR cut frees up money that banks can lend or invest for a return.

How the CRR has moved

The Master Direction's text (as last updated in December 2024) records the CRR falling in steps: 4.50% until 13 December 2024, 4.25% from the fortnight beginning 14 December 2024, and 4.00% from the fortnight beginning 28 December 2024. RBI's home page shows the CRR at 3.00% on 6 November 2026, so it has been cut by a further one percentage point since then.

Under the Master Direction, every bank must keep in India, in addition to its cash reserves, assets worth not less than a percentage of its demand and time liabilities that RBI notifies. That percentage cannot exceed 40%. The current level is 18% of NDTL, and it must be met at the close of business on any day.

Only the unencumbered part of a bank's investment in approved securities counts as SLR assets. A security already pledged elsewhere does not count, with some exceptions RBI lists.

The link with the MSF

Under the marginal standing facility, a bank can borrow overnight from RBI by dipping into its SLR portfolio up to 2% of NDTL. A bank that uses this facility does not need to seek a specific waiver for the resulting SLR default. Within the SLR requirement, government securities to the extent allowed under the MSF are also counted. See repo, reverse repo, SDF and MSF for the full rate corridor.

RBI's monetary policy overview describes the Bank Rate as the penal rate for shortfalls in meeting reserve requirements. The Bank Rate now equals the MSF rate, which is 5.75%.

Multiply each ratio by NDTL. For a bank with NDTL of ₹1,00,000 crore:

  • CRR at 3.00% means a balance of ₹3,000 crore with RBI, earning nothing.
  • SLR at 18.00% means ₹18,000 crore in SLR assets, mostly interest-bearing government securities.
  • Together, ₹21,000 crore (21%) of NDTL is tied up, leaving ₹79,000 crore for loans and other uses.

A cut in CRR from 4.00% to 3.00% on the same NDTL would release ₹1,000 crore of cash.

  • CRR: cash with RBI, no interest, Section 42 of the RBI Act.
  • SLR: liquid assets with the bank, Section 24 of the BR Act, ceiling 40%.
  • Both are measured against NDTL as on the last Friday of the second preceding fortnight.
  • Daily CRR must be at least 90% of the requirement.
  • As of November 2026: CRR 3.00%, SLR 18.00%, repo 5.50%, Bank Rate 5.75%.

What is the main difference between CRR and SLR?

CRR is a cash balance held with the RBI that earns no interest. SLR is a stock of liquid assets, typically government securities, cash and gold, that the bank holds itself.

Why did the Master Direction show a CRR of 4.00% when RBI's home page shows 3.00%?

The Master Direction text was last updated in December 2024, when the CRR was cut to 4.00%. RBI's home page shows the current level, 3.00%, on 6 November 2026.

Does a bank earn anything on its SLR holdings?

SLR is mostly held as government securities, which carry their normal interest. That is the key contrast with CRR, on which RBI pays no interest at all.
