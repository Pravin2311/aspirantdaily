Current Account Deficit: Meaning, Components and India's Q1 2026-27 Balance of Payments Read Line by Line

Balance of trade • Invisibles • NCERT vs BPM6 labels • Q1 2026-27: goods −86.1, services +51.6, CAD −4.2 • Financing and reserves • Autonomous vs accommodating

UPSC Banking 📅 Last updated: 2026-11-06

A current account deficit (CAD) means a country's payments to the rest of the world on the current account exceed its receipts. The current account records trade in goods, trade in services, income flows and transfers. NCERT puts it plainly: a surplus means the nation is a lender to other countries, and a deficit means it is a borrower from them.

India's latest figure, from the RBI release of 1 September 2026, is a CAD of US$ 4.2 billion, or 0.5 per cent of GDP, in April-June 2026 (Q1 2026-27). The figure a year earlier was US$ 3.4 billion, or 0.4 per cent of GDP. This page explains the components using NCERT Class XII "Open Economy Macroeconomics" (Reprint 2026-27), then reads the RBI table line by line.

NCERT splits the balance on current account into two parts:

PartWhat it measures
Balance of trade (BOT)Value of exports of goods minus value of imports of goods. Exports are credits and imports are debits.
Net invisiblesExports minus imports of invisibles: services, transfers and income flows between countries

Within invisibles, NCERT separates factor income (net earnings on labour, land and capital) from non-factor income (net sale of services such as shipping, banking, tourism and software).

  • Trade surplus: goods exports exceed goods imports.
  • Trade deficit: goods imports exceed goods exports.
  • Current account deficit: total current receipts are less than total current payments.

A country can run a large trade deficit and only a small CAD if its invisibles surplus is large. India's RBI figures below show this.

The RBI's table uses the IMF's sixth-edition manual (BPM6). NCERT notes this change in its Box 6.1: transactions are divided into current, capital and financial accounts, and trade in financial assets such as bonds and shares sits in the financial account. Matching the labels:

What it shows:

  • The goods deficit is the big number. At US$ 86.1 billion, it was up from US$ 68.9 billion. Petroleum, oil and lubricants alone had a net deficit of US$ 37.6 billion.
  • Invisibles cover most of it. Services, primary income and secondary income net to 51.6 − 10.5 + 40.8 = US$ 81.9 billion.
  • Remittances grew. Personal transfer receipts, mainly money sent home by Indians working abroad, rose to US$ 42.9 billion from US$ 33.2 billion.
  • Income outflow fell. The net outgo on primary income, mainly investment income payments, came down to US$ 10.5 billion from US$ 13.3 billion.

NCERT's rule: a country with a CAD "must finance it by selling assets or by borrowing abroad", so any CAD must be financed by a capital account surplus, a net capital inflow. If inflows fall short, the central bank sells foreign exchange from its reserves. NCERT calls this an official reserve sale, and the fall in reserves is the overall balance of payments deficit.

That is what happened in Q1 2026-27:

  • Net FDI inflow: US$ 6.1 billion, up from US$ 5.2 billion.
  • Foreign portfolio investment: net outflow of US$ 9.6 billion, against an inflow of US$ 1.6 billion a year earlier.
  • NRI deposits: net inflow of US$ 2.8 billion. External commercial borrowings: net inflow of US$ 3.3 billion.
  • Capital and financial account excluding reserves: net US$ 2.6 billion, too little to cover the US$ 4.2 billion CAD.
  • Foreign exchange reserves were depleted by US$ 8.1 billion on a BoP basis, against an accretion of US$ 4.5 billion a year earlier.

Errors and omissions of US$ 1.6 billion close the account. NCERT calls this the third element, needed because it is difficult to record all international transactions accurately.

NCERT separates two kinds of transactions:

  • Autonomous ("above the line"): made for their own reasons, such as profit, independent of the state of the BoP. Trade, FDI and portfolio flows are autonomous.
  • Accommodating ("below the line"): made to bridge the gap. Official reserve transactions are the accommodating item.

In the Q1 2026-27 figures, the reserve drawdown of US$ 8.1 billion was the accommodating item that bridged the gap left by autonomous flows.

  • Balance of trade covers goods only; the current account adds services, income and transfers.
  • A CAD makes a nation a net borrower; a surplus makes it a net lender.
  • India's CAD in Q1 2026-27 was US$ 4.2 billion, 0.5 per cent of GDP.
  • Remittances are secondary income under BPM6, the "transfers" in NCERT's terms.
  • A CAD is financed by net capital inflows or by drawing down reserves.

Q1. Balance of trade covers: easy

✅ Answer: (a) Goods only

💡 Explanation: NCERT defines balance of trade as exports minus imports of goods in a given period.

Q2. Under BPM6, remittances by Indians working abroad appear under: medium

✅ Answer: (b) Secondary income

💡 Explanation: The RBI places personal transfer receipts, mainly remittances, under the secondary income account.

Q3. India's current account deficit in Q1 2026-27 was: easy

✅ Answer: (b) 0.5% of GDP

💡 Explanation: RBI's 1 September 2026 release: US$ 4.2 billion, 0.5 per cent of GDP.

Q4. Which item is accommodating in the balance of payments? medium

✅ Answer: (c) Official reserve transactions

💡 Explanation: NCERT: reserve transactions bridge the BoP gap, so they are accommodating; others are autonomous.

Q5. A current account deficit means the country is a: easy

✅ Answer: (b) Net borrower from the world

💡 Explanation: NCERT: a deficit current account means the nation is a borrower from other countries.

Why is India's CAD small despite a large trade deficit?

Invisibles offset most of it. In Q1 2026-27 the goods deficit was US$ 86.1 billion, but services, income and transfers netted US$ 81.9 billion.

How was the Q1 2026-27 deficit financed?

Net capital and financial inflows of US$ 2.6 billion fell short, so foreign exchange reserves were drawn down by US$ 8.1 billion on a BoP basis.
