NPA Classification: The 90-Day Rule, SMA-1 and SMA-2, Substandard, Doubtful and Loss Assets, and Provisioning

10 NPA triggers • Out of order • SMA dates • 12-month substandard • 25/40/100% doubtful • Standard asset provisions • Borrower-wise rule • Upgrade

Banking UPSC 📅 Last updated: 2026-11-07

RBI defines a non-performing asset (NPA) as an asset, including a leased asset, that has ceased to generate income for a bank. The rules for commercial banks are in the Reserve Bank of India (Commercial Banks – Income Recognition, Asset Classification and Provisioning) Directions, 2025. This page follows the version updated as on 1 October 2026.

For these Directions, "commercial banks" means banking companies, corresponding new banks and the State Bank of India. Small finance banks, payments banks and local area banks are covered by separate directions.

Every loan is classified as either a standard asset or an NPA. NPAs are then graded by age into three classes: substandard, doubtful and loss.

Para 42 lists ten triggers. The ones asked most often:

The crop season is the period up to harvest, as fixed by the State Level Bankers' Committee (SLBC) in each state.

"Out of order" for CC/OD accounts

A cash credit or overdraft account is out of order if any one of these holds:

  • the balance stays continuously above the sanctioned limit or drawing power for 90 days;
  • the balance is within the limit but there are no credits continuously for 90 days;
  • the balance is within the limit but credits do not cover the interest debited in the previous 90 days.

Banks flag an account as overdue in the day-end process on its due date. SMA and NPA dates are the calendar dates on which that process runs. RBI's illustration uses a loan due on 31 March 2021 that stays unpaid:

  • 31 March 2021: overdue;
  • 30 April 2021: SMA-1, after 30 days continuously overdue;
  • 30 May 2021: SMA-2;
  • 29 June 2021: NPA.

Banks must state due dates and examples of SMA/NPA dates in the loan agreement. They must also explain these concepts to customers on their websites.

Unsecured exposure means the realisable value of the security is not more than 10% of the outstanding exposure from the start.

Skipping a stage: a fall in the value of security can move an NPA straight to a later class. If the realisable value of security falls below 50% of the value the bank assessed, the NPA may go straight to doubtful. If it is below 10% of the outstanding, the security is ignored and the account goes straight to loss. Frauds by the borrower can also trigger direct classification.

  • Borrower-wise, not facility-wise: if one facility of a borrower becomes an NPA, all of that borrower's facilities and securities are treated as NPA.
  • Record of recovery: classification follows the record of recovery. Temporary deficiencies, such as a late stock statement or briefly exceeding a limit, do not by themselves make an account NPA.
  • Security is ignored: collateral or net worth is not considered when deciding whether an account is NPA. The only exception is the erosion rule above.
  • Upgrade: an NPA can be upgraded to standard only when the borrower pays the entire arrears of interest and principal. A borrower with several facilities must clear the arrears on all of them.
  • Partial write-offs: a bank that makes a partial or technical write-off cannot show the remaining part of the loan as standard.
  • Income: banks can recognise income on an accrual basis only on standard accounts.
  • Automation: NPA tagging and upgrades must be system-driven, through straight-through processing without manual intervention.
  • An NPA becomes doubtful after 12 months as substandard, which is roughly 15 months from the first overdue date. It does not become doubtful after 12 months from the due date.
  • The 90-day count is "more than 90 days": SMA-1 comes at 30 days and SMA-2 at 60. The SMA-0 stage (1–30 days) is not described in these Directions.
  • Agricultural loans use crop seasons, not 90 days.
  • Loss assets are identified losses not yet written off. Age alone does not make an asset a loss asset.

Q1. A term loan becomes an NPA when interest or principal is overdue for: easy

✅ Answer: (a) More than 90 days

💡 Explanation: Para 42(1) of the RBI Directions sets the trigger at overdue for more than 90 days.

Q2. A substandard asset is one that has been an NPA for: easy

✅ Answer: (b) 12 months or less

💡 Explanation: RBI defines a substandard asset as one that has remained NPA for twelve months or less.

Q3. The general provision on a secured substandard asset is: medium

✅ Answer: (c) 15%

💡 Explanation: Para 85 requires 15 per cent on total outstanding; unsecured substandard exposures need 25 per cent.

Q4. For a crop loan for a short-duration crop, NPA is triggered after overdue for: medium

✅ Answer: (d) Two crop seasons

💡 Explanation: Short-duration crops use two crop seasons; long-duration crops use one crop season.

Q5. Provision on the secured portion of an asset doubtful for one to three years is: medium

✅ Answer: (a) 40%

💡 Explanation: The Para 91 table gives 25 per cent up to one year, 40 per cent for one to three years and 100 per cent beyond.

Q6. In RBI's illustration, a loan due on 31 March 2021 becomes SMA-1 on: hard

✅ Answer: (b) 30 April 2021

💡 Explanation: SMA-1 is tagged after 30 days continuously overdue; SMA-2 follows on 30 May and NPA on 29 June.

Q7. The standard asset provision for commercial real estate loans is: medium

✅ Answer: (c) 1.00%

💡 Explanation: CRE carries 1.00 per cent; CRE-RH 0.75 per cent, farm and housing 0.25 per cent, others 0.40 per cent.

Q8. If the realisable value of security falls below 10% of the outstanding, the asset is: hard

✅ Answer: (d) Classified straight as a loss asset

💡 Explanation: Para 68(2) says the security is ignored and the asset is straightaway classified as loss.

When does a cash credit account become an NPA?

When it is out of order: the balance stays above the limit for 90 days, there are no credits for 90 days, or credits do not cover interest debited over the previous 90 days.

Can an NPA become a standard asset again?

Yes, but only when the borrower pays the entire arrears of interest and principal. A borrower with several facilities must clear arrears on all of them.
